## (a) The spine, in one paragraph

A pod is paid for one number: the part of its P&L that the platform could not have bought for a few basis points through an index. Every chapter of this book owns a tool that changes what that number is. So the thread is not "mention pairs"; it is one question asked of every tool, in that tool's own vocabulary: *what does this let me be long, what does it let me be short, and what is left after the hedge has eaten the beta?* The answer is given through one fictional pod's book of pairs, the **Pair Book**, which the reader watches being built, sized, stressed, explained, regulated, automated and finally paid for, in reading order: Part I introduces the seat and the trade (a pod PM's Tuesday, the same bond seen from seven chairs, the RSSG instinct recognised as intra-capital-structure RV); Part II formalises it (hedge ratios from bond math, a pair as the difference of two jump processes, capital-structure arbitrage as the textbook pair, the residual as the object of prediction, cointegration and clustered inference); Part III makes Chapter 8 and a new Chapter 8-D the canonical home of the mechanics, 8-B the home of the pod risk regime, 8-C the home of the pair explain, then stresses the book against factors, trend, defensive HY and cross-asset allocation; Part IV runs the pairs through the restructuring machine, where the short leg gaps up on an exchange and seniority turns out to be a document variable; Part V operates the two legs in the market (legging, portfolio trades, ETFs, loans, the borrow rules); Part VI makes Chapter 24 the home of the short leg's basis; Parts VII and VIII scale it (screening, infrastructure, pod economics and capacity). Each beat teaches one RV truth the reader could not get from any other chapter, and each is written as a scene or a labeled `> **The Pair.**` box that states, every time, what is hedged, what idio remains, and what leaks.

## (c) Canonical homes, and the decision on a dedicated chapter

- **Mechanics: Ch 8 and the new Ch 8-D.** Ch 8 keeps what it has: the return decomposition (§8.1), DTS (§8.4, including the DTS-neutral pair that carries nothing), the hedge matrix (§8.6) and the IDO ledger (§8.8). It gains the T1 proof as a scene (the Pair Book's twelve residuals regressed on each other) and hands off the rest. **Ch 8-D, "The Pair Book: Long One Credit, Short Another"** (`08_ch08d_the_pair_book_long_one_credit_short_another`) becomes the canonical home for the *life of a pair*: sourcing a pair from an RSSG-shaped view; the short-leg menu (borrowed bond, single-name CDS, CDX, ETF, future, equity) with its costs and bases; the ruler comparison (T2) with the Ben Dor experiment rebuilt and a replication spec; sizing a pair to the stop (T4, T10 combined); the Pair Book ledger as an artifact (thesis, legs, ratio, idio share, capture ratio, what leaked, exit rule); and the exit. It does not re-derive CDS pricing (Ch 24), the risk model (8-B), the explain (8-C) or pod pay (Ch 28).
- **Risk regime: Ch 8-B.** Factor-exposure limits, gross and net, the JTD limit per pair, the stop arithmetic (POD §4), scope, the centre book, leverage. The limit sheet is Dov's x-ray from the risk manager's chair; 1-B already says so.
- **P&L explain: Ch 8-C.** The pair explain with the four line groups (idio, leak, friction, net) and the capture ratio, computed with betas fixed at the start of the day; the FCM roll mismatch from 1-B is its worked morning.
- **Basis and the short leg's legal layer: Ch 24.** Which short leg; the borrow fee and the basis as one variable; cheapest-to-deliver, successors, orphans, manufactured events as risks of the CDS leg. 8-D's menu table points here for every row that says "basis".
- **Pod economics and capacity: Ch 28.** Pass-through, netting, the Sharpe cap, the stop as a short put the platform holds, talent churn, capacity; Dov's annual review is the scene.

**Decision: endorse the dedicated chapter.** Three reasons. First, the short-leg menu and the borrow market have no home anywhere in the book (Ch 24 owns the basis, not the borrow; Ch 22 owns the rules, not the mechanics), and they are the single thing a reader coming from PJT RSSG does not know: at PJT, "short" was a word in a pitch. Second, the ruler comparison is an experiment, not a formula, and Ch 8 is already 21k words. Third, the Pair Book needs a place where the ledger itself is the figure and the reader can see twelve pairs at once; a running thread through 50 files cannot show the book, only its entries. The chapter is narrow by design (8–11k words), scene-built in the pod, and every section that could be stated in another chapter instead points there. Its brief is `state/advisor_briefs/08_ch08d_the_pair_book_long_one_credit_short_another.md`.

## (d) The Pair Book

### The pod (fictional composite; label it so once per scene)

- **Platform.** Vantress Capital Management, a multi-manager platform with about forty pods on the floor where the credit pods sit (Ch 4-C's floor, "forty pods and one coffee machine"). It is a composite built from public descriptions of such firms (Mercer 2023, Cambridge Associates 2025, the *Odd Lots* episodes in POD §9) and names no real firm. Pass-through, overnight factor x-ray, a central risk team, a centre book that nets the pods' index hedges.
- **PM: Dov Adler** (he/him). Already canon in Ch 1-B §1B.2 (not the Dov of Ch 28, as 1-B says). Ex-dealer HY trader, five years on the platform, runs a book of 10–16 credit pairs. Habit: writes every thesis as a sentence with a date in it. His line: "IDO or it didn't happen" is on his monitor, which is the book's Law 1.
- **Analyst: Wen Zhao** (she/her). New to the cast. Ex-sell-side desk analyst, two years in the pod, owns the documents and the borrow sheet: she calls the prime broker's lending desk every morning for availability and fee on every short. Her tic: she keeps the recall history of every borrowed bond as a time series and calls it "the weather."
- **Platform risk manager: Marcus Oyelaran** (he/him). New to the cast. Runs the credit pods' x-ray; the person who calls when the orange line appears. He never asks what the view is; he asks what the book is long "that it did not mean to be." The scope conversation (POD §4.1) is his.
- **The pod next door: Owen Achterberg** (he/him), the capital-structure pod of Ch 4-C §4C.3, on the same floor and platform. His pairs are intra-issuer (secured against unsecured, bond against stock); Dov's are inter-issuer. Part IV uses Owen's book; the Pair Book ledger records both, with the owner named.
- **Theo's link.** Ch 4-C: a pod PM Theo knew before the desk (Owen). Theo sees the Pair Book from outside, as a market maker's counterparty (Ch 1-B §1B.1, Ch 18-B) and as a reader of its explain in the Ch 8-C and Ch 28 drills. He never trades in the pod.

### Mandate and limits (illustrative composite; not any firm's rule)

Allocated capital $250mm; gross up to 6× allocated capital; net market exposure within ±5% of gross in DTS terms and a beta to CDX HY within ±0.05 of gross; sector and rating-bucket DTS cells capped at 15% of gross; single-issuer jump-to-default (long or short, at the risk model's recovery) capped at 2% of capital, $5mm; no borrowed short above 20% of the lender's inventory on loan in that bond, and a recall plan on every short; horizon one to nine months, stated per pair; drawdown rule in the press-reported shape (review at −3.5%, capital halved at −5%, closed at −7.5%, measured peak to trough on allocated capital; POD §4.2, which says this is reported for one firm and no firm publishes a rule); scope North American and European corporate credit in cash, single-name CDS, CDX and iTraxx, credit ETFs and futures, equity only as a delta hedge. The pod does not join ad hoc groups, co-ops or steering committees, which keeps it free to trade and keeps the cast out of real coalitions (cast bible rule 5).
