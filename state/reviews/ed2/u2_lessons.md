# U2 Lessons for the Brief Writer and the Revisers of the Remaining Chapters

*From the quality read of revised Ch 1, 5 and 6 (`u2_quality_read.md`). The process worked where it produced scenes and failed where it stacked new text on old. These rules fix the stacking. Every later brief and every writer should read this before touching a chapter.*

1. **Budget the growth at 1.6–1.9×, not 2.5×.** Ch 1 went 9.9k → 26.8k words and says most things twice. A new scene must retire the old paragraphs that said the same thing. The brief's "Must not change" list protects *topics, numbered equations, Source Extractions with page numbers, fact-checked Trend Watch wording, exercises and URLs*. It does not protect ordinary paragraphs. Say so in every brief.

> **Orchestrator ruling on lesson 1 (2026-10-18, after the Part IV lessons passes).** The 1.6–1.9× growth band measures *duplication*, not length. Once every fact is told once, a chapter may stay above the band. Never cut a single-telling scene, a sourced case, or a protected item just to hit the number: the user asked for longer stories and for nothing to be removed. Ch 13 (2.34×), Ch 14 (2.79×), Ch 15 (2.55×) and Ch 16 (1.99×) are accepted as they stand.

2. **One telling per fact.** Before writing a scene, list the numbers and claims it will use and grep the section for them. Whichever telling survives (usually the scene), the other becomes a one-line pointer. The brief should name the paragraph that dies when the scene is born ("Act 1 replaces §1.8.3 paragraph 1").

3. **How to turn a rule into a scene (120–250 words):** the day the rule was broken → who noticed → what it cost → the sentence the desk says now. Ch 6's `sigma_frequency` vignette and Ch 1's red build are the models. The rule itself is then stated once, in the Finder table (lesson 11), not in a paragraph after the scene.

4. **Ban the templates.** Never write "It breaks in three places", "a seasoned trader checks three things", "the first question to ask is", "Use it for... do not mistake it for... The edge case that bites", or "First... Second... Third..." inside a paragraph. If a character says "The first chair... The second chair...", that is a table in costume: give the table and dramatize one row.

5. **"When to reach for it" lives inside the scene.** Give the judgment to a character at the decision point (Mags: "fallen angel or falling knife?"). Do not append a separate judgment paragraph to a section whose scene already shows the break; it is the fourth telling.

6. **Box budget: at most one Math, Gently per 1,200 words, never two adjacent, at most ten per chapter.** "First use" means first *load-bearing* use. A survey mention (Ch 1 touching Itô on its way past Merton) gets a one-sentence gloss and a pointer to the chapter that owns the concept. When two building blocks are needed at once, write one box in two parts.

7. **Every Math, Gently has a picture, a tiny number the reader can redo in the margin, then the formula.** No box without a number (Ch 1's regression-slope box). Vary the sign-off; when the full version is the next paragraph, say "next", not "§X". A box must not solve an exercise; use different numbers.

8. **Never more than two boxed elements in a row.** Thread one line of narration or dialogue between boxes. Trend Watches and Source Extractions are protected text, so move them: Trend Watches go at the end of their section, after the scene has used their numbers, never between a scene and its payoff.

9. **Scene sizes.** Cold open 800–1,500 words. Long scene 2,000–3,000 words, built on a dated spine, at most four beats, one decision point posed as a card ("write three things: what you bid, in what size, and why") before any answer. The cast disagrees; the cards are graded against what happened; the reader's card is graded too. Write the answer paragraph first, then cut from the beats everything the answer repeats, and from the answer everything the beats showed.

10. **Derivations: box → one-line statement with the equation number → The Full Derivation immediately below.** Not three boxes, then the equation inside a blockquote annotation, then the derivation two subsections later. A numbered equation new to the chapter never lives only inside a blockquote. Keep equation numbers unchanged.

11. **Keep a findable reference layer: the Finder table.** At the top of every Desk Debrief, six to ten rows: *concept · equation · intuition (box) · judgment (scene) · code (listing)*. Promote Long Scenes and their decision points to `####` headings so they appear in the table of contents. Keep the Debrief bullets and the Spec; delete duplicate Specs in the body.

12. **Code explanations open with the moment the function was needed**, one sentence from the chapter's own scene ("At 7:52 the test that failed was this one"), not with "Use it for". If a vignette already shows the edge case, point to it instead of restating it. Place the vignette *before* the Spec it motivates, not after the listing explanation and before a link list.

13. **Fiction labels: once per scene, at the top, in italics,** naming exactly what is invented; "(illustrative)" on the first appearance of every invented desk number; invented model outputs on real names say "the desk's fictional run". Never leave "CHECK", "secondary, CHECK" or "as summarized in search results" in book text: hedge the sentence ("press reports put...") or cut the number, and log the open item in `state/`. Fictional accounts in real transactions must not imply conduct (trading while restricted) that a real participant could be thought to have done.

14. **Cast continuity.** Before writing a war story for Mags, Ines or Kaz, check `state/cast_bible.md` (create it from Ch 1, 5 and 6 now: Mags's 2020 desk and her Serta position; Theo's PJT deals; the running gags and their counters). Add what you invent. Two chapters already tell slightly different versions of Mags's Serta trade.

15. **Cross-chapter numbers travel with their reconciliation.** If Ch 5 explains why \$734mm and \$875mm are both right, Ch 1's Docket Diary needs the same clause. When a dossier gives a figure in two forms, the brief chooses one and says where the other is explained.

16. **What to keep doing.** The cold opens of Ch 5 and 6, Ford in four acts, the Serta two-RFQ story, SVB's five beats, the wobble-plus-coin and odds-form Bayes boxes, and the boring vignettes are the standard. Real event, dated spine, cast that is wrong on purpose, tool arriving as the thing that would have helped. Write more of that and less around it.
