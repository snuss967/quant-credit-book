*Reviewer role: senior partner, elite restructuring advisory group (PJT RSSG-style). Date: 18 Oct 2026. ## 1. Verdict and the three biggest problems

**Verdict.** Where the book touches the documents, it is better than most restructuring associates: §13.2 has the Serta section numbers right (§2.18, §9.02(b), §9.05(g)) and uses Buccola–Nini as a vintage covariate; the §1123(a)(4) indemnity excision, Rule 3001(e)'s publicly-traded-notes carve-out, the §502(b)(6)/(b)(7)/503(b)(9)/*World Imports* block, the Hertz diary's impairment and solvent-debtor points, and the Sound Point and Marathon MNPI orders are all accurate. A partner would trust those pages.

What a partner would *not* trust is the book's picture of the case itself. Edition 1 knows the LMT zoo and the post-reorg aftermarket intimately and knows almost nothing about the eighteen months in between. The corpus has 3 hits for §1129, 1 for cramdown, 0 for "fair and equitable," "unfair discrimination," "best interests," "new value," "liquidation analysis," "credit bid," "stalking horse" (outside the glossary), "KEIP/KERP," "Purdue," "third-party release," "§506," "§1111(b)," "§547/preference," "equitable subordination," "Part 26A," "WHOA," "scheme of arrangement" and "sauvegarde," while Altice France, Thames Water and Chapter 15 are *mentioned* and never explained. "DIP" appears 46 times and is never priced; "roll-up" appears 7 times and is never sized. The planned 12-B state machine is the right skeleton, but at 8–11k words it cannot carry plan valuation, DIP and exit economics, confirmation law, claims trading, tax and cross-border as well. Those need their own homes or they become a paragraph each, which reads as naive.

**The three biggest problems.**

1. **The plan is a black box between "LMT" (Ch 13–14) and "effective date" (Ch 16).** Nothing in the book explains how plan value is *set* (the disclosure-statement range, the expert fight, the market test), how the fulcrum *moves* while the case runs (DIP priming, roll-ups, adequate-protection claims, post-petition interest, administrative burn, the §1129(a)(9) cash wall), or what §1129(b) actually tests (fair and equitable per class type, unfair discrimination, the §1129(b)(2)(A) cram-down rate). Ch 16 says "plan equity value is a negotiated number" and stops; a desk model needs the *distribution* of that number and the identities of who sets it.
2. **The desk never learns the economics of the room it is modeling.** Ad hoc groups are a running gag (64 hits) and a Rule 2019 panel (56 hits), but the book never explains why groups form (fee reimbursement, backstop premiums, consent fees, exit-roll economics), what the RSA/TSA binds (milestones, fiduciary outs, termination events, lock-ups and the "joinder" dynamics that make the co-op counter move), how information walls actually operate (restricted vs public side, cleansing 8-Ks, big-boy letters), or how advisers are paid and what their retention signals. The Priming Risk Score has no covariate for any of this, and every one of those is public and dated.
3. **The book is confidently US-loan-centric at the exact moment the market is not.** Altice France (sauvegarde accélérée, cross-class cram-down), Thames Water (Part 26A, Court of Appeal), Ardagh and Intrum are the 2024–26 cases that moved European credit; none has a forum section, and §14.6's excellent court-as-random-variable framework has no non-US column. Likewise the tax line (§382, COD/§108) appears 49 times as an acronym and never as arithmetic, though it decides whether an exchange happens in or out of court.

## 3. Red-pen items

Specific errors, inconsistencies or naive passages found in the ten opened sections. Items marked *verify* are 2025–26 facts I could not check from here.

1. **`90_appA_notation_and_conventions.md` §A.9, "Cram-down": "Nobody gets crammed up."** Wrong, and a restructuring reader will notice. "Cram-up" is a live term: a junior class confirming a plan over senior objection by *reinstating* the senior debt under §1124(2) (curing defaults, leaving maturity and rate untouched) or by handing seniors a §1129(b)(2)(A) note at a below-market rate (*Charter* 2009, *DBSD*, *Momentive*). It is also the mechanism that makes cheap legacy debt an asset of the estate, which matters to every 2021-vintage capital structure in the book. Replace the joke; add "Reinstatement" and "Cram-up" entries.
2. **§A.9, "Absolute priority rule."** As written it reads as a rule of all plans. It binds only in cram-down against a *rejecting* class and only as to classes junior to that class; consensual deviations (gifting, tips to old equity) are routine. Add the qualifier, since Ch 6 §6.4 already cites Franks–Torous's 78% deviation rate and the two passages currently seem to contradict each other.
3. **`13_ch13_codifying_lmt_game_theory.md` §13.3.1.** The main text blends first and second lien ($F = 2{,}400$, $K_1 = 1{,}075$, "44.8 points") and flags the approximation, then quotes a remand summary that supplies the unblended inputs (\$1.887bn first lien, \$734mm first-lien second-out, \$895mm excluded) *(verify the July 2026 opinion and the \$261.13mm figure)*. The chapter has the data to do the calculation it defers to Exercise 1. Do it in the text: the excluded first-lien lenders' pre-deal recovery at $V=K_1$ is not 44.8 cents, and the transfer to the second-lien participants who jumped a lien is the larger story. The blended number reads as a tell that no two-lien waterfall was run.
4. **§13.9 Docket Diary (Wesco/Incora) vs `14_ch14…` §14.6 Docket Diary.** The two chapters date the same events differently: §13.9 has Judge Isgur "send[ing] to trial" in 2024 and District Judge Crane reversing in "2025"; §14.6 has the bankruptcy court ruling on 17 January 2025 and the district court reversing on 8 December 2025. At least one is wrong, and §13.9's framing of an *indenture* case as a fight over "open market purchase" (a credit-agreement concept) is suspicious; the Incora questions were the two-thirds lien-release threshold, the additional-notes issuance that manufactured it, and whether the steps were one integrated transaction. Reconcile against the opinions by docket number; give Incora one home (13-B).
5. **§13.9, Mitel outcome cited to "Wikipedia citing Bloomberg."** Not an acceptable source in a chapter whose thesis is that the court is a random variable with timestamps. Cite the docket or the 8-K.
6. **§13.2, Family 2.** "Sacred rights are $\theta=1$ for the affected lender" is right, but the paragraph omits the three thresholds that decided Serta-era fights: the *Required Lenders* denominator excludes defaulting lenders and (in post-2017 documents) caps or excludes affiliated-lender and sponsor-held paper; the "amend the amendment section" protection; and the fact that *supermajority* (66⅔%) thresholds exist in loans for lien releases on all or substantially all collateral. Add both as features.
7. **`16_ch16…` §16.1.1.** "The plan equity value is a negotiated number, not an estimate" is correct and then unused: no section says *who* negotiates it, against what exhibit, or what the judge does when the sides are \$300mm apart. Point to 12-C and add the sentence a partner expects: confirmed plan values sit systematically below post-emergence trading values when the fulcrum controls the process (Moyer p.355/369, already cited).
8. **§16.6, leg 1.** "Old equity in a Chapter 11 case is a deep out-of-the-money call on enterprise value struck at all claims, including post-petition interest" is naive by one term: it is struck at all claims *plus administrative and priority claims plus the professional-fee run rate to the effective date*, which in Hertz was a nine-figure number. Admin burn is the theta of bankruptcy equity and the book never names it.
9. **`03_ch03…` §3.6.3.** "The desk itself, the moment it is wall-crossed, stops quoting the name" is the compliance rule stated as if it were the whole process. It omits the cleansing mechanism (the NDA's outside date and the 8-K that follows) and therefore misses that restriction is *temporary and dated*, which is the tradable feature: the desk can forecast when the wall comes down.
10. **`25_ch25…` §25.8.** Accurate on Rule 3001(e); naive on what the feed is for. The transfer notice gives face, not price, but the *transferee identity* maps to a known playbook (a handful of claims funds buy to a blocking position in the GUC class), so the signal is coalition formation in the class that elects the committee. Connect to §25.7's HHI and blocking-share logic.
11. **`05_ch05…` §5.5.1.** The "debt cushion" sentence is right; the section never mentions that the *liquidation analysis* in every disclosure statement is a published, auditable floor for the same distribution. Free data, zero hits in the corpus.
12. **§14.6 Docket Diary, Robertshaw entry.** The remedy point (damages, not restored Required-Lender status) is right; add that the court found the May 2024 transaction a breach *and* that the sponsor-side lenders then filed the company in Chapter 11 within months, which is the "LMT is not the terminal state" law in one line and is currently missing from the entry *(verify dates)*.
13. **Throughout Part IV.** "Fulcrum" is defined in five places with four different sentences (Ch 1, 3, 6, 8, App A). Define it once in 12-B as $\{j : A_j \le V < D_j\}$ under the plan-value distribution and back-reference.

## 5. The five things that would most raise the book's value for this reader

1. **Write 12-C before anything else in Part IV.** The restructuring reader's first question of a book with "PJT RSSG" in the title is "do they know how a plan gets confirmed?" Today the answer is a glossary entry. 12-C plus 12-B turns Part IV from an LMT bestiary into a model of the whole case, and `plan_value_engine.py` is the one engine the ex-banker can build on day one because its inputs are the exhibits she already made.
2. **Price the DIP and the exit note as the uptier with a judge's signature.** This is the book's own thesis (an LMT is the difference of two waterfalls, §13.3) applied to the in-court transaction the book ignores. It reuses the Ch 13 engine, it is where 2026's CLO-and-private-credit DIPs live, and it reframes every roll-up headline as a computable recovery transfer.
3. **Put the room's economics into the PRS.** Fee letters, backstop premiums, consent fees, joinder deadlines, RSA milestones, cleansing dates and adviser retentions are all dated public events. Adding them as covariates, with their lead times measured, is the cheapest alpha upgrade in Part IV and the one only the RSSG reader knows how to source.
4. **Give the court-as-random-variable framework a passport.** A forum atlas and a non-US column in §14.6 convert a US-loan book into a credit book. Altice, Thames, Ardagh and Intrum are the cases a European desk will test the book against.
5. **Ship the Statute Block and the three-model translation table.** Two short appendices that cost a day and buy trust: every legal claim checkable on one page, and every old model mapped to its new function.
