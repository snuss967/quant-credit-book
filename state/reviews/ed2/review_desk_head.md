# Edition 2 Desk-Head Review: Can This Book Run a Real Book?

*Fable desk-head reviewer, 18 Oct 2026. Reader persona: head of a systematic credit trading desk at a top market maker / multi-strategy fund who hires ex-bankers. Inputs: `state/digests/ed1_review_digest.md`, `state/digests/book_outline.md`, the summary sections of `state/reviews/ed1/engagement.md` (§1, §4–6) and `state/reviews/ed1/foundations.md` (§2–3, §6), case-sensitive word-bounded grep counts over `book_src/`, `build_book.sh`, `tools/check_book.py`, `state/exploratory_space.md` §2.4–2.6, and ten opened sections (Ch 8 §8.1, §8.6, §8.8; Ch 9 §9.8; Ch 12 §12.5, §12.6; Ch 26 §26.8; Ch 27 §27.4, §27.8; Ch 28 §28.9). No whole chapters were read and no web research was done. The Edition 2 primers already planned (4-B, 6-B, 7-B, 9-B, 10-B, 12-B, 13-B, 17-B, 18-B, App D, App E) are taken as given and are not re-requested; where a gap below is partly covered by one of them, the entry says so.*

---

## 1. Verdict and the three biggest problems

**Verdict.** The quant content is implementable and, in the ten sections I opened, arithmetically correct (every worked number I recomputed foots: the 37-cent table in §8.1 and §8.8, the 232-contract hedge in §8.6, the EWMA half-life and the AR(1) desmoothing constants in §12.5, the Nuveen Sharpe range in §12.6, the FIX tags and Rule 15c3-5 citation in §27.4). It is more honest about costs, measure, breadth and stale marks than any practitioner text on my shelf, and the "Specification for AI Implementation" blocks are a genuine asset: all 28 chapters ship engines with asserts (153 Python blocks in total), and `tools/check_book.py --run-code` executes every fenced Python block cumulatively per chapter, so the code is at least known to run.

But the book is written by, and for, the person who *designs* the engines. It is close to silent about the person who *runs* them at 7:15 a.m. with yesterday's P&L unexplained by 40 bp and a limit breach e-mail from risk. Between "understands the math" and "can run risk and make money" sit six production disciplines that the book treats as asides, and the corpus counts are blunt about it (case-sensitive, word-bounded, across 474k words): "P&L attribution" 0, "implementation shortfall" 0, "arrival price" 0, "position limit" 0, "corporate action" 0, "variation margin" 0, "run book" 0, "escalat-" 0, "Kupiec"/"backtesting exception" 0, "TCA" 1 file, "stress test" 6 lines, "risk limit" 6 lines, "risk report" 5 lines, "restricted list" 1, "MNPI" 1, "model risk" 6 lines (all in the LLM chapter), "SR 11-7" one section that calls itself unchecked. Capacity, by contrast, is covered well (25 files, 182 lines, with closed forms in §4.8, §10.8, §11.9), and the data layer is half built (provenance rule, bitemporal security master, App B atlas).

**Problem 1: there is no risk model a desk could run tomorrow morning.** Risk *concepts* are everywhere and good (DTS as the unit of beta, ES beside VaR, JTD per unit of par, LVaR, condition-number alarms). Risk *models* exist at two scales only: eleven MAC sectors (§12.5) and one 150-line basket (§19.5). Nothing specifies the bond-level multi-factor model for a 2,000-line book (factor set, loadings for bonds with no history, idiosyncratic variance by rating and price bucket, the Collin-Dufresne–Goldstein–Martin common residual as a factor, the JTD overlay as a separate non-Gaussian layer, the stress library), nothing backtests that model against realized P&L, and no chapter writes the limit sheet that hangs off it. The Ch 28 dashboard (§28.9), the book's closest thing to a risk pack, has no risk-versus-limits panel and no P&L panel at all.

**Problem 2: P&L attribution is a one-bond illustration, not a process.** §8.1 derives the decomposition beautifully for Theo's bond and then says of the production version, "nobody reads it, which is why this section exists," and never specifies it. §8.8's "IDO ledger" is a research ledger (residual vol, monthly IC, its *t*-stat). A desk needs the explain by position and by book, every day, footing to the official P&L, with separate lines for accrued versus pull-to-par, hedge legs, basis, financing, new-trade P&L, fees and FX, a tolerance on the unexplained, and an escalation rule. None of that is in the book, and the PM's actual question, "which signal made the money?", has no engine.

**Problem 3: there is no research-to-production path and no model governance for the book's own engines.** Twenty-eight chapter engines, 153 Python blocks, one cumulative-execution harness, and no description of versioning, parameter freeze, the paper-to-limited-capital-to-full promotion ladder, champion/challenger (present only for LLMs in §26.8), a model inventory, tiering, independent validation, drift monitoring, or post-mortems. SR 11-7 appears once, applied to the stochastic reader, with the parenthetical "the guidance text was not re-checked for this edition." A desk head would not let a single one of these engines near capital without that layer, and the book never tells the reader so.

## 2. Ranked gap list

Severity: **Critical** (the book cannot be used to run a book without it), **High** (a first-year analyst will be caught out), **Medium** (a desk head will notice), **Low** (completeness).

1. **Critical. Bond-level risk model and limit framework.** *Belongs in:* new Ch 8-B (§4). *Write:* the factor taxonomy for a cash-and-derivatives credit book (key-rate DV01s; rating × sector DTS-scaled spread factors; a liquidity factor built from LCS changes; the estimated common residual of §8.8 promoted to a named factor; CDX, ETF and futures basis factors from §8.6); loading estimation with Dimson sums for stale bonds and issuer-level fallbacks for new issues (Man's recipe in §9.8.1, made explicit); idiosyncratic variance by rating and price bucket, with the §8.3 discontinuity (sub-80 price → equity-like) as a regime switch; JTD as a separate issuer-level overlay using Ch 5's LGD distributions, never folded into the Gaussian; one-day and ten-day ES on the mixture; a stress library of at least six dated scenarios (Mar 2020, 2022 rates, Mar 2023 SVB, Oct 2022 LDI, Apr 2025, the 2026 CCC decompression) plus the hedge-basis-with-spreads scenario the §8.6 annotation demands; the model's own backtest (ex-ante versus realized vol by bucket, exception counts with a stated test); and the limit sheet: DTS by rating × sector, CS01 ladder, KRD DV01, issuer JTD top-ten, ES, days-to-liquidate, percent of issue outstanding, with soft/hard limits, an override procedure and a named escalation path. Ship `risk_model.py` and have Chapters 9, 12, 19, 28 import it.
2. **Critical. P&L attribution as a daily production process.** *Belongs in:* new Ch 8-C. *Write:* extend §8.1 from one bond to a book: coupon accrual (reconciled to the custodian) separated from pull-to-par and roll-down (model), rates by key rate, spread by factor, idiosyncratic, convexity, each hedge leg with its own lines (futures variation margin and roll; CDX coupon accrual, price and series-roll basis; ETF price-to-NAV basis), financing and borrow, fees and commissions, new-trade P&L versus composite (day-one P&L), FX; the foot to official P&L and the unexplained tolerance (state a number per book and the escalation when breached); the month-end evaluated-price true-up and the dispute process; attribution by signal with issuer netting (the PM's question); and a worked day in which the unexplained turns out to be a stale CDX mark. Ship `pnl_explain.py` consuming `ido_engine` outputs. The ex-banker hook writes itself: sources and uses.
3. **High. Hedging as an operation.** *Belongs in:* Ch 8 §8.6 expansion plus App D rows. *Write:* rebalance triggers as DV01-drift bands traded off against cost (the "hedge less" result of §8.6 has a time dimension: rebalance less, too); CDX roll mechanics for a protection book (20 Mar / 20 Sep, series basis, index skew versus intrinsic, the roll-cost line; 10-B covers the trend sleeve's view, not the hedger's); single-name CDS as the issuer JTD hedge, importing $N^{\text{JTD}} = (P-R)/(1-R)$ from §24.3 (the §8.6 matrix has equity puts and no single-name CDS, which is backwards for any name with a liquid contract); futures CTD switches and the tail; the cash-flow asymmetry of an unfunded bond against margined hedges, which is a liquidity demand in exactly the stress the hedge was for ("variation margin" has zero hits).
4. **High. Transaction cost analysis.** *Belongs in:* new Ch 2-B. *Write:* the buy-side mirror of 18-B: benchmarks (composite at RFQ time, arrival mid, cover), implementation-shortfall decomposition for RFQ markets (delay, dealer half-spread, impact, the opportunity cost of unfilled and done-away orders from the §27.4 state machine), the leakage test (did the composite move after you asked and before you traded?), dealer scorecards (response rate, hit, cover gap, the dealer's markouts *against you*), protocol scorecards (RFQ versus all-to-all versus PT versus voice, by size bucket and regime), and the cost curves by size and liquidity score that feed $c_i$ in §9.8 and the fill-probability service in §27.4. Close the loop explicitly: the optimizer's cost is estimated from the desk's own TCA, not from LCS averages.
5. **High. Research-to-production and model governance.** *Belongs in:* new Ch 27-B and App F. *Write:* the research platform contract (point-in-time data layer, feature store, the backtest engine's fill model with provenance-typed prices, cost model, survivorship rules, the experiment registry that supplies the trial count to Ch 7's deflated Sharpe); code review, tests, parameter freeze; the promotion ladder (backtest → paper with the §27.4 fill service → limited capital with kill criteria → full) and the evidence required at each rung; champion/challenger for every engine, not only LLMs; the model inventory with tier, owner, inputs, validation tests, monitoring metric and retire criteria (App F); the post-mortem template; the "no retuning in a drawdown" rule, derived (a retune conditioned on the drawdown is look-ahead). Also fix the plumbing: `check_book.py` executes per chapter, so the cross-chapter imports promised for `bondmath.py`, `creditstats.py` and the rest need a `book_src/lib/` (or `code/`) convention on `sys.path`, or "imports" stay prose.
6. **High. The first-year desk analyst's day, and the ex-banker's year-one mistakes.** *Belongs in:* new Ch 3-B. *Write:* the day (6:45 feed and security-master diffs, corporate actions; 7:15 explain and unexplained; 7:30 risk pack against limits; pre-open calendar: new issues, calls, tenders, coupon and record dates, month-end index rebalances, CDX roll weeks, FOMC; intraday: fills loop, hedge drift, RFQ monitoring; 3–6 p.m.: marks, evaluated-price disputes, EOD risk); the week (markouts, hit ratios, model monitoring); the month (index rebalance, true-up, factor review); the quarter (roll, model review). Then the twelve mistakes, each tied to a section: treating a composite as a price; hedging to face not JTD; the long-protection sign; a spread parsed as a price (§27.4's gateway bug); researching one name while the book needs breadth (§28.2); the PM option (§28.6); $\mathbb{Q}$ PDs in $\mathbb{P}$ sizing; retuning in a drawdown; raw markouts; "adjusted" anything (Mags); the information wall after years on the private side; the 40-page memo where the desk wants a five-line Edge Ledger entry. The Edge Ledger of §3.5 becomes the daily artifact.
7. **Medium. Optimizer constraints that bind in practice.** *Belongs in:* Ch 9 §9.8 expansion. *Write:* minimum trade size and lot constraints ("odd lot" 1 hit, "round lot" 0); percent-of-issue-outstanding caps; cash and financing constraints; borrow availability for shorts (TRS, 5 hits); new-issue allocation as a separate flow; the factor-alignment problem (alpha loading on factors the risk model omits); soft constraints with shadow prices (Ch 12 reports them, Ch 9 does not); infeasibility handling; DTS cells as bands, not equalities; and the partial-fill re-optimization loop wired to §27.4. Give Ch 9 (bond level) and Ch 12 (sector level) one constraint vocabulary.
8. **Medium. Data engineering from vendor file to risk number.** *Belongs in:* App B §B.16 expansion and Ch 27 §27.2. *Write:* bond corporate actions (calls, partial calls, tenders, exchanges and CUSIP changes on exchange, coupon steps, defaults, rating and index-membership changes, all point-in-time); the identifier crosswalk (CUSIP/ISIN/FIGI; FIGI has 3 hits); daily data-quality gates (stale marks, decimal shifts, TRACE duplicates, missing marks, spread moves implausible against DTS); position and trade reconciliation to custodian and prime broker; and the signal-price/return-price rule already in Ch 9. 9-B covers the *research* panel; this is the *production* feed.
9. **Medium. Capacity measured from the desk's own fills.** *Belongs in:* Ch 4 §4.8 or Ch 2-B. *Write:* impact regressions by size bucket from TCA, participation rates, a "we are the market" detector (desk share of TRACE volume in traded names), and the crowding signals of §9.10 as a monitored series. The theory is complete; the measurement is absent.
10. **Medium. Compliance and information barriers for people who used to be inside.** *Belongs in:* Ch 3 §3.6.3 expansion or Ch 3-B. *Write:* restricted lists, wall crossings, the private-side/public-side distinction in loans, big-boy letters, and what a pre-trade compliance check actually blocks. One hit each for "restricted list" and "MNPI" in a book aimed at ex-RSSG bankers is the single most surprising omission in the corpus.
11. **Low. Market-maker daily metrics.** *Belongs in:* §28.9 or 18-B. *Write:* inventory aging (0 hits), markout summary by tier, hit ratio and cover gap, residual risk after hedges, fails. The current dashboard is a PM sizing worksheet with no market-maker panel.
12. **Low. Financing and settlement operations.** *Belongs in:* App D (planned) plus §28.5. *Write:* the daily funding ladder, repo roll dates, margin calls as a runway item (an exercise in §28.10 already has it), settlement fails and the fails charge from §22.3 as a desk line.

## 3. Red-pen items

- **§28.9, `DESK = {"sharpe": 2.0, ...}` (28_ch28_pm_playbook.md, Block "Final").** The leverage band of (28.22)–(28.23) is set with an assumed desk Sharpe of 2.0. The book's own rule (§9.12: "Budget for a live net IR near 0.5, not 2"; §4.12 Exercise 2) says 2.0 is a backtest number. At 0.5–0.8 the band may be empty, which is the honest demo result. Make SR an input with a standard error, run the demo at 0.7, and assert the band narrows as SR falls.
- **§28.9 dashboard table.** No P&L panel (daily, MTD, YTD versus budget; unexplained; hedge and basis lines) and no risk-versus-limits panel. A desk-head dashboard without P&L is a sizing worksheet.
- **§8.6 "The instruments, in DV01 terms."** Four instruments, no single-name CDS. For any issuer with a liquid contract, single-name protection is the JTD hedge; Ch 24 derives the ratio and §8.6 never imports it. Equity puts as "the tail leg" is a CCC-book special case presented as the general case.
- **§8.6 "Non-synchronous closes."** The production fix is not Dimson-summed covariances (a research estimator) but snapping every mark to one timestamp (the 3 p.m. New York futures settlement, a 3 p.m. CDX snapshot, a 3 p.m. composite). Say which, and say that the risk system and the P&L system must use the same snap.
- **§8.1 carry line.** $y\,\Delta t$ mixes coupon accrual (certain, reconciled to the custodian) with pull-to-par (model). In a production explain they are separate lines; the chapter's one-bond table hides the distinction that product control lives on.
- **§8.8 "The IDO ledger: proving it."** Three numbers per strategy per month is research hygiene, not a desk ledger. The same residual is needed daily, by position, against a tolerance; the section opened by calling the daily explain "the table nobody reads" and then did not write it.
- **§9.8.1 constraint list.** DTS cell matching as equalities ($\sum_{i\in g} w_i\,\text{DTS}_i = \text{DTS}^{\text{bm}}_g$) across dozens of rating × sector cells, with a 10%/month turnover cap and long-only, is infeasible after a month of drift; practice uses bands with penalties. The cost term $c_i$ is a constant while the section's own Trend Watch says cost is state- and size-dependent; the objective should carry $c_i(\text{size}, \text{regime})$.
- **§12.5 "treating the Treasury factor as independent of the spread factor."** Ch 8 §8.2 and §8.7 are about $\rho_{y,s}$ changing sign twice since 2021. A MAC covariance with $\rho = 0$ hard-coded contradicts Ch 8; make $\rho$ a regime input from the §12.4 HMM.
- **§26.8 SR 11-7 table.** "(paraphrased; the guidance text was not re-checked for this edition)" should not ship. The three pillars (conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring, outcomes analysis) and the date (4 April 2011; OCC 2011-12) are standard and should simply be stated. More important: the table applies SR 11-7 to the LLM only; it is the frame for every engine in the book.
- **§27.4 reference architecture.** The OMS box says "orders, allocations, compliance" and the text covers none of the three beyond Rule 15c3-5 price collars. The pre-trade compliance check that matters most for this book's reader is the restricted list and the private-side flag on a loan; account allocation and post-trade affirmation are absent.
- **§27.8 "Paper versus live."** Correct and important, but the chapter never specifies how a paper RFQ is filled (which dealer, at what cover, with what probability from the §27.4 service). Without that, the paper-live gap cannot be attributed to the components the section lists.
- **§5.10 / corpus-wide.** The book's rule is "report ES and JTD next to any VaR," and "VaR" appears as a word in only four files; good. But no section specifies how book-level ES is computed (historical, parametric mixture, horizon) or backtested. The rule has no engine.
- **Code plumbing (`tools/check_book.py`, `SYSTEM_PROMPT.md` Edition 2 additions).** The harness runs blocks cumulatively *per chapter* in a fresh interpreter. The five Ed2 modules are described as imported by other chapters; without a shared module directory on `sys.path` the imports will fail under the harness, and WP7 ("run per file") will not catch it until the end.

## 5. The five things that would most raise the book's value for this reader

1. **Write 8-B and 8-C as a pair sharing `risk_model.py`.** The risk pack and the explain are the two documents every desk produces every day, and the book has neither. Once they exist, Ch 9, 12, 19 and 28 import one risk model instead of each building a covariance, and the book stops being a set of engines and becomes a desk.
2. **Write 3-B.** The subtitle promises a route from PJT RSSG to the automated desk; the route currently skips the first year. A chapter a desk head can hand a new hire, with the day, the calendar, the Edge Ledger and the twelve mistakes, is the cheapest way to make the book the thing hiring managers buy in fives.
3. **Write 27-B and App F.** Until the promotion ladder and the model inventory exist, the honest answer to "which of these 28 engines may trade?" is "none." Those two files turn the Specification blocks from impressive into usable, and they are the pages a model-risk function would ask to see.
4. **Write 2-B and close the cost loop.** The optimizer (§9.8), the capacity formulas (§4.8, §11.9), the fill service (§27.4) and the paper-live gap (§27.8) all consume a cost model the book never shows how to estimate from the desk's own fills. TCA is the missing input to four existing chapters and the buy-side mirror of 18-B.
5. **Fix §28.9 before it becomes the book's ending.** Add the P&L, risk-versus-limits and market-maker panels, replace the Sharpe of 2.0 with an input and its standard error, and make the Fourteen-Sigma Tuesday risk report the figure that grows across Parts into this dashboard. The last page of the book should be the page a desk head would actually print.
