# U2 Quality Read: Did the Narrative Revision Work? (Ch 1 in full; Ch 5 and Ch 6 sampled)

*Fable quality reader, 18 Oct 2026. Read: `state/user_directives_ed2.md`; style bible §8; `tools/prescriptiveness.py --pre 9ac1c37` for Ch 1, 5, 6; revised Ch 1 end to end (805 lines, 26,758 words); Ch 5 cold open, §5.1.2–5.1.4, §5.6.2; Ch 6 cold open, §6.1, §6.4.4; the Ch 1 brief and log; pre-revision Ch 1 where needed. No web research. Numbers below were re-checked by hand where they appear in boxes.*

## (a) Verdict

The revision did the hard thing. The cold opens of Ch 5 (Carvana, "sit in the chair, 2 June 2023") and Ch 6 ("Column N", SVB on the right-hand monitor), the Ford replay in four acts, Mags's two-RFQ Serta story, and the five dated SVB beats are narrative in the sense the user asked for: a real event with a dated spine, a cast that disagrees and is sometimes wrong, a decision posed before it is answered, and the tool arriving as the thing that would have helped. The best new paragraphs in the three chapters (Ines on "adverse selection on the *franchise*", Mags on the fair coin versus the coin someone has looked at, Kaz's "That is not a diffusion. That is $F$'s maturity jumping to zero", the Ch 5 "wobble plus coin" box whose 12.5% is the chapter's thesis in one number) are better teaching than anything in Edition 1. Intuition now precedes most formulas. The user's headline complaint is answered in roughly forty percent of the text.

The other sixty percent is the problem, and it has three causes, all fixable in the process rather than in the writers.

**First, the chapter is a palimpsest.** The brief's "Must not change" list protected not just topics, equations and fact-checked numbers but whole paragraphs, so the writer laid scenes *over* the old text instead of *in place of* it. Chapter 1 now says most things twice: Ines walks six chairs for 800 words and then the table says the same six things; Act 1 of the Ford replay tells the LQD story and §1.8.3 retells it almost verbatim; Acts 2 and 3 tell the SMCCF and the rebalance, and the retained "What happened, and the answer" tells them again; Theo lists the toxicity features in dialogue and "The market maker's version" lists them in prose. This is why the chapter is 2.5× rather than the 1.6–1.9× a merge would have produced, and it is the main reason the result reads as a novel with a manual stapled to every scene rather than as a novel.

**Second, the prescriptive register did not leave; it moved into new containers.** The modal density rose in Ch 1 (2.9 to 4.2 per 1,000 words) because the revision *added* rule-shaped prose in paragraph form: four "When to reach for it, and when it breaks" sections built on one template ("It breaks in three places... a seasoned trader checks three things"), four "Reading Listing" explanations on another ("Use it for... do not mistake it for... The edge case that bites"), two copies of the Spec, and eighteen Math, Gently boxes that each end "The full version is §X." Bullets fell from 14% to 5%, which the tool rewards, but a numbered list rewritten as "First... Second... Third... Fourth... Fifth" in a paragraph (the chapter's promise, line 29) is the same list. Dialogue that says "The first chair... Their rule is... Their weakness is..." is a table in costume.

**Third, the box density.** Ch 1 carries about 48 boxed elements (18 Math, Gently, 4 Seats, 4 Trend Watches, 14 Source Extractions with annotations, 2 Poker Tables, 2 Specs, Ways This Blows Up, Desk Myth, Docket Diary, Quant-Shop Life): one every 560 words. §1.5 has seven Math, Gently in 3,258 words, three of them consecutive before the reader has seen eq. (1.4). §1.6 has four boxes in a row (Seat, Trend Watch, Trend Watch, Extraction). A reader handed a stack of index cards is not reading a story, however good each card is.

So: **the process works and should be applied to the remaining files, with three changes to the brief template** (permission to merge the old text, a box budget, and a ban on the templates), **plus a fixer pass on Ch 1, 5 and 6** (`u2_fixes_ch01_05_06.md`). Applied unchanged, the process would produce a 900,000-word book in which every chapter takes two hours and says everything twice.

## (d) Bloat and pacing

**Lengths.** Ch 1: 26,758 words against 9,923 (2.7× on the tool's count). The heavy sections are §1.5 (3,258), the Serta scene (2,909), the Ford scene (2,634), §1.4 (2,574), §1.6 (2,164), §1.3.3 (1,982), §1.2 (1,800), and the §1.3 Spec with its vignette (1,539). Ch 5 and Ch 6 are 32,500 and 36,341 words. At this rate the book is 900,000 words, which is not a book a banker reads on the train. The target should have been 1.6–1.9×, reached by merging rather than by cutting topics; the twelve merges in (c) alone return Ch 1 to roughly 24,000 words with nothing lost.

**Scenes that run long without teaching.** Fewer than feared. The Ford replay earns its length; the only soft tissue is the framing (pastries "technically pastries", Mags at the door, Kaz writing the date), perhaps 150 words. The Serta scene's middle (Ines on passing as adverse selection on the franchise, Mags's two coins) is the best teaching in the chapter; its opening 300 words (the quarterly review, the Risk analyst with a train to catch) can lose half. The Ch 5 Carvana cold open is long (about 2,300 words) but every paragraph moves either the stack or the vote; it is the model for the series. The Ch 6 SVB scene is excellent through Beat 4 and then runs a second, un-dramatized version of itself ("The facts", "What the tools say"), which is where the old section was not removed. The one scene that does not teach beyond its table is the §1.2 chair walk.

**Boxes that interrupt.** Three places break the flow badly. (i) Ch 1 §1.5, lines 376–380: three Math, Gently boxes back to back (call option, random walk, Itô), about 550 words of boxes between Kaz's hockey stick and eq. (1.4). (ii) Ch 1 §1.6, lines 470–476: Seat, Trend Watch, Trend Watch, Source Extraction, four boxes in a row, the two Trend Watches being 330-word walls of dated numbers. (iii) Ch 6 §6.1.1, lines 69–79: three Math, Gently in a row; the content is the right content, but the reader meets 900 words of boxes before the first displayed equation. The Trend Watches are protected text, so the fix is placement: move them to the end of their sections, after the narrative has used their numbers, never between a scene and its payoff.

**Is 18 too many?** Yes, but the count is the wrong variable; clustering is. Nine of Ch 1's eighteen are first load-bearing uses that belong in Ch 1 (conditional expectation, distribution and CE, indifference, Bayes in odds, normal versus Poisson, additive versus proportional, exponential waiting, risk-neutral, conservation and half-life). The other nine (call option, random walk, Itô, fat tails, regression slope, Monte Carlo, decile and IR, one-factor, and arguably the half-life as a separate box) are survey mentions whose load-bearing use is in Ch 5, 6, 7-B or 9, where they already have or will have fuller boxes. The style bible's "required at the FIRST use" rule, applied literally to a chapter that surveys the whole shelf, imports the entire building-block course into Chapter 1. Amend it to "first *load-bearing* use; a survey mention gets a one-sentence gloss and a pointer". The right density is at most one Math, Gently per 1,200 words, never two adjacent, and at most ten in any chapter. Ch 5 (17 in 32,500 words) and Ch 6 (17 in 36,000) are at the density limit but still cluster.

**The sign-off tic.** Seventeen of Ch 1's eighteen boxes end with a variant of "The full version is §X." Twice the full version is the very next paragraph. Vary it, or drop it when the derivation follows immediately.

**Vignette placement.** The Quant-Shop Life vignette is good and boring in the right way (the red build, the capacity flags, the markout off by one trade), but it sits *after* "Reading Listing 1.1" and before a Resource Graph, so the reader goes code explanation → story → link list → §1.4. Put it at the top of the Specification section, so the failed test motivates the spec, and let "Reading Listing 1.1" follow it.

## (e) Fact versus fiction, and real-event accuracy

**Labelling is good and should be the standard.** Each Long Scene opens with an italic disclaimer naming what is invented; the vignettes say "fictional composite"; desk numbers carry "(illustrative)"; the Serta scene says in terms that no real lender is depicted. Ch 5's cold open and Ch 6's SVB scene cite the filing for nearly every number and mark secondary sources as such. The book's fact discipline survived the move to narrative.

**Concerns, in priority order.**

1. **Ch 6 §6.4.4 leaks the research process into the text.** "(Market Rebellion, 10 March 2023; secondary, CHECK)", "(Bloomberg, 13 March 2023, as summarized in search results; secondary, CHECK)", "(via search summary; CHECK)", "(Wall Street Journal via Axios... secondary, CHECK)". Five CHECK flags and two "search summary" attributions are in the reading text. Resolve them, hedge them ("press reports put the holdco paper near 40") or cut the number; move the open items to a state file.
2. **Ch 1, line 764, Serta: "no co-op among the lenders who were left behind."** The excluded lenders did organize, in court, within weeks (the dossier records the October 2020 New York suit, No. 655175/2020, and the brief itself asked for the June 2020 filing date, which the log says went unverified). The sentence is defensible only in the narrow sense of no pre-deal cooperation agreement. Reword: "no cooperation agreement before the deal; the excluded lenders organized afterwards, as plaintiffs."
3. **Ch 1 Serta scene, the second account.** The scene leaves open whether the urgent seller was inside the group (restricted) or had been pitched and passed. Mags's own caveat ("the dangerous seller is rarely the one in the room") is the right answer; make the second account explicitly a pitched-and-passed lender so the fiction does not imply that a real participant traded while restricted.
4. **Cross-chapter numbers on Serta.** Ch 1's Docket Diary says the majority exchanged "about \$992mm... into about \$734mm" of second-out; Ch 5 says "roughly \$875mm of second-out" and reconciles the two (\$734mm is the first-lien participants' share, \$875mm is all second-out issued). Ch 1 needs the same clause, or a reader who meets both will think one chapter is wrong. The 25-cent retained value (\$223.75mm on \$895mm) matches the court dossier.
5. **Mags's continuity.** Ch 1: she ran a bank's distressed loan book in June 2020, bought \$10mm of Serta first lien from a CLO the week before the 22nd, and was primed. Ch 5: she "was in this one, a little", held a small piece, sold half in July 2020 "in the low twenties" and the rest in 2023. Compatible, but neither chapter mentions the other's facts. Add one clause to Ch 5 ("the ten million I bought from the CLO, which Chapter 1 told you about") and put Mags's 2020 positions in a cast bible before the next writer invents a third version.
6. **Dates to confirm against the dossier, not the web:** Fifth Circuit rehearing denied 18 Feb 2025 (both chapters say so; the dossier grep did not surface it); certiorari denied 10 Nov 2025 (Ch 5 gives the docket number; Ch 1 does not); Serta's Chapter 11 petition date of 23 January 2023 (Ch 1) against "January 2023" (Ch 5).
7. **Invented model output on a real name.** Ch 6 Beat 1: "SIVB's raw DD comes out at about 4 *(illustrative)*." The label is there, but say "the desk's fictional overnight run", so nobody quotes a DD of 4 for SVB as if a vendor published it. Same for Ch 1's "Call it π = 0.6" on Halvorsen, which is fine because Halvorsen is fictional.
8. **Arithmetic in the boxes checks out** (0.6 × 78 + 0.4 × 89 = 82.4, which corrects the brief's 83.6; 0.02 × 1.270 = 0.025 per \$mm; sd of a 2% chance of −50 = 7; 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99; 0.0975 for the one-factor example; half-life 13.9 days; 5.12 × (1 − e^{-3}) = 4.87; Ch 5's coin variance 0.04 × 0.96 × 0.64² = 0.0157, sd 12.5%; Ch 6's 106.04 / 267.83 = −60.4%). One wobble: the Ch 1 Bayes box gives π ≈ 0.36 for LR 18 and the Serta scene gives π ≈ 27% for LR 12; both are right, but a reader who has just seen "×12" in the box will expect 36% and get 27%, which is another reason to change the box's numbers.

## (f) Does the math now build intuition?

Mostly yes, and in places better than the derivations that follow. The boxes that work share a shape: a picture, then a tiny number the reader can redo in the margin, then the formula. Bayes in odds (3% becomes 36% from two multiplications), indifference pricing (each \$mm already held costs 0.025 points), normal versus Poisson (same mean, same variance, one-in-fifty versus seven sigma), Ch 5's wobble-plus-coin (the coin alone is 12.5% against Theo's 4.1%), the seesaw for skew, and Ch 6's 121/99/99/81 two-step for the lognormal and its log-return sequel for the volatility tax are the standard the rest of the book should meet. Ch 6 §6.1.2's two-sentence argument that squared Brownian increments sum to $T$ (mean $T$, variance $2T^2/n \to 0$) is the clearest version of $(dW)^2 = dt$ in the book and should be pointed to from everywhere.

Three weaknesses. (i) The regression-slope box in Ch 1 is the only one without a worked number; it reads as a dictionary entry. (ii) The Ch 1 versions of random walk and Itô are thinner than Ch 6's and arrive 400 pages earlier, so the reader meets the weaker telling first; let Ch 1 gloss and point. (iii) The credit triangle is told three times in §1.5 (box: $2\% \times 60\% = 120$ bp; annotation: $s = \lambda(1 - R)$; Full Derivation: the two integrals). The one-factor model does this better: the box gives 9.7%, the derivation shows where it came from, and nothing in between repeats it. The pattern to copy is box → one-line statement with equation number → Full Derivation immediately below.

The Full Derivation moves themselves are correct: Ch 5's third and fourth moments sit after the table they explain; Ch 1's CARA formula sits after the Poisson intuition; Ch 6's Itô derivation sits after three boxes (too many, but in the right order). Nothing was cut.

## (g) Is the chapter still usable as a reference?

Partly. Equations (1.1)–(1.7) are numbered and intact; the Debrief bullets restate the bid equation and the main results; the consolidated Spec is where it was. A reader looking for "the formula" will find (1.1), (1.2), (1.6) and (1.7) quickly. They will have more trouble with (1.3), (1.4) and (1.5), which live inside blockquoted annotations, (1.4) after three consecutive boxes and (1.5) inside a Full Derivation after two more. The judgment content ("when it breaks") is findable precisely *because* it is in templated paragraphs, which is the one argument for them; if those paragraphs become scenes, as (b) recommends, the chapter needs a cheap replacement.

Recommendation: give every chapter a **Finder** table at the top of the Desk Debrief, six to ten rows, columns *concept · equation · intuition (box) · judgment (scene) · code (listing)*. It costs 120 words, it is the reference layer the user will actually use after the first read, and it lets the scenes carry the judgment without a rule paragraph trailing each one. Also promote the Long Scenes and their decision points from bold paragraphs to `####` headings, so the table of contents shows "Long Scene 2: The Counterparty Who Knew" and a reader can get back to it.
