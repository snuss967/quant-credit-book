## 1. The spine

The reader should close the cold open with one uneasy question: **"Who actually owns this option, and who owns the clock?"** Theo sees a bond pay 101 because a judge said no. It looks like the holder owned a put. By the end of the chapter the reader has learned that every clause in the table of §15.1.2 is drafted as a holder's option and lived as an issuer's counter-option (tender below par, consent strip, defeasance, portability, an extendable outside date), and that the only party who sets the date is a regulator or a court who counts in working days. The chapter answers the question in three movements: the 101 put is an option whose moneyness is a coupon-vintage accident and whose survival is the issuer's choice (§15.2–15.3); the SMR is the one place the market prices the clock honestly, and even it goes blind (§15.4); the clock itself is a survival problem with informative censoring and regime shifts (§15.5). The three cases (Kroger, EA, WBD) are the same lesson told three times: the credit event was the paragraph nobody read, not the merger.
