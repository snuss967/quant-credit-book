# Edition 2 Revision Brief: Chapter 16, Post-Reorganization Equities, Rights Offerings & Bankruptcy Claims

*For one Opus writer. Source chapter: `book_src/16_ch16_post_reorg_equities_and_claims.md` (18.4k words, five figures already). Remove no topic, derivation, case, box, exercise or resource. Everything below adds, reorders or re-voices.*

## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel by the end of the cold open: **on the morning a plan's securities are born, who is forbidden to hold what they just received, and is the price on the screen a flow I can schedule or a value I have to underwrite?** Theo has three windows open and one offer sitting "like a confession"; he does not yet know whether to lift it. The chapter answers the question one instrument at a time, and every answer has the same shape: find the holder the waterfall chose and the market would not have (CLO, bank, insurer, old stockholder, vendor), write down what forces their hand (an OC test, a disposition clock, payroll), and price the thing they hold as the option it is. §16.2 turns the forced seller into a supply schedule with a half-life. §16.3 and §16.4 show that the rights, the backstop and the warrants are a call, a put and a call-on-a-call, and that the plan's own numbers tell you which volatility the other side assumed. §16.5 does the same for the humblest instrument, a trade claim whose denominator is random. Hertz runs the whole circuit in one company and ends back in the room in 2026; Spirit shows the left tail arriving in five months. §16.8 closes the loop: the premium is real, it is a portfolio property, and it does not scale. By the Debrief the reader should be able to look at Theo's 9:31 screen and say, out loud, which part of the discount is flow and which part is value, and what they would do about each.

The chapter's existing voice is good at the third act and thin in the first: the cold open is 500 words of setup, the Hertz material is split across three sections, and seven derivations arrive before the reader has felt why they are needed. The edit is mostly about letting the scenes run and putting the intuition in front of the algebra.
