## 1. The spine

The cold open already ends on the right question: **how do you turn a document and a rumor into a number you can quote against?** The Edition 1 reviewers called it the best cold open in Part IV, so keep its first 350 words intact. What the reader should *feel* by the end of the expanded open is sharper than the question: that a loan is not a claim on a company, it is a claim on a seat, and the seat is decided by three people the reader has never met, reading one clause the reader has not finished reading. Every tool in the chapter answers "which seat am I in, and what is that seat worth?" The waterfall (§13.3, §13.6, §13.7) prices the seat; the game theory (§13.4, §13.5) says who gets it; the PRS (§13.8) says how often the dealer deals; the autopsies (§13.9) say the court can reshuffle the seats years later. The chapter should close the loop by returning to the Larkspur bid at 78 one last time, in the Desk Debrief, with Theo giving a number and a reason.

Two things the Edition 1 draft gets wrong in tone: it states the Serta arithmetic before the reader has lived through June 2020, and it states the co-op theory before the reader has seen a co-op win. Edition 2 earns both with long scenes built on Serta (the auction the excluded lenders lost) and Lumen (the co-op that became the deal), and it lets the cast be wrong about Lumen on the way in.
