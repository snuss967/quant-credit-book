## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel at the end of the cold open: **whose number is it?** Four prices get attached to the same loans in this chapter (the manager's NAV, the listed sibling's share price, a pension buyer's par bid, a secondary buyer's unit bid), and each is honest about a different question. The chapter answers by teaching the reader to treat a mark as a *process* (a moving-average filter whose kernel can be estimated), to invert the one public price that exists (the listed BDC) into a markdown and a bad-loan carrying value, to see a redemption queue as a run with a cap, and then to trade the public wrappers. The emotional arc: Kaz begins thinking NAV is a price, Theo begins thinking it is a lie, and by the Debrief both have moved to Ines's position, which is that it is a slow signal whose speed you can measure. The chapter already has the right skeleton and the right real events (engagement review: "the strongest TREND chapter"). Its Edition 1 faults are specific: the two best stories (OBDC/OBDC II; the 2026 redemption quarters) are 400-word summaries with the trades stated rather than earned; the derivations in §17.2 arrive before any picture; §17.5 is a survey (engagement.md line 55); and the same 16.8%/5%/86%/13-week facts are told three or four times each.
