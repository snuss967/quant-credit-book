## 1. The spine

The cold open already asks the right question through Ines: *when the equity surface and the credit curve disagree, which one is lying?* Edition 2 sharpens it to the thing the reader should feel in their stomach by the end of the open: **the two markets are strikes on one distribution only while a diffusion decides who gets paid; the moment a document or a run decides, the "arbitrage" is a bet on who wrote the document.** The chapter answers it in three movements. §23.1–23.5 build the translation layer (Merton, CreditGrades, Carr–Wu, the jump floor, spread Greeks) and let the reader believe it. §23.6 breaks it on GM in May 2005, where both legs lost because two different shocks hit two different claims. §23.8 breaks it three more ways: SVB (a run, not a diffusion), Credit Suisse AT1 (a trigger reorders the waterfall), Hertz (the right tail nobody modeled). Theo's edge, the man who asks what the indenture says, is the payoff, not a running joke.
