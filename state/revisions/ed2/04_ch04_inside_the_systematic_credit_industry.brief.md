## 1. The spine

Ines's line in the cold open is already the right question, but the reader does not yet *feel* it: "Tell me which of these desks we are, and which one is going to eat us." By the end of the cold open the reader should feel a specific dread that an RSSG banker knows well: six pitch books that all say the same thing, and the suspicion that the thing they all say is not where the money is. The chapter answers it in three moves. (a) The alpha column is one memo (value, momentum, quality, carry), and the 2019–2026 replication audit shows most of that memo evaporates once prints are cleaned honestly, so the edge cannot be the factor list. (b) The money is in the plumbing: data timestamps, costs, routing, capacity, and the 2021–2023 execution migration shows a real firm moving along that axis without changing a single signal. (c) The cells no framework fills (distressed, LMT exposure, loans, bond choice within an issuer) are exactly the cells where someone who has read a credit agreement is scarce. The reader should close the chapter with Theo's six-column sheet in their head and the blanks glowing.

The novel's engine for this chapter is *disagreement among the cast*. Kaz believes the math is the product; Ines believes the fills are; Mags believes neither matters without knowing who defaults; Theo keeps asking "as of when?" Let each of them be partly right and let the reader watch them change position after each real event.
