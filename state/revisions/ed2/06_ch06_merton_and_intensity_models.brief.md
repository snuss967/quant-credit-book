## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel by the end of the cold open: **"The stock price already knows something about column O. How much does it know, and who is being paid for the part it does not?"** Theo's 13-week model and the CDS desk's hazard curve are the same object in two measures; the stock is a third witness. The chapter answers in three movements: (i) Itô and Merton turn a balance sheet and a stock into a $\mathbb{P}$ default probability; (ii) Girsanov and the bootstrap turn a CDS curve into a $\mathbb{Q}$ one and name the gap; (iii) SVB, Carvana, Hertz and First Brands show the three ways the witness lies: the asset is not lognormal (banks), the strike is negotiable (Carvana, Hertz), or there is no witness at all (First Brands). Every scene ends on a decision the tools then price.
