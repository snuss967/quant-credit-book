## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel at the end of the cold open: **"Every number in this spreadsheet was produced by someone with a reason to produce it that way. Which ones can I trust, how much, and what do I do with the ones I can't?"** Kaz's 1.9% volatility is the hook: the best asset anyone has ever seen is a valuation committee with a deadline. The chapter answers in three movements. First, the inputs are not opinions but an identity with four terms, each owned by a different person and lying in a different way (§12.2–12.3). Second, the covariance and the regime are estimates with error bars wider than the differences the optimizer is choosing between, so the objective is flat and the constraints decide (§12.4–12.6). Third, the constraint that binds is the exit door, and the exit door's width depends on what everyone else is doing (§12.7–12.9). Theo's three restructuring numbers (what it recovers, where it sits, how fast you get out) turn out to be the three inputs the eleven tabs lacked. The cold open ends on a card: "Before reading on: which of the eleven tabs would you cap, and what single number would you demand for each?"
