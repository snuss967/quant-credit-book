## 1. The spine

The cold open already poses the question in Ines's voice: *how do you avoid the defaults without missing the rebounds?* The reader should feel it as a trap with two jaws. One jaw is 2000 (CCC −30.6%); the other is 2003 (+50.8%), and the industry's answer to date, the dimmer switch (BlackRock's 89.56%/92.24%), is a way of being bitten gently by both. The chapter's answer, which the reader should be able to say in one breath by the Debrief: **the tier is a crowd and you are buying one person in it.** So: price the person (a PD at the issuer, labeled for the soft-default world, mapped empirically rather than through $N(-\text{DD})$), validate the PD against the question vendors do not answer (hit rate *per exclusion rate*), size the dangerous tier by expected loss rather than by fear, keep the beta at one so the defense is selection and not a dimmer, and add risk when the market overpays for it. Two real episodes carry the two jaws: Saks Global (Dec 2024 to Jun 2026) is the default the old label could not see; the April 2025 tariff shock is the rebound the nervous committee sold.
