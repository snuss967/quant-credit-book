## 1. The spine

The cold open already asks the chapter's question (which document, which clock, which value in which state). Edition 2 narrows it to what the reader should feel at 8:05 on the Monday after the missed coupon: **I own the 7.25% secured notes at 61; which of the next twelve months' events can move my recovery, and who holds the pen for each?** The documents say who holds the pen (§12B.1–12B.3), the automaton says which pens can be used in which order (§12B.4–12B.5), the call-spread identity prices each signature (§12B.6), the resolvent prices the wait (§12B.7), the claims ladder says how the money moves (§12B.8). Halvorsen (§12B.9) runs the fictional case through the whole machine; Saks Global's five months in court (Jan–Jun 2026) and Momentive's cramdown fight (2014–2017) supply the dated spines. The last beat is PB-02, Owen's Halvorsen pair, built jump-neutral in Ch 4-C and discovering here that the hedge ratio depends on the state the machine is in.

Division of labor: Ch 13-B owns the LME halves of Saks (Aug 2025 exchange) and Revlon (BrandCo, the Citi wire); this chapter owns petition to effective date. Ch 12-C owns Hertz, Ch 16 the rights-offering mechanics, Ch 13 and 25 Serta. One sentence and a pointer for each.
