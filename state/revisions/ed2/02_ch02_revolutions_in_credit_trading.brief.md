## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel at the end of the cold open is not the current "what changed in the plumbing" (a syllabus). It is Theo's question after his first solo fill: **"What did I actually pay for that bond, who knew it before I did, and could a machine have told me in advance?"** Ines's "which one of them just got off a lender call" plants the second clause; Kaz's "the dataset does" plants the third. Keep the existing dialogue, then let Theo open the tape to find his own print. Fifteen minutes later it shows as `1MM+` (Halvorsen is B2, so the HY cap hides even his own \$4mm), and next to it sits another `1MM+` print from an hour earlier that he never saw and cannot size. That hidden print is the chapter's ghost.

The chapter answers in three movements. The tape made cost knowable after the fact but censored the trades Theo cares about (§2.2). The wire made cost knowable at the moment of trade and turned fills into labeled data (§2.3–2.8). The payoff: a lower cost per ticket is more breadth, so the same signal becomes a better strategy, $\alpha^{\text{net}\star} \propto 1/c$ (§2.9). The engagement review (`state/reviews/ed1/engagement.md` line 31) asked for that result in the opening and for the six revolutions to read as evidence, not a listicle. So after Kaz's line, Ines says it in words ("every basis point off a ticket buys more tickets, and more tickets is more edge; the math is at the end, the evidence is in between"), and each section ends with one sentence on what it did to $\Pr(\text{fill})$ or $\mathbb{E}[c]$. The chapter closes on the ghost print: the edge lives where the exhaust is thinnest, which is Theo's old neighborhood.
