## 1. The spine

The sticky note already carries it: *Of the thirty-seven cents this bond moved today, how much was the bond?* The reader should feel by the end of the cold open that this is not an accounting question but a question about *why rates moved*, and that nobody on the desk can answer it from a formula, because the honest answer has changed sign twice since 2021. The chapter answers in three movements. First (§8.1–8.3) it shows that a bond's rate sensitivity is a bet on the source of the rate move, with two separate roads to zero (correlation and recovery) and a quoting convention sitting at the IG/HY line. Second (§8.4–8.7) it shows what happens when you place that bet with real instruments in real years: 2022, March 2023, April 2025. Third (§8.8) it offers the way out: stop betting on $\rho$, hedge each factor with its own instrument, and call what is left IDO only after the common residual has been removed. The emotional arc is Theo moving from "my duration says seventy" through "every hedge ratio is defensible" to "6" written on the sticky note. The chapter's thesis sentence, which the writer should let Mags say once and nobody repeat: *the empirical hedge ratio is a bet on why rates move.*
