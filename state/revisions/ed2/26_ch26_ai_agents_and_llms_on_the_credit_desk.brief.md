## 1. The spine

The cold open already asks the right question and should keep asking it: *how do you put a machine that is sometimes wrong, and confident when it is, into a process that may never be wrong silently?* What the reader should **feel** by the end of the cold open is narrower and more RSSG-shaped: the machine's three right paragraphs are more dangerous than its wrong one, because they teach you to stop reading, and Theo has spent five years being paid precisely to keep reading. The chapter answers in one arc: make the *claim* diagnosable since the model never will be (§26.4), pay for abstention with the desk's own costs (§26.5), count every step and every retry as a factor below one (§26.6), count every trial the agent discarded (§26.7), govern it like the vendor model it is (§26.8), and write the spec the way you used to read an indenture: adversarially (§26.9–26.10). The two long scenes carry the two halves of that arc. Scene A (February 2026, the software sell-off) is the reading machine under time pressure, where the question is *what may it decide?* Scene B (ROBO's 47 pairs) is the research agent, where the question is *how many of its wins are luck, and how many are memory?* Ines's line is the refrain: the robot is just the one talking loudest.
