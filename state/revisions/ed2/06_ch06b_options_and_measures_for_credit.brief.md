## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel at the end of the cold open is the one Theo asks and nobody answers: **"Which probabilities go into the expectation, mine or the market's?"** The chapter's answer, built in order, is: a price is the cost of a factory that manufactures a payoff (replication deletes your opinion); the probabilities that fall out of prices are a second derivative of the price list, not a belief; a credit desk's whole book is a set of such payoffs; the underlying can jump, which no factory replicates, so the jump gets its own risk line and its own measure change; and the gap between the market's hazard and yours is not an inconsistency but a *premium*, which is tradable only if every leg says which measure it is in. The Halvorsen worked case (§6-B.8) is the payoff of the spine and must stay the climax: the cold open's eleventh column ("MEASURE: Q") is answered the same afternoon on one bond and one put.
