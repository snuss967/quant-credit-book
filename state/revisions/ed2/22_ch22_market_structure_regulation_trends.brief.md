## 1. The spine

The cold open already has the right question, but it is stated, not felt. The reader should close the cold open thinking: *nothing moved, and my price moved; someone charged me, and I cannot see the invoice.* Ines's answer ("the rule is a price, and the day it changed is a date in every series you trained on") is the chapter in one line. Everything after it is two machines: the pricing machine (rent, delay, netting, capacity, clearing) that reads the invoice, and the dating machine (Table 22.1, difference-in-differences) that proves the invoice was for the rule and not for the weather. The chapter must *earn* both: first by showing a fortnight in March 2020 when the invoice was the whole market, then by showing a rule that was approved, feared, priced, and never took effect, so the reader learns that the dating machine needs placebos as much as effective dates. The RV beat closes the loop from the pod's side: the short leg of a pair has rules too, and most of them were written for equities.
