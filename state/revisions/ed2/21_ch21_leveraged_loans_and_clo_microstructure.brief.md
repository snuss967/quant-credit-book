## 1. The spine

The question the reader should feel by the end of the cold open: **if nobody prints a price for this thing, who decides what it is worth, and when does their decision reach me?** The chapter's answer, delivered in order: the price is a composite of opinions (§21.2), capped by a borrower who can take the upside back (§21.1, §21.5), moved by holders who sell by rulebook rather than by view (§21.3–21.4), delivered to a fund three weeks after it needs the cash (§21.6), and smoothed before it reaches a backtest (§21.7). The ex-RSSG reader already knows the last page of this story (§21.8): the same rulebook decides who can sign and fund an uptier. Theo's bridge is one sentence he says twice, once in the open and once in the Debrief: "At RSSG I read the credit agreement. Here I read the indenture of the lender."

**Story clock.** Day One is Tue 22 Sep 2026 (cast bible). Set the cold open live on **Wed 14 Oct 2026**: a loan bought Fri 11 Sep 2026 is exactly 23 business days unsettled that morning. Replace the anachronistic "well over half the index above par, January repricing launches" headline with the October 2026 tape: refis above resets for the first time since April, new issue only \$13.8bn of \$41.1bn September CLO activity *(Trepp via CRE Daily, 14 Oct 2026, dossier `trend_loans_clos` §2)*, ratings decompression with CCCs weakening *(Voya, 12 Oct 2026)*, and "a fifth of the market above par" *(21.7% at end-June, Eagle Point N-CSRS)*. Ines's "above par is where the borrower takes chips" line survives but now points back to January ("58% at year-end, and look what January did to them"). Everything dated before 22 Sep 2026 (March 2020, April 2025, Trinseo) is a **replay**, labeled once, at the top, in italics.
