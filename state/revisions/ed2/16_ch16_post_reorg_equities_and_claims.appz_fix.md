## 1. Z.16.2 / §16.2.3: the "slow component" is not the capital-arrival term, and it does not decay at rho
- Note: §16.2.3 says the term $Q_t\dot M_t/M_t^2$ "decays at the speed of the slow money ($\rho$), not the speed of the sellers ($\theta$)", and that this term already supplies the second, slower component. In the model it decays at $\theta+\rho$, the whole discount at $\theta+\dot M/M \ge \theta$, so $w_3/w_2 < e^{-\theta\Delta}$ always; Dynkin's 0.86% cannot be reached at $\theta = 3.42$.
- Recomputation (python3, D(t) = A e^{-theta t} m / (1 - (1-m) e^{-rho t}), m = M0/M*): theta_hat = -2 ln(1.04/5.75) = 3.4200; single-exponential w3 = 1.04 e^{-1.71} = 0.1881%; data 0.86% = 4.6 times that line. Grid search over rho in (0.001, 200) and m in (0.01, 1) at theta = 3.42: max w3/w2 = 0.1809 = e^{-1.71} (a supremum approached as rho grows), so w3 <= 0.188%; even with capital leaving (m up to 3) the ratio stays at or below 0.26, w3 <= 0.27%. The ratio bound also follows analytically: E(t) = e^{theta t} D(t) is decreasing and convex, so $e^{-\theta\Delta}w_k - w_{k+1} > 0$. Exercise 2 inputs (theta 3.42, rho 0.5, M0 30, M* 60): windows 25.557%, 3.739%, 0.588%; theta_hat from the first two = 3.844; predicted w3 = 0.547%; true/predicted = 1.075 (7.5% lift). The data ratio w3/w2 = 0.827 requires theta <= 0.380 in this model (capital as the fast component); fsolve gives an exact fit at theta = 0.38 with rho about 22 to 25 and M0/M* about 0.62, i.e. the roles reversed.
- Ruling: note right (fixed). Slow capital can only speed the discount's decay, so it cannot be the slow tail; it lifts w3 only against a single exponential fitted to the first two windows (7.5% in the Exercise 2 setup). The tail needs a slower seller compartment, which the model does not have. The chapter's 3.42 calibration, the Exercise 2 text and Listing 16.1 (its comment "data need a slow component" is generic) are unchanged.
- Chapter §16.2.3: "In our model that component already exists. It is the capital-arrival term $Q_t\dot M_t/M_t^2$, which decays at the speed of the slow money ($\rho$), not the speed of the sellers ($\theta$)." -> "Our model cannot supply that component. The capital-arrival term $Q_t\dot M_t/M_t^2$ decays at $\theta + \rho$, not at $\rho$, and the whole discount decays at $\theta + \dot M_t/M_t \ge \theta$, so $w_3/w_2 < e^{-\theta\Delta}$ whatever the capital does: a seller speed of 3.42 caps the third window at $w_2\,e^{-\theta\Delta} \approx 0.19\%$, and slow money cannot reach the 0.86%. It lifts the third window only against a single exponential fitted to the first two, which reads the early, faster decay, and Exercise 2 measures the lift. The slower component is better read as a slower group of sellers, a second compartment on the supply side that our model leaves out."
- Chapter §16.2.3 (Ines, so her gloss no longer contradicts the fix; the beat "two edges, early vs. patient" is untouched): "The slow decay is the table filling up with players who could have sat down on day one but didn't know the game was running." -> "The slow decay is the stragglers still at the table, who could have folded on day one but didn't know the game was running."
- Solution (Z.16.2, trap): "It does not; see the note." -> "It does not: the capital-arrival term decays at $\theta + \rho$ and the whole discount never decays slower than $\theta$, so slow capital can lift $w_3$ only a little (here 7.5% over the single exponential, 0.588% against 0.547%), whereas Dynkin's third window is 4.5 times its single-exponential line (0.86% against 0.19%). The bond data need a slower seller, not slower capital (§16.2.3)." NOTE deleted.
