## 1. Thesis and the "aha" for an ex-PJT-RSSG reader

**Thesis.** "Systematic credit" is not one business. It is at least five businesses that share a word, and the five framework papers on the shelf are five *different answers* to the same six engineering questions: where alpha comes from (alpha families), what you are allowed to know (data stack), how you turn a forecast into positions (portfolio construction), how you get filled (execution), how big you can be before you are the market (capacity), and what you hedge versus what you harvest (risk). Lay the papers on those six axes and you get a map, and the empty cells on that map are the book's exploratory space. The industry is small (systematic strategies run roughly 20% of global equities but under 2% of fixed income, per `Blackstone_Systematic_Credit p.2` and, independently, Bloomberg via BBH, March 2026), it is growing fast (quant-screened IG/HY funds at \$90–140bn per Barclays QPS, cited by Impax, September 2025; "systematic credit funds doubled in size in 2023" per Bloomberg, May 2024, via BBH), and it became possible for one reason: execution became measurable (Man Numeric moved from ~30% HY / ~60% IG electronic order flow to over 90% by May 2023, `Man_Revolutions_in_Credit_Trading_2023 p.3`).

**The aha.** The RSSG reader expects the quant shops to compete on math. They mostly compete on *plumbing*: who has a transaction-cost model that is right about dealer fade, who has a security master that survives an LMT exchange, who can get \$2mm clips filled in 400 names on a Tuesday. The math (value, momentum, quality, carry, default-adjusted spread) is published, and much of the academic version of it just failed replication (Dickerson, Mueller & Robotti, *JFE* 2023, Fama–DFA prize; Dickerson, Robotti & Rossetti, arXiv April 2026: most of 108 bond signals lose significance once TRACE price errors and ex-post return filtering are corrected). So the edge is not the factor; it is the *implementation gap* between a paper factor and a filled portfolio, and that gap is exactly where an RSSG brain is useful: knowing which bond in a capital structure is the cheapest-to-deliver into your own optimizer (`Man_Quantitative_Credit_Alpha_Early_Mover_2022 p.8` calls this "negative selection"), knowing why a fallen angel is a forced-seller event and not a signal, and knowing that "distressed" is the one cell every framework paper leaves empty ("Quant credit is yet to move into distressed debt," `Man_Quantitative_Credit_Alpha_Early_Mover_2022 p.42`). The map tells Theo where his old skills are scarce.

**The second aha (the Q-minus-P point).** Every "value" signal in the industry, from DCI's structural-model spread comparison to BlackRock's "default-adjusted spread," is literally the gap between the market's risk-neutral loss rate embedded in the spread ($\lambda^{\mathbb{Q}} L$) and the manager's physical estimate ($\lambda^{\mathbb{P}} L$). That gap is risk premium plus liquidity plus mispricing. The frameworks differ in which of the three they think they are harvesting. Say this once, clearly, in §4.4; it organizes the whole chapter and pre-wires Chapters 5–6 and 9.

## 3. Derivation skeletons

All expectations below are under $\mathbb{P}$ unless stated. Notation follows the style bible ($s$ spread in decimal, $D^{\text{spr}}$ spread duration, $\lambda$ default intensity, $L=1-R$).

**D1. The fundamental law with issuer clustering.** Grinold's law $IR = IC\sqrt{BR}\cdot TC$ assumes independent bets. Credit has $N$ bonds from $M$ issuers, $m = N/M$ bonds per issuer, with intra-issuer residual correlation $\rho$. For an equal-weighted average of $N$ unit-variance bets,
$$\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i \epsilon_i\right) = \frac{1}{N^2}\big[N + N(m-1)\rho\big] = \frac{1+(m-1)\rho}{N},$$
so the effective number of independent bets is
$$BR_{\text{eff}} = \frac{N}{1+(m-1)\rho}, \qquad IR = IC\sqrt{BR_{\text{eff}}}\;TC. \tag{4.1}$$
Boundary checks: $\rho=0 \Rightarrow BR_{\text{eff}}=N$; $\rho=1 \Rightarrow BR_{\text{eff}}=M$ (47 bonds of one issuer are one bet). Paleologo's binomial form (`Paleologo p.149`) $IR=(2p-1)\sqrt{252\,N_e}$ with $N_e = 1/\sum w_i^2$ is the same idea with hitting probability $p$; use it for intuition. Error-prone spots: using bond count as breadth; ignoring that $TC$ (transfer coefficient) collapses when fill probability is below one (`Man_…_2022 p.19`: an unfilled order leaves "unintended and potentially unmodelled risk exposures"); assuming $IC$ is constant across breadth (Paleologo warns it is not).

**D2. Capacity under square-root impact.** Let gross alpha per dollar per year be $\alpha_0$, one-way annual turnover $T$ (fraction of AUM), half-spread cost $s_{\text{tc}}$ per dollar traded, and impact $k\sqrt{A/\mathcal{L}}$ per dollar traded where $A$ is AUM and $\mathcal{L}$ is the annual liquidity actually accessible to the strategy's names. Net alpha per dollar and dollar alpha are
$$\alpha(A) = \alpha_0 - T\Big[s_{\text{tc}} + k\sqrt{A/\mathcal{L}}\Big], \qquad \Pi(A) = A\,\alpha(A).$$
Maximizing $\Pi$: $\Pi'(A) = (\alpha_0 - T s_{\text{tc}}) - \tfrac{3}{2}Tk\sqrt{A/\mathcal{L}} = 0$, hence
$$A^{*} = \mathcal{L}\left[\frac{\alpha_0 - T s_{\text{tc}}}{\tfrac{3}{2}\,T k}\right]^{2}, \qquad \alpha(A^{*}) = \frac{\alpha_0 - T s_{\text{tc}}}{3}. \tag{4.2}$$
The punchline: at the manager's fee-maximizing size, the client keeps one-third of spread-net gross alpha; the client's IR-maximizing size is $A\to 0$. That is the principal–agent gap behind every "capacity" slide. Assumptions to state: impact is square-root in participation; $\mathcal{L}$ is strategy-specific (Dynkin's cap of 5% of issuer market value is the discrete analogue); costs are linear in turnover. Error-prone: mixing one-way and round-trip turnover; using index ADV instead of the ADV of the names the signal actually selects; annual vs per-trade units.

**D3. Dynkin cap weights and the non-invested fraction** (`Dynkin p.207–208`). With $n$ eligible issuers, an issuer concentration limit $w_{\max}$, and a cap of $c$ times issuer market value $MV_i$,
$$w_i = \min\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n},\; w_{\max},\; \tfrac{c\,MV_i}{A}\right), \qquad f_{\text{cash}}(A) = 1 - \sum_i w_i. \tag{4.3}$$
Identity: the portfolio depends on $c/A$ only (a \$500mn book at 5% equals a \$250mn book at 2.5%). As $A$ grows the book drifts from equal-weight to market-value-weight and $f_{\text{cash}}$ rises; Barclays finds a \$1bn three-month-reversal fallen-angel book only 53% invested in August 2004 vs 98% for Buy-All.

**D4. Nuveen's forward-return identity** (`Nuveen p.3`). For sector $j$ over horizon $h$,
$$\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[R_j] = y_j - \underbrace{PD^{\mathbb{P}}_j\,L_j}_{\text{expected credit loss}} - D^{\text{mod}}_j\,\Delta y^{\text{TSY}} - D^{\text{spr}}_j\,\Delta s_j, \qquad \Delta s_j = \kappa_j\,(\bar{s}_j - s_j)\,h. \tag{4.4}$$
Measure: $y_j$ and $s_j$ are market (risk-neutral-ish) quantities; $PD^{\mathbb{P}}$ must be physical (rating-agency or model). Error-prone: using yield-to-worst carry for callable HY while also charging a spread-MTM term (double counting optionality); the z-score mean-reversion assumption when index composition shifts (HY index 51% BB at YE2024 vs 37% in 2004, `Nuveen p.4`). Note for the writer: the PDF extraction scrambles the labels in Figure 3; the arithmetic only works as yield 6.97 − credit loss 2.55 − rates MTM 0.37 − spread MTM 0.49 = **forward return 3.56%** for US HY as at 19 Nov 2025 (CHECK against the figure before quoting).

**D5. "Value" is the Q−P gap.** With the credit triangle $s_i \approx \lambda^{\mathbb{Q}}_i L_i$ and a structural-model physical intensity $\lambda^{\mathbb{P}}_i$, the default-adjusted spread is
$$V_i = s_i - \lambda^{\mathbb{P}}_i L_i \approx \big(\lambda^{\mathbb{Q}}_i - \lambda^{\mathbb{P}}_i\big)L_i = \text{risk premium}_i + \text{liquidity}_i + \text{mispricing}_i, \tag{4.5}$$
then neutralized cross-sectionally: $\tilde V_i = V_i - \hat{\mathbf{\beta}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_i$ where $\mathbf{x}_i$ holds rating, sector, duration and size exposures (Man's three-step "raw → risk-adjust → peer-relative," `Man_…_2022 p.24`, with raw value $\text{OAS}_i / \max(\text{NetDebt}/\text{EBITDA}, 0.1)$). Error-prone: negative or near-zero EBITDA (the clip), $\lambda^{\mathbb{P}}$ from equity vol used without the Merton leverage mapping, and treating $V_i$ as mispricing when it is mostly premium (which is Blackstone's carry argument, not its value argument).

**D6. Why beta-tilt defense loses.** Define upside and downside capture $UC = \sum_{t: r_{m,t}>0} r_{p,t} / \sum_{t: r_{m,t}>0} r_{m,t}$ and $DC$ analogously. For a pure beta tilt $r_p = \beta r_m$, $UC = DC = \beta$ and expected excess return is $(\beta-1)\,\mathbb{E}[r_m] < 0$ whenever the credit premium is positive. BlackRock's numbers (average HY manager $UC \approx 92.2\%$, $DC \approx 89.6\%$, 2005–2025; 168 up months vs 72 down months, `BlackRock p.3–4`) say the average manager is $\beta \approx 0.9$ plus a sliver of selection. Defense that survives the rebound must come from $IC$ on default avoidance, not from $\beta$. Error-prone: capture ratios computed on total vs excess returns; regime bins defined ex post.

**D7. Transaction costs and break-even turnover** (`Man_…_2022 p.21`, `Dynkin p.711`).
$$TC_i = \tfrac{1}{2}(P^{\text{ask}}_i - P^{\text{bid}}_i) + \text{slip}_i(\text{size}, \text{char}) + \text{calib}_i(\text{dealer fade}), \qquad T_{\max} = \frac{\alpha_{\text{gross}}}{2\,\overline{TC}}.$$
Barclays' ESP+EMC book: 2.66%/yr gross → 1.78%/yr net 2007–2019 (0.88%/yr of costs, worse in 2007–2012 at 1.15%/yr, 0.64%/yr in 2013–2019). Momentum has the highest turnover of the factors, size and quality the least (`Man_…_2022 p.19`). Error-prone: the Chen–Lesmond–Wei (2007) bid-ask estimates (25bp AAA, 180bp CCC) still used in papers are stale (`Man_…_2022 p.20`).

**D8 (TREND). Equity-to-credit lead–lag as the momentum family's spine.** $\Delta s_{i,t+1} = -\beta\, r^{E}_{i,t-k:t} + \epsilon_{i,t+1}$ with EMC excess returns of 6.34%/yr (IG) and 15.9%/yr (HY) 1993–2017 before costs, IR above 1.5 (`Dynkin p.628`). Keep to a paragraph; Chapters 9–10 own the derivation.

## 4. Code spec: `capacity_breadth_engine.py`

**What it computes.** One seeded, numpy-only module with four functions that score any row of the §4.3 map:

1. `effective_breadth(n_bonds, n_issuers, rho)` → $BR_{\text{eff}}$ per (4.1); and `fundamental_law_ir(ic, br_eff, tc)`.
2. `net_alpha_curve(alpha0, turnover, half_spread, k, liquidity, aum_grid)` → arrays of $\alpha(A)$ and $\Pi(A)$ per (4.2), plus `aum_star_closed_form(...)`.
3. `dynkin_cap_weights(mv, aum, cap_frac=0.05, w_max=None)` → weights and $f_{\text{cash}}$ per (4.3) on a synthetic issuer universe (lognormal market values, seeded).
4. `framework_score(row: dict)` → a dataclass with `br_eff`, `ir_gross`, `ir_net_at_aum`, `aum_star`, `cash_drag`, for a config dict `{alpha0, turnover, half_spread, k, liquidity, n_bonds, n_issuers, rho, ic, tc, aum}`; the chapter runs it for five illustrative rows (labelled *illustrative*, not the firms' numbers).

**Schemas and shapes.** `mv: float64[n]` in \$mm; `aum_grid: float64[G]`; returns dicts of arrays of the same length; all rates annual decimals; turnover one-way; `k` in (decimal alpha) per $\sqrt{\text{participation}}$.

**Numerical guards.** Clip `liquidity > 0`; `np.sqrt(np.maximum(aum/liquidity, 0))`; `rho ∈ [0,1]`; raise on `n_bonds < n_issuers`; cap weights with `np.minimum.reduce`.

**Built-in sanity checks (assert in `__main__`).** (a) `rho=1` gives `br_eff == n_issuers` to 1e-12 and `rho=0` gives `n_bonds`. (b) The numeric argmax of $\Pi(A)$ on a fine grid is within 1% of `aum_star_closed_form`, and `net_alpha(aum_star)` equals `(alpha0 - turnover*half_spread)/3` to 1e-9. (c) As `aum → 0`, `net_alpha → alpha0 - turnover*half_spread`. (d) For tiny AUM, `dynkin_cap_weights` returns equal weights and `f_cash == 0`; for huge AUM, weights are proportional to `mv` (correlation 1.0 with market-value weights). (e) The Barclays scaling identity: weights at (AUM=500, cap=5%) equal weights at (AUM=250, cap=2.5%).

**ROBO literalism line for the box.** Vague spec: "maximize breadth." ROBO buys all 47 bonds of one issuer and reports $BR = 47$. Precise spec: breadth is $BR_{\text{eff}}$ with $\rho$ estimated from residual returns within issuer; report both.

## 7. Case studies

**CS #1. From KMV to Blackstone Corporate Bond Strategies (1989–2026).** Facts: Black–Scholes–Merton (1973–74) → Kealhofer, McQuown, Vasicek build KMV and the commercial EDF in the early 1990s; Moody's buys KMV in 2002 (`Bohn_Stein p.123`). Kealhofer, McQuown and David Solo found DCI in 2004 (II, 30 Nov 2020). DCI reaches ~\$7.5bn (IG, HY, EM; long-only and L/S) and is acquired by Blackstone, announced 30 Nov 2020; Blackstone Credit (ex-GSO) then \$135bn (II, 30 Nov 2020; \$149bn by May 2021 per II). Rationale: scale liquid IG/HY/EM "without hiring many more analysts"; Blackstone's private-issuer database covers ~85% of private leveraged-loan issuers (II, 5 May 2021). Today BCBS claims >\$40bn and "largest pure-play" status (Blackstone page; `Blackstone p.1`). How a systematic desk reads it: the shop that scaled was the one with a *physical* PD engine (the $\lambda^{\mathbb{P}}$ in D5), which is also why its "value" is default-adjusted spread rather than peer-relative OAS; and its sales pitch is capacity (insurer mandates, Solvency II constraints) rather than IR. What an RSSG reader notices: 40% of HY is private companies, so the data moat is a *covenant and financials* moat, not a TRACE moat. Approximate figures flagged as such; AUM as-of dates CHECK.

**CS #2. Man Numeric's execution migration, 2021–2023.** Facts: 2021 "the year of portfolio trading" with high fill rates and aggressive pricing from tier-one and tier-two dealers; 2022 PT transaction costs rose through H1 as dealers pulled balance sheet under QT and volatility and custom ETF creation baskets were lost; electronic venues held up best; order flow went from ~30% HY / ~60% IG electronic to >90% (as of 1 May 2023); RFQ routing analytics (availability, participant quality, completion rate, digestible size) and "the vast majority of our trades are now sent as RFQs, leading to significant savings in transaction costs versus portfolio trades" (`Man_…_2023 p.2–5`). Desk lesson: the strategy did not change; the cost model and router did, and that moved the firm's position on the execution axis more than any signal research. Pair with the 2025–2026 platform data in §6.3.

**CS #3. The day the factor zoo got audited (2019–2026).** Facts: Bai–Bali–Wen (JFE 2019) four-factor model, >400 citations, widely used by quant investors; Dickerson, Mueller & Robotti find lead/lag timestamp errors and ex-post left-tail trimming, publish "Priced risk in corporate bonds" (JFE 2023), win the Fama–DFA Prize (Sept 2024); BBW is retracted (WBS, Sept 2024); Dickerson–Robotti–Rossetti extend the critique to 108 signals (arXiv, Apr 2026) and release corrected TRACE data. Desk lesson: in equities a one-month timestamp error is a bug; in credit, where TRACE prints are stale and sparse, it is an entire literature. Spec consequence: every backtest in this book pins the information timestamp of every field, and any filter that uses future returns is forbidden by construction.
