**Hertz make-whole / post-petition interest (law-firm summaries; CHECK against opinion):** Third Circuit, *In re Hertz Corp.*, No. 23-1169, decided 10 Sep 2024. **Jiang, Wang & Yang, "The Disappearing Outperformance of Post-reorg Equity" (SSRN; summarized on Duke FinReg Blog, 17 Sep 2021; VERIFIED-URL fetched):** Sample: all US public firms with ≥\$50mm assets filing Ch. 11 in 1981–2016 and emerging public, tracked through 2019. Characteristics-adjusted buy-and-hold returns "outperform all benchmarks in the 200-day window after emergence, generating abnormal returns between 40% and 50%" but "the median abnormal return is zero suggesting that gains concentrate in the right tail." Equal-weighted monthly-rebalanced portfolio from Jan 1992 (~20 stocks) beats risk-adjusted benchmarks by 7.2%/yr, concentrated in OTC names. 2000–2010: 0.8–1.2%/month alpha vs. five-factor benchmark; after 2010 alpha falls to zero; exchange-listed post-reorg stocks show *negative* alpha in the most recent decade. Institutional ownership of post-reorg equity rose from ~40% (1990s) to ~80% (2000s); authors attribute disappearance to institutional arbitrage. Explanations canvassed: thin analyst coverage and conservative management projections, senior-creditor incentive to lowball plan value in loan-to-own, stigma, operating improvement, market learning. **Implication for Ch 16:** the "post-reorg premium" is a decayed anomaly; what survives is (a) right-tail skew, (b) OTC/illiquid segment, (c) flow-timing around forced sellers, not a buy-and-hold factor.

**Ivashina, Iverson & Smith (2016), "The Ownership and Trading of Debt Claims in Chapter 11 Restructurings," *JFE* 119(2): 316–335 (Jensen Prize).** Claim-level data: 136 large US Ch. 11 filers 1998–2009, >71,000 investors, tracked filing→plan vote. **§502 text (VERIFIED-URL fetched https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/502 ):** (b)(2) disallows a claim "for unmatured interest"; (b)(6) caps a real-property lessor's termination damages at "the rent reserved by such lease, without acceleration, for the greater of one year, or 15 percent, not to exceed three years, of the remaining term" plus unpaid rent at the earlier of petition or surrender; (b)(7) caps employment-contract termination claims at one year's compensation plus unpaid compensation; (c) requires estimation of "any contingent or unliquidated claim, the fixing or liquidation of which ... would unduly delay the administration of the case." These four are the pool-size uncertainty engine: the GUC denominator is a random variable with statutory truncations.
