## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** Credit now has three doors into the same basket: a thousand bonds, a cleared CDX ticket, or a listed future, and what distinguishes them is not price but the bundle of frictions each one sells (ISDA or none, borrow or none, a one-legged exposure or a two-legged one, margin that nets or margin that does not). The index itself is a rulebook, not a portfolio: it rolls on a calendar, it admits and expels names by methodology, it settles to a single evaluated close, and it trades at a skew to the sum of its parts. A systematic desk that uses the index as its short leg, its trend leg or its hedge is therefore trading the rulebook, and the rulebook has a timetable the desk can read.

**The aha for the ex-banker.** In RSSG a price was a claim on a waterfall; here a price is a claim on a rule (FUT §4). Theo already knows what happens to a bond between downgrade and default; what he has to learn is what the container does to it: CDX swaps the name out at the next series, iBoxx drops it at a monthly rebalance, and the NY Fed found that merely being added to CDX.NA.IG widened an issuer about 12 bp over seven days (FUT row 2015-03-30). The forced flow is mechanical, dated and legible to someone who reads documents for a living.

**Five beats.** (1) Volume is a policy variable of the exchange; open interest, block share and quoted width are the market's vote (FUT trend 2). (2) Liquidity is calendared: CDX roll weeks and the last week before futures expiry are where the dealer balance sheet is (FUT trend 6, rows 2026 YTD). (3) The index is not its intrinsics: the skew is near zero in calm and reached 60 bp in 2008–09 (Bai–Shachar). (4) Dealer-to-client index trades cost more than interdealer because client flow has lasting impact, and customers ask few dealers (Collin-Dufresne–Junge–Trolle; Riggs et al., FUT row 2020). (5) For the pod, the index is the cheapest short leg by margin (Eurex deck: USD HY future 2.46%/1.21% IM against CDX HY S38 8.12%/5.34%, vendor, not like-for-like, FUT row 2025-02-17) and the dirtiest by basis, and the basis is worst when the hedge is needed (RV truth T5).

## 2. Section outline, as scenes

**Cold Open: Four Prices for One Basket (≤600 words).** *Setting:* Ines's market-making desk, a quiet Thursday in early October 2026, the week after the September roll. *Task:* an RFQ arrives for a $30m IBHY block (about 160 contracts at ~$185k each, FUT row derived) from a client who has never traded CDX. Theo has four screens: CDX HY on-the-run, IBHY, HYG, and the Bloomberg HY Very Liquid index close. *What goes wrong:* the four prices imply four different spreads and none of them is "the market". Ines: "What's the pot? Four pots. Which one are you being asked to make?" *Learned:* the chapter's question, stated once: which rulebook is this price a claim on?

1. **10B.1 Three Doors Into One Room.** *Setting:* a fictional futures-only CTA (composite, labelled) in a Connecticut office, whose compliance officer will not sign an ISDA and whose rates desk already margins at CME. *Task:* the PM wants credit beta in the trend book. *What goes wrong:* the first attempt uses HYG and the borrow desk calls about recall; the second uses CDX and legal says eight weeks for documents. *Learned:* the futures' pitch is the absence of frictions, not a better price (FUT §3 "Why a listed layer at all"); Susquehanna's three-phase forecast (asset managers, CTAs, XVA desks) and where it stands (FUT row 2024-05-28). **Math, Gently:** what a futures price on a bond index is (index level, multiplier, cash settlement, no coupon leg), with one worked contract: IBHY at $1,000 × index, tick $10, final settlement at the iBoxx close (FUT row 2018-09-10).

2. **10B.2 Four Exchanges, Four Index Philosophies.** *Setting:* Kaz's whiteboard, a Monday model-review. *Task:* ROBO was asked to "build a credit future tracking series" and built four, because there are four index families: Cboe (benchmark intersected with HYG/LQD holdings), CME and Eurex (Bloomberg US Corporate, 8,611 bonds, $7.3tn; HY Very Liquid, 1,102 bonds, 2% issuer cap), ICE (MSCI with MarketAxess liquidity scores) (FUT rows 2025-07, 2026-10 deck, 2024-12-12). *What goes wrong:* Kaz asks whether "Very Liquid" is a legal term. *Learned:* index design is the microstructure; CME's duration-hedged pair (DHB about 60% of CME ADV in August 2026, FUT row 2026-08) is a rate hedge run by a rule the trader does not control; the vendor tracking errors (1.57%, 2.33%, 2.47% annualised, CHECK, search summary only, FUT row 2024-2026) are unaudited. Cross-reference Ch 8 §8.6's Trend Watch rather than repeat it.

3. **10B.3 Volume Is Not Liquidity.** *Setting:* Lucía Ferrer's systematic asset manager (canon from Ch 10 §10.1.1), a Friday afternoon. *Task:* the PM asks whether the trend sleeve can move its HY beta leg from CDX to futures "now that volume is up 11x". *What goes wrong:* Lucía computes turnover (ADV/OI) by exchange and month: CME 0.76 in February 2026 and about 0.16 in August; Cboe 0.22 and 0.19; Eurex about 0.04 (FUT trend 2, rows 2026-02, 2026-08). The DESK attributes CME's turnover to market-maker rebates; CME declined to specify and filed a FOIA confidential-treatment request (FUT row 2026-02). The 2023 Cboe collapse to 26% of 2022 volume has no published explanation (FUT row 2023 to 2025, CHECK). *Learned:* the diagnostic set (OI, block share, quoted width, turnover), and Mags's "back in my day" beat. **Math, Gently:** turnover as a ratio and why a ratio near 1 with a thin top of book means rented liquidity.

4. **10B.4 Where the Size Goes: Blocks, TAIC, BTIC, EFP.** *Setting:* a bank credit-trading desk (composite), the ETF desk and the futures desk sharing one P&L. *Task:* a client wants to swap $100m of HYG into IBHY at the index close. *What goes wrong:* a $100m clip is more than a whole quiet day's lit volume (FUT §3 "How they trade"), so it goes as a block or a basis trade at the close; Cboe blocks passed $1bn a month in July and August 2026, about a third of IBHY volume (FUT row 2026-05 to 2026-10, with the CHECK that the electronic bucket does not reconcile). *Learned:* the index close is the single print every contract settles to, and Rapp, Shin, Zhou and Zhu find about 19 bp of end-of-day distortion from delayed reporting and Treasury spotting (FUT row 2025-12, CHECK: search summary). Keep this short; Ch 20-B owns the basket triangle.

5. **10B.5 Reading the CFTC File.** *Setting:* the same systematic manager, Lucía and a data engineer. *Task:* build a positioning feature from the Traders in Financial Futures file, which added HYB on 2026-03-31 and IQB on 2026-05-05 (FUT row 2026-03-31). *What goes wrong:* on 2026-07-14 about 4,400 IQB contracts move from the asset-manager to the dealer bucket with OI nearly unchanged, a reclassification, not trading (FUT row TFF, CHECK). *Learned:* asset managers net short, leveraged funds net long, dealers between (IQB 05-05: 1,369 long / 6,656 short against 3,006 / 1,026); Cboe's contracts are absent from the file; a structural-break filter before any signal. **Quant-shop life beat:** the e-mail to the PM explaining that the signal's best week was a data artefact.

6. **10B.6 The Roll: Twice a Year the Index Changes Identity.** *Setting:* Monday 21 September 2026 (CHECK: 20 September 2026 is a Sunday; confirm the effective date), a dealer's index desk during the roll. *Task:* roll a protection book from the old series to the new. *What goes wrong:* the new series is three months longer and has different names, so the series basis is a real price; cleared peaks follow the IMM dates (IMM 2025-09-17, cleared peak 2025-09-22, FUT row 2025-09); ICE's September 2021 week cleared over $3tn (FUT row 2021-09-24, CHECK). *Learned:* the Bai–Shachar jump at each six-month roll; the index-versus-intrinsic skew (duration-weighted constituent spreads), near zero in calm and 60 bp in 2008–09; the 12 bp entry effect. **Math, Gently:** intrinsic value as a duration-weighted average, with a 5-name toy index. Cross-reference Lucía's spliced-series phantom (Ch 10 §10.1.1, §10.2.3) and Ch 8-C's stale-CDX-mark morning.

7. **10B.7 Two Tiers and the Few-Dealer RFQ.** *Setting:* Ines's Poker Table. *Task:* explain why the client in the cold open paid more than the interdealer print. *Learned:* Collin-Dufresne, Junge and Trolle (JF 2020): dealer-to-client trades cost more because client flow carries lasting impact; Riggs, Onur, Reiffen and Zhu (JFE 2020): a typical customer contacts only a few dealers, trading competition against winner's curse and relationships (FUT row 2020). Swaptions as the fastest-growing slice (cleared CDX swaptions $52.9bn in March 2026, +80%; about 12% of SDR USD index notional; only 33.9% of "other" index products cleared, FUT rows 2026-03, H1 2026; PV row H1-2026). The hand replayed through markouts. Avoid "dealer price discrimination" as a bare claim; it is the Collin-Dufresne finding, cited.

8. **10B.8 The Pod's Cheapest Short Leg (the RV beat).** *Setting:* Dov Adler's pod, Wen Zhao on the phone with the prime broker. *Task:* PB-01's beta residue is hedged in CDX HY; Marcus asks why the margin line is what it is. *What goes wrong:* Wen prices the futures alternative (Eurex IM comparison, vendor, FUT row 2025-02-17; CME's automatic rate offsets, FUT row 2024-04-23), and the margin halves, but the futures–CDX basis is not published anywhere (FUT §3 "The index market underneath"), and the roll-day series basis is a line on the explain. *Learned:* the `> **The Pair.**` box per `state/rv_thread.md` row for 10-B: H: beta through the index; I: unchanged; L: series roll basis, skew to intrinsic, and cheaper margin against an unmeasured basis. Wen's rule: "A short leg with no published basis is a short leg whose basis you are about to discover."

9. **10B.9 What the Contracts Do to the Trend Sleeve's Cost Curve.** *Setting:* back at the CTA. *Learned:* capacity: combined Cboe and CME ADV about $660m is roughly 2% of CDX IG plus HY ADV ($36.9bn, Q2 2026) and OI about 0.1% of cleared CDS OI ($3.08tn) (FUT trend 1, CHECK: mixed months); an average CDX HY ticket ($21.5m) is about 115 IBHY contracts, a third of a quiet day; a sleeve above roughly $50m of HY beta is a block customer (FUT §4). The 2026–2028 extrapolation, labelled as such. **When to apply it / when it breaks:** March 2026 (Eurex record EUR 742m ADV while Cboe fell from $435m to $240m; resilience claims are the exchanges' own, FUT row 2026-03); April 2025 tariff week printed a few tenths of a billion a day (FUT row derived).
