## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** Every game a market-making or systematic credit shop plays in an interview is a section this book has already derived. The adverse-selection puzzle is §3.2; "make me a market" is §18.4 and §18.7; the sizing question is §28.4; the hedge-ratio defence is §4B and §8.4; the coding screen is App B §B.16 and §7.7; the quant-research grilling is §7.6 and Ch 4 exercise 2. The chapter does not teach new mathematics. It re-indexes the book for the one day the reader has not yet lived: the day they are examined on it. It is also Theo's origin story (COACH red-pen 11; synthesis D6), told as a labeled flashback to the summer of 2026, before the "three weeks since PJT" of Ch 1 and Ch 3 (Theo starts Mon 21 Sep 2026 by the C10 clock: Fri 9 Oct is week three; CHECK against the preface once D6 lands).

**The aha.** The ex-banker walks in believing the interview tests what they know. It tests *how they lose*. Every game has a live counterparty who trades against the candidate's answer, and the only passing move is to update in the open: narrow the market after being hit, cut the Kelly fraction because the recovery is a document variable, say "which measure?" before saying a number, and deliver the credit pitch as a distribution with a horizon, a hedge and a markout instead of a memo with a recommendation. The reader's RSSG skill is not disqualified; it is relabeled. "Downside case" becomes a left tail; "cushion" becomes distance to a barrier; "the lenders" become a holder vector. The chapter should end where §A.10 (the dictionary) begins.

**Position in the book.** Follows 4-C (other seats, *their* view of one bond); precedes Part II. 3-B owns the first year and "How You Will Be Measured" (§3B.7) and the on-seat ninety days (§3B.8); `00_02` owns the pre-seat ninety days. 4-D owns the interview, the pitch and the Portfolio (coach App I folded in as §4D.11; the Portfolio appendix is deferred to Edition 3). Do not re-derive anything: every section opens a scene, works one number, and points at the home section.

3. **§4D.2 Probability and expected value in credit form.** Setting: Monday, the bank desk, a conference room with a whiteboard still showing someone's swap-spread trade. Task: "A bond pays 100 or 40. The market says 85. What default probability is quoted?" Theo answers 25% and is asked, "In which measure?" What goes wrong: he had never been asked. Worked: $85 = 100(1-q) + 40q \Rightarrow q = 15/60 = 25\%$, a $\mathbb{Q}$ number at zero rates and one period; the $\mathbb{P}$ number is lower by whatever the spread pays for bearing the jump (§5.1, 6-B). Second puzzle: three outcomes (par, exchange at 70, default at 40) and the variance the two-point answer hides (§3.1.1–3.1.2). **Math, Gently** here: what a distribution is, why a price is an expectation under one measure and a forecast under another, with the 85/100/40 picture.

4. **§4D.3 Conditional probability against a live counterparty (§3.2 as a game).** Setting: same room, the interviewer turns the puzzle into a trade: "I'll sell you the bond. Now what is it worth?" Worked: value 100 or 40, prior one-half; fraction informed $\pi = 0.2$; informed sell only at 40, uninformed sell half the time. $P(\text{sell}\mid 40) = 0.6$, $P(\text{sell}\mid 100) = 0.4$, so $P(40 \mid \text{sell}) = 0.6$ and the bid is $0.6(40) + 0.4(100) = 64$ against a mid of 70; by symmetry the ask is 76; the 12-point spread is adverse selection with no inventory cost at all. The interviewer sells again: update again. What goes wrong: Theo updates on the second sale as if it were the first (base-rate trap, §3.4.3). Lesson: the hand history (§3.3) is the sequence of posteriors. **Poker Table** box.

5. **§4D.4 Make me a market (§18.4, §18.7).** Setting: back to the cold open, replayed slowly. The three variants: width as a confidence interval (the interval that is wrong one time in ten is the right width, not the comfortable one); inventory skew (after being hit twice, the reservation price moves by $q\gamma\sigma^2(T-t)$, §18.4.3); the hit-ratio trap (a candidate who widens to zero never trades and never learns; the desk must "answer everything and win the hands we should", §18.7). Ines's rule for candidates: move the mid before you widen. **Ways This Blows Up**: the candidate who quotes a market on a number that has a document answer (the page count exists; the interviewer may know it).

6. **§4D.5 Kelly with a trapdoor (§28.4).** Setting: Tuesday, the pod floor at Vantress (labeled fictional platform; Owen Achterberg's referral from business school per 4-C; the interviewing PM is a third pod, not Dov's, not Owen's, so the cast bible's "Theo never trades in the pod" holds; log the new unnamed PM under Minor cast). Task: "B3 bond, 4.5% carry, 3.1% PD, 40% recovery. How much?" Theo computes $f^\ast = p/L - q/c = 0.926$ and says 93%, then hears himself. Worked from §28.4: sensitivities $-2.69$ to $L$ and $-23.9$ to $q$; $\pm 1.5$ points of PD moves $f^\ast$ by 36 points of NAV; the trapdoor is that $L$ is a document variable (is the first lien first?). Then the pod's own answer: a stop on allocated capital caps the size in dollars before Kelly is consulted; on $200m with a 58-point loss, the largest long that does not breach a 7.5% firing line alone is $25.9m, and limiting one default to 2.5% of capital gives $8.6m (POD §4.4; thresholds press-reported for one firm, label as such). The "half" answer: fractional Kelly as the price of not knowing $q$ and $L$. **View From Another Seat**: the same bond sized by a long-only systematic manager (DTS budget, §8.4), a dealer (inventory limit, §18.5), and Ironshield's public rule, max size = loss budget / downside in points (POD §4.4, sourced).

7. **§4D.6 The pod interview: pitch a pair and defend the hedge ratio (RV thread beat).** Setting: same floor, the platform risk manager joins; his only question is the platform's sentence, "What is this book long that it did not mean to be?" (the Vantress line; attribute to the unnamed risk manager, not Marcus, unless the cast bible is updated). Task: pitch long A, short B. Worked from POD §5.2 (illustrative inputs): long $40m of A (380bp, spread duration 4.2) hedged with B (520bp, 4.6) is $36.5m short DV01-neutral, $26.7m DTS-neutral, $21.9m at an empirical beta of 0.60; the four-state table shows the alpha ($0.25m for 15bp of idio tightening) is the same size as the hedge-ratio error ($0.2$–$0.35m$); carry net of a 75bp borrow is $-0.07m. What goes wrong: Theo defends DV01 because it is "the formula"; the PM asks which state he is paid in. Lesson (T2 of `rv_thread.md`): idio is whatever the hedge model leaves; say which ruler and why, then size to the stop and the borrow at once, smaller number wins. Point to Ch 8-D for the short-leg menu and 8-B for the stop; do not re-derive. This is the chapter's mandatory `> **The Pair.**` beat; label it as an interview exercise, not a Pair Book entry.
