## 1. Thesis and the aha

Chapter 18 ends with one bid on one RFQ, solved by hand on an afternoon. Chapter 18-B is the machine that answers thousands a day, and its thesis is that **every number sales calls a target is a price the engine pays, and every number the platform calls a count is a smaller number in disguise.** The hit ratio is not a score; it is a Lagrange multiplier per client tier, a subsidy the desk hands out, and the only question is who receives it. The responder count on the screen is not the competition; the effective dealer count $n_{\text{eff}} = n/(1+(n-1)\phi)$ is, and the desk can estimate $\phi$ from its own log. The cover price looks like data about the market but is observed only when the desk wins, so it is data about the desk's own winner's curse, identified from one side.

The aha, which the reader should feel in the Altice scene before the math names it: **a pod's pair is two RFQs sent to two engines, and the second engine has already watched the first one lose.** Legging is not a timing cost; it is the dealer's toxicity term reading the pod's first ticket.

Two corrections the chapter must carry (RFQ dossier, findings 1–2): the five-term quote is **Niang's** (RFQ [2]); Barzykin–Ciceri have three terms (riskless spread, inventory correction, hit-ratio correction) (RFQ [1]). The SYSTEM_PROMPT entry and Ch 18 §18.7 both blur this; 18-B states it cleanly and §18.7's attribution gets a one-clause fix. And Fermanian–Guéant–Pu's "quotes become more conservative as $n$ rises" is their full-participation result; their preferred model reverses it (RFQ [7]). Ch 3 §3.2 carries the wrong half; 18-B says so with a § pointer.

## 2. Section outline as scenes

**18B.0 Cold open: the green tile that was lying.** *Setting:* Monday 7:40 a.m., the RFQ loop screen of §3B.1, Theo on the rota. *Task:* the mundane one. The weekly tile shows hit ratio up two points and cover gap wider by a third, both green because each is inside its own band. *What goes wrong:* Ines reads the two together and says the desk has been paying clients to trade with it for a week (§3B.3 wrote the rule; this is the first time Theo sees it bite). Nobody set a parameter wrong. The target was met. *Lesson:* a met target with a widening cover gap is the engine doing exactly what it was told. "Give me the tier," Ines says, "not the average." Keep the §18.7 panel-review e-mail to one sentence and a § pointer; this scene is its sequel, not its retelling.

**18B.1 The target is a price.** *Setting:* Kaz's whiteboard, the same morning. *Task:* show that Ch 18's flat $\nu$ is Barzykin–Ciceri's $\tilde\xi$: dualizing the quadratic hit-ratio penalty $-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(r-r^\star)^2$ keeps the HJB separable by bond, tier, side and size; the exact quote is $\delta^\star = f^{-1}(-H'(p)/\lambda)$ with $p = \Delta u - \tilde\xi$ for targeted tiers only; the BEGV quadratic ansatz gives a Riccati equation $A' = ADA - \phi\Sigma$ and the dual $\tilde\xi = \tilde\kappa\,y$ with $y$ loading the inventory curvature $A_{mm}$ (RFQ [1], eqs. 12–19, 26–38; synthetic ladder 1/5/20mm, $r^\star = 0.1$). *What goes wrong:* the desk ran a constant $\nu$ through a day of large inventory, and [1] shows a constant dual fails there because $\tilde\xi(q)$ is roughly quadratic. Untargeted "background" flow, and a correlated background bond, cut the skew and raise the achieved ratio: the index-rebalancing client the desk never targets is a free inventory hedge. *Lesson:* the multiplier moves with the book; re-solve it when curvature changes. All of [1]'s numbers are synthetic and unreplicated; say so.

**18B.2 Three terms to five.** *Setting:* Thursday markout review (§3.3), Theo with the hand history sorted by tier. *Task:* decompose markouts into factor, carry/rolldown, issuer-RV, index/ETF and residual; only the residual is toxicity $\chi_i$; weights $w_i = e^{-\gamma\chi_i}$ enter the dual as a Hamiltonian shift $z_iH_i(p_i + \chi_i - w_i\xi)$; logistic fills give the Lambert-W closed form (RFQ [2]). Niang's synthetic numbers, labelled as his: at an 8% target, raw targeting earns $-14.32$ bp·MM/day against $+5.25$ residual-targeted; raw wins 7.47% of toxic flow, residual-targeted 0.87%. *What goes wrong:* Kaz first uses gross markouts as toxicity, and the carry/RV client's hit ratio collapses to about 1% ("naive gross toxicity," [2]); sales calls within the hour. Then the collider: conditioning on a win sits on the path $\delta \to RS \leftarrow \delta_{\text{res}} \leftarrow IA \to \mu \to R$, so revenue-on-hit is not identified from observables and information asymmetry must be modelled (RFQ [5]; BBVA government-bond RFQs, 102,437 inquiries, AUC 0.742 generative vs 0.743 LightGBM vs 0.684 logistic, and the tree violates monotonicity in the quote). *Lesson:* "IDO or it didn't happen" applied to the client, with the warning that the only critique on record says Niang's gains are built into his simulation (automated referee note; CHECK before quoting it as more than "reported"). Add Cartea–Sánchez-Betancourt's parameter-light alternative (RFQ [27]) as the benchmark Kaz is told to beat.

**18B.3 The cover price is one-sided data.** *Setting:* the log export, a dull Friday, Theo building the schema with ROBO (App B). *Task:* FGP's outcome taxonomy (Done, Tied Traded Away, Covered, Other Traded Away, Not Traded; 2014–15 BNP Paribas on Bloomberg FIT, 209,069 buy and 272,189 sell RFQs) and the likelihood: Done with cover $C$ is $n f(C)(1-F(C))^{n-1}(1-G(Y))$, Not Traded is $\int^Y (1-F)^n g$ (RFQ [7]). Only about 40% of asked dealers answer; expected answers sit near two for every panel size ($p(n)$ = 1.00, 0.655, 0.498, 0.417, 0.351); the client's reservation value sits about 1.7 half-spreads from CBBT mid. A worked log-likelihood from Table 1 counts (Done share from 35.1% at one rival to 19.0% at five; the dossier's own arithmetic, label it). *What goes wrong:* Kaz fits the rival distribution $F$ on wins alone and gets a shading estimate that says the desk never suffers a winner's curse. The cover is observed only when the desk wins; the losses and the not-traded carry the other half of $F$. *Lesson:* the Covered and Traded-Away rows are the estimator; the blotter is not. Hand-off to Ch 2-B §2B.1, which reads the same inquiry from the client's screen, and to Jacobsen–Venkataraman–Xu's finding that untraded RFQs move CP+ (RFQ [24], vendor data, not peer-reviewed).

**18B.4 How many dealers are in the room.** *Setting:* model review, a conference room, the risk committee asking "why is the competition parameter 4 when the screen says 27?" *Task:* estimate $\phi$ from the desk's own responder counts by beta-binomial and report $n_{\text{eff}}$ (notation from §2.3.2). Anchors: Hendershott–Madhavan Table VI, 25–28 dealers queried, response 15–29% by size, no-response 8–15%, HY worse (about 60% of HY auctions draw two or fewer bids); ln(dealers queried) coefficient 0.54 IG, 0.45 HY (RFQ [14]). The dossier's beta-binomial fit implies $\rho \approx 0.06$–$0.30$ and 3–11 effective invitees out of 25–28 (illustrative; the dossier's arithmetic, CHECK and label). Index CDS: 4.1 queried, 3.6 responses (RFQ [17]). Theory: the client should call two (RFQ [18]) and ration contacts to limit leakage (RFQ [19]); relationship networks have a cost minimum below 20 dealers (RFQ [21]). *What goes wrong:* the committee asks whether $\phi$ is the same number as the $\phi$ in Riccati above (it is not; name them apart, $\phi$ for response correlation, $\phi_\Sigma$ or $\kappa_q$ for risk aversion). *Lesson:* shade the winner's curse by $n_{\text{eff}}$ and the answer rate, not by $n$. No paper estimates this from a desk log (dossier, Debate 3); the book's estimator is the first, and says so.

**18B.5 Stress-day response counts and the switch.** *Setting:* same review, second agenda item: "the stress parameters." *Task:* build the switch from public numbers. MarketAxess: average prices per US HG RFQ "dropped from 13.2 to 6.6 on April 9" 2025 and "rebounded to 11.3 within a week"; HG bid-ask widened "by as much as 231%" and averaged 9.11 bp against 44.62 bp at the COVID peak; algos "consistently around 80%" of liquidity (PV row 2025-04-02 to 04-09, AxessPoint 2025-07-14). Tradability, the platform's expected-response model, fell "roughly 20%" market-wide and "nearly an entire response" hour-over-hour (PV row 2026-03-23). Map 13.2 → 6.6 → 11.3 through $n_{\text{eff}}$ at the desk's own $\phi$: at $\phi = 0.45$ it moves from about 2.0 to about 1.9 (§2.3.2 already does this arithmetic; cite, do not redo), so the damage sat in $\theta$ and in $\phi$ itself. Trend Watch: the FILS Europe panel, "help more in quiet markets," "concentration to the usual banks," algos "stayed there" in April 2025 but were turned off in Russia/Ukraine (PV row 2026-10-15, three practitioners, no numbers); Bank of Canada's bifurcation model (PV row 2025-01-08). Tradeweb's April 2025 blog is second-hand: CHECK, do not cite numbers. *What goes wrong:* ROBO's first switch triggers on $n < 7$ and fires every afternoon at 3:50 because afternoon panels are small anyway; the fix is a switch on the Tradability-style expected-response residual by tier and hour. Hedge leg outside the band from RFQ [10]. The day itself is told in §2.9.3, §12.9 and §22; here one sentence and the § each.

**18B.6 The pod's pair hits two engines.** *Setting:* Wednesday 20 March 2024, two dealer desks, told from the dealers' side; PB-14 (Owen Achterberg's capital-structure pod at Vantress, fictional composites; Altice France instruments, dates and plan terms real, see Ch 18 §18.6.3's Pair box). *Task:* decompose the two quotes tier by tier. Dealer A fills the secured leg at 10:41 (wanted out: inventory skew dominates, subsidy positive). Dealer B lost that RFQ as Covered, so its log already holds the pod's direction (Baldauf–Mollner: a losing dealer learns, RFQ [19]); at 11:20 the holdco sell RFQ arrives from the same tier and B's residual-toxicity term and price-reading channel (RFQ [3]: adverse selection $\tilde\zeta$ and skew-sniffing $\tilde J$) both price a capital-structure pair, so the short fills about a point and a half below the 10:40 composite *(illustrative)*. Add the all-to-all leakage estimate, 0.1–0.3 bp same-direction impact within 100 minutes, larger for sells and illiquid bonds (RFQ [25], Tradeweb, vendor-authored). *What goes wrong:* the pod paid the jump twice, in drift and in B's fear; from B's chair the second RFQ was the toxic one and the engine was right. *Lesson:* legging is two engines sharing a log. Ch 18 opens and closes PB-14; 18-B is its stress and must update `state/rv_pair_book.md` row PB-14 in the same commit. Mirror with §19.8's Halvorsen leg (the line the basket hands back).

**18B.7 Gates, campaigns and the relationship discount.** *Setting:* the platform's quarterly dealer review, a slide with the desk's rank. *Task:* Barzykin's two-tier gate: tier A flow multiplied by a gate $G(R)$ of a slow win score, fold bifurcations and hysteresis, "campaign versus harvest" (RFQ [4], FX-framed, inference for bonds). Evidence: quoting raises a dealer's chance of trading that bond-day 4.8 pp and improves client execution 6–7 bp, yet the best-quoting dealer wins only 15% of trades and 17–40% of trades are traded through (RFQ [15], BondCliQ runs, JF 2026); the top 1% of relationship clients pay 51% below the median and the discount doubles in stress (RFQ [22], BIS WP 1140, abstract only); non-financial clients pay 10.9 pips more in FX derivatives (RFQ [23]). Citadel Securities' proposed LP metrics and self-reported 14.2% of IG block risk transfer (PV; self-reported, say so). *What goes wrong:* the desk ran a campaign to recover rank and found it could not harvest without losing it. *Lesson:* if the platform ranks on hit ratio, $\xi$ prices future access and the ratio is a state variable with memory.

**18B.8 Fill curves, and when every desk runs this.** *Setting:* Kaz's backtest. 1,073,926 HSBC European corporate RFQ responses, 216 features, AUC about 0.63 for every classical model, flat across blinding; the quantum-feature 0.97 that the authors call "questionable" (RFQ [6]). *What goes wrong:* a junior's 0.97 that decays with the blinding gap. *Lesson:* a leak, not a signal; 0.6–0.65 is production truth. Then the endogeneity: if every desk fits the same $f$ on the same platform, $f$ is endogenous, and learning quoters reach supracompetitive spreads without communicating in simulation (RFQ [26], Cont–Xiong, abstract). Regulatory scrutiny pointer to Ch 22.
