## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** In corporate credit the dataset is not an input to the strategy; it *is* the strategy. A bond's "price" at month-end is a composite of a stale print, a capped volume flag and a cancel that arrived three days later, and the same error sits in the sorting signal and in the return denominator. Most of what the factor literature found between 2013 and 2019 was that error, read with hindsight. The audit of 2026 did not discover new economics; it discovered a build order. A desk that builds the panel in that order, with the signal price separated from the return price and every filter chosen before it sees the returns, inherits a short list of things that still pay: credit-spread value, a within-issuer spread reversal, and residual momentum inside an issuer's own curve. Everything else on the list is gross of costs and smaller than its own non-standard error.

**The aha** (end of Scene 4). Theo, who built 13-week models in which every number had a source document, realises that a month-end bond return has *two* prices in it and the paper he is reading used one price twice. The fix is the restructuring banker's reflex: never let the same number be both the question and the answer. Kaz's version: "the noise in the denominator is the alpha in the numerator." The second aha (Scene 7, RV beat): after every bias is removed, the only momentum left is residual momentum ranked *within one issuer's bonds*. The literature's survivor is the pod's trade. The residual is the signal.

**Law of the Desk** (new, PAP §6): *The signal price is never the entry price.*

## 3. Box placement

- **Math, Gently** (first uses): (a) §9B.3 *a return has two prices*: picture a bond marked 98 on Friday and trading 99 on Monday; the Friday-to-Friday return and the Monday-to-Friday return differ by the gap, and only one of them is yours to earn. (b) §9B.4 *correlated errors-in-variables*: the same ±0.50% noise in signal and denominator, worked to the 1.00% phantom; full version is Theorem B.1 (point to Ch 9 §9.9's Jensen box rather than repeating it).
