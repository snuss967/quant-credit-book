# Advisor Brief: Chapter 4-C, "Other Seats at the Table: One Bond, Seven Owners"

Target file: `book_src/04_ch04c_other_seats_at_the_table.md`. Written 2026-10-18 for the Opus drafter. Brief length ~2,300 words. The user's headline verdict on Edition 1 ("a lot of prescriptiveness and not narrative") is the governing constraint: this chapter is a sequence of scenes with one tape running through them, not a taxonomy with anecdotes attached. The seat table belongs at the end, as the thing the reader can now draw for themselves, never at the start.

## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

Theo's desk is the only seat in the room that is marked to market every day and has no rule that forces its hand. Every other owner of the Halvorsen 7.25% notes due 2029 has a clock, a charge, a test or a gate: a 7.5% CCC bucket, an NAIC risk-based-capital factor, a 5% quarterly redemption cap, a G-SIB surcharge, a fund life, a vote threshold. When the bond drops fourteen points in a month, the seven owners do not disagree about the credit so much as they obey different rules, and the price path the desk sees is the sum of those rules firing in sequence. The desk's edge is not a better opinion of Halvorsen. It is pricing the rule that binds the seller on the other side of the RFQ, and knowing which rule fires next (dossier: `state/research/perspectives_buyside_credit_managers.md` §9, "Read across").

Second aha, for the closing scene: the seat map is the coalition map. On the first day of the grace period the seven owners stop being investors with horizons and become the classes of Chapter 12-B and the DIP lender, backstop party and claims fund of 12-C. Who sat where in the bad month decides who sits where in the courtroom (`state/reviews/ed2/synthesis.md`, the 4-C entry).

The reader's "I didn't know that": the firms' own public words say the same thing their trades say. Apollo's "principal's mindset" line, Ares's "we will walk away from transactions based on documentation terms," Howard Marks's "because there's no market, private credit can't actually mark to market," Arougheti's "pro-cyclical" wealth flows, Kundu's haircut-at-lowest-price rule, the Fed's "two-thirds" RBC saving, Orlan's "it's hard for investment banks to run large balance sheets in this environment" are not opinions about Halvorsen; they are each seat describing its own leash.

## 2. The backbone: one bond, one month, in canon

Use Halvorsen, not a real issuer, as the backbone; real events enter as the public record each seat points to. Canon, with a CHECK the drafter must resolve first:

- Chapter 1's cold open: Friday 2:58 p.m., an all-to-all RFQ to sell \$15mm of "Halvorsen Building Products 7.25% senior unsecured notes due 2029, B3/B-", after a lender call. Chapter 8-B's cold open says the notes were 92 on Friday and opened at 78 on Monday, that the desk owns \$12mm face, and that Theo cut recovery from 40 to 15 after reading an 8-K about new-money financing sitting in a subsidiary that holds the patents. Chapter 12-B's §12B.9 cap table (used "from here through Chapter 16") calls the company "Halvorsen Packaging" and the 7.25% 2029s *senior secured*, pari passu with a \$900mm TLB, with \$600mm of unsecured 6% 2031s and \$250mm of 3% 2045s below. **CHECK:** the two descriptions conflict (name, secured/unsecured). The forthcoming `00_01` issuer bible settles it; until then follow 12-B's cap table, since Parts IV and V are built on it, and keep the 7.25s as the secured notes. If the bible decides the 7.25s are unsecured, the CLO and insurer scenes still work because they hold the TLB and the structured paper, not the notes.
- 4-C's month sits *between* Ch 1's Friday and 12-B's Monday (the missed \$18mm coupon on the 6% 2031s). Day 1 is the RFQ; day 30 is the morning the coupon is not paid. Ch 1 already carries two View From Another Seat boxes on this bond (the RV pod and the CLO manager, Ch 1 §1.1 and the "hybrid" subsection). Do not repeat them; 4-C picks those two seats up a week later, after the rule has fired.
- Illustrative price path (label it so): 92 Friday; 78 Monday; drifts to 81 by Wednesday as the fast sellers finish; the TLB is cut to Caa1 on day 9; 74 on day 12 after the month-end CLO sales; a \$40mm block prints at 72 on day 16; 76 on day 23 on a drop-down rumour that the new money will be unsecured-friendly; 70 on day 30. Every seat sees the same seven prints; the chapter is about what each one books.

## 3. Section outline as scenes

**Cold open (600 words). "Seven people bought the same bond and only one of them is losing money."** Theo's morning explain on day 2 shows a 14-point loss on \$12mm. Mags: "Somewhere there is a man who owns this bond at 99 and has not lost a cent, and he is right, and so are you." Kaz asks how both can be true. The chapter promises the answer by walking around the table. Set up the seven seats as seven chairs the Ad Hoc Group has not yet pulled together.

**4C.1 Friday, 2:58 p.m.: the dealer desk (Jefferies/BofA-style).** *Setting:* a mid-sized dealer's HY desk, the voice trader and the e-trading pricer running one book (Orlan: "seamless integration between our low touch electronic trading and our high touch voice desks," Investor Day 16 Oct 2025). *Task:* answer the same RFQ Theo saw, except this desk also led the issuer's 2021 bond deal and the client is a fund that wants allocations. *What goes wrong:* the dealer wins the \$15mm at 90.75, cannot find the other side by Monday, and learns the bond is outside every ETF creation basket, so the usual hedge is gone (Fed SCOOS June 2025: three-fifths of dealers "frequently hedge" baskets with credit ETFs). The desk head's limit, not the credit, decides the Tuesday sale at 79. *Lesson:* the dealer's horizon is days, its capital is someone else's balance sheet, and its edge is the franchise; its number for the bond is the price at which it can be rid of it (`perspectives_dealers_and_systematic_shops.md` §1.1, §1.2, 5B.5, §6 table). Mention, by its public record only, that a dealer's credit exposure arrives through affiliates too: Jefferies' Point Bonita fund held about US\$715mn of First Brands receivables (GTR, 8 Oct 2025; **CHECK** the US\$43mn direct-exposure figure, search summary only). Hand off: "the five-term quote this desk should have run is Chapter 18-B's."

**4C.2 Monday, 6:45 a.m.: the systematic fund.** *Setting:* a long-only systematic credit manager's rebalance, benchmark-relative, 1,600 to 2,600 issuers (Robeco's breadth, `perspectives_dealers_and_systematic_shops.md` §3.6; Man Numeric's ~90% electronic execution, `trend_systematic_credit_industry.md` row Sep 2023, **CHECK**). *Task:* the overnight run flags Halvorsen: value says cheap, momentum says sell, the default-probability guard says exclude. *What goes wrong:* the optimizer's "if filled" sell goes out as an RFQ at 8:02 and comes back at 77 against a composite of 80; the TCA line eats a quarter of the month's alpha budget. Nobody on this desk reads the 8-K; the document risk is a residual (AQR's stance: a defensive or quality screen excludes exactly this name; Palhares–Richardson find no liquidity premium worth holding, 5B.8). *Lesson:* the systematic seat is a price taker whose cost is the dealer's spread, whose horizon is months, and whose rule is the risk budget. Hand off to 8-B §8B.9 (the limit sheet) and Ch 11 §11.6 (the CCC problem). Humor: ROBO, asked to "model the other holders," returns seven copies of our own desk.

**4C.3 Monday, 10:00 a.m.: the multi-strategy RV pod (DE Shaw-style).** *Setting:* a capital-structure pod inside a multi-manager, no obligation to quote. *Task:* choose the hand: long TLB / short 7.25s, or long 7.25s / buy CDS protection, or nothing. *What goes wrong:* the pod's loan-level work says the market is over-extrapolating the last three drop-downs; it buys \$10mm of notes at 79 on day 3 and is down four points by day 12 because the CLO month-end selling it modelled came a week early. The pod survives because its capital is pooled by horizon. *Public record to lean on:* D. E. Shaw bought "in excess of \$400 million in CAS and STACR bonds at a significant discount to par" in March–April 2020 from holders "put under stress by redemption requests and margin calls," judging 2001 the better analogue than 2008 (`perspectives_dealers_and_systematic_shops.md` §3.1); its Diopter and Alkali funds buy banks' capital-relief risk (5B.8). State plainly that D. E. Shaw's public credit record is structured and private, not flow HY; the pod is a generic composite. **CHECK:** the "above 30× NAV" RV leverage figure (`trend_systematic_credit_industry.md`) is secondary; use only with the flag or omit. *Lesson:* same tape, opposite trade to 4C.2, and both are coherent given each seat's horizon.

**4C.4 Day 9 to day 12: the CLO manager.** *Setting:* a manager's month-end compliance run; the TLB is cut to Caa1 on day 9. *Task:* keep seven numbers inside their boxes (CCC share, WARF, diversity, WAS, WAL, OC, IC). *What goes wrong:* the bucket is at 7.9%; the excess is marked at the lowest CCC price in the pool, which is a different loan at 58; the manager sells a *better* CCC loan at 86 to shrink the excess and keeps Halvorsen (Kundu, ESRB WP 141: "typically set to 7.5%"; excess "marked at the lowest market value"; "the median CLO operates within 4% of the most stringent covenant threshold"). Three other managers in the same name sell Halvorsen instead, and the desk's bid on day 12 competes with sellers who want out "at the cheapest approved moment." *Public record:* CLOs buy "nearly three-quarters of all leveraged loans issued" (Deutsche Bank Flow, 7 Oct 2025); market CCC averaged 4.3% and junior OC cushions 3.9% (Majewski, Eagle Point, 18 Feb 2026); S&P 2023: 42% of 554 reinvesting CLOs above 7.5% but just over 1% failing junior OC. Managers sit on both sides of LMTs: Serta's prevailing lenders were Barings, Eaton Vance, Invesco and others; the objectors included LCM lenders (Fifth Circuit, 31 Dec 2024; LCM's CLO status is background knowledge, say so). *Lesson:* a rule-bound buyer is a predictable seller; the best entry is after the test date (`trend_loans_clos.md` §4, the `forced_pressure` hook). Hand off to Ch 21 §21.4 (tests as an option book) and §21.8.

**4C.5 Day 12: the life insurer.** *Setting:* a general-account portfolio review, quarterly, the kind of meeting with a printed agenda. *Task:* the insurer does not own the 7.25s. It owns a BBB CLO tranche with Halvorsen's TLB inside it, and a private placement from a Halvorsen competitor, and the question is capital charge per unit of spread, not price. *What goes wrong:* nothing, this quarter, and that is the point; the review notes a one-notch tranche watch and moves on. The scene's tension is the calendar: the NAIC's June 2026 C-1 factors take effect for year-end 2026 reporting, with an 11.77% pretax surcharge on below-IG BSL CLO tranches 4% thick or thinner (Sidley, 9 Jul 2026), so the *date* that forces this seat is 31 Dec, not day 12. *Public record:* life-insurer BSL and MM CLO exposure "a record \$212 billion" at end-2023; packaging B loans into a CLO and buying the whole stack cuts RBC "by two-thirds" (Fed note, 21 Mar 2025); Rowan's "Spread is created by having an asset origination machine, by having a liability origination machine at a low cost" (9 Feb 2026); Kelleher's "looming systemic risk" against Rowan's "People have really just lost their minds" (Nov 2025). Athene's \$292B invested assets and 120 bps net spread (Zelter, 9 Feb 2026) size the seat. *Lesson:* this seat sells when a designation changes, never when a price does; its demand is slow and discontinuous at regulatory dates (`perspectives_buyside_credit_managers.md` §8). Cross-ref 17.5 and 22.

**4C.6 Day 14 to day 16: the direct lender / alternative-credit manager (Ares-style).** *Setting:* a BDC's investment committee, Tuesday, slide 14 of 60. *Task:* the sponsor has asked this lender to provide the new money at the IP subsidiary (the financing Theo read about in the 8-K). The lender also holds a \$35mm piece of a sister company's unitranche. *What goes wrong:* the committee likes the LTV ("high 30% to low 40%" is ARCC's range, Miller, 4 Feb 2026) and hates the documents; it walks ("We will walk away from transactions based on documentation terms," same call). Meanwhile its non-traded vehicle's redemption requests are running at 8% against a 5% cap, and the CFO asks whether the liquid sleeve, which holds Halvorsen's TLB, is the release valve (Arougheti: "there is a risk that some of the wealth flows could be more pro-cyclical than people thought," 31 Jul 2026; BCRED 10% requests vs. 5% repurchased, ACI 23 Jul 2026; HPS 5% limit Mar 2026, summary only). *Public record on marks:* Pluralsight, ARCC's loan at 89.0 vs. Blue Owl's 96.8 on the same date, then 48.0 (`perspectives_buyside_credit_managers.md` §2; Marks: "because there's no market, private credit can't actually mark to market," 6 Mar 2025). *Lesson:* the hold-and-amend seat has no daily price but has a queue; when the queue binds, public paper is what it sells, and the desk is on the other side. Cross-ref 17.2–17.3 and 17-B. Humor: the lender's analyst runs a 13-week model too, lovingly maintained; Theo recognizes his own species.

**4C.7 Day 16: the opportunistic credit fund (Apollo-style).** *Setting:* the dislocation desk, "0–50% LTV, dislocation-driven" in Apollo's own ladder. *Task:* the dealer from 4C.1 has a \$40mm block from two CLO sellers and nowhere to put it; the fund bids 72 for all of it, against a liability it must earn after a capital charge. *What goes wrong:* the fund asks whether a structure can turn the B into a BBB (its stated model: 2025 origination "approximately 80% IG with an average rating of single A," Zelter; "350 basis points over treasuries with an average rating of BBB," Rowan) and discovers that the pari passu intercreditor makes that a 12-B problem, not a trade. It buys anyway, because it does not mark to the screen and does not need to sell ("A principal's mindset approaches every asset ... as if they're going to own it for the long term because they do," Rowan, 9 Feb 2026). *Public record:* Carvana 2023 ad hoc group with PIMCO and Ares (8-K Ex. 99.1); Hertz plan sponsor (bought equity, not debt); Optimum co-op defendant alongside Ares, Oaktree, PGIM (allegations, not findings). *Lesson:* the block buyer's price is a function of its funding, not of the next print; "price insensitive in a rally, slow to sell in a drawdown." Cross-ref 16.2 (forced selling as supply) and 14.5 (co-ops).

**4C.8 Day 30: the coalition map.** The coupon is missed. Walk the table once more: the dealer is flat; the systematic fund is out; the pod is long notes and short the 2031s; the CLO holds a Caa1 TLB it cannot exchange into equity; the insurer's tranche is on watch; the direct lender holds TLB in its liquid sleeve and declined the priming money; the opportunistic fund holds \$40mm of notes and is talking to the first-lien ad hoc group. Then the sentence that opens Part IV: these are no longer seats; they are classes. Hand off to 12-B §12B.9 and 12-C.

**4C.9 The table, drawn by the reader; When to Apply It; Desk Debrief; Resource Graph.** The seat table (`perspectives_buyside_credit_managers.md` §9 and `..._dealers_and_systematic_shops.md` §6, merged) appears here for the first time. ## 5. Boxes: where they go and what they carry

**Math, Gently** (first use of each idea in the book's reading order; 4-C sits after 4-B, so duration is already known).

1. *4C.1, after the dealer sells at 79.* **Horizon changes the sign of a trade.** A bond with an expected 3-point recovery over a year and 2 points of daily noise is a buy at a one-year horizon and a sell at a twenty-day horizon, because the stop fires before the drift arrives: expected drift scales with $T$, noise with $\sqrt{T}$. Tiny number: drift 3/252 a day against noise 2 a day; the ratio crosses one only after about 180 days. Full version: §3.1.3 and §28.6.
2. *4C.2, when the systematic fund books the TCA line.* **A composite is an average of quotes, not a price.** Worked: composite 80, bid 77, the 3 points are the dealer's half-spread plus the toxicity charge; the fund's alpha was 1.5 points. Full version: Ch 2-B and §18.3.
3. *4C.4.* **The OC test with the haircut.** Numerator = par + defaulted value + purchase price of discount obligations − CCC excess adjustment. Bucket 7.9%, limit 7.5%, excess 0.4% of a \$500mm pool = \$2mm of par marked at 58 instead of 100 costs the numerator \$0.84mm; selling \$2mm of an 86-priced CCC removes the excess and realizes a 14-point loss, which is why the "wrong" sale is rational. Full version: §21.4.
4. *4C.5.* **Accounting is a filter on the same price.** Three ledgers for a 92-to-78 move: mark-to-market books −14; book yield books 0 until impairment; a quarterly NAV books a step on the next mark date. A smoothed series has lower measured volatility and the same realized loss. Full version: §17.2.
5. *4C.5, second box or fold into the fourth.* **Spread per unit of capital.** A BBB private at T+250 with a low C-1 factor beats a BB public at T+300 with a factor several times higher: compare $s/\text{charge}$, not $s$. Full version: Ch 22 and the 17.5 insurer material.
6. *4C.7.* **The LTV ladder is a set of call spreads.** Apollo's 0–50 / 50–80 / 80–100 buckets are claims on $V$ between attachment points; a bond at 72 on a tranche attaching at 0 and detaching at 50% LTV is a deep in-the-money spread with a jump. Full version: §6-B and §12-C (the fulcrum as $\mathbb{P}(A_j \le V < D_j)$).

**View From Another Seat.** The chapter *is* seven of these, so use the box for the seats that did not get a chair:
- *The agent bank* (4C.6): who funds the wire for the drop-down; cite the Revlon wire (Second Circuit, 8 Sep 2022, "approximately \$500 million" while participations traded at 20–30 cents) as public record, no new facts.
- *The venue* (4C.1 or 4C.2): MarketAxess 2022 letter, "as spreads widened, and dealer liquidity was less prevalent," Open Trading penetration 36%; the venue is paid in volume when every other seat is paid in pain.
- *The sponsor* (4C.6): the one owner not at the table who wrote the documents; keep it generic.
- *The excluded lender who becomes a buyer* (4C.8): Elliott's public record (Veritas co-op, Citgo/Amber bid, Judge Stark's "best overall combination of price and certainty of closing"), with the caveat that no Elliott letter was reachable.

**Poker Table** (Ines, in the cold open or 4C.8): a seven-handed table where every player is playing a different game; the question "who at this table knows more than you" has a new answer: nobody knows more about the credit, but each knows one rule you do not. Replay the month as a hand history: the CLO's forced fold on day 12 was visible in the trustee report three weeks earlier.

**Desk Myth vs. Desk Reality** (4C.9): *Myth:* the smart money disagreed with us about Halvorsen. *Reality:* most of the money that moved did not have an opinion; it had a test date.

**Trend Watch** (dated, one per chapter minimum): (a) Ares closes BlueCove, ~Feb 2026, "systematic credit is now bought, not built" (`trend_systematic_credit_industry.md`); (b) NAIC C-1 CLO factors, 23 Jun 2026, effective YE2026 (Sidley); (c) Q1 2026 first net outflows from semi-liquid private-credit vehicles (Fed FSR, 8 May 2026). Each is a change in one seat's rule.

**Ways This Blows Up** (4C.9): modelling the seat from the logo. The same firms are co-op defendants in Optimum and prevailing lenders elsewhere; a "forced-seller pressure" score that keys on manager names instead of trustee-report state vectors will be wrong in both directions.
