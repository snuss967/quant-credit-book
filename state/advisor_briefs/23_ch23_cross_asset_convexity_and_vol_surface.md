## 1. Thesis and the "aha" for an ex-PJT-RSSG reader

**Thesis.** A company's capital structure is one asset-value distribution sliced at different strikes. Equity is the call; the bond is a short put; the CDS is the put stripped of rates; the deep-OTM equity put is a CDS that forgot to tell anyone; the convertible is the bond with the call sewn back on. Because every slice prices the *same* distribution, the volatility surface of one slice is a forecast of the spread of another: $\sigma_E E=\sigma_V V N(d_1)$ (Merton), $\sigma=\sigma_S\,S/(S+\bar L D)$ (CreditGrades), $U(t,T)=[P(B)-P(A)]/(B-A)$ (Carr–Wu), and the steepness of the put skew at low strikes is a hazard rate in disguise. The chapter builds the translation layer between the equity-vol surface and the credit curve, shows how to express credit Greeks in spread units, and then runs the three autopsies where the translation broke in instructive ways: May 2005 (correlation and convertible arbitrage both lost "on both legs" when equity rallied 18% and credit gapped wider the next day), March 2023 (SVB: a bank's equity looked like a 30-vol stock until it was a zero in 48 hours, and Credit Suisse's AT1s got zero while equity got CHF 3bn), and 2025–26 (Wolfspeed's converts as the fulcrum; the AI-driven software sell-off hitting public equity first, HY spreads second, and private marks not at all). The trend story is twofold: structurally, credit now has its own listed vol surface (CDX/iTraxx swaptions, with cleared CDX swaption volume up 100–150% YoY in 2025 and the Cboe Credit VIX family since Oct 2023), and as a strategy, equity vol and equity momentum lead credit (Dynkin ch.15–16; Man 2022 p.25), so the cross-asset surface is itself a trend signal.

**The five ahas, in the order they should land on Theo:**

1. **Your waterfall is a payoff diagram, and the volatility is the thing you never priced.** In RSSG Theo built recoveries conditional on an EV; the systematic desk prices the *distribution* of EV, and the market tells you that distribution through option prices. A CCC bond is an equity option that wears a tie to work; a 20-delta put is a CDS contract that forgot to wear one.
2. **Equity vol is leveraged asset vol, so cheap stocks are mechanically high-vol stocks.** $\sigma_E=\sigma_V\,(V/E)\,N(d_1)$: halve the equity cushion and equity vol roughly doubles with asset vol unchanged. This single identity explains the skew in HY names (CreditGrades p.11 says it in one sentence), why "vol targeting" shorts distressed equity at the worst time, and why the equity-implied spread is a *value* signal (Blackstone's and BlackRock's PD engines both ingest it; Blackstone p.1, BlackRock p.6).
3. **The put you can buy on the screen *is* the CDS you can't.** Carr–Wu (RFS 2011): if the stock cannot sit inside a corridor $[A,B]$ (above $B$ alive, below $A$ dead), the American put spread $[P(B)-P(A)]/(B-A)$ is a unit recovery claim, model-free. Single-name CDS liquidity has shrunk (cleared single-name volumes ≈ 6% of index volume in Q2 2025), so the listed put surface is often the *only* tradable single-name credit instrument for an ex-RSSG analyst's name.
4. **Hedge ratios are the part of Merton that works; prices are the part that doesn't.** Schaefer–Strebulaev (JFE 2008) and Dynkin p.87 show Merton hedge ratios match realized bond–equity elasticities within ~5%, even though Merton misprices spreads. Cap-structure arbitrage is a bet on the *hedge ratio being right and the price being wrong*; Yu (FAJ 2006) and Kapadia–Pu (JFE 2012) show that at short horizons the two markets are only weakly integrated, so single trades blow up and only the portfolio earns the Sharpe (0.75 for speculative grade in Yu).
5. **The surface breaks exactly where the process is not a diffusion: runs, regulators, and votes.** SVB's equity vol was wrong because a bank's default is a liquidity event with a reflexive deposit process (Nagel–Purnanandam, RFS 2020: bank asset vol *rises* after bad shocks). Credit Suisse's AT1 zero with positive equity recovery is a *contractual* inversion of the waterfall. May 2005 was a *correlation* break. Hertz 2020–21 is the happy inversion: old equity got 3% plus warrants because the call was not out of the money. The ex-RSSG reader's edge is knowing where the contract, not the diffusion, decides who gets paid.

## 3. Derivation skeletons

All pricing expectations are under $\mathbb{Q}$ unless flagged. Notation per the style bible: $V_t$ assets, $E_t$ equity, $F$ face, $\sigma_V,\sigma_E$, $\tau$, $\lambda_t$, $S(t)$ survival, $R$, $L=1-R$, $s$ spread (decimal in formulas). CreditGrades uses $S$ for the stock price and $D$ for debt-per-share; the writer should rename to $S_t$ (stock, as in the market-making chapters) and $F^{\text{ps}}$ (debt per share) to avoid the collision with $D$ = duration, and say so in one sentence.

### 3.1 Merton's volatility identity and why cheap stocks are high-vol stocks

Setup: $dV_t=\mu V_t dt+\sigma_V V_t dW_t$ ($\mathbb{P}$), zero-coupon debt $F$ at $T$, $E_T=(V_T-F)^+$. Then $E_0=V_0N(d_1)-Fe^{-rT}N(d_2)$, $d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(V_0/F)+(r\pm\sigma_V^2/2)T}{\sigma_V\sqrt T}$.
Step: apply Itô to $E(V,t)$: $dE=E_V\,dV+(\ldots)dt$, so the diffusion coefficient of $E$ is $E_V\sigma_V V=N(d_1)\sigma_V V$. Match to $dE=\ldots+\sigma_E E\,dW$:
$$\sigma_E E=\sigma_V V N(d_1)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma_E=\sigma_V\,\frac{V}{E}\,N(d_1).\tag{23.1}$$
(Bohn–Stein p.169 writes it as $\sigma_E=\frac{A}{E}\frac{\partial E}{\partial A}\sigma_A$.) Assumptions: diffusion only, constant $\sigma_V$, single debt maturity. Measure: the identity is measure-free (volatilities are invariant under Girsanov).
Consequence 1 (skew): holding $\sigma_V$ fixed, $\partial\sigma_E/\partial E<0$ because $V/E$ rises faster than $N(d_1)$ falls as $E\downarrow$; equity vol rises as the stock falls, which is a negative skew in the surface. CreditGrades p.11 states the same from its calibration $\sigma=\sigma_S S/(S+\bar LD)$.
Consequence 2 (the inverse problem): observe $(E,\sigma_E)$, solve two equations for $(V,\sigma_V)$ (Ch 6 solver), then $s^{\text{model}}(T)=-\tfrac1T\ln\!\big[N(d_2)+\tfrac{V}{Fe^{-rT}}N(-d_1)\big]$.
Error-prone spots: using $\sigma_E$ as $\sigma_V$ (overstates PD by a factor of leverage); $F$ for a multi-tranche structure (KMV default point: short-term debt + ½ long-term; for banks, deposits are debt and the identity collapses, §3.7); using 1-month implied vol for a 5-year horizon (CreditGrades found 1000-day historical matched 5y CDS-implied asset vol best, 88% within 10%); negative $d_1$ regions where $N(d_1)\to0$ and the solver loses conditioning.

### 3.2 CreditGrades: a barrier model you can calibrate before the coffee is ready

Model (CreditGrades pp.6–8): $V_t=V_0e^{\sigma W_t-\sigma^2t/2}$ (zero drift relative to the barrier, argued on p.11); barrier $LD$ with $L=\bar L e^{\lambda Z-\lambda^2/2}$, $Z\sim N(0,1)$ independent of $W$; default is the first time $V_t<LD$.
Step 1: default iff $X_t:=\sigma W_t-\lambda Z-\sigma^2t/2-\lambda^2/2\le\log(\bar LD/V_0)-\lambda^2$. $X_t$ has mean $-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}(t+\lambda^2/\sigma^2)$ and variance $\sigma^2(t+\lambda^2/\sigma^2)$: a Brownian motion with drift $-\sigma^2/2$ *started at time* $-\xi$, $\xi=\lambda^2/\sigma^2$.
Step 2: first-passage law for $Y_t=at+bW_t$: $\Pr\{Y_s>y\ \forall s<t\}=\Phi\!\big(\tfrac{at-y}{b\sqrt t}\big)-e^{2ay/b^2}\Phi\!\big(\tfrac{at+y}{b\sqrt t}\big)$ (reflection principle; p.8 cites Musiela–Rutkowski).
Step 3: substitute $a=-\sigma^2/2$, $b=\sigma$, $y=\log(\bar LD/V_0)-\lambda^2$, $t\to t+\xi$:
$$P(t)=\Phi\!\Big(-\frac{A_t}{2}+\frac{\log d}{A_t}\Big)-d\,\Phi\!\Big(-\frac{A_t}{2}-\frac{\log d}{A_t}\Big),\qquad d=\frac{V_0e^{\lambda^2}}{\bar LD},\quad A_t^2=\sigma^2t+\lambda^2.\tag{23.2}$$
Step 4 (calibration, pp.10–11): boundary conditions near default ($V\to LD$, $\eta\approx1/\sigma_S$) and far from default ($S/V\to1$) are both satisfied by $V_0=S_0+\bar LD$ and
$$\sigma=\sigma_S\,\frac{S}{S+\bar LD}.\tag{23.3}$$
Step 5 (CDS spread, p.9): with constant $r$ and recovery $R$ on the reference obligation, the par spread $c^*$ equates premium and protection legs; closed form (2.15)–(2.16) via Rubinstein–Reiner's $G(u)$. Parameters: $\bar L=0.5$, $\lambda=0.3$ (≈300 US non-financial defaults 1987–97), lower $\lambda$ for financials.
Error-prone spots: (i) $P(0)<1$ by construction (the time-shift trick puts mass at $t=0$; the doc says so); (ii) the closed-form in Finger et al. (2002) was reported to contain an error by Kiesel & Veraart in the *Journal of Credit Risk* (**CHECK** citation; the writer should derive (23.2) independently and verify against a Monte Carlo first-passage with Brownian-bridge correction before printing numbers); (iii) debt-per-share must include leases, under-funded pensions, preferreds (p.12); (iv) $R$ (instrument recovery) $\ne\bar L$ (global recovery); (v) "implied asset vol" from a CDS quote is a well-posed inversion only when the spread is above the model's floor at $t\to0$.

### 3.3 Carr–Wu: the put spread that is a CDS

Define the unit recovery claim $U(t,T)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_t\big[e^{-r(\tau-t)}\mathbf 1_{\{\tau\le T\}}\big]$.
Assumption (default corridor): $S_t>B$ for $t<\tau$ and $S_t<A$ for $t\ge\tau$, $0\le A<B$. The stock never trades inside $[A,B]$.
Claim: for American puts $P^A(K)$ with strikes $A<B$ and expiry $T$, $U(t,T)=\dfrac{P^A(B)-P^A(A)}{B-A}$.
Sketch: pre-default both puts are OTM, so the spread has zero intrinsic value and (because the stock cannot enter the corridor) no positive-probability path on which only one leg finishes in the money; at $\tau\le T$ the stock jumps below $A$, both puts are deep ITM, and immediate exercise of the spread pays exactly $(B-S_\tau)-(A-S_\tau)=B-A$; immediate exercise is optimal because the spread payoff is capped at $B-A$ and time value is only lost by waiting. Hence the spread is a claim paying $B-A$ at $\tau$ if $\tau\le T$; no assumption is made about $S$ dynamics above $B$ or below $A$ (hence "robust"). With $A=0$: $U=P^A(B)/B$.
CDS-implied URC (for the basis): with flat hazard $\lambda$ and recovery $R$, $s=\lambda(1-R)$ (credit triangle) and $U^{\text{CDS}}(T)=\int_0^T e^{-rt}\lambda e^{-\lambda t}dt=\frac{\lambda}{r+\lambda}\big(1-e^{-(r+\lambda)T}\big)$. For standard-coupon CDS use the bootstrapped curve from Ch 24 instead of the triangle. Signal: $\text{basis}_U=U^{\text{opt}}-U^{\text{CDS}}$, traded as put spread vs. CDS.
Error-prone spots: American vs European (listed single-stock puts are American; Cruz López–Ibáñez 2021 show the European version holds under endogenous default with no equity injections); dividends and hard-to-borrow fees distort low-strike puts; bid–ask at 5–10 delta is enormous, so compute $U$ bounds from bid and ask separately; the corridor fails for "slow bleeds" (GM 2005→2009 drifted through every corridor) and holds for jump defaults (SVB: 60% drop March 9, halt March 10); recovery mismatch between the equity-side "unit" claim and the CDS deliverable (Ch 24 CTD).

### 3.4 Skew as jump-to-default

Model (Merton 1976 jump-diffusion with a single jump to zero; same as the pre-default dynamics in Carr–Wu 2010 with constant $\lambda$): under $\mathbb{Q}$,
$$\frac{dS_t}{S_{t^-}}=(r+\lambda)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t-dN_t,\qquad N_t\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda),$$
so $S$ drifts up at $r+\lambda$ to compensate for the jump to zero (martingale condition on $e^{-rt}S_t$).
Step: conditional on no default, $S_T=S_0e^{\lambda T}\cdot(\text{GBM at rate }r)$; the call is $C=e^{-\lambda T}C_{\text{BS}}(S_0e^{\lambda T},K,T,r,\sigma)=S_0N(d_1)-Ke^{-(r+\lambda)T}N(d_2)$, $d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(S_0/K)+(r+\lambda\pm\sigma^2/2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T}$: Black–Scholes with the rate bumped by the hazard. Put–call parity ($P=C-S_0+Ke^{-rT}$):
$$P(K)=Ke^{-rT}\big[1-e^{-\lambda T}N(d_2)\big]-S_0N(-d_1)\ \xrightarrow{K\to0}\ Ke^{-rT}\big(1-e^{-\lambda T}\big)\approx K\lambda Te^{-rT}.\tag{23.4}$$
Reading: deep-OTM puts have a *linear-in-$K$ price floor* that pure diffusion cannot produce; $P(K)/K\to e^{-rT}(1-e^{-\lambda T})$, which is exactly the Carr–Wu URC with $A=0$ and a flat hazard (consistency check for the code). The implied vol must blow up as $K\to0$ to reproduce a positive floor, which is the steep low-strike wing of HY names. Term structure: the floor grows like $\lambda T$, so the skew's term structure is a hazard term structure.
Extensions to mention: state-dependent hazard $\lambda(S)=\lambda_0(S_0/S)^p$ (Andersen–Buffum 2003 for converts; Linetsky 2006 gives closed forms for the CEV-with-default case); stochastic vol plus Cox default (Carr–Wu 2010, estimated on eight firms from B to AAA).
Error-prone spots: confounding stochastic-vol skew with JTD skew (both steepen the wing; the JTD signature is $P(K)/K$ flattening to a positive constant as $K\to0$, the SV signature is $P(K)/K\to0$); fitting $\lambda$ from one strike; forgetting the dividend yield $q$ (replace $r$ by $r-q$ in $d_{1,2}$ but not in the discount); American early exercise on listed puts (the floor becomes $K(1-e^{-\lambda T})$ without the $e^{-rT}$ when exercise at default is immediate).

### 3.5 Greeks in spread terms (Natenberg for the credit desk)

Bond: $\frac{dP}{P}\approx-D^{\text{spr}}ds+\tfrac12C^{\text{spr}}ds^2$ (Veronesi p.152's duration–convexity identity with $r\to s$); CS01 $=D^{\text{spr}}P\times10^{-4}$. DTS: writing $ds=s\,(ds/s)$ gives $\frac{dP}{P}\approx-\text{DTS}\cdot\frac{ds}{s}$, so DTS is the exposure to *relative* spread moves, whose volatility is stable across spread levels (Ben Dor p.70's MLE spec $\sigma_t(s)=\alpha+\beta s_{t-1}+\gamma\hat s^2_{t-1}$ is the test).
CDS: $\text{MTM}=(s^{\text{mkt}}-s^{\text{c}})\cdot\text{RPV01}$, $\text{RPV01}\approx\frac{1-e^{-(r+\lambda)T}}{r+\lambda}$ with $\lambda=s/(1-R)$; "delta to spread" is RPV01; "gamma" $\partial\text{RPV01}/\partial s<0$, so long protection is *short* spread convexity and the bondholder is long it; vega is the sensitivity to $\sigma_V$ via the model spread.
Equity Greeks of a credit instrument (chain rule): $\frac{\partial P}{\partial S}=\frac{\partial P/\partial V}{\partial E/\partial V}$; in Merton, $\partial P/\partial V=N(-d_1)$ per unit of debt and $\partial E/\partial V=N(d_1)$, so the elasticity hedge ratio is $h=\frac{N(-d_1)}{N(d_1)}\cdot\frac{E}{P}$ (Dynkin p.87; Schaefer–Strebulaev 2008 found it matches realized elasticities). CreditGrades p.14 defines delta as the *number of shares* that offsets a CDS; gamma rises as the stock falls; asset vega converts to equity vega through (23.3).
JTD is the Greek that is not a derivative: at default the delta hedge recovers only $\Delta\cdot S_{\tau^-}$ while the protection leg pays $(1-R)\cdot\text{Notional}$; the gap is the JTD exposure that Ch 18 prices into quotes.
Error-prone spots: delta sign flips for the equity hedge of a *long bond, short stock* book when the stock rallies on an LMT rumor (equity up, bond down: the May 2005 pattern); hedge ratios from leverage alone (Dynkin p.88's Netflix problem); using modified duration where spread duration is needed for floating-rate or callable paper.

### 3.6 Cap-structure arbitrage as a stated rule (Yu 2006, adapted)

Signal: $z_t=\dfrac{s^{\text{mkt}}_t-s^{\text{CG}}_t}{s^{\text{CG}}_t}$ with $s^{\text{CG}}$ from (23.2)–(23.3) and 1000-day (or implied) $\sigma_S$.
Entry: if $z_t>\alpha$ (market spread rich to the equity), sell protection and short $\delta$ shares; if $z_t<-\alpha$, buy protection and buy $\delta$ shares; $\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2\}$; $\delta$ = CreditGrades delta.
Exit: $|z_t|<\epsilon$ (convergence) or 180 days (Yu) or a stop. P&L: $\Delta\text{MTM}_{\text{CDS}}-\delta\,\Delta S+\text{carry}-\text{costs}$, decomposed ex post into convergence, carry, gamma ($\tfrac12\Gamma\,\Delta S^2$), and JTD.
Why single trades lose (Yu; Kapadia–Pu): at daily–weekly horizons equity and CDS returns are weakly correlated and a large share of discrepancies are "anomalous" rather than mean-reverting; only the cross-section diversifies the noise (Yu: speculative-grade portfolio Sharpe 0.75). Duarte–Longstaff–Yu: strategies needing "intellectual capital" earn alpha after factors and fees, with positive skew.

### 3.7 Why banks break the surface (for the SVB section)

Nagel–Purnanandam (2020): bank assets are loans = short puts on borrower assets, so the bank's asset payoff is concave; its asset volatility rises after bad shocks and a constant-$\sigma_V$ Merton understates default risk in good times. Add the run: deposits $D_t$ (94% uninsured at SVB) exit at an intensity that increases in observed equity losses, $dD_t=-\kappa(\Delta\ln S_t)\,D_t\,dt$, and default occurs when liquidity $L_t=\text{cash}+(1-h)\cdot\text{saleable securities}-\text{outflows}<0$, not when $V_t<F$. SVB sold \$21bn AFS at a \$1.8bn loss on March 8, lost >\$40bn of deposits on March 9 with \$100bn more expected on March 10, and was closed before the HTM book (6.2-year duration) ever had to be marked. Spec consequence: for banks, the state vector needs (uninsured share, HTM unrealized loss/CET1, duration gap, deposit beta) and a run-intensity function; equity vol is an output, not an input.

- `EquityState`: `S0: float` (last), `sigma_S: float` (annualized decimal; source tag `"hist1000d"|"atm_impl_longest"`), `q: float` dividend yield, `shares_out: float`.
- `DebtPerShare`: `F_ps: float` with a dict of components `{financial_debt, capital_leases, pension_deficit, preferred}` and `asof` (CreditGrades Appendix B rules).
- `CGParams`: `Lbar=0.5`, `lam=0.3` (`lam_financial=0.2`, flagged), `R: float` instrument recovery, `r: float`.
- `OptionChain` (pandas): columns `expiry (date)`, `T (years, ACT/365)`, `K`, `cp ∈ {"P","C"}`, `bid`, `ask`, `american: bool`, `open_interest`; tensor shape after pivot: `(n_expiries, n_strikes)` for bid and ask separately.
- `CDSQuote`: `tenor_years`, `par_spread_bp` or (`upfront_pct`, `coupon_bp`), `recovery`, `curve_date`.

**Functions.**
1. `cg_survival(t, S0, sigma_S, F_ps, Lbar, lam) -> float`: eq. (23.2) with $V_0=S_0+\bar LF^{\text{ps}}$, $\sigma=\sigma_S S_0/(S_0+\bar LF^{\text{ps}})$. Guard: `A_t = sqrt(sigma**2*t + lam**2)`; if `d<=0` raise. Vectorize over `t`.
2. `cg_cds_spread(T, ..., R, r, dt=1/12) -> float`: numerically integrate premium and protection legs on a monthly grid from `cg_survival` (do *not* rely on the closed form (2.15) until the Kiesel–Veraart point is resolved); return decimal spread.
3. `cg_implied_asset_vol(spread_mkt, T, ...) -> float`: `scipy.optimize.brentq` on `sigma_S` in `[0.02, 5.0]`; return `nan` if the market spread is below `cg_cds_spread(T, sigma_S=0.02)` (floor from the $t=0$ mass).
4. `cg_greeks(...) -> dict(delta_shares, gamma, vega_asset, vega_equity)`: central finite differences in `S0` (±1%) and `sigma` (±1 vol pt); delta in *shares per \$1,000 notional* (CreditGrades p.14 convention).
5. `urc_from_puts(chain, T, A, B) -> (U_bid, U_ask)`: Carr–Wu; `U_bid=(P_bid(B)-P_ask(A))/(B-A)`, `U_ask=(P_ask(B)-P_bid(A))/(B-A)`; with `A=0` use `P(B)/B`. Require `american=True` or flag. Interpolate missing strikes linearly in price (never in vol).
6. `urc_from_cds(spread, R, r, T) -> float`: $\lambda=s/(1-R)$, $U=\frac{\lambda}{r+\lambda}(1-e^{-(r+\lambda)T})$; accept an optional bootstrapped hazard curve from Ch 24.
7. `jtd_put_price(S0, K, T, r, q, sigma, lam) -> float`: eq. (23.4) generalized with $q$; `jtd_fit_lambda(chain, T, r, q)`: fit $\lambda$ by least squares on `P_mid(K)/K` for strikes below 30% moneyness, with `sigma` fitted on ATM; report `lambda_hat`, `se`, and the diagnostic `floor_ratio = P(K_min)/K_min / (exp(-r*T)*(1-exp(-lam*T)))`.
8. `spread_greeks(price_fn, s, bump_bp=1) -> dict(cs01, spread_convexity, dts)`; `cds_rpv01(s, R, r, T)`.
9. `capstruct_backtest(panel, alpha=1.0, hold_max_days=180, cost_bp_cds=5, cost_bp_eq=10, rebalance="daily") -> DataFrame`: Yu's rules in §3.6; outputs per-trade P&L decomposition and portfolio Sharpe; `panel` has daily `S, sigma_S, F_ps, cds_5y, r, R` per issuer.
10. `bank_run_dd(cash, afs, htm, haircut_afs, uninsured_share, deposits, kappa) -> dict`: liquidity-default time under a deterministic outflow path with intensity `kappa * uninsured_share * max(0, -ret_equity)`; purely illustrative, labeled so.

**Numerical guards:** clip `d1,d2` to ±40 before `norm.cdf`; use `norm.logcdf` for tails in (23.2); `T` in ACT/365, spreads decimal; all vols annualized; raise on `sigma_S<=0`, `S0<=0`, `F_ps<0`; never silently coerce American/European.

**Sanity checks (must print PASS/FAIL):**
- (a) `cg_survival(t)` is non-increasing in `t` for `t>0`; as `lam→0` and `t→∞` matches the Black–Cox-style limit; Monte Carlo first-passage (200k paths, daily steps, Brownian-bridge hit correction, `seed=23`) agrees with (23.2) within 3 MC standard errors for $(S_0,\sigma_S,F^{\text{ps}})=(30,0.4,30)$.
- (b) `cg_cds_spread` with $R=\bar L$ and small $\lambda$ ≈ $(1-R)\times$ average hazard from the survival curve (within 5%).
- (c) `jtd_put_price(lam=0)` equals Black–Scholes; for `K=0.05*S0`, `P/K` within 1% of $e^{-rT}(1-e^{-\lambda T})$.
- (d) `urc_from_puts` applied to a synthetic chain generated by `jtd_put_price` (with `A=0`, `B=0.2*S0`) equals `urc_from_cds(spread=lam*(1-R), R, r, T)` to 4 decimals (the Carr–Wu/JTD consistency check).
- (e) Delta-hedged CDS P&L under a simulated GBM equity path (no default) has mean ≈ carry and variance dominated by gamma; with one injected default the hedged P&L gap equals $(1-R)-\Delta S_{\tau^-}/\text{notional}$.
- (f) `capstruct_backtest` on a synthetic panel where the market spread is the model spread plus AR(1) noise recovers positive Sharpe that *declines* toward zero as the AR(1) coefficient → 1 (the Kapadia–Pu lesson).
