## 1. Thesis and the "aha" for the ex-PJT-RSSG reader

**Thesis.** Language models moved *reading* to machines and *specifying* to humans, and on a credit desk the scarce skill in 2026 is writing a spec, a test, and a citation contract that a machine cannot misread. A model is a stochastic reader: it must be validated like a PD model (power, calibration, robustness, transparency, diagnosability, Bohn–Stein p.394), governed like a vendor model (SR 11-7 lineage; FINRA Notice 24-09, June 27, 2024; FINRA's December 9, 2025 oversight report), and wired into the desk like an untrusted data feed (provenance on every claim, deterministic replay of every output, a kill switch on every action). The chapter has three load-bearing ideas:

1. **Hallucination is an incentive problem before it is a model problem.** Kalai, Nachum, Vempala and Zhang (arXiv:2509.04664, Sept 4, 2025) show that binary-graded evaluations reward guessing over abstention, so a model that is uncertain is *trained* to make something up. The desk's eval therefore has to pay for "I don't know," and the threshold at which the model should abstain is a cost ratio the desk sets, not a vibe. Derivation in §3.1.
2. **A citation that cannot be checked is a hallucination with a footnote.** The legal profession ran this experiment for us: Stanford's preregistered audit found the leading RAG-based legal research tools still hallucinated 17–33% of the time (Magesh et al., arXiv:2405.20362, May 30, 2024), and Damien Charlotin's database of court decisions involving hallucinated citations stood at **2,199 cases on October 16, 2026** (1,501 in the USA; 871 involving lawyers rather than pro se litigants). The chapter's citation contract (§3.2) makes a claim machine-verifiable: document id, page, character offsets, and a support test, or it does not ship.
3. **Workflows first, agents last.** Anthropic's "Building effective agents" (Dec 19, 2024) separates workflows (LLM steps on predefined code paths with checks between them) from agents (the LLM directs its own tool use). τ-bench (Yao et al., June 2024) found frontier function-calling agents succeed on under 50% of tasks and that pass^8 falls below 25%. The reliability of a chain is a product (§3.3), and the only things that raise it are verification, retries, and fewer steps. On a credit desk that means: agents for surveillance and research triage, workflows for anything that touches a number, and nothing autonomous between the model and a quote.

**The aha.** The RSSG reader already knows how to write a spec that cannot be misread: it is called a credit agreement, and the LMT era is a ten-year clinic in what happens when a spec leaves an edge case open. Serta's non-pro-rata uptier was, structurally, a prompt injection against the pro-rata sharing clause: a literal reading of "open market purchase" that the drafters never intended and never excluded. ROBO is Serta's counsel with a Python interpreter. Moyer's advice to the non-lawyer analyst (p.302) is the specifier's creed verbatim: read every document asking "Is there any way this can be read or interpreted so that I don't get the result I want?" The pure quant knows what a trapdoor looks like in code; the ex-banker knows what it looks like in prose; the AI specifier is the person who does both, and that person is rare enough to be paid for it.

**Second aha (the Ch 7 bridge).** A language model's training cutoff is a look-ahead leak. Lopez-Lira and Tang (arXiv:2304.07619, v6 Oct 28, 2025) used only headlines that postdate the models' knowledge cutoff for exactly this reason, and found that strategy returns decline as LLM adoption grows. Meanwhile the most-cited "GPT-4 beats analysts" paper (Kim, Muhn, Nikolaev, arXiv:2407.17866) **was withdrawn** after a co-author found inconsistencies while replicating earlier work, as was the same team's "Bloated Disclosures" (withdrawn Oct 9, 2025). LLM-finance research in 2025–26 is where bond-factor research was in 2019–23 (Ch 7's replication crisis): the discipline that saved factor research, honest out-of-sample tests and deflated performance statistics, is the discipline that will save agentic research, except now the number of trials is set by a machine that never gets tired.

## 3. Derivation skeletons

All probabilities in this chapter are physical ($\mathbb{P}$): they are error rates, agreement rates, and costs. No LLM output is ever a $\mathbb{Q}$ quantity; $\mathbb{Q}$ objects (spreads, hazards, implied vols) enter only as inputs to or outputs of the deterministic pricers the model feeds. The writer should say this once, early.

### 3.1 Hallucination as scored guessing, and the abstention threshold

Let the model's (internal) probability that a candidate answer is correct be $p$. Under 0/1 grading with no credit for abstaining, the expected score of answering is $p$ and of abstaining is $0$, so the score-maximizing policy answers for every $p>0$: the eval manufactures confident errors (Kalai et al. 2025). Give abstention a score $t\in(0,1)$ and the policy answers iff $p>t$. The desk sets $t$ from costs: a wrong shipped claim costs $C_{\text{FP}}$ (a mis-sized position, a compliance incident), an abstention costs $C_{\text{abs}}$ (a human reads the page). Answer iff $(1-p)\,C_{\text{FP}} < C_{\text{abs}}$, i.e.
$$p > p^\ast = 1 - \frac{C_{\text{abs}}}{C_{\text{FP}}}.$$
With $C_{\text{FP}}/C_{\text{abs}} = 20$, $p^\ast = 0.95$. **Error-prone spots:** (i) $p$ is not observable; the model's *stated* confidence must be calibrated first (§3.5), and verbalized confidence is often not; (ii) the model's cost of abstention is not the desk's, so the threshold must be imposed by the harness, not requested in the prompt; (iii) Kalai et al. also give a reduction from generation error to a binary "is-it-valid" classification error with an explicit constant (CHECK the constant in the full paper before quoting it).

### 3.2 The citation contract and the three rates

A claim is a tuple $c = (\text{text}, d, \pi, [a,b], \hat p)$: the asserted sentence, source document id $d$, page $\pi$, character offsets $[a,b]$ in the canonical text of $d$, and a confidence. Define three indicators: $A(c)=1$ if the assertion is correct; $V(c)=1$ if $(d,\pi,[a,b])$ resolves (the document exists in the store, the page exists, the offsets are within bounds); $S(c)=1$ if the span $d[\pi][a{:}b]$ entails the assertion under a fixed support test. Over a labelled set of $N$ claims report
$$\text{acc}=\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum A,\qquad \text{valid}=\tfrac1N\sum V,\qquad \text{support}=\tfrac1N\sum S,\qquad \text{footnoted\ hallucination}=\tfrac1N\sum V(1-S).$$
The last one is the dangerous quantity: a real page that does not say what the model says it says (Charlotin's "misrepresented": 931 of 2,199). **Error-prone spots:** offsets computed on a different text normalization than the store (PDF extraction, hyphenation, ligatures: "efﬁciently"); page numbering (PDF index vs. printed folio, a problem this very book solves by citing PDF indices); the support test itself being an LLM (then its bias is in the metric, §3.5); treating "no citation" as neutral when the contract should treat it as a failed claim.

### 3.3 Compounding reliability in agent chains, verification, and pass^k

A workflow of $n$ steps with independent per-step success $p_j$ succeeds with probability $\prod_j p_j$: ten steps at $0.95$ give $0.60$; at $0.90$, $0.35$. τ-bench's pass^k estimates $\Pr(\text{all }k\text{ trials succeed})$; for a task with per-trial success $p$ it is $p^k$, and the unbiased estimator from $n$ trials with $c$ successes is $\binom{c}{k}/\binom{n}{k}$. Adding a verifier with detection power $d=\Pr(\text{reject}\mid\text{wrong})$ and false-reject rate $f=\Pr(\text{reject}\mid\text{right})$, and allowing up to $m$ retries of an idempotent step, the probability that an *accepted* output is correct is, by Bayes,
$$\Pr(\text{right}\mid\text{accepted}) = \frac{p(1-f)}{p(1-f) + (1-p)(1-d)},$$
which is the only number the downstream step should be told. With $p=0.9$, $d=0.8$, $f=0.05$: $0.977$. **Error-prone spots:** (i) errors are not independent: a judge drawn from the same model family shares the generator's blind spots (self-enhancement bias), which shows up as $d$ falling exactly on the cases that matter; (ii) retries that re-sample until the verifier passes are a multiple-testing procedure against the verifier, so $d$ must be the power *per attempt* and the accepted-output posterior degrades with $m$; (iii) positively correlated step failures make the product a lower bound on chain success but make the *diagnosis* harder, because one upstream misread propagates as $n$ consistent downstream "successes."

### 3.4 Cost, accuracy, and the Pareto frontier (Kapoor et al. 2024)

Each harness configuration $\theta$ (model, retries, context size, verifier) has accuracy $a(\theta)$ and expected cost $c(\theta)$ in dollars per task. Choose $\theta^\ast = \arg\min\{c(\theta): a(\theta)\ge a^\ast\}$ and report the frontier, not a leaderboard. Cost per task is $c = n_{\text{in}}\,\pi_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}\,\pi_{\text{out}}$ summed over calls, with cache reads at a fraction of $\pi_{\text{in}}$. Dated prices in §6.H; a 300-page agreement is roughly 150k words, roughly 200k tokens on older tokenizers and about 30% more on the newer ones (the pricing page says so), so one full read on a mid-tier model is tens of cents *(illustrative; prices dated)*. **Error-prone spot:** accuracy on a held-out set that the agent's authors also iterated on is in-sample (Kapoor et al.'s "inadequate holdout sets").

### 3.5 Calibration and selective prediction

Bin claims by stated confidence $\hat p$ into $B$ bins with counts $n_b$; $\text{ECE} = \sum_b \frac{n_b}{N}\,\lvert \text{acc}_b - \overline{\hat p}_b\rvert$. For coverage $\phi$ (fraction of claims answered when the threshold is the $(1-\phi)$-quantile of $\hat p$), selective risk is
$$R(\phi) = \frac{\sum_i \mathbf 1\{\hat p_i \ge \theta_\phi\}\,(1-A_i)}{\sum_i \mathbf 1\{\hat p_i \ge \theta_\phi\}},$$
and the area under the risk–coverage curve summarizes abstention quality. This is Bohn–Stein's power/calibration split exactly: a reader can rank claims well (power) and still state wrong probabilities (calibration). **Error-prone spot:** post-hoc calibration (Platt/temperature scaling) fitted on the same questions used to choose the prompt is the in-sample sin of p.466 fn.31.

### 3.6 Determinism and the replay contract

Floating-point addition is not associative, and inference kernels change their reduction order with batch size, so two identical requests at temperature 0 can diverge (He et al. 2025: divergence at token 103 of 1,000; 80 unique completions in 1,000 runs). The replay contract: an LLM output is a **point-in-time datum** keyed by $(\text{prompt hash}, \text{model id}, \text{sampling params}, \text{timestamp})$, stored, and never recomputed to "check" a past decision; the measurable is the two-run agreement rate $\kappa = \Pr(y^{(1)}=y^{(2)})$ on a fixed prompt set, reported per model version. The *deterministic* parts of the pipeline (parsers, capacity calculators, pricers) must replay bit-for-bit from seeds and pinned versions; the stochastic part is logged, not replayed.

### 3.7 Multiple testing when the researcher is a machine

Reuse Ch 7's expected-maximum-Sharpe and DSR formulas with $N$ equal to the agent's total hypothesis count, including rejected ones, which the harness must log; the effective $N$ is the number of clusters in the agent's trial-return correlation matrix. The one new term is the knowledge-cutoff purge: features derived from a model trained through date $T_c$ cannot be used for labels before $T_c$ (the model may "know" the outcome), which is a purge on the *feature generator*, not just the label.

## 4. Code spec (reference implementation)

**File/role:** `llm_desk_harness.py`, one python block, numpy/scipy/pandas only, seeded; no network, no API calls. It simulates a stochastic reader against a synthetic document store so every metric in §3 is computable and testable offline.

1. **Synthetic corpus.** `make_agreement(seed)` builds a synthetic credit agreement as a list of sections with ids like `6.08(b)(xiv)`, canonical text, and a `(doc_id, page, char_start, char_end)` index for each defined term and basket. Baskets are stored structurally: `{"kind": "greater_of", "fixed": 50e6, "pct_ebitda": 1.0, "builder": True}`.
2. **Deterministic calculators.** `basket_capacity(basket, ebitda, used)` implementing "greater of" with explicit handling of `ebitda <= 0` (returns the fixed amount, never a negative), `used` clipped at capacity, units asserted in dollars (float64), and `capacity_series` over a fiscal-quarter table. These are the things the LLM must *not* compute.
3. **Stochastic reader model.** `StochasticReader(p_hit, calib_slope, fab_rate, misrep_rate, seed)` answers a question by returning a claim tuple; with probability `fab_rate` it fabricates a section id that does not resolve ($V=0$); with probability `misrep_rate` it cites a real span that does not support the claim ($V=1,S=0$); its stated confidence is a miscalibrated transform of its true hit probability. `verify_claim(claim, store)` computes $(A,V,S)$ deterministically from the store (support test = the cited span contains the structural fields the claim asserts).
4. **Metrics.** `three_rates`, `footnoted_hallucination_rate`, `ece(bins=10)`, `risk_coverage_curve` and `aurc`, `abstain_threshold(C_fp, C_abs)`, `pass_at_k_unbiased(n, c, k)`.
5. **Chain simulator.** `simulate_chain(n_steps, p, d, f, m, n_paths)` Monte Carlo of §3.3 with an imperfect verifier and retries; returns empirical $\Pr(\text{right}\mid\text{accepted})$ and chain success.
6. **Replay check.** `run_twice(seed)` hashes the full output table of the deterministic calculators across two runs; `agreement_rate` for the stochastic reader across two seeds.
7. **Property tests (hand-rolled, no `hypothesis` dependency).** For 10,000 random `(fixed, pct, ebitda, used)` draws assert: capacity $\ge 0$; capacity $\ge$ fixed when `ebitda<=0`; capacity non-decreasing in `ebitda`; remaining $= \max(\text{capacity}-\text{used},0)$; and the "ROBO trap": `basket_capacity` with `ebitda=-1e9` returns exactly `fixed`.

**Built-in sanity checks (asserted at the bottom):**
- *Abstention economics:* with `C_fp/C_abs = 20`, `abstain_threshold` returns 0.95 to 1e-12; selective risk at the implied coverage is below the unconditional error rate.
- *Verifier Bayes check:* for `p=0.9, d=0.8, f=0.05, m=1`, the Monte Carlo posterior is within ±0.01 of $0.9\cdot0.95/(0.9\cdot0.95+0.1\cdot0.2)=0.9771$ with 200,000 paths.
- *pass^k check:* with per-trial `p=0.7`, `n=20` trials per task over 2,000 tasks, the mean unbiased pass^5 estimate is within ±0.02 of $0.7^5=0.168$, and the naive "fraction of tasks with ≥1 success" is far higher (document the gap).
- *Calibration check:* a perfectly calibrated reader (`calib_slope=1`) gives ECE < 0.02 on 20,000 claims; an overconfident one (`calib_slope=0.5`) gives ECE > 0.10.
- *Footnoted-hallucination check:* `misrep_rate=0.2` recovers a footnoted rate within ±0.02 of 0.20 and `fab_rate=0.1` recovers validity within ±0.02 of 0.90.
- *Replay check:* identical seeds give identical SHA-256 of the deterministic outputs; different seeds give different reader outputs (agreement rate < 1) while the calculators' hash is unchanged (they do not depend on the reader's seed).
- *Lost-in-the-middle toy:* optional; a reader whose hit probability decays with the distance of the cited span from the ends of the document reproduces a U-shaped accuracy-by-position curve (plot-free; assert that middle-tercile accuracy is the lowest).

Edge cases to warn ROBO about: offsets after text normalization (store the normalized text and compute offsets on it only); `used > capacity` (clip, log); fiscal quarters with restated EBITDA (capacity is as-of the quarter's *then-reported* figure, point-in-time); page numbering (PDF index, 1-based); a claim with `confidence` exactly at threshold (define `>` vs `>=` once); `k > n` in pass^k (return NaN, not 0).

## 7. Case studies

**A. *Mata v. Avianca* and the hallucination-cases curve (2023–2026).** Key facts: a personal-injury brief in S.D.N.Y. cited non-existent cases generated by ChatGPT; the court sanctioned counsel in June 2023 (\$5,000; CHECK). The structural lesson is in the database that grew from it: 2,199 decisions by Oct 16, 2026, 1,501 in the US; 871 involve lawyers; "fabricated" 1,803 and "misrepresented" 931 overlap, which means a large share of professional failures are *real sources misdescribed*, the footnoted hallucination of §3.2. A systematic desk's version of this case is a covenant summary citing a real section that does not say what the summary claims; the control is the citation contract plus a support test, and the metric to tape to the monitor is $\sum V(1-S)/N$. Sources: Charlotin database; Dahl et al. 2024; Magesh et al. 2024; *Park v. Kim* (2d Cir. Jan 30, 2024).

**B. Knight Capital, August 1, 2012: deployment without review.** Key facts (SEC Rel. 34-70694, Oct 16, 2013): new RLP code was staged onto SMARS servers from July 27; one technician failed to copy it to the eighth server; no second technician reviewed the deployment; no written procedures required one; a repurposed flag activated dormant "Power Peg" code on that server; in ~45 minutes SMARS sent millions of child orders, >4 million executions, >397 million shares, a ~\$3.5bn unintended net long, a \$460mm loss; there was no control comparing orders leaving SMARS with those entering and no procedure to halt the system on its own error messages ("BNET rejects" referencing Power Peg). For Ch 26 it is the deterministic half of the operating manual: replayable deployments, a second pair of eyes (human or agent) on every diff, invariants checked at the boundary (orders out = orders in), and a kill switch owned by someone awake. Cross-reference Ch 27.

**C. The LLM-finance replication wobble (2023–2025).** Key facts: Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev's "Financial Statement Analysis with LLMs" (first posted July 25, 2024; v3 Feb 20, 2025) and "Bloated Disclosures" (June 17, 2023) were both withdrawn by October 9, 2025 after a co-author could not replicate earlier results; Lopez-Lira and Tang (v6, Oct 28, 2025) document that LLM-headline strategy returns decline as adoption grows and that only post-cutoff headlines constitute a clean test. The desk lesson is Ch 7's: cutoff-aware purging, out-of-time evals, deflated statistics with the agent's trial count, and Dynkin p.747's cross-asset out-of-sample design. The joke writes itself: the first thing the reading machine should read is the retraction notice.

**D (short). SEC "AI-washing," March 18, 2024.** Delphia (claimed 2019–2023 to use client data in AI/ML models it did not have) and Global Predictions ("first regulated AI financial advisor") paid \$225k and \$175k. For a desk marketing a "systematic, AI-driven" credit strategy, the eval log is also the compliance file.
