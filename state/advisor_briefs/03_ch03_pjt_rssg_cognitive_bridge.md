## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** The restructuring banker and the market maker are solving the same inference problem with different clocks. RSSG asks *"Will this company survive, and who gets the keys?"* and answers it over 90 hours with a 13-week cash flow model and a credit agreement. The desk asks *"Given that someone just asked me for a bid, what is the conditional distribution of this bond's value?"* and answers it in three seconds. Both answers are posteriors. The chapter's job is to show that every RSSG skill is a **likelihood ratio** the desk can use, and that the desk's two instruments for measuring whether it was fooled, the Glosten–Milgrom posterior and the markout, are just the banker's "who's on the other side of this exchange offer?" instinct with timestamps.

**The "aha" for an ex-RSSG reader.** Three of them, in escalating order:

1. *A survival question is a degenerate distribution.* Moyer already does this: two pari passu bonds, the near one at 85 and the far one at 60, imply a 62.5% refinancing probability because "the probability-weighted value of each outcome should be equal: 15 × 0.625 = 25 × 0.375" (Moyer p.407). The reader has been pricing distributions all along; they just called it "upside/downside." The desk's version adds a time index and a counterparty.
2. *A quote is a question you pay to ask.* Ben Dor's LCS chapter says the quiet part: an attractive indication "can be used to generate inquiries (which provide the trader with information)" (Ben_Dor p.121). The RFQ is an information exchange before it is a trade. The adverse-selection charge is the price of the information you are about to lose.
3. *The LMT is the purest adverse-selection event in credit.* In Serta (June 22, 2020), 52.6% of the first-lien class knew they were about to prime the other 47.4% (Mayer Brown, July 2026, VERIFIED). Anyone selling first-lien paper to the future participating lenders in May 2020 was the uninformed trader in Glosten–Milgrom. An ex-RSSG reader knows exactly what the participating lenders knew and when; the chapter teaches them to turn that into $\pi(x)$, the posterior that the counterparty is informed, and to charge for it.

**Framing sentence for the writer:** "You used to be the informed trader. Now you are the house. The house wins by knowing exactly how much it does not know."

## 3. Derivation skeletons

### 3A. Glosten–Milgrom for an RFQ (measure: $\mathbb{P}$, the desk's physical beliefs)

**Setup.** A client sends a sell RFQ for size $Q$ in bond $i$. Let the dealer's prior on the bond's fair value one holding period ahead be two-point: $V \in \{m+\sigma_V,\, m-\sigma_V\}$ with equal probability, so the prior mean is the mid $m$ and the prior standard deviation is $\sigma_V$. With probability $\pi$ the sender is informed (knows $V$); with probability $1-\pi$ uninformed, selling for liquidity reasons with probability $\tfrac12$ regardless of $V$. An informed sender sells only when $V = m-\sigma_V$.

**Step 1 (likelihoods).** $\mathbb{P}(\text{sell} \mid V = m+\sigma_V) = \tfrac{1-\pi}{2}$ and $\mathbb{P}(\text{sell} \mid V = m-\sigma_V) = \tfrac{1-\pi}{2} + \pi$.

**Step 2 (posterior).** $\mathbb{P}(V = m+\sigma_V \mid \text{sell}) = \dfrac{\tfrac{1-\pi}{2}}{\tfrac{1-\pi}{2} + \tfrac{1-\pi}{2} + \pi} = \dfrac{1-\pi}{2}$.

**Step 3 (zero-profit bid).** $b = \mathbb{E}[V \mid \text{sell}] = (m+\sigma_V)\tfrac{1-\pi}{2} + (m-\sigma_V)\tfrac{1+\pi}{2}$, hence

$$b^{\,\text{GM}} = m - \pi\,\sigma_V, \qquad a^{\,\text{GM}} = m + \pi\,\sigma_V. \tag{3.1}$$

The half-spread equals the informed probability times the prior uncertainty. This is the $\Lambda_{\text{tox}}$ term in Chapter 1's bid equation, now with a formula.

**General (non-symmetric) version** for the writer who wants it: with prior $q = \mathbb{P}(V^+)$ and gap $\Delta = V^+ - V^-$,
$$m - b = \Delta\,\frac{q(1-q)\,\pi}{\tfrac{1-\pi}{2} + \pi(1-q)} \;\approx\; 2\pi\,q(1-q)\,\Delta \quad (\pi \text{ small}).$$

**Step 4 (RFQ features).** Replace the constant $\pi$ with a posterior on the sender given the RFQ's observable vector $x$:
$$\pi(x) = \sigma\!\left(\beta_0 + \beta^\top x\right), \qquad \text{equivalently}\quad \frac{\pi(x)}{1-\pi(x)} = \frac{\pi_0}{1-\pi_0}\prod_k \text{LR}_k(x_k)$$
under conditional independence (naive Bayes), where $x$ includes: $\log Q$ relative to ADV; hours to the next scheduled event (earnings, lender call) and days since the last 8-K; counterparty type (dealer, systematic fund, distressed fund, CLO, ETF AP, retail aggregator); number of dealers in competition $n$; the sender's trailing shrunken markout score (3B); whether the RFQ direction is *against* the dealer's published axe; the issuer's Priming Risk Score $\text{PRS}$ (Ch 13). Each $\text{LR}_k$ is estimated from the desk's own labeled history, where the label is the realized 20-day IDO markout sign.

**Step 5 (many dealers: the winner's curse).** With $n$ dealers quoting on a common value, you trade only when your bid is the highest. Conditional on winning, the other $n-1$ signals were lower than they looked unconditionally, so the equilibrium bid shades further:
$$b_n^{*} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[V \mid s_i,\; \max_{j\neq i} s_j \le s_i\right] < \mathbb{E}[V \mid s_i],$$
with the shading increasing in $n$ (Milgrom–Weber 1982 logic). FGP (2016) find exactly this in BNP Paribas RFQ data: dealer quote distributions become more conservative as the number of requested dealers rises. Also note the empirical twist that part of the effect is *non-answer*: the probability each dealer answers falls with $n$, and clients typically wait for only about two answers. Writer's compact form:
$$b^{*}(x,n) = m - \pi(x)\,\sigma_V - w(n)\,\sigma_V - \tfrac12\gamma\sigma_{\text{IDO}}^2\,(2q + Q),$$
where $w(n)$ is the winner's-curse shading (a monotone increasing function of $n$ calibrated from cover prices) and the last term is the Avellaneda–Stoikov inventory skew previewed from Chapter 18. State loudly that $\sigma_V$ here is the dealer's *physical* uncertainty over the holding horizon, not an implied vol.

**Error-prone spots.** (i) Using $\sigma_V$ in yield or spread units but $m$ in price; convert with $D^{\text{spr}}$. (ii) Conditioning on the trade happening when calibrating $\pi$ from markouts (survivorship: only fills have markouts; use FGP's Done/Covered/Traded-Away outcomes to see the counterfactual). (iii) Treating $n$ as exogenous: FGP show clients choose $n$, and HM Table VI shows IG clients query *fewer* dealers for larger tickets, consistent with leakage rising in size, so $n$ is itself a signal. (iv) Forgetting that the all-to-all responder on the other side may be another client (Open Trading was 37% of MarketAxess eligible credit volume in 2025), so "counterparty type" is a distribution, not a label.

### 3B. Markouts and counterparty toxicity (measure: $\mathbb{P}$)

**Definition.** For a dealer fill at price $p_0$ at time $t_0$ with side $\eta = +1$ if the dealer bought, $-1$ if it sold, and a reference mid $m_t$:
$$\text{MO}_h = \eta\,\frac{m_{t_0+h} - p_0}{p_0}\times 10^4 \ \text{bp}, \qquad h \in \{1, 5, 20\}\ \text{business days}.$$
Positive is good for the dealer.

**Decomposition.**
$$\text{MO}_h = \underbrace{\eta\,(m_{t_0} - p_0)}_{\text{captured half-spread}} + \underbrace{\eta\,(m_{t_0+h} - m_{t_0})}_{\text{post-trade drift (adverse selection + noise)}},$$
and under the GM model with the zero-profit bid, $\mathbb{E}[\text{MO}_h \mid \text{fill}] \to 0$ as $h$ grows, because the half-spread exactly pays for the expected drift. A persistently negative long-horizon markout against a counterparty means your $\pi(x)$ for them is too low.

**IDO markout.** Replace $m$ with a beta-stripped reference: $m^{\text{IDO}}_t = m_t - D^{\text{spr}}_i\,\beta_i\,\Delta s^{\text{index}}_{[t_0,t]} - D^{\text{mod}}_i\,\Delta y^{\text{TSY}}_{[t_0,t]}$ so that a CDX rally is not booked as "the client was dumb."

**Counterparty score with shrinkage.** For counterparty $c$ with $n_c$ fills and sample mean $\overline{\text{MO}}_c$ at horizon $h$, assuming per-fill noise variance $\sigma^2_{\text{MO}}$ and a prior on true counterparty effects $\theta_c \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2)$:
$$\hat\theta_c = \frac{n_c}{n_c + \sigma^2_{\text{MO}}/\tau^2}\;\overline{\text{MO}}_c,$$
with $\tau^2$ estimated by method of moments across counterparties (variance of means minus average sampling variance, floored at zero). This is the feature "trailing markout score" fed into $\pi(x)$.

**Error-prone spots.** Stale composite mids (the markout of a bond that does not trade for ten days is a markout against a model); mixing price and spread markouts; size-weighting (a \$50k odd lot and a \$15mm block are not the same observation); ignoring the dealer's own hedging footprint at short horizons.

### 3C. Bayesian PD update from an 8-K (measure: $\mathbb{P}$ for the PD; $\mathbb{Q}$ only when translating to spread)

Let the prior probability of default within horizon $T$ be $p = 1 - e^{-\lambda T}$ with $\lambda$ the physical hazard (from EDF, Ch 6). An 8-K event $E$ (e.g., Item 2.04 filed, or Item 1.01 attaching a forbearance agreement) has likelihood ratio $\text{LR}_E = \mathbb{P}(E \mid \tau \le T)/\mathbb{P}(E \mid \tau > T)$, estimated from a labeled corpus of EDGAR filings. Then
$$p' = \frac{\text{LR}_E\,p}{1 - p + \text{LR}_E\,p}, \qquad \lambda' = -\frac{1}{T}\ln(1-p'),$$
and the implied spread move under the credit triangle is $\Delta s \approx L\,(\lambda'^{\mathbb{Q}} - \lambda^{\mathbb{Q}})$, which requires a $\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}$ risk-premium map (say so explicitly; do not pretend the 8-K moves $\mathbb{Q}$-hazard one-for-one). **Base-rate correction** when the LR corpus has sample default rate $S$ but the live book's is $T$ (Bohn_Stein p.252, Elkan): $p^* = \dfrac{T\,(p - pS)}{S - pS + pT - ST}$, which, unlike the naive $p^* = (T/S)\,p$, never exceeds 1. **Error-prone spots:** double-counting (the 8-K is public, so by the time your parser fires the spread may already embed it; the edge is parse latency and LR calibration, not the fact of the filing); item mislabeling (companies bury 2.04 content under 8.01); treating "furnished" 7.01 as "filed."

### 3D. Pre-LMT flow detector (measure: $\mathbb{P}$)

Let $y_t$ be the day-$t$ net buying of tranche $i$ by counterparties in the "LME-prone" set (distressed funds, sponsor-affiliated accounts, known cooperation-agreement signatories), scaled by trailing ADV. Under $H_0$ (no deal) $y_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_y^2)$; under $H_1$ (accumulation toward a threshold) $y_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_y^2)$. A CUSUM statistic
$$S_t = \max\!\left(0,\; S_{t-1} + \frac{\mu_1}{\sigma_y^2}\Big(y_t - \frac{\mu_1}{2}\Big)\right),\qquad \text{alarm when } S_t > h(\text{PRS}),$$
with the alarm threshold decreasing in the Priming Risk Score (a prior-weighted detector). Output: a probability of an LMT within 60 days that enters $\pi(x)$ in 3A for *any* RFQ in that tranche and, separately, a flag to stop building long inventory in the paper most likely to be left behind. **Error-prone spots:** labeling (announcement dates must come from 8-Ks/press releases, not from when the desk noticed); the counterparty set drifts; survivorship of the training set toward deals that happened.

## 4. Code spec (reference implementation, Python, runs as written)

**Module:** `ch03_rfq_adverse_selection.py`, numpy/scipy/pandas only, `rng = np.random.default_rng(3)`.

1. `simulate_rfq_flow(n_rfq=200_000, pi_true=0.12, sigma_v=1.5, n_dealers_dist=...)` → DataFrame with columns `[side, size_bucket, hours_to_event, cpty_type, n_dealers, informed (latent), v_true, mid, filled, cover]`. 2. `fit_pi(df)` → logistic regression (scipy `minimize` on the log-likelihood) of the realized 20-day markout sign on RFQ features; returns $\hat\beta$ and standard errors.

4. `markouts(df, horizons=(1,5,20))` and `shrunk_cpty_scores(df)` → per-counterparty $\hat\theta_c$.
5. `update_pd_from_8k(p_prior, lr, S=None, T=None)` → posterior with optional Elkan base-rate correction.
6. `cusum_lmt_detector(y, mu1, sigma_y, h)` → alarm times.

**Built-in sanity checks (assert, not print):**
- With $\pi$ known and the GM bid used, the sample mean 20-day markout on fills is within $3\,\text{SE}$ of zero (zero-profit property), and switching to $\pi = 0$ produces a significantly negative markout equal to $-\pi_{\text{true}}\sigma_V$ within MC error.
- `fit_pi` recovers the true coefficient on the `informed`-correlated features within 2 SE on the simulated data.
- `update_pd_from_8k(p, 1.0) == p`; posterior is monotone in `lr`; Elkan-corrected output is in $[0,1]$ for all inputs, while the naive $T/S$ scaling violates the bound for $p > S/T$.
- CUSUM false-alarm rate under $H_0$ matches the design ARL within tolerance over 500 seeded paths.

**ROBO literalism line for the box:** told to "minimize markouts," ROBO quotes 40 points through the market and never fills again: markout zero, hit rate zero, franchise zero. The precise spec is maximize expected PnL *subject to* a hit-ratio target, which is exactly the dualized constraint in Barzykin–Ciceri (HSBC, 2026): quote = riskless spread + inventory correction + hit-ratio correction.

### 6.1 Microstructure theory and RFQ empirics

- **Glosten & Milgrom (1985)**, "Bid, ask and transaction prices in a specialist market with heterogeneously informed traders," *JFE* 14(1): 71–100. ### 6.2 Dealer quoting models with hit ratios (the modern RFQ engine literature)

- **Guéant (2017)**, "Optimal market making," *Applied Mathematical Finance* 24(2): 112–154, DOI 10.1080/1350486X.2017.1342552 (VERIFIED).
- **Bergault & Guéant**, "Size matters for OTC market makers: general results and dimensionality reduction techniques," arXiv 1907.01225 (v5, Sept 21, 2022) (VERIFIED): size-dependent quotes and factor-based dimension reduction across a bond universe.
- **Barzykin, Bergault & Guéant**, "Algorithmic market making in dealer markets with hedging and market impact," arXiv 2106.06974 (v6, Dec 11, 2022) (VERIFIED): internalize inside an inventory band, hedge outside it; the band widens with franchise size.
- **Barzykin & Ciceri (HSBC, 2026)**, "Bond Market Making with a Hit-Ratio Target," arXiv 2604.20406 (v1 Apr 22, 2026; v2 Jul 27, 2026) (VERIFIED; PDF text extracted). Size ladder, quadratic inventory penalty, running size-weighted hit-ratio target penalized as $\tfrac{\kappa_\tau W_\tau}{2}(r_\tau(\delta) - r_\tau^\star)^2$; dualizing the target keeps the reduced HJB separable; linearized quotes decompose into riskless spread + inventory-risk correction + hit-ratio correction. Their introduction states the thesis the chapter needs: the dealer's hit ratio "is not an exogenous statistic, but an equilibrium object shaped by platform design, client search, and rival participation."
- **Marín Martínez, Ardanza-Trevijano & Sabio (2026)**, "Causal interventions in bond multi-dealer-to-client platforms," *PLOS ONE* 21(1): e0341369, published Jan 27, 2026 (VERIFIED). BBVA European government bond RFQs: 102,437 RFQs, 5,738 hits (≈5.6%); a generative FGP-style model (AUC 0.742) matches LightGBM (0.743) while enforcing spread monotonicity; client features had no predictive power within a homogeneous top-tier client segment (a warning about the limits of "counterparty type" when your clients are all alike).
- **HSBC & IBM (Sept 22, 2025)**, "Enhanced fill probability estimates in institutional algorithmic bond trading using statistical learning algorithms with quantum computers," arXiv 2509.17715 (VERIFIED). Dataset: 1,073,926 RFQs, 5,166 bonds, 747 tickers, Sept 1, 2023–Oct 29, 2024, European corporates in a blind auction; ~37% of responses filled; 216 classical features; classical out-of-sample AUC ≈0.63. Use as a dated data point on the *scale* of one dealer's RFQ flow (over a million RFQs in 14 months), and treat the quantum claims with the authors' own caveat (no generalization guarantees; noiseless simulation did not reproduce the gain).

## 7. Case studies

### 7.1 Serta Simmons 2020 uptier, as a market maker lived it (primary)

Sourced facts (see 6.5): \$1.95bn first-lien term loan from the Nov 2016 refinancing; June 22, 2020 transaction; participating lenders ≈\$992mm (52.6%) of ≈\$1.887bn first lien, excluded ≈\$895mm (47.4%); \$200mm new money first-out; \$1.2bn of first- and second-lien loans exchanged into ≈\$875mm second-out super-priority debt; the credit agreement permitted non-pro-rata buybacks only via a Dutch auction open to all lenders or "open market purchases." Bankruptcy court (June 2023) found the term unambiguous and the deal permitted; Fifth Circuit reversed Dec 31, 2024 (revised Feb 14, 2025); remand decision July 7, 2026 awarded \$261.13mm plus 9% prejudgment interest from June 22, 2020. Post-deal liquidity in the left-behind paper: up to \$186mm face traded through Dec 2021 with Barclays as lead market maker and quotes above 74 in parts of 2021 (expert testimony). Desk angle: in May–June 2020 the informed set was literally 52.6% of the class; any dealer bidding first-lien paper from an excluded-type account was pricing against a known-size informed population; the chapter can compute the GM bid with $\pi$ set to the fraction of flow plausibly originating from future participants and show the realized markout. Note for the writer: the pre-deal price path and trading volumes must be marked *approximate* unless taken from the remand opinion's exhibits.

### 7.2 Sound Point (Aug 2024) and Marathon (Sept 2024): the information wall as a feature (secondary)

Both SEC orders (6.5) establish that lender-group and creditors'-committee membership is treated as an MNPI channel. Desk reading: counterparties who are *on* the committee should be modeled as restricted (their silence is informative: the absence of their usual axes is a feature), while counterparties who are *not* on the committee but trade the same tranche heavily just before an announcement are the live adverse-selection risk. Penalties: \$1.8mm and \$1.5mm.

### 7.3 Mitel vs. Serta, same day, opposite answers (vignette)

Dec 31, 2024: Fifth Circuit (Serta) says no; N.Y. Appellate Division (Mitel) says yes, on different language. Use as the "Kaz vs. legal vocabulary" beat and as the reason the LR table in the Edge Ledger is document-conditional.
