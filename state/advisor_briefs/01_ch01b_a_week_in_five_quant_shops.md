## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** Quantitative credit is not mostly models. It is mostly *custody of a number*: who marked it, which feed it came from, whether yesterday's version of it still exists, whether a human is allowed to know it today, and what happens at 4:40 p.m. when it does not tie. Five very different shops run five very different businesses on the same bond, and in all five the senior people spend their week on the same unglamorous questions. The models in Parts II–VIII are built on top of the answers.

**The aha (for an ex-RSSG banker).** The reader believes they are moving from a world of documents to a world of mathematics. The chapter shows them that a quant shop is also a world of documents, just different ones: the P&L email, the exception queue, the change log, the price-testing tolerance, the restricted list, the alert history, the pass log. The banker already has the one skill these documents reward, which is reading the thing that governs the number rather than the number. The last line of the chapter should land this: the week looked like five different jobs and was one job, the custody of a number, and the reader has been doing it since their first 13-week cash flow.

**Why ethnography, not curriculum.** The synthesis (l.105) is explicit: 1-B *shows* the risk pack, the explain and the limit e-mail from the corridor and does not teach them. 8-B teaches the limit sheet, 8-C the explain, 3-B Theo's own year. 1-B's job is to make the reader want those chapters, and to make the numbers in them feel like things that live in rooms.

**Design decisions.**
1. **Five narrators, none of them Theo.** One fictional composite per shop, labelled as such in a one-line italic preface to each day (synthesis l.105). Suggested names, all invented, all explicitly fictional: *Monday*: Priya Raman, production-rota engineer, non-bank market maker. *Tuesday*: Dov Adler, credit PM, multi-strategy pod. *Wednesday*: Ngozi Achebe-Lund, data and research lead, systematic asset manager. *Thursday*: Hal Brennan, junior marker on a bank credit-trading desk, and his VCG counterpart Mei Tanaka. *Friday*: Sol Ferreira, analyst at a distressed fund. The canon cast visits (see §5) but does not narrate.
2. **One bond threads the week.** The Halvorsen Building Products 7.25% senior unsecured 2029, B3/B−, trading at 78 (Ch 1's Monday RFQ; Halvorsen is fictional). It crosses each narrator's screen in a mundane way, and each day's **View From Another Seat** box is what *this* seat does with it. Keep those boxes short: 4-C does the seven-seat anatomy properly; 1-B only shows the reflex.
3. **Every real fact is a dated, cited public record; every voice is fictional.** Real firms and people appear only through LIFE and PERSP citations. Nothing a real person did not say in a cited source goes in quotation marks. Machine-transcript quotes carry a **CHECK** in the draft until re-listened.
4. **No bullet lists in the chapter body.** Boxes and the Resource Graph may list. The prose may not. The clocks (TRACE 15 minutes, quarterly best-execution review, annual 15c3-5 certification) appear as things that interrupt lunch.
5. **Distinct from 3-B §3B.9.** 3-B already has a reconciliation vignette (a custodian cash receipt with no trade) and a model-review vignette (a DTS cell moved on a rating migration). 1-B's reconciliation is a *derivatives margin* break on a CDX roll; its model review is a *rank that changed because yesterday's data changed*. Do not reuse 3-B's two.

## 2. Section outline, built as scenes

Target 9,000–12,000 words of chapter: a cold open (~600), five days (~1,800–2,200 each), a closing section (~600), Desk Debrief, Resource Graph. Each day is one continuous scene with a clock running down the left margin of the prose (times in the text, not as a table).

### Cold Open: Five Alarms (approx. 600 words)
Five alarm clocks go off in five cities between 5:40 and 6:45 a.m. The narrator of each day is introduced by what they check before coffee: a pager app, a P&L against a line, a data-load log, a Bloomberg chat from the night desk, a restricted list. Close the cold open on the bond: Halvorsen 7.25s, 78 bid on the composite, a lender call last Friday, and in five shops nobody is thinking about Halvorsen because something more boring is broken. *Humor beat:* the only shop where the first thing checked is the market is the distressed fund, and that is because its analyst is checking whether he is still allowed to look at it.

### Monday. The Market Maker: "Save what you knew when you knew it" (approx. 2,000 words)
*Fictional composite, non-bank electronic market maker; the rhythm is sourced, the firm is not.*
**Setting and task.** 6:30 wake, 7:30 desk boot, fifteen minutes on the news, the morning standup on how the open should behave, scripts that check limits and prices before anything quotes (Optiver recruiting accounts, LIFE §4.2 [S9]; this is an equity-options firm's day, so the credit version is a labelled transposition). Priya is on the intraday support rota: not overnight on-call, "present and responding to issues that are popping up live" (Jane Street *Signals and Threads* Ep 20, 7 Oct 2024, LIFE §4.1 [S8]; use the quote, it is from a published transcript). A quarter to a third of her time is this (same source).
**What goes wrong, twice.** 7:40: the evaluated-price vendor's overnight file restates Friday's history for 1,900 CUSIPs. The desk's quote engine seeds from that file. Priya's team saved every vendor file with the time it arrived, so Friday's Friday still exists next to Friday's Monday; the vendor's "whoops, made a mistake" (Ep 29, 2 Sep 2026 [S8]) costs an hour instead of a back-test. 11:10: a deployment. A new fat-finger band on the quote engine, shipped that morning, rejects a legitimate 9-cent quote on a defaulted name; the band assumed bond prices live between 40 and 120. The credit version of 20 April 2020's negative crude (Ep 29 [S8]; LIFE seed V6): assumptions "baked into your trading and risk systems" break order entry. Roll back, re-deploy with the band keyed to price bucket. 2:00: the alert that flickers. Pages "open and close and you did nothing to resolve them ... taking up mental space" (Ep 20 [S8]); Priya runs the alert-history report and moves one threshold.
**The quiet hour and the lesson.** 4:30: the desk's end-of-day reflection, modelled on Optiver's "what we did, why we did it ... what we didn't do" [S9]. The trades not done are reviewed as data. Halvorsen crossed the screen at 10:52 as an all-to-all RFQ for \$2mm; the engine passed because the hedge (HYG, CDX HY) was expensive relative to the clip. At 4:30 the passed trade has a price path too.
**What the reader learns.** Two ideas the book develops: a point-in-time archive is what makes a back-test honest (§27.2, §7, 9-B), and the passed trade has a markout (§3.3, §3.3.5 survivorship). **Math, Gently #1 (markout):** the price path after a decision, including a decision not to trade; one worked number (passed at 78, composite 77.25 an hour later: passing "earned" 75 cents the desk never booked). The full version is §3.3.
**View From Another Seat (market maker on Halvorsen):** a leg in a basket with HYG, a CDX tranche and the equity; quotes tight and small when the ETF and portfolio-trading channels give a cheap hedge, pulls back when the model sees a jump rather than a diffusion (PERSP §2.2, Jane Street firm pages: "consistent fixed income liquidity provider ... in all market environments", fetched 18 Oct 2026; Jane Street states it prices "more than 25,000 bonds"). Also sourced: Flow Traders "strategically reduced the capital deployed to fixed income in 2025" (AR 2025 p.18, LIFE §4.3 [S11]); a market maker's credit balance sheet is a quarterly committee decision, not a given.
**Do not use:** Virtu's "one losing day in 1,238" (not in the text read, LIFE §9c).

### Tuesday. The Pod: "IDO or it didn't happen" (approx. 2,000 words)
*Fictional composite, credit PM on a multi-manager platform.*
**Setting and task.** 7:15, P&L against the line. Dov runs a long-short single-name HY book that is supposed to be market- and factor-neutral; the platform's bought factor model is the daily "x-ray of your body" (Paleologo, *Odd Lots*, 20 May 2024, LIFE §5.1 [S12], machine transcript, **CHECK**). The x-ray shows spread beta leaking from the long side after Friday's rally. Summed across hundreds of PMs that leak is a firm exposure, which is why the risk desk, not Dov, will notice first.
**What goes wrong.** The reconciliation that does not tie: it is the CDX HY roll week (20 Mar / 20 Sep family; 10-B has the roll mechanics). The FCM's variation-margin call on the old series does not match the book's mark on the new series by about \$140k; the hedge was rolled in the OMS on Friday and in the FCM's books on Monday. Operations finds it by 11:00. In the interim the morning explain shows an unexplained line, and an unexplained line in a pod is a phone call. Then the risk call itself, about scope: Dov has drifted from single-name HY into index hedges and a CCC position that behaves like equity; the risk manager's three jobs are the stop-loss, true diversification across pods, and keeping people "in scope" (Paleologo [S12], **CHECK**).
**The sizing scene.** Dov is two-thirds of the way to a capital-halving threshold. The thresholds that are public are press reports about one firm only (eFinancialCareers, 5 Dec 2025, citing the WSJ; Hedgeweek, 10 Mar 2025; LIFE §5.3 [S14]): state them as what has been reported about Millennium, with "Millennium declined comment", and do not generalise. Dov sizes down at 2:40 p.m. before the call from risk at 3:00, which is the point: the stop is a line he manages toward, not a line that happens to him.
**What the reader learns.** The PM "is a little bit like somebody who's holding a call option," and a stop-loss "is effectively a sort of a primitive tail insurance" (Paleologo [S12], **CHECK**). **Math, Gently #2 (the PM as option holder):** a payoff picture, a two-outcome worked number showing why a PM paid a percentage of P&L with a floor at zero wants variance and why the platform's stop is the platform buying back some of that variance. Full version §28.4 and §6-B. Leverage is on the residual, not the beta (Falk-Wallace, *Odd Lots*, 25 Jul 2024, LIFE §5.2 [S13]), which is the pod-shop statement of "IDO or it didn't happen" (§8.8).
**View From Another Seat (pod on Halvorsen):** a residual after the CDX HY beta is stripped; the only question is whether 78 is cheap *to the hedge*, and whether a lender-call name is in scope at all this week. Generic seat; no firm named.
**Quiet afternoon:** 3:30 to 5:00 nothing happens, and the chapter should let it. Dov reads a sell-side note and a 10-Q and does not trade.

### Wednesday. The Systematic Asset Manager: "The dataset is exhaust" (approx. 2,200 words)
*Fictional composite, systematic long-only credit team inside a larger asset manager.*
**Setting and task.** A week of TRACE cleaning compressed into one working day, told as a backlog: Monday's prints arrive as Wednesday's corrections. Ngozi's morning is the exception queue on her own side of the tape: cancels, corrections, as/of prints, the dissemination cap (FINRA Rule 6730: reported within 15 minutes, late reports marked as/of; LIFE §2.3 [S3]; the filter list is 9-B's). Then earnings season: "a supplier company reporting before a customer company ... multiply that by hundreds of issuers" (Man Numeric transcript, recorded Mar 2024, LIFE §6.1 [S16], **CHECK**), so the signal refresh runs twice.
**The model review.** 11:00, the fortnightly review. The PM's one question is the oldest one: why is this name ranked 41st today and 206th yesterday when nothing happened to it? The answer takes Ngozi until 1:30: a Monday print on the Halvorsen 7.25s at 71 was cancelled Tuesday evening and re-reported as/of at 77; yesterday's "return" was a ratio with a wrong numerator. Nothing in the model changed. The data changed, backward. **Math, Gently #3 (a return is a ratio of two prices, and one of them can be retracted):** the worked arithmetic of 71 versus 77 on a 78 bond, and why a back-test that reads today's tape thinks it knew on Monday what it only learned on Tuesday. Full version 9-B and §27.2.
**The governance thread.** A researcher asks whether she can "just fix the parameter in prod." The ticket for the proper fix has been open since the spring. The scene cites, without naming any employee, the SEC's January 2025 order: employees found model vulnerabilities "in or before March 2019"; the firm "waited until August 2023"; unauthorized changes "to more than a dozen models"; \$90 million penalty, \$165 million repaid (SEC Press Release 2025-15, 16 Jan 2025, LIFE §6.2 [S17]). The firm is Two Sigma and may be named for that record; the composite shop is not Two Sigma. The parameter is not fixed in prod.
**The routing decision.** 2:30: a \$20mm sale. One dealer block, or slice across venues and protocols, or add to the month's portfolio trade; "venue selection ... high touch or low touch ... circumstances where the best course of action is to trade voice" and a 10–50 million block through one dealer "might not be the optimal way" (Man Numeric [S16], **CHECK**). Also usable, from a shelf PDF and so not CHECK: Man Numeric reported over 90% of its order flow through electronic venues as of 1 May 2023 and that the "vast majority" of its trades were RFQs (PERSP §3.3).
**What the reader learns.** TRACE is the data exhaust of other people's trades (§2.2 "the Dataset Appears", §2.9), and a systematic shop's week is spent making exhaust breathable. Change control is not bureaucracy; it is the difference between a rank and a rumour (SR 11-7's "all changes are logged and can be audited", LIFE §2.1 [S1] p.12; §26.8).
**View From Another Seat (systematic manager on Halvorsen):** a ranked name with a cost estimate and a probability of fill; trades in small clips by RFQ; avoids the name when the cost model or the liquidity score says no (PERSP §3.3 "how Man looks at our bond", the dossier's characterization). Scale context, sourced: pure systematic credit is under 2% of the investable fixed-income universe; Man's systematic long-only credit sat at about \$2.8–3.1bn while discretionary almost doubled (TREND §1 items 2; LSE statements). This is the smallest room in the chapter and the one the reader most expects to be large.
**Also sourced for texture:** the DWS credit-trader posting (7 May 2026, LIFE §6.1b [S22b]): "liquidity dialogue", "foster successful relationships ... with Operations, Technology, Compliance, Risk and Audit", tools Bloomberg, MarketAxess, Tradeweb, Aladdin, Python, SQL. A job description, labelled as one.

### Thursday. The Bank Credit-Trading Desk: "A mark is a choice" (approx. 2,200 words)
*Fictional composite bank desk. The structure comes from the 2013 Senate PSI report on JPMorgan's CIO book; the people and the bank in the scene are invented and no routine is attributed to any named bank (LIFE §3.3 gap).*
**Setting and task.** Hal is the junior who marks the book at the close and sends the daily P&L email. Mei sits in the Valuation Control Group. The morning P&L explain is the first scene: carry, rates, spread, the hedge legs, and the one line that reads "unexplained" (8-C owns the method; here we only watch it foot, or not). The desk's morning is also compliance: the TRACE exception queue from yesterday (a wrong capacity flag, a late as/of; Rule 6730 [S3]) and a 9:00 reminder that the quarterly best-execution review is due (Rule 5310: "regular and rigorous reviews ... at a minimum ... quarterly", LIFE §2.2 [S2]), built from the same RFQ log the desk uses to track its hit ratio.
**What goes wrong.** Three things, in sequence, all sourced to the Senate report's structure [S5] with the real figures quoted as history, not as the scene's numbers: (1) marks. Early in the quarter the book is marked "at or near the midpoint price in the daily range"; later a trader asks Hal to mark toward the favourable side (the report, p.5; recorded call "I am not marking at mids as per a previous conversation"). Hal keeps a second column, booked against mid, the "shadow sheet" (p.5: reported YTD loss \$161 million versus \$593 million at mids, 12–16 Mar 2012). (2) Price testing. Mei's tolerance lets the IG index "vary from the midpoint ... by up to 6 basis points" (fn 675, p.112); internal audit later found no "formally applied price sourcing hierarchy" (p.141). (3) The collateral call. Another desk at the same bank marks the identical position differently; the counterparty disputes; the deputy CRO settles it by making the desk mark the way the other desk does, off "an independent pricing service" (p.6; disputes peaked at \$690 million). The four-hours-of-typing subplot: the VaR owner hand-keys data into spreadsheets "which often took hours" (p.185); the review group called it "error prone"; the automation "was never automated" (p.186). Hal's bank has a ticket for that too.
**The lunch hour nobody covers.** 12:15: half the desk is away and the auto-quoter carries more of the midday than it did in 2021. Barclays FICC research via The DESK (17 Sep 2025, LIFE §3.2b [S24]): trading 12–1 p.m. "up from 11.3% to 12.5% in US IG" since 2022; "As human traders step back, algos and portfolio trades step in"; August and December now nearly 8% of annual volume each.
**Messaging.** 4:00: the annual off-channel communications training. Twelve firms paid \$63.1 million in January 2025; PJT Partners "self-reported" and paid \$600,000 (SEC Press Release 2025-6, LIFE §7.3 [S21]). State it; do not joke at PJT's expense beyond the reader's own recognition.
**What the reader learns.** A mark is a choice inside the bid-ask; independent price verification exists because of that; two desks can hold two prices for one position until a counterparty's collateral call picks one. **Math, Gently #4 (a tolerance in basis points becomes dollars):** 6 bp of index spread times a CS01 of, say, \$50,000 per bp is \$300,000 of "legitimate" P&L that depends on who is holding the pen; the Controller found the whole book's value "could legitimately fall anywhere between \$719 million and \$1.2 billion" (p.98). Full version 8-C and 8B.9.
**View From Another Seat (bank desk on Halvorsen):** an inventory line that must earn its balance sheet. Citable mid-size-dealer economics: Fred Orlan, Jefferies, The DESK (26 Oct 2023): "It's hard for investment banks to run large balance sheets in this environment because it isn't making a lot of money"; "You're really making your money trading with and helping clients" (PERSP §5B.5). Large-bank constraint: Barnum calls G-SIB "a significant disincentive to a particular type of business, in particular some markets business" (JPMorgan Q1 2026 call, 14 Apr 2026, PERSP §1.3). Flow-franchise economics: Borthwick, BofA, 14 Oct 2026: "Clients tend to slow their activity" when rates gap (PERSP §1.2). None of these people are in the scene.

### Friday. The Distressed Fund: "Information has a legal state" (approx. 1,800 words)
*Fictional composite distressed fund.*
**Setting and task.** 7:00: Sol checks the restricted list before he checks the market. The fund has been invited onto an ad hoc group in a name it holds; joining requires "signing 'confi'", which "can result in the holder becoming 'restricted'"; to "maximize the time period members remain unrestricted" the information goes first to "the financial advisors and counsel"; members "cannot become unrestricted until the information becomes public ... Form 8-K, 10-Q, or 10-K, or ... nonmaterial" (Moyer, *Distressed Debt Analysis*, printed pp.62–64, LIFE §7.1 [S19], shelf pages `page_0079–0081`). The compliance check is the day's spine: a 10:00 call with the fund's GC about which analysts go behind the wall, a trade in an *unrelated* bond of the same issuer that now needs a "big-boy letter" "arranged by a broker-dealer intermediary" (same pages), and the Ad Hoc Group forming, "represented by counsel". (Cooperation-agreement counter: do not increment; nothing is signed on Friday but confi.)
**The pass log.** 11:30: a sponsor calls about a dividend recap. Milwood Hobbs, Oaktree, *Odd Lots* 28 Nov 2024 (LIFE §7.2 [S20], **CHECK**): "the relationship is getting the first call ... out of 20 deals, I only care about the last two. So how do I pass 18 times?" Sol passes; the pass goes in a log, with a reason. The reader should see the log as a dataset the systematic desk would kill for (§3.5 Edge Ledger).
**The quiet afternoon.** 2:00 to 5:00, nothing trades in the restricted name, because nobody who knows anything may trade it. From the outside, on Wednesday's systematic desk, the name's liquidity score just fell and no one knows why. That is the chapter's hinge: the restricted interval is a dated feature (3-B §3B.5 tells the wall as Theo's scene; 1-B shows it from the inside of the fund). Moyer's image of the restricted seller as the man who "has just watched the third race" (pp.63–64) is the box.
**What goes wrong.** The 8-K that would cleanse everyone is expected Friday after the close and does not come; the fund stays restricted over a weekend and Sol cannot touch Halvorsen, which he has been watching since 78.
**What the reader learns.** Who is allowed to know is a state variable with timestamps (§3.6 "Who at this table knows about the LMT?", §13, 12-B). **Math, Gently #5 (conditional probability, told through the restricted list):** the probability the name is in trouble given that its best-informed holders have stopped trading, with a tiny two-by-two. Full version §3.2 and §3.4.
**View From Another Seat (distressed fund on Halvorsen):** a document first, a price second; a possible restricted interval third. Citable long-horizon buyer behaviour (not distressed-corporate but the cleanest public case): D. E. Shaw bought "in excess of \$400 million in CAS and STACR bonds at a significant discount to par" in March–April 2020 from investors "put under stress by redemption requests and margin calls" (D. E. Shaw research paper, Nov 2020/2021 web version, PERSP §3.1). Use as the counterexample to the systematic seat's liquidity score: the forced seller's discount is someone's week.

### Closing section: Which Room Is Yours? (approx. 600 words)
The five narrators' Friday 5:30 p.m., in five sentences each, then the question the synthesis asks 1-B to end on: which of these rooms is the reader's, and what does the first year there look like? Hand off to 3-B by name. Theo may appear here for the first time in the chapter, reading five weeks and recognising one job. The 13-week model gag: Sol has one too, and his is a survival curve he does not call a survival curve.

## 4. Humor beats with the canon cast (cameos only)

1. **Ines, Monday 4:30.** Phones Priya's desk as a counterparty on a markout dispute and ends up narrating the reflection meeting as a hand: "What's the pot, what are the odds, and who at this table knows more than you?" The passed Halvorsen RFQ is "a hand you folded; you still get to see the river."
2. **Kaz, Friday 10:00.** On the GC call as the fund's new quant. Hears "big-boy letter" and asks, in writing, whether there is a small-boy letter and which one applies to a fund under \$1bn. (Gag 5: one legal term per appearance.) Later, told the name is "restricted", suggests Gaussian.
3. **Mags, Thursday and Friday.** On the shadow sheet: "Price is what you pay. The mark is what you say you paid." Back in her day the restricted list was a Post-it on the Bloomberg and the confi was a handshake, "and that was worse, and I miss it." Grudging respect for the point-in-time archive: "You kept Friday's Friday? Good. We kept it in a drawer."
4. **ROBO, Monday 11:10.** The fat-finger band that rejected the 9-cent quote was built from the vague version of the spec ("reject unreasonable prices"). One line: what ROBO built from the vague version, then what the precise spec says (bands by price bucket, sub-10 allowed for defaulted and recovery-priced paper).
5. **The Ad Hoc Group, Friday.** Forms at 11:00, "represented by counsel", and speaks exactly once, in unison, to ask whether anyone has seen the 8-K.
6. **Running line across the days:** every shop has a ticket to automate the thing that is done by hand; the ticket's age is the only number that rises all week.
