## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** In equities, cost is a number; in credit, cost is a *choice of counterfactual*. Every bond fill can be judged against four prices that disagree (the composite at RFQ time, the arrival mid at decision time, the cover quote, the next TRACE print), and the one you pick decides whether the desk looks skilled, lucky or leaky. The chapter's job is to make the reader choose on purpose, then to extend the cost ledger to the three things an equity TCA never has to price: the order that did not fill, the quote that was shopped, and the second leg of a pair.

**The aha** (Scene 1). Theo is asked what the week's trading cost and answers "nothing, bonds have no commissions." Ines replies "cost relative to what?", and the chapter is the long answer. The second aha (Scene 3) is that the largest line in a systematic desk's cost is usually the opportunity cost of the 30% of inquiries that fail (RQ [16], Kargar et al. 2025), which never appears in any dealer's spread. The third (Scene 8) is that a pair's cost is not the sum of two legs' costs; the covariance between the legs' fills is its own line.

**Framing rule.** Benchmarks, shortfall, scorecards, cost curves and capacity are five views of one object: the desk's RFQ log, read through the §27.4.2 order state machine. Give the log one schema in §2B.2 (order id, `known_at` stamps for `NEW/ROUTED/QUOTED/terminal`, protocol, panel size, responses, cover, fill, composite at each stamp, TRACE print after) and derive everything else from it. This is the buy-side mirror of 18-B's dealer log; use Fermanian-Guéant-Pu's outcome taxonomy (Done, Tied Traded Away, Covered, Other Traded Away, Not Traded; RQ [7]) for the dealer side and the state-machine terminals for the client side, and show the mapping in one table.

## 2. Section outline as scenes

**§2B.0 Cold Open: Friday, 4:10 p.m., the markout meeting.** *Setting:* the systematic desk's Friday markouts clock from 3-B (`03_ch03b` §"the week"); fictional composite. *Task:* Ines asks each trader what their fills cost this week; Theo, three weeks in, has the number from the EMS "cost vs composite" column: minus 0.8 bp, a gain. *What goes wrong:* Kaz has the same fills against arrival mid: plus 2.1 bp. Mags has them against the cover: plus 0.4 bp. Three honest numbers, one week. *Lesson:* the benchmark is a model of what would have happened, and in an RFQ market each candidate is observed under different conditioning. Close with Ines: "The composite tells you where the bond was, arrival tells you what you decided, the cover tells you what the second dealer thought, and the print tells you who was right. Pick one and say why."

**§2B.1 Benchmarks for an RFQ Market.** *Setting:* Monday, Kaz and Theo rebuild the benchmark table from the log. *Task:* define arrival mid (composite at `NEW`), RFQ-time composite (at `ROUTED`), cover (second-best response at `QUOTED`), and post-trade print. *What goes wrong, two ways:* (a) Kaz treats the composite as truth; Theo finds that CP+ is partly built from untraded RFQs, including the desk's own (RQ [24], Jacobsen-Venkataraman-Xu 2025: a did-not-trade RFQ between prints raises CP+'s edge over last trade 1.4x to 5.0x; the typical bond has no non-retail print on 60% of bond-days). Benchmarking against a composite that your own inquiries feed is circular, and after ICE closes on MarketAxess (PV: $167/share, close H1-2027) two of the four composites may share an owner (EL §4.1.3). (b) The cover exists only when you win: FGP's likelihoods are one-sided (RQ [7]), so "cost vs cover" is a survivor statistic (§3.3.5). **Math, Gently #1** (first use): a benchmark as a counterfactual; a tiny worked number showing the same fill scored three ways; "the full version is §2B.3." **Facts:** FGP: about 40% of dealers answer, clients wait for about two answers, reservation value about 1.7 half-spreads from CBBT mid (RQ [7]). HM 2015 Table VI: response 15-29%, no response 8-15%, fill rate 73% (micro) to 51% (max); cost falls from 24-45 bp with few responders to about zero past ten (RQ [14]). Cross-ref §2.3.2 (eleven anchored dealers worth about two) and §2.9.1 (LCS units).

**§2B.2 Implementation Shortfall, Credit Edition.** *Setting:* the §27.4 rebalance that filled 31 of 40 bonds, retold from the trader's screen at 9:40. *Task:* decompose shortfall per order into delay (decision to `ROUTED`), dealer half-spread (fill vs RFQ-time mid), impact (print drift after the fill, residualised), and the opportunity cost of `EXPIRED`, `EXPIRED_PARTIAL` and `DONE_AWAY` orders, priced as the paper return the optimiser expected times the unfilled size until the order is retried or abandoned. *What goes wrong:* the nine unfilled sells were in bonds that rallied, so the naive ledger shows the failures as gains; Kaz points out the book is now short spread duration in exactly the names it could not buy (Man 2022 p.19 quote already in §27.4.2; do not repeat it, point to it). *Lesson:* the unfilled term is the biggest and the only one with a sign that lies. **Facts:** Kargar-Lester-Plante-Weill 2025: about 30% of MarketAxess inquiries fail; a well-connected client's odd-lot IG buy takes about two days to complete after a failed inquiry; sells are about twice as fast as buys; the bottom 60% by connectedness take nearly twice as long; clients who reject first quotes trade later about 40% of the time at about 10 bp better (RQ [16]). That last number is the chapter's "patience premium" and the reason retry logic belongs in the fill service. **Math, Gently #2:** Perold's shortfall as a telescoping sum, with the paper portfolio drawn as a dotted line. Full derivation after the scene. Cross-ref §27.4.2 (terminal states emit residual-risk events), §9.8.3 (buffers).

**§2B.3 The Leakage Test.** *Setting:* a Tuesday; a $4mm HY sell went `DONE_AWAY` at 10:52 and a TRACE print appeared at 11:14 three-eighths lower. The trader wants a dealer cut. *Task:* build the test before the accusation. Event study of composite drift from `ROUTED` to the terminal stamp and 100 minutes beyond, split by protocol (disclosed RFQ, all-to-all, PT line, voice), by panel size and by outcome; placebo on orders the optimiser generated but the trader never sent. *What goes wrong:* the placebo drifts the same way. The desk's own signal is predictable (§9.8.2's dealer-seat box already says a momentum book that sold yesterday sells today), so the "leak" is partly the strategy's footprint. *Lesson:* leakage is identified only against a placebo and only net of the signal's own drift; then the residual is real and small. **Facts (CHECK, vendor-authored):** Tradeweb white paper, May 2026, IG above $1mm, 2025-01 to 2026-04: all-to-all RFQs show same-direction impact 0.1-0.3 bp larger than voice, larger for sells and illiquid bonds; design is a GLM on 5-minute cumulative Ai-Price spread changes between acceptance and TRACE publication (RQ [25]). Baldauf-Mollner 2024: an extra contact raises competition and helps a *losing* dealer front-run, so the client optimally reveals no direction (RQ [19], abstract only). Hendershott et al. 2026: quote presence is worth 6-7 bp; the best quoter wins only 15%; trade-throughs 17-40% (RQ [15]), so a "leak" can be a trade-through. **Poker Table box** (Ines): "Who saw your hand? Everyone you asked, and everyone they told." Cross-ref §3.2, §18.7, §19.8 (mystery box), §27.7.2 (CUSUM on markouts as the standing alarm).

**§2B.4 Dealer and Protocol Scorecards.** *Setting:* the quarterly dealer review, the most mundane meeting of the year: a conference room, a spreadsheet, a salesperson calling afterwards to ask why her firm dropped to tier two. *Task:* one row per dealer per protocol per size bucket per regime: response rate, hit rate, cover distance when they lose, residualised 1/5/20-day markout (IDO; §3.3.3), no-quote rate on stress days, done-away rate, time to quote. Same table for protocols (RFQ, all-to-all, PT, voice, session). *What goes wrong:* the top-ranked dealer by hit rate has the worst markouts because the desk only sees markouts on wins (collider bias: Marín Martínez et al. 2026, RQ [5]), and the dealer who "never answers" answered on 9 April 2025 when almost nobody did. *Lesson:* a scorecard without a stress column rewards fair-weather liquidity. **Facts:** MarketAxess: US HG prices per RFQ 13.2 to 6.6 on 9 April 2025, 11.3 within a week; HG bid-ask widened by as much as 231% vs a 44.62 bp COVID peak; algos about 80% of liquidity (PV, AxessPoint 2025-07-14). FILS Europe, 2026-10-15: "When it becomes very stressful, you see clearly a concentration to the usual banks"; ELPs "help more in quiet markets" (PV). Jurkatis et al. BIS WP 1140: top 1% relationship clients pay 51% less and the discount more than doubles in stress (RQ [22]). Citadel's self-reported 14.2% IG block share (PV, CHECK) as the example of a ranking the desk must reproduce before believing. Trumid 88% no-touch, Vanguard about 70% automated with "stress parameters" (EL). **View From Another Seat #1 (dealer desk):** the same quarter as the dealer scores *us*: Niang's residual-toxicity weight (RQ [2]) is their markout of our flow; 18-B's three-term quote is what we are being charged. **View From Another Seat #2 (insurer / relationship client):** one-third of insurers use a single dealer and cost is non-monotone in network size (RQ [21]); their scorecard has one row.

**§2B.5 Size-Bucketed Cost Curves and the Handoff.** *Setting:* ROBO builds $c_i(\text{size},\text{regime})$ for §9.8 and $\Pr(\text{fill}\mid \text{size},\text{protocol},\text{regime})$ for §27.4's fill service. *What goes wrong (ROBO literalism):* "regime" was undefined, so ROBO fit one curve; then, told to split by regime, it fit a square root through a $10mm+ bucket with four observations and declared impact negative. *Lesson:* buckets need counts, regimes need a definition (bid-ask percentile x response-count drop x ETF/cash ratio; PV §5 signals), and the curve's top end is a prior, not an estimate. **Facts:** Kozora et al. NY Fed SR 938: dealer ATS participation lowers customer costs 24-32 bp (EL). HM Table VI fill rates by size. Fill-model realism: AUC about 0.63 on 1.07mm RFQs (RQ [6]), 0.74 on government bonds (RQ [5]); an AUC of 0.97 is a leak. Man 2023 p.5's pre-trade filter (RFQ availability, dealer quality, RFQ frequency, completion rates, size digestibility; shelf) is the buy-side ancestor. Cross-ref §2.5.1 (router), §9.8.2, §10.8, §11.9.

**§2B.6 Capacity From Your Own Fills: the "We Are the Market" Detector.** *Setting:* month-end; Mags watches the participation series tick above 20% in a sleeve. *Task:* impact regression on own fills by bucket, participation rate (desk volume over TRACE volume, same bonds, same window; §4.8), and the detector: participation above a threshold *and* markouts turning against the desk *and* response counts falling. *What goes wrong:* the denominator moved. Duplicate affiliate prints inflated HG TRACE volume by up to 8% in June 2026 (EL), FINRA sized them at up to 5.7% of 2025 transactions, the indicator is voluntary from 2026-08-31 and mandatory 2027-08-02 (PV), so the participation series needs a break dummy. **Mags beat:** "Back in my day you knew you were the market because the dealer called you by your first name." Cross-ref §4.8 (this section is its measured half), §9.10, §22.

**§2B.7 The Two-Leg Cost of a Pair (RV thread beat).** *Setting:* Dov Adler's pod at Vantress (fictional composite from 1-B and 8-D), Pair Book PB-01 Kestrel/Halvorsen (invented issuers). Wen Zhao is told to add a TCA line to the ledger. *Task:* two-leg shortfall = long-leg shortfall + short-leg shortfall + a legging term (the market move between the first and second fill times the unhedged DTS) + the short leg's standing costs (borrow fee, recall risk, or the basis if the short is CDS, CDX or an ETF). *What goes wrong:* the long leg fills at 9:50, the borrow is recalled at 10:30 (31.2% of loans shrink before closing, RV R11), and for forty minutes the pod is outright long; then the only short available is CDS, which introduces a basis. *Lesson:* the hedge leg's cost is a function of the signal (distress raises the fee), and GM/Ford 2005 is the canonical case of legs failing on different days (BIS QR June 2005 box, RV R22: stock +18% on 4 May, downgrade widening on 5 May). **Facts:** Asquith et al.: mean 33 bp, median 18 bp borrow; 173 bp before and 245 bp after a bankruptcy filing; 102% collateral; on-demand recall (RV R11). Worked comparison: cash short 155 bp vs CDS short 110 bp on $15mm, $67.5k a year saved for ISDA, clearing and auction risk (RV §5.2). Attribution month: idio $40.5k, net $13.3k, capture ratio 33% (RV §5.6); the TCA line is the "friction" row of 8-C and feeds 8-D's ledger. March 2020 bases of -186 bp IG and -401 bp HY (Risk.net via JPMorgan, RV §8, CHECK secondary). **Math, Gently #3:** legging risk as the variance of a partial hedge over a short window; a two-line number. **View From Another Seat #3 (multi-strategy RV pod):** platform risk manager Marcus Oyelaran's rule: a pair that is one-legged for more than N minutes is a position, with its own limit (8-B).

**§2B.8 Two Stress Days on the Scorecard: March 2020 and April 2025.** *Setting:* the same TCA engine replayed over both weeks (§27.8 replay). *March 2020:* the composite went stale and the ETF was the only price (§2.7 "The Week the ETF Was the Only Price", §20.5); tracking error above 200 bp against 0.7 bp normally (Todorov, BIS; EL), so arrival and composite benchmarks were fiction and the only honest benchmark was the ETF-implied price; HG bid-ask peaked at 44.62 bp (PV). *April 2025:* prices per RFQ halved to 6.6 on 9 April and recovered within a week; 8 April was a $55.9bn HG TRACE day (MarketAxess) and a reported $89.7bn all-in record with 12% PT (Greenwich, CHECK definitions: PV reconciles the conflicting $92bn and $84.7bn entries); $99bn of fixed-income ETFs traded on 7 April (EL); Tradability predictions fell about 20% (PV, CHECK); the robots stayed on with wider parameters (Vanguard, FILS). *What goes wrong:* the scorecard scored dealers on the week as a whole and missed that two of eleven answered on 9 April. *Lesson:* weight the scorecard by regime; the ten worst volatility days are the days that decide the dealer tier (HM: response falls to 12-26% on those days, RQ [14]). Reuse §2.9.3's 10:40 a.m., 9 April decision point rather than re-tell it; add the EUR IG March 2026 half-life (bid-ask +88% off the February low, 4-14 weeks to normalise; PV, EL) as the slow-stress contrast.

**§2B.9 When to Apply It, and When It Breaks.** TCA works when the log is complete and bitemporal, when the composite is independent of the desk's own flow, and when the sample has stress days in it. It breaks on survivor statistics (cover, winner-only markouts), on moved denominators (affiliate prints, platform series breaks after 2026-07-30), on composite reflexivity, and on two-leg trades scored as two one-leg trades. A seasoned practitioner checks the placebo first, the stress column second, the denominator third.

**§2B.10 The Engine (`tca.py`) and Desk Debrief.** Specification for AI Implementation: inputs are the RFQ log schema above plus composite snapshots and TRACE; outputs are the per-order shortfall table, the scorecards, the bucketed curves with counts, the participation series with break dummies, the leakage event study with placebo, and the pair TCA line. Edge cases: orders with no composite, PT lines with one basket price, done-away with no cover, affiliate-duplicate prints. Code in App F; spec box opens with what ROBO built from "minimise cost vs composite" (it waited until the composite was stale enough to beat, which is exactly when the composite was wrong).
