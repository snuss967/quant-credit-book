### 6.1 Latency economics of credit: seconds for quotes, microseconds for hedges

- **Automation share (dossier, `trend_electronification_pt_etf_liquidity.md` rows 22, 60, 61, 76).** Trumid: 88% of eligible RFQ line items executed fully no-touch (Sep 2025; The DESK 2025-10-08). Tradeweb AiEX: more than 40% of institutional trades in 2025 vs 30% in 2021 (Tradeweb 10-K FY2025). Vanguard: about 70% of traded volume automated, up from about 40%, "but sends nothing without a trader" (The DESK, Oct 2026). Buy-side desks switch robots to "stress parameters" or off when markets gap. Use: the auto-responder is the credit desk's "fast path"; its parameters are the credit analogue of an FPGA trigger table, only 10^6 times slower.
- **Composite pricing as the fair-value anchor for auto-quoting (dossier rows 26, 102, 139).** CP+ (MarketAxess, "AI-driven pricing engine"), Ai-Price (Tradeweb; about 30k US corporates and about 1m munis), BVAL, ICE evaluated pricing. ICE agreed 2026-07-29 to buy MarketAxess (\$167/share, EV about \$5.7bn). Design consequence: the fair-value service in Ch 27's architecture must be composite-agnostic and must log which composite priced each quote.
- **TRACE reporting timing (dossier row 72; FINRA Regulatory Notice 25-17, 2025-12-04).** FINRA is "not moving forward" with the 1-minute outer limit; 15 minutes stays. Aggregate allocation reporting optional from 2026-06-08 with dissemination caps \$5mm IG / \$1mm HY. Consequence: the public tape arrives up to 15 minutes late and capped; the desk's own fills are the only sub-second truth it has.
- **Duplicate prints (dossier rows 55, 73).** Duplicate affiliate trade reports inflated US HG TRACE volume by up to 8% in June 2026 (MarketAxess volume release). SEC approved extending the TRACE affiliate-principal indicator 2026-07-24; voluntary from 2026-08-31, mandatory 2027-08-02 (FINRA Notice 26-16). Consequence: the ingestion layer needs a dedupe stage keyed on the affiliate flag, and volume-based features need a regime tag.

### 6.2 The kill-switch canon: incidents and rules

- **Knight Capital, 2012-08-01.** SEC Order, Release No. 34-70694, File No. 3-15570, dated 2013-10-16 (VERIFIED-URL: https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/2013/34-70694.pdf; PDF metadata confirmed; body text extracted below in this subsection once parsed). Canonical facts to confirm from the order text: about 45 minutes, about \$460mm loss, new RLP code deployed to 7 of 8 servers, the repurposed flag that reactivated dormant "Power Peg" code, 97 pre-open automated emails that nobody treated as alerts, and the \$12mm penalty under Rule 15c3-5 (CHECK each number against the extracted text before printing).
- **Knight, confirmed from the order text (VERIFIED-URL, SEC Release 34-70694, 2013-10-16; text extracted with pdftotext).** ¶1: "On August 1, 2012, Knight Capital Americas LLC ('Knight') experienced a significant error in the operation of its automated routing system for equity orders, known as SMARS. While processing 212 small retail orders ... SMARS routed millions of orders into the market over a 45-minute period, and obtained over 4 million executions in 154 stocks for more than 397 million shares." Net long about \$3.5bn in 80 stocks and net short about \$3.15bn in 74 stocks; "Knight lost over \$460 million." ¶13–14: the new RLP code reused a flag that formerly activated "Power Peg," functionality Knight "had discontinued using many years earlier" (ceased 2003); in 2005 Knight moved the cumulative-fill tracking earlier in SMARS and "did not retest the Power Peg code." ¶15–16: the deployment missed one of the servers, which ran the dormant Power Peg code and sent child orders without the fill counter. Rule 15c3-5 initial compliance date 2011-07-14 (fn). (Penalty and the "97 emails" count: see the follow-up extraction note below in this subsection; print only what the text shows.)
- **Citigroup Global Markets, 2022-05-02 fat finger; FCA fine 2024-05-22 (web search 2026-10-17; sources: FCA final notice coverage by Foot Anstey, Investment Executive, Financial Planning Today; URLs in §9; CHECK against the FCA final notice itself).** Trader meant to sell a \$58mm basket; an OMS input error produced a \$444bn basket. Controls blocked \$255bn; \$189bn went to an algorithm; about \$1.4bn of shares sold on European venues before the trader cancelled, coinciding with a short, sharp drop in several European indices. FCA: no hard block to reject the whole basket; a pop-up warning the trader could click through without reading; real-time monitoring "too slow to escalate." FCA £27.77mm plus PRA £33.9mm = £61.6mm; 30% settlement discount. Lesson: a soft warning is not a control; the control is the one that cannot be clicked past.
- **US Treasury flash rally, 2014-10-15 (VERIFIED by web search 2026-10-17: Liberty Street Economics 2019-10 "From the Vault: a look back at the October 15, 2014 flash rally"; Joint Staff Report released 2015-07-13 by Treasury, Fed Board, NY Fed, SEC, CFTC; SEC press release 2015-144; URLs in §9).** Between 9:33 and 9:45 a.m. ET the 10-year yield fell 16 bp and rebounded, "without an obvious reason"; the report calls it unprecedented in recent Treasury history and names no single cause. PTFs accounted for more than half of activity (exact share and window: CHECK in the report). Use: this is the chapter's picture of what "quote pulling" looks like at market scale, in the instrument the credit desk hedges with.
- **Knight, follow-up extraction (same order).** ¶15: "one of Knight's technicians did not copy the new code to one of the eight SMARS computer servers. Knight did not have a second technician review this deployment ... Knight had no written procedures that required such a review." ¶16: "The seven servers that received the new code processed these orders correctly. However, orders sent with the repurposed flag to the eighth server triggered the defective Power Peg code." ¶18: for 75 stocks Knight was more than 20% of volume with price moves above 5%; for 37 stocks, above 10% and more than 50% of volume. ¶19: from about 8:01 a.m. ET, "Knight's system sent 97 of these e-mail messages [BNET rejects, 'Power Peg disabled'] to a group of Knight personnel before the 9:30 a.m. market open. Knight did not design these types of messages to be system alerts." ¶10: "Knight remained connected to the markets and continued to send orders." Section IV: civil money penalty of \$12,000,000. All numbers now VERIFIED from the order text.
- **SEC Market Access Rule, Rule 15c3-5, Release No. 34-63241, File No. S7-03-10, dated 2010-11-03 (VERIFIED-URL: https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2010/34-63241.pdf; text extracted).** Summary text: controls must be "reasonably designed to prevent the entry of orders that exceed appropriate pre-set credit or capital thresholds, or that appear to be erroneous," and to "assure appropriate surveillance personnel receive immediate post-trade execution reports"; controls "must be under the direct and exclusive control of the broker or dealer with market access." Effective 60 days after Federal Register publication; compliance six months after that (initial compliance date 2011-07-14 per the Knight order, fn. 2; certain requirements extended 2011-06-30). Use: the US rule is principles-based (no 5-second number); RTS 6 is the prescriptive one.
- **Reg SCI, SEC press release 2014-260, 2014-11-19 (VERIFIED-URL: https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2014-260).** Covers SROs, certain ATSs, plan processors and certain exempt clearing agencies; requires "comprehensive policies and procedures in place for their technological systems," corrective action, SEC notification, member notification, business-continuity testing and annual reviews. Effective 60 days after FR publication; compliance nine months after; coordinated testing 21 months. (The 2023 proposal to extend Reg SCI to large broker-dealers and SBS data repositories: CHECK status.)
- **MiFID II RTS 6, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589, adopted 2016-07-19, OJ 2017-03-31, applies from 2018-01-03 (VERIFIED-URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R0589).** Art. 12 Kill functionality: the firm must be able to "cancel immediately, as an emergency measure, any or all of its unexecuted orders" on any venue, and "identify which trading algorithm and which trader, trading desk or, where applicable, which client is responsible for each order." Art. 15 pre-trade controls: price collars, maximum order values, maximum order volumes, maximum message limits, execution throttles, market and credit risk limits. Art. 16 real-time monitoring by traders and an independent risk function: "Real-time alerts shall be generated within five seconds after the relevant event." Art. 17 post-trade controls: continuous, with reconciliation against external records. Art. 13 automated surveillance with alerts operable "at the beginning of the following trading day." Use: RTS 6 is the only regulation that puts a number (5 s) on alert latency; it defines the slow lane of the kill-switch hierarchy.

### 6.3 Clocks, timestamps and the public tape

- **FINRA Rule 4590 (VERIFIED-URL: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4590).** Computer clocks: one-second general tolerance; clocks recording events in NMS securities and OTC equities "must be synchronized within a 50-millisecond tolerance of the NIST clock." Manual time stamps: one second. The tolerance covers NIST-to-provider difference, transmission delay and drift. Amended by SR-FINRA-2016-005 effective 2016-08-15. Consequence for the chapter: the regulator's clock is 50 ms for equities and 1 s for the rest of the book (TRACE events are not NMS events); the desk's internal clock (PTP, sub-microsecond) is three to six orders of magnitude finer than anything a regulator will ever ask for, and the gap is where markout analysis lives.

### 6.4 FPGAs, ASICs and the arms race

- **STAC-T0 tick-to-trade record (web search 2026-10-17; AMD blog "AMD Sets STAC Benchmark World Record for Fastest Electronic Trade Execution", 2024; AMD press release 2024-10-14 on Alveo UL3422; URLs in §9; fetch pending for exact wording).** AMD with Exegy reported a minimum 13.9 ns "actionable latency" on the Alveo UL3524 under STAC-T0, down from the prior record of 24.2 ns (about 49%). Alveo UL3422 claims less than 3 ns FPGA transceiver latency. These numbers used Exegy's application IP, not an open-source design. The "Accelerated Algorithmic Trading" open-source reference design was NOT confirmed by search; treat as CHECK / TITLE-ONLY.
- **Dean & Barroso, "The Tail at Scale," Communications of the ACM, vol. 56 (2013), pp. 74–80 (VERIFIED-URL: https://research.google/pubs/the-tail-at-scale/; abstract only).** The canonical fan-out result (a 1-in-100 slow server makes 63% of 100-way fan-out requests slow) is from the full paper; state it as the paper's example (CHECK exact numbers from the paper body).
- **Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill, "Quantifying the High-Frequency Trading 'Arms Race': A Simple New Methodology and Estimates," FCA Occasional Paper 50, January 2020 (VERIFIED-URL: https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-50.pdf; abstract extracted verbatim). Later published in the QJE (2022; volume/pages CHECK).** Abstract: "latency-arbitrage races are very frequent (one per minute for FTSE 100 stocks), extremely fast (the modal race lasts 5-10 millionths of a second), and account for a large portion of overall trading volume (about 20%). Race participation is concentrated, with the top-3 firms accounting for over half of all race wins and losses. Our main estimates suggest that eliminating latency arbitrage would reduce the cost of trading by 17% and that the total sums at stake are on the order of \$5 billion annually in global equity markets." Use: the "modal race lasts 5–10 µs" line is the single most useful number for explaining why the credit desk's hedge leg (futures, ETFs) must be on the same clock as the equity HFTs, even though its RFQ leg is measured in minutes.
- **Budish, Cramton & Shim (2015), "The High-Frequency Trading Arms Race: Frequent Batch Auctions as a Market Design Response," Quarterly Journal of Economics 130(4) (TITLE-ONLY; Chicago Booth PDF link returned 404 this session).** Cite for the ES–SPY correlation breakdown at millisecond horizons and the "sniping" mechanism, which is exactly the stale-quote option the credit desk writes every time it streams a firm price on an all-to-all venue while HYG and TY move.

### 6.5 The listed hedging layer: credit index futures, ETFs, CDX

- **CME Bloomberg credit futures (web search 2026-10-17; CME press releases 2025-03-17 and 2025-09-08, URLs in §9; fetch pending).** Launched June 2024 on Bloomberg US corporate indices (IG and HY, duration-hedged variants). Record OI 3,200 contracts (\$320mm notional) in March 2025; cumulative volume above 450,000 contracts and OI 6,800 by 2025-09-04; CME factsheet: more than 580,000 contracts since launch as of 2025-10-10, OI above 6,800 (more than \$730mm notional), Q3-2025 ADV above 2,900 contracts. No 2026 figures found yet (CHECK for update).
- **Cboe iBoxx credit futures (dossier row 75).** IBHY/IBIG block trades topped \$1bn per month for two consecutive months (Jul–Aug 2026); IBIG block volume passed IBHY for the first time (The DESK, 2026-10-16, Cboe sponsored content; CHECK as sponsored).

It is the hinge between the "seconds" world of RFQs and the "microseconds" world of the FPGA.

### 6.6 Security master, identifiers and the LMT CUSIP problem

- **LMTs create new securities (dossier `trend_lmt_default_recovery_cycle.md` rows 28, 31).** Serta 2020-06-22: \$200mm new-money first-out plus about \$1.2bn of 1L/2L exchanged into about \$875mm of second-out super-priority debt (Proskauer, 2025-01-02). Wesco/Incora Mar 2022: \$250mm new money via new 2026 notes (Octus, 2024-06-26). Each new tranche is a new instrument with new identifiers, a new ranking and a new issuer-guarantor map; a security master keyed on "the Serta term loan" breaks the day the exchange closes.
- **OpenFIGI (VERIFIED-URL: https://www.openfigi.com/).** Site text: OpenFIGI is "your entry point to multiple tools for identifying, mapping and requesting the free and Open Symbology dataset," provided "under the MIT Open Source license at no cost and as a public good." (That Bloomberg is the Registration Authority and FIGI is an OMG standard is general knowledge; the FIGI hierarchy of share-class / composite / exchange-level identifiers is general knowledge; neither is stated on the landing page: mark as general knowledge, not quoted.)
- **CUSIP antitrust: Dinosaur Financial Group LLC et al. v. S&P Global Inc. et al., S.D.N.Y. No. 1:22-cv-01860 (web search 2026-10-17; WatersTechnology coverage; complaint filed March 2022; URLs in §9).** Plaintiffs Dinosaur Financial Group, Hildene Capital Management and Swiss Life Investment Management; defendants CUSIP Global Services, S&P Global, FactSet and the American Bankers Association. Judge Katherine Polk Failla dismissed most claims but let a Sherman Act §2 monopolization claim proceed; a later ruling certified a class of end-user firms and data vendors (dates of both rulings CHECK on the docket via CourtListener). In a 2024 SEC comment letter (file S7-2024-05) plaintiffs' counsel backed requiring FIGI in regulatory filings. Use: the identifier your security master is keyed on is a licensed, litigated asset; design the master around an internal surrogate key with CUSIP/ISIN/FIGI as attributes.
- **TRACE identifiers (general knowledge; CHECK against FINRA TRACE specs in App B).** TRACE disseminates by FINRA symbol and CUSIP; 144A and Reg S tranches of one deal carry different CUSIPs and, after a registered exchange offer, a third; the security master must map all three to one "economic line" while keeping them separate for settlement and TRACE dissemination-eligibility (144A dissemination began 2014-06-30, CHECK).

### 6.7 Operational-resilience incidents (beyond Knight)

- **ICBC Financial Services ransomware, 2023-11-08/09 (web search 2026-10-17; sources: Reuters via Yahoo Finance "BNY keeps ICBC off Treasury platform", VOA 2023-11, PYMNTS 2023-11; URLs in §9).** LockBit ransomware hit ICBC's US broker-dealer; BNY Mellon, the sole Treasury settlement agent, disconnected it and settled its trades manually; ICBC FS owed BNY about \$9bn in unsettled trades and the parent injected capital to settle. Entry vector reported as a Citrix NetScaler vulnerability ("Citrix Bleed"; CHECK). The widely repeated "settled via USB stick" detail was NOT confirmed by the search results; print only as "reported by some outlets (CHECK)" or omit. Lesson for the chapter: your fastest system is bounded by your slowest counterparty's recovery plan.
- **Treasury flash rally detail (VERIFIED-URL: Liberty Street Economics, 2019-10-15, Fleming, Johansson, Keane and Meyer, https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2019/10/from-the-vault-a-look-back-at-the-october-15-2014-flash-rally/).** The 10-year traded in a 37 bp range and closed 6 bp below its open; between 9:33 and 9:45 a.m. ET the yield fell 16 bp and rebounded "without a clear cause." PTFs "accounted for more than half of traded volume in the electronic interdealer market." The Joint Staff Report (Treasury, Fed Board, NY Fed, SEC, CFTC) was released 2015-07-13 (SEC press release 2015-144; the report PDF URL on home.treasury.gov returned 404 this session: TITLE-ONLY for the report itself).
- **CME Globex halts (web search 2026-10-17; BNN Bloomberg 2019-02-27, The Trade News, Hedgeweek, Rigzone 2025-11-28; URLs in §9).** 2019-02-26/27: a technical error halted Globex for about three hours, from 6:39 p.m. to 9:45 p.m. CT, covering Treasury and S&P 500 futures; iLink gateways restarted. 2025-11-27/28: a cooling failure at a CyrusOne data centre near Chicago stopped Globex for about ten hours (Hedgeweek; duration CHECK), in Thanksgiving week, halting Treasury and SOFR futures. - **Bloomberg terminal outage, 2015-04-17 (web search 2026-10-17; NPR, DatacenterDynamics, The Drum; URLs in §9).** Terminals went dark for roughly 2.5–4 hours from about 8:30 a.m. London (sources disagree on length); the UK DMO postponed a roughly £3bn gilt operation citing "ongoing issues with the third-party platform supplier," completing it in the afternoon. Bloomberg cited "a combination of hardware and software failures." Use: the single-vendor dependency most credit desks still have for chat, pricing and even auction access.
