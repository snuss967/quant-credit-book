## 1. Thesis and "aha"

**Thesis.** A corporate bond dealer's quote is a *posterior*, a *risk charge*, and a *bid for future business*, added together and then rounded to the nearest eighth. Classical microstructure gives each piece separately: Roll (1984) tells you how to *see* the spread in the autocorrelation of prints when nobody will show you a two-way market; Glosten–Milgrom (1985) and Kyle (1985) price the posterior (who knows more than you, and how much does size move the price); Avellaneda–Stoikov (2008) and Guéant–Lehalle–Fernandez-Tapia (2013) price the inventory (how scared should you be of what you already own, and for how long); and the 2026 hit-ratio papers (Barzykin–Ciceri; Niang) price the franchise (how much you pay to keep being asked). The chapter's job is to derive each term from first principles, then weld them into one **market maker's equation** for an RFQ in a bond that can gap 20 points on a Tuesday, and then show how to read the market's own ledger, TRACE, well enough to calibrate it.

**The "aha" (three of them, in ascending order of how much they annoy Kaz):**

1. **The Avellaneda–Stoikov spread has two halves and only one of them is about risk.** $\text{spread} = \gamma\sigma^2(T-t) + \tfrac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1+\gamma/k)$. The first term is fear (risk aversion × variance × horizon). The second term is *greed*: it is what a risk-neutral monopolist charges when demand is $\Lambda(\delta)=Ae^{-k\delta}$, and it tends to $2/k$ as $\gamma\to0$. The famous skew, $r^{\text{res}} = s - q\gamma\sigma^2(T-t)$, is just the risk half moving the center. In a market where primary dealers now run *net short* corporate bonds (Crisil Coalition Greenwich, 2026: ≈\$4bn average net short, first time in ≥25 years), the fear half is often near zero and the greed half, plus adverse selection, is the whole spread. That is why "agency or riskless-principal" markets still have spreads.

2. **Jump-to-default does not change the structure of AS; it changes $\sigma^2$ and adds a drift.** With a Poisson jump of size $J$ at intensity $\lambda$ (default or LMT), the small-$\gamma$ reservation price becomes $r^{\text{res}} = s - \lambda J (T-t) - q\gamma(\sigma^2 + \lambda J^2)(T-t)$. Expected loss moves the mid; jump *variance* $\lambda J^2$ inflates the inventory penalty even when the dealer's view is neutral. The exact exponential-utility version is asymmetric in $q$ through $e^{\gamma q J}$: a long dealer fears the next bond more than a short dealer wants it. And the measure matters: if $s$ is already the $\mathbb{Q}$-fair mid, the drift term is $(\lambda^{\mathbb{P}}-\lambda^{\mathbb{Q}})J(T-t)$, which is the dollar value of an RSSG analyst's private read of the credit agreement. This is the chapter's bridge: the PJT reader's "will this company survive the quarter?" becomes a number that moves a bid.

3. **TRACE is not a tape; it is a censored, lagged, double-counted ledger, and the censoring is the signal.** Sizes above \$5mm (IG) and \$1mm (HY) print as "5MM+" / "1MM+", the uncapped size arrives six months after quarter-end (FINRA Notice 25-17), the 15-minute reporting clock survived the 1-minute proposal, portfolio trades carry a modifier only since May 15, 2023, and duplicate affiliate prints inflated HG volume by up to 8% in June 2026. A quant who treats TRACE prices as a time series gets Roll's formula wrong (positive autocovariance from order splitting, per Dynkin p.771) and Kyle's $\lambda$ wrong (sizes are censored exactly where impact lives). A quant who treats TRACE as an *auction record* with known rules can back out who was the informed side.

**Why it belongs in a TREND-heavy book.** Every structural trend in Part V runs through this chapter's equations: auto-quoting (Trumid 88% no-touch, Tradeweb AiEX >40%) is the $\Lambda(\delta)$ curve being fitted by machines; dealer inventories going to zero and then negative is the $q\gamma\sigma^2(T-t)$ term disappearing from the market's average spread; non-bank LPs at ≈10% of credit market-making revenue and Citadel's self-reported 14.2% of IG block risk transfer are a new population of agents whose $\gamma$ and $k$ differ from banks'; composites (CP+, Ai-Price, BVAL) are the market's attempt to agree on $s$; and the BWIC market (>€1.5bn of CLOs listed in one week in the LDI crisis) is a first-price auction with a leaky list. On the strategy side, the chapter is where *momentum at the microstructure scale* lives: post-RFQ drift is momentum conditional on informed flow, Roll's bounce is reversal conditional on uninformed flow, and the desk's job is to tell them apart in three seconds.

**Scope boundaries.** Ch 3 already derives Glosten–Milgrom for an RFQ and markouts; this chapter recalls the result in one display equation and spends its budget on Roll, Kyle, AS, GLFT, the JTD extension, hit ratios, TRACE and BWICs. Ch 19 owns basket pricing; Ch 20 owns ETF arbitrage; Ch 22 owns the regulatory calendar; Ch 27 owns latency and quote-pulling; Ch 28 owns Kelly-with-JTD and LVaR.

### 6.2 Shelf facts (verified page text; see §5 for ≤60-word quotations)

### 6.3 TRACE decoding: rules and data facts

- **Rule 6750 (VERIFIED, finra.org):** FINRA disseminates all TRACE-eligible transactions, including 144A, "immediately upon receipt of the transaction report," except: affiliate-principal-indicator trades (Rule 6730(d)(4)(E)); certain M&A proprietary position transfers; list/fixed-offering-price and takedown transactions; non-periodically-disseminated CMBS/CDO/CMO; Treasuries other than on-the-run nominal coupons; foreign sovereigns. Amendment SR-FINRA-2026-009 effective 2027-08-02 listed on the rule page. The rule text itself contains **no numeric dissemination cap**; the caps live in the dissemination protocol and in FINRA notices.
- **Dissemination caps:** IG \$5mm (shown as "5MM+") and HY \$1mm ("1MM+"), per Ben_Dor p.364 n.8 (2010-era) and FINRA Regulatory Notice 25-17 (Dec 2025; caps of \$5mm IG and \$1mm HY restated for aggregate allocation reports, per the electronification dossier). Uncapped volumes appear only in the delayed historic/academic files (CHECK the current delay: historically 18 months for Historic TRACE; confirm on FINRA's data page, which returned 404 at the old URL this session).
- **Reporting clock:** 15 minutes remains the outer limit; FINRA announced 2025-12-04 it is "not moving forward" with 1-minute reporting (Notice 25-17; dossier).
- **Portfolio-trade modifier:** mandatory 2023-05-15 for trades between two parties, ≥10 unique issues, single basket price (Notice 22-12; Rule 6730 Supp. (d)(4)(H)).
- **Affiliate-principal indicator expansion:** SEC approved 2026-07-24; voluntary from 2026-08-31, mandatory 2027-08-02 (Notice 26-16). Rationale: duplicate affiliate prints inflated US HG TRACE volume by up to 8% in June 2026 (MarketAxess Q2-2026 release; dossier).
- **Aggregate allocation reporting:** optional from 2026-06-08 (Notice 25-17).
- **Key fields a decoder must handle (standard Enhanced TRACE layout; general knowledge, CHECK against the current FINRA file spec):** reporting-party side (buy/sell from the reporting dealer's view), contra-party type (customer vs. dealer; ATS indicator), reported price, volume (capped in disseminated feed), trade status/cancel/correction codes, as-of flag, special-price flag, when-issued flag, commission flag, trading-market code (primary vs. secondary), sale-condition codes, and the PT modifier. ### 6.6 Structural trend: dealer inventories went to zero, then negative (Trend Watch #2 raw material)

- **Coalition Greenwich (late 2025; via search summary, CHECK the exact note):** primary dealers' average net *short* in corporate bonds >5y reached ≈\$9.5bn in September 2025, exceeding their ≈\$7.4bn net long in ≤5y bonds.
- **Bloomberg / Hedgeweek (early 2026; CHECK date):** primary dealers net short US corporate bonds "for the first time in at least 25 years"; Crisil Coalition Greenwich (Fed data) average net short ≈\$4bn so far in 2026 vs. ≈\$16bn average long in 2017. Interpretations: banks cutting exposure at tight valuations vs. demand so strong that dealers pass risk instantly; McPartland: trading is increasingly agency-based so inventories matter less. Source: NY Fed Primary Dealer Statistics (weekly, Thursdays 4:15 p.m. ET; series since Jan 28, 1998 in split structures; VERIFIED page, numbers not on page).
- **Pre-history (general knowledge, CHECK):** dealer corporate inventories peaked near \$235bn in 2007 (old NY Fed series mixed corporates with other debt) and collapsed after 2008; Bessembinder–Spatt–Venkataraman (2020 *JFQA* survey, in the industry dossier): non-bank dealers 12.5% of customer–dealer volume in 2014–16 vs. 2.4% pre-GFC.
- **Why it matters for AS:** when the representative dealer holds $q \approx 0$ (or $q<0$), the inventory term vanishes and the quoted spread is driven by the adverse-selection and hit-ratio terms; "agency" trading means the dealer is running $T-t \to 0$ (immediate hedge/offset), which is the GLFT limit where the optimal spread collapses to $\frac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1+\gamma/k)$-type constants. Kargar et al. (2021) is the stress-case mirror: risky-principal costs spike, flow shifts to agency, customers' willingness to pay for immediacy +≈200 bp.

### 6.8 Second-pass verifications (fetched 2026-10-17)

- **Barzykin & Ciceri, "Bond Market Making with a Hit-Ratio Target," arXiv 2604.20406 (v1 22 Apr 2026, v2 27 Jul 2026; q-fin.RM). VERIFIED.** OTC bond market making on a *size ladder*; quadratic inventory penalty plus a running target on the size-weighted hit ratio; "dualizing the hit ratio target term" keeps the reduced HJB separable, giving exact controls "through the inverse of the fill-probability function and the Hamiltonian derivative"; quadratic approximation (after Bergault et al.) gives a Riccati equation for inventory curvature; linearized quotes decompose into "riskless spread, inventory-risk correction, and hit-ratio correction"; covers multiple bonds and client tiers.
- **Niang, "Quality-Adjusted Hit-Ratio Targeting in Corporate Bond Market Making," arXiv 2605.30643 (28 May 2026). VERIFIED.** Replaces the raw hit ratio with a residual-quality-adjusted one: post-trade markouts are decomposed into observable credit factors, carry/rolldown, issuer relative value, index/ETF demand effects, and *residual* adverse selection; only the residual counts as toxicity. Under the quadratic approximation, quotes = riskless spread + inventory skew + credit-alpha skew + residual-toxicity charge + quality-hit-ratio subsidy. Synthetic simulations: raw hit-ratio targeting subsidizes toxic flow. No proprietary RFQ data. (This is "IDO or it didn't happen" applied to markouts, now in a 2026 control paper; cite it next to the Ch 3 IDO markout.)
