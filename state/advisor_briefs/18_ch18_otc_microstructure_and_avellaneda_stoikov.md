### 6.2 Shelf facts (verified page text; see §5 for ≤60-word quotations)

- **Ben_Dor p.120–121 (LCS method):** trader bid-ask *indications* vs. live markets; simultaneity advantage over TRACE ("for most bonds the time between a broker-dealer buy and sell transaction for a given CUSIP can be very long"); inventory contaminates quotes ("temporarily long inventory … tighter spread … to entice a bid"); indications for seasoned bonds are "theoretical" and tighter than a tradeable market because "a trader can use an attractive indication to generate inquiries (which provide the trader with information)". June 2009: traders quoted 1,177 of ≈3,600 IG index issues; 2,456 non-quoted. Benchmark bond = on-the-run and/or >80th percentile volume.
- **Ben_Dor p.128 (LCS vs TRACE volume):** LCS negatively related to trading volume (Fig 5.9/5.10, June 2009).
- **Ben_Dor p.364 n.8 (TRACE caps):** "TRACE truncates the sizes of large transactions at \$1 million and \$5 million for high-yield and investment-grade bonds, respectively"; imputed true caps by matching aggregate volume: \$4mm HY and \$12.5mm IG; trades <\$100k excluded to focus on institutions.
- **Man_2022 p.19–22 (transaction cost modelling):** RFQ process "has hardly changed for decades"; dealer runs "not firm and actionable"; "if filled" trades as an indictment; probability of fill <100% breaks optimizer optimality; momentum has the highest turnover. p.20: Chen, Lesmond & Wei (2007) bid-ask estimates of 25 bp (AAA) and 180 bp (CCC), "markedly tighter in the current market"; HY bid-ask 20-day average chart (Bloomberg, 1 Jul 2017–2 Feb 2021) gapping in Feb/Mar 2020 and normalizing. p.21: TC = inside market spread + slippage + spread calibration ("whether dealers tend to fade from or improve their runs (this can be modelled with data from TRACE)"); explicit/implicit/hidden cost taxonomy. p.22: HY and IG transaction cost charts by size (1/5/10/15/20mm), 2004–2021.
- **Man_2023 p.4–5:** Greenwich MarketView protocol shares 2021→2022 (disclosed RFQ 48%→42%; anonymous RFQ, session-based, CLOB, PT, streaming growing); >90% of Man order flow electronic; "trades that were attempted but not executed" as an imbalance signal; RFQ case study: route by "whether a security is actually available for RFQ, filtering to only high-quality RFQ participants … frequency of RFQs, the rate of completed trades, and whether a given trade size is digestible in RFQ form"; "liquidity sweet spot".
- **Paleologo p.152–153:** market impact dominates commissions and bid-ask; price as a spring; impact grows faster than linearly in size, between (size)^{3/2} and (size)^2; optimal event trade size ∝ α·V·T/(2σ) with a market-specific constant C. (Equity framing; the chapter adapts it: in bonds the "spring" is the dealer's inventory, and the relaxation is the dealer re-offering.)
- **Dynkin p.702, p.771 (Barclays LCS, TES):** LCS used to measure rebalancing costs; Trade Efficiency Scores combine LCS bid–offer and volume (CHECK by reading pages before quoting).
- **Moyer p.42–44:** uses "adverse selection" in the ratings-pool sense (Caa pools grow via downgrades and new issues), a useful contrast joke: the banker's adverse selection is about who ends up in the CCC bucket; the dealer's is about who is on the other side of the RFQ.

### 6.3 TRACE decoding: rules and data facts

- **Rule 6750 (VERIFIED, finra.org):** FINRA disseminates all TRACE-eligible transactions, including 144A, "immediately upon receipt of the transaction report," except: affiliate-principal-indicator trades (Rule 6730(d)(4)(E)); certain M&A proprietary position transfers; list/fixed-offering-price and takedown transactions; non-periodically-disseminated CMBS/CDO/CMO; Treasuries other than on-the-run nominal coupons; foreign sovereigns. Amendment SR-FINRA-2026-009 effective 2027-08-02 listed on the rule page. The rule text itself contains **no numeric dissemination cap**; the caps live in the dissemination protocol and in FINRA notices.
- **Dissemination caps:** IG \$5mm (shown as "5MM+") and HY \$1mm ("1MM+"), per Ben_Dor p.364 n.8 (2010-era) and FINRA Regulatory Notice 25-17 (Dec 2025; caps of \$5mm IG and \$1mm HY restated for aggregate allocation reports, per the electronification dossier). Uncapped volumes appear only in the delayed historic/academic files (CHECK the current delay: historically 18 months for Historic TRACE; confirm on FINRA's data page, which returned 404 at the old URL this session).
- **Reporting clock:** 15 minutes remains the outer limit; FINRA announced 2025-12-04 it is "not moving forward" with 1-minute reporting (Notice 25-17; dossier).
- **Portfolio-trade modifier:** mandatory 2023-05-15 for trades between two parties, ≥10 unique issues, single basket price (Notice 22-12; Rule 6730 Supp. (d)(4)(H)).
- **Affiliate-principal indicator expansion:** SEC approved 2026-07-24; voluntary from 2026-08-31, mandatory 2027-08-02 (Notice 26-16). Rationale: duplicate affiliate prints inflated US HG TRACE volume by up to 8% in June 2026 (MarketAxess Q2-2026 release; dossier).
- **Aggregate allocation reporting:** optional from 2026-06-08 (Notice 25-17).
- **Key fields a decoder must handle (standard Enhanced TRACE layout; general knowledge, CHECK against the current FINRA file spec):** reporting-party side (buy/sell from the reporting dealer's view), contra-party type (customer vs. dealer; ATS indicator), reported price, volume (capped in disseminated feed), trade status/cancel/correction codes, as-of flag, special-price flag, when-issued flag, commission flag, trading-market code (primary vs. secondary), sale-condition codes, and the PT modifier. Classic pitfalls: double-counted interdealer legs in older academic data (Dick-Nielsen cleaning procedure, TITLE-ONLY: Dick-Nielsen 2009 "Liquidity Biases in TRACE," *Journal of Fixed Income*; Dick-Nielsen 2014 "How to Clean Enhanced TRACE Data," SSRN, 403 this session), cancels/corrections applied out of order, agency vs. riskless-principal pairs showing as two prints, and the 2026 affiliate duplicates.

### 6.4 RFQ hit rates and the modern dealer-quoting literature (the 2026 frontier)

- **Barzykin & Ciceri (HSBC), "Bond Market Making with a Hit-Ratio Target," arXiv 2604.20406 (2026).** Per the search summary: reduces the HJB, gives explicit controls through the fill-probability function, quadratic approximation yields a Riccati equation for inventory curvature; the hit-ratio target enters as a constraint whose multiplier subsidizes the quote. CHECK by fetching the abstract (next batch). This is the formal version of "paying to stay at the table" (Ch 3 brief, humor beat 5).
- **"Quality-Adjusted Hit-Ratio Targeting in Corporate Bond Market Making," arXiv 2605.30643 (2026; authors CHECK).** Optimal quotes decompose into "a riskless spread, inventory skew, credit-alpha skew, residual-toxicity charge, and quality-hit-ratio subsidy." This five-term decomposition is the chapter's organizing equation for §18.6 (the RFQ quote = AS spread + GM adverse-selection charge + hit-ratio subsidy + credit alpha).
- **Hendershott & Madhavan (2015):** Table VI facts (from Ch 3 brief; CHECK against the paper): ≈25–28 dealers queried on MarketAxess auctions, 15–29% respond, modal IG response count 3, HY modal 0; clients query fewer dealers for larger tickets (leakage rises with size). Auctions (electronic) vs. search (voice): electronic used for smaller, liquid, more "boring" trades.
- **Fermanian, Guéant & Pu (2017):** dealer quotes become more conservative as the number of requested dealers rises; clients often wait for only ≈2 answers (CHECK numbers in the PDF).

### 6.6 Structural trend: dealer inventories went to zero, then negative (Trend Watch #2 raw material)

- **Coalition Greenwich (late 2025; via search summary, CHECK the exact note):** primary dealers' average net *short* in corporate bonds >5y reached ≈\$9.5bn in September 2025, exceeding their ≈\$7.4bn net long in ≤5y bonds.
- **Bloomberg / Hedgeweek (early 2026; CHECK date):** primary dealers net short US corporate bonds "for the first time in at least 25 years"; Crisil Coalition Greenwich (Fed data) average net short ≈\$4bn so far in 2026 vs. ≈\$16bn average long in 2017. Interpretations: banks cutting exposure at tight valuations vs. demand so strong that dealers pass risk instantly; McPartland: trading is increasingly agency-based so inventories matter less. Source: NY Fed Primary Dealer Statistics (weekly, Thursdays 4:15 p.m. ET; series since Jan 28, 1998 in split structures; VERIFIED page, numbers not on page).
- **Pre-history (general knowledge, CHECK):** dealer corporate inventories peaked near \$235bn in 2007 (old NY Fed series mixed corporates with other debt) and collapsed after 2008; Bessembinder–Spatt–Venkataraman (2020 *JFQA* survey, in the industry dossier): non-bank dealers 12.5% of customer–dealer volume in 2014–16 vs. 2.4% pre-GFC.
- **Why it matters for AS:** when the representative dealer holds $q \approx 0$ (or $q<0$), the inventory term vanishes and the quoted spread is driven by the adverse-selection and hit-ratio terms; "agency" trading means the dealer is running $T-t \to 0$ (immediate hedge/offset), which is the GLFT limit where the optimal spread collapses to $\frac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1+\gamma/k)$-type constants. Kargar et al. (2021) is the stress-case mirror: risky-principal costs spike, flow shifts to agency, customers' willingness to pay for immediacy +≈200 bp.

### 6.7 Jump cases for the JTD-adjusted reservation price (dated, sourced via search summaries; CHECK against primary reports)

### 6.8 Second-pass verifications (fetched 2026-10-17)

- **Barzykin & Ciceri, "Bond Market Making with a Hit-Ratio Target," arXiv 2604.20406 (v1 22 Apr 2026, v2 27 Jul 2026; q-fin.RM). VERIFIED.** OTC bond market making on a *size ladder*; quadratic inventory penalty plus a running target on the size-weighted hit ratio; "dualizing the hit ratio target term" keeps the reduced HJB separable, giving exact controls "through the inverse of the fill-probability function and the Hamiltonian derivative"; quadratic approximation (after Bergault et al.) gives a Riccati equation for inventory curvature; linearized quotes decompose into "riskless spread, inventory-risk correction, and hit-ratio correction"; covers multiple bonds and client tiers.
- **Niang, "Quality-Adjusted Hit-Ratio Targeting in Corporate Bond Market Making," arXiv 2605.30643 (28 May 2026). VERIFIED.** Replaces the raw hit ratio with a residual-quality-adjusted one: post-trade markouts are decomposed into observable credit factors, carry/rolldown, issuer relative value, index/ETF demand effects, and *residual* adverse selection; only the residual counts as toxicity. Under the quadratic approximation, quotes = riskless spread + inventory skew + credit-alpha skew + residual-toxicity charge + quality-hit-ratio subsidy. Synthetic simulations: raw hit-ratio targeting subsidizes toxic flow. No proprietary RFQ data. (This is "IDO or it didn't happen" applied to markouts, now in a 2026 control paper; cite it next to the Ch 3 IDO markout.)
- **Guo, Lehalle & Xu, "Transaction Cost Analytics for Corporate Bonds," arXiv 1903.09140 (v1 21 Mar 2019, v4 8 Dec 2021). VERIFIED.** TRACE Enhanced; estimates trade initiator, bid-ask, mid dynamics; weekly regularized regressions for expected cost; transaction-level transient impact model with a non-parametric kernel for liquid bonds; headline finding: asymmetry of impact between customer-buy and customer-sell orders.
- **Hedgeweek, "Banks turn net short corporate bonds for first time as market structure shifts" (Jul 13, 2026). VERIFIED.** 2026 YTD average net short ≈\$4bn (first in ≥25 years) vs ≈\$16bn average long in 2017; net short ≈\$13.7bn in >5y bonds, long ≈\$9.7bn in shorter paper; source Crisil Coalition Greenwich on Fed data; quoted: Kevin McPartland (Crisil), Sam Berberian (Citadel Securities global head of credit trading: "a more efficient market"), Benjamin Dietrich (Lazard AM: squeeze risk if spreads tighten, since pensions/insurers rarely sell long paper).
