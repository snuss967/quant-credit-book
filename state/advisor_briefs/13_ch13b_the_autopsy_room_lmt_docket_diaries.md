## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

Chapter 13 built the Priming Risk Score and Chapter 14 said recovery is a mixture over coalition membership and the court is a regime variable. Neither chapter ever lays the bodies on the table. This chapter is the morgue: twelve dated, sourced Docket Diaries, each about 300 words, each ending with the same two lines: *which PRS features would have fired, and when* and *what the excluded holder's markout was*. The reader who came from PJT reads a docket as a story with villains. The desk reads it as a time series with timestamps, and the aha is that the two quantities no spread model carries, **which side of the door you sat on** and **which judge read which two words on which day**, explain more of the excluded holder's P&L than seniority, leverage or rating did. A first lien was worth 74, 25 or 95 depending on the door (COURTS §4). Twelve autopsies produce one law of the desk: *the court is a random variable with timestamps* (App E), and one engine: the litigation map as a Markov chain whose transition matrix jumps on ruling dates (the log-odds jumps App C cites).

The pod version, which the RV thread requires: every autopsy names the pair a pod would have run (long the likely participant, short the likely excluded, or long opco, short holdco) and what it isolated. The answer, every time, is that the pair's P&L *is* the markout of the excluded leg; the hedge removes default and sector, and what remains is participation status plus a judge. Owen's line from Ch 13 §13.5 ("My long leg became the short") is the chapter's epigraph.

**§13B.4 The litigation map as a Markov model.** Scene: Kaz at the whiteboard with Mags correcting the states. States: announced, challenged, MTD denied, trial, ruled (+1 or −1), reversed, settled, Chapter 11, and the cooperative absorbing state (TSA/A&E closed: Lumen, Zayo; prepack 99 days: Wolfspeed). Transition probabilities are time-varying: they jump on the ruling dates in the courts table. Use COURTS §1 lags (transaction to regime-setting ruling: 13, 15, 16, 22, 26, 27, 54 months; median 22) and US §0 (deal to filing median about 13.5 months) as the chain's two clocks. State plainly that there are 19 rulings and that jump sizes are elicited priors, not fitted (COURTS §11). The "lesson" is that the ruling usually lands when the issuer is already in or near Chapter 11: courts reallocate the estate, they rarely prevent the filing. Link to §14.7's Hamilton filter rather than rebuild it.
