## 1. Thesis and "aha"

**One-sentence thesis.** A portfolio trade is a one-price, all-or-none auction for a *bundle* of credit risk, and the price a dealer can defend in 90 seconds decomposes into exactly four terms, each of which has its own data source, its own failure mode, and its own chapter in this book:

$$
P^{\text{PT}}_{\text{bid}} \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_i x_i\,\widehat{P}_i}_{\text{fair value (composite)}} \;-\; \underbrace{\sum_i c_i^{\text{liq}}(x_i)}_{\text{per-line liquidation cost}} \;-\; \underbrace{\tfrac12\,\gamma\,\mathbf{x}^\top\Sigma^{\text{res}}_{T}\,\mathbf{x}}_{\text{residual risk after the hedge}} \;-\; \underbrace{\text{AS}(\mathcal{B})}_{\text{the mystery box}}
$$

with $\mathbf{x}$ the signed notional vector of the basket $\mathcal{B}$, $\Sigma^{\text{res}}_T$ the covariance of what is *left* after the dealer's best index hedge over the unwind horizon $T$, $\gamma$ the dealer's balance-sheet risk aversion, and $\text{AS}$ the adverse-selection charge for the lines the client chose to include. Everything in this chapter is a story about one of those four terms, in a regime where they are small and independent (2021, 2024) or large and correlated (March 2020, H1 2022, April 2025, March 2026).

**Aha #1 (for the ex-RSSG reader): the dealer is not pricing 150 bonds. The dealer is pricing one ETF-shaped lump plus a residual, and the residual is where everything you learned at PJT lives.** Meli & Todorova's average IG PT has a 0.85 return correlation with LQD and 60% of its lines are in LQD; the dealer hedges the lump with ETF shares, CDX, iBoxx TRS or credit futures in the same minute (Li 2022; SCOOS 2025: three-fifths of active dealers "frequently hedge… with credit ETFs," half use CDS). What cannot be hedged is the idiosyncratic residual: the 12 off-index lines, the two names with ad hoc groups forming, the \$18mm line in a bond that last printed in August. That residual is priced with a covariance matrix nobody can estimate from data alone (150 bonds, 60 days, mostly non-trades) and with a toxicity judgment that is pure Chapter 13. Pure math PhDs price the lump well; the reader prices the residual well. The chapter's job is to make both parts explicit and put them in one engine.

**Aha #2: the PT premium is the shadow price of all-or-none certainty.** Man Group moved its flow *into* PTs in 2021 and *back out* to RFQs in 2022–23 ("significant savings in transaction costs versus portfolio trades," Man 2023 p.5). Both decisions were right. The buy side pays the PT premium when partial fills would "create unintended and potentially unmodelled risk exposures" (Man 2023 p.4) and declines to pay it when line-by-line RFQ is cheaper and the residual-exposure risk is tolerable. Formally the premium is the Lagrange multiplier on the fill constraint; §19.9 derives it.

**Aha #3: in calm markets the four terms are nearly additive; in stress they become one term.** The empirical anchors are specific: IG LCS went from 0.59% to 3.40% in 2007–09 and its correlation with VIX was 0.90 (Ben_Dor p.112, p.166); PTs "did not have lower transaction costs during March 2020" when flows were one-sided (Meli–Todorova); PT costs rose through H1 2022 as dealers pulled balance sheet and custom ETF baskets vanished (Man 2023 p.3); a net one-fifth of active dealers pulled back from PT in April 2025 (Fed SCOOS). ETF ownership itself raises liquidity commonality in IG (Cotelioglu 2024), so the same plumbing that makes the lump cheap to hedge makes the residual's liquidation costs co-move. The engine must carry a regime multiplier and a rank-one "liquidity spike" in its covariance, not a single calm-weather $\Sigma$.

**Aha #4: "90 seconds" is a data-architecture statement, not a compute statement.** $\mathbf{x}^\top\Sigma\mathbf{x}$ for $N=150$ is 22,500 multiply-adds; a phone does it in microseconds. The 90 seconds (down from "about 30 minutes" in 2019 per The DESK, and "over US\$2bn in an hour" in 2021 per IFR) is consumed by *staleness*: is the composite for line 83 from today or from the last print 11 days ago, is the LCS at the right size, is the factor loading post-downgrade, is the hedge basis quoted now. Everything $O(N^2)$ or $O(\text{universe})$ must be precomputed overnight and refreshed intraday; the live path is lookups, one quadratic form, a 3×3 hedge solve, and a greedy eviction loop. "Pricing a PT in 90 seconds is 89 seconds of precomputation and one second of linear algebra" is the sentence to tape to the monitor.

**Why this chapter matters for trends.** PT went from ~1% of IG volume (2018) to 7% (2021), ~10% of IG / 8% of HY (2024), 18% of the record \$84.7bn day on 2025-09-30, and 20.6% of MarketAxess US credit volume in Q2-2026 (upstream dossier). On the "share of institutional risk trades" denominator it was already ~25% in March 2025 (Bloomberg Market Dialogues). Two-thirds of dealers run PT desks and four-fifths expect to do more (SCOOS). Whoever owns the basket pricing engine owns the marginal price of credit risk transfer in IG, and increasingly in HY (MarketAxess HY PT share hit a record 25.9% in September 2025). This is the chapter where "the execution revolution" (Ch 2) becomes an object the reader can build.

## 3. Derivation skeletons (LaTeX; assumptions, measure, error-prone spots)

Notation follows the style bible: $\gamma$ is risk aversion, $q$ inventory, $\lambda$ is reserved for default intensity (never used for arrival or for Almgren–Chriss urgency here), $D^{\text{spr}}$ spread duration, $\text{DTS}_i = D^{\text{spr}}_i s_i$, $\widehat P_i$ the composite mid in fraction of par, $x_i$ signed notional in dollars (positive = dealer buys). All expectations in this chapter are under $\mathbb{P}$: the dealer is pricing its own P&L over a few days, not replicating a derivative. Say that once, loudly, because ROBO will otherwise discount at the risk-free rate under $\mathbb{Q}$ and call it rigor.

### DER A — The dealer's basket bid as a certainty equivalent (the master equation)

*Setup.* Dealer buys basket $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^N$ at per-unit prices $\mathbf{p}$, unwinds over horizon $T$, receiving random proceeds $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_T$ per unit of par and paying liquidation costs $C^{\text{liq}}(\mathbf{x})$. Terminal P&L
$$
W \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^N x_i\big(\widetilde P_{i,T}-p_i\big)\;-\;C^{\text{liq}}(\mathbf{x}).
$$
*Assumptions.* (i) $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\widetilde P_{i,T}] = \widehat P_i$ (composite is unbiased over days; carry and roll-down are second order at $T\le 10$ days, and the rate leg is spotted and hedged separately); (ii) $\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{\mathbf P}_T) = \Sigma_T$; (iii) exponential utility with absolute risk aversion $\gamma$ (units: $\$^{-1}$), so the certainty equivalent is $\text{CE} = \mathbb{E}[W]-\tfrac12\gamma\operatorname{Var}[W]$, exact under Gaussian $W$ and a second-order approximation otherwise.

*Derivation.* The dealer's reservation basket price is the $\mathbf{p}$ at which $\text{CE}=0$. Writing $P^{\text{PT}} = \sum_i x_i p_i$ for the basket's dollar price:
$$
P^{\text{PT}}_{\text{res}} \;=\; \sum_i x_i\widehat P_i \;-\; C^{\text{liq}}(\mathbf{x}) \;-\; \tfrac12\gamma\,\mathbf{x}^\top\Sigma_T\,\mathbf{x}.
\tag{19.1}
$$
A quoted bid in competition is $P^{\text{PT}}_{\text{bid}} = P^{\text{PT}}_{\text{res}} - \pi$ where $\pi\ge 0$ is the margin; with $n$ dealers in comp the winner is the max bid, so this is a first-price common-value auction and $\pi$ should include winner's-curse shading (cross-reference the BWIC derivation in Ch 18). The adverse-selection term $\text{AS}(\mathcal{B})$ of DER G enters as a downward adjustment to $\widehat P_i$ conditional on inclusion.

*Error-prone spots.* (1) **Units.** If $x_i$ is in dollars of par and $\widehat P_i$ in fraction of par, then $\Sigma_T$ must be the covariance of *price changes in fraction of par* over horizon $T$, and $\gamma$ is per dollar; mixing bp-of-notional costs with price-point costs is the single most common bug. (2) **Horizon scaling.** $\Sigma_T \ne T\,\Sigma_{1d}$ when the unwind is a schedule rather than a hold-to-$T$; the exact variance of a schedule is in DER B. (3) **The measure.** No $\mathbb{Q}$, no discounting at $r$; the only "risk-neutral" object is the composite itself. (4) **Sign discipline.** For a client *buy* (dealer sells), flip the sign of $\mathbf{x}$; the risk term is unchanged (quadratic), the cost term flips to an ask-side half-spread, and the AS term flips direction.

### DER B — Per-line liquidation cost: LCS at size, then the Almgren–Chriss unwind

*LCS as the seed.* From Ben_Dor p.118, for spread-quoted bonds $\text{LCS}_i = D^{\text{spr}}_i\,(s^{\text{ask}}_i - s^{\text{bid}}_i)$ (already a fraction of price) and for price-quoted bonds $\text{LCS}_i = (P^{\text{ask}}_i - P^{\text{bid}}_i)/P^{\text{bid}}_i$; both are *round-trip* costs at a standard size $x^{\text{std}}\approx\$3\text{–}5$mm. The immediate one-way cost of a line is
$$
c_i(x_i) \;=\; \tfrac12\,\text{LCS}_i\;\widehat P_i\;|x_i|\;\Big(\frac{|x_i|}{x^{\text{std}}}\Big)^{\eta},\qquad \eta\in[0.5,1],
\tag{19.2}
$$
where $\eta$ is a size-scaling exponent (calibrate on your own PT prints; equity impact laws give $\eta\approx\tfrac12$ for the impact *component*, Paleologo p.193, but the credit half-spread component is roughly flat in size up to $x^{\text{std}}$ and then steepens). Note the two regimes: for $|x_i| < x^{\text{std}}$ the bracket is $<1$, which is why a 150-line basket averaging \$0.5–1.4mm per line can print *inside* LCS (Meli–Todorova's 41.7% saving is partly this); for a \$20mm line the bracket is $\approx 5^{\eta}$.

*Almgren–Chriss for the residual line.* The dealer unwinds $X=|x_i|$ over $T$ in $n$ slices $v_k$ (shares per unit time), with temporary impact $h(v)=\varepsilon\operatorname{sgn}(v)+\theta_1 v$ and permanent impact $g(v)=\theta_0 v$, and price volatility $\sigma_i$ (fraction of par per $\sqrt{\text{day}}$). Expected cost and variance of a schedule $\{x_k\}$ are
$$
\mathbb{E}[C] = \tfrac12\theta_0 X^2 + \varepsilon X + \Big(\theta_1 - \tfrac12\theta_0\tau\Big)\sum_k \frac{(x_{k-1}-x_k)^2}{\tau},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}[C] = \sigma_i^2\,\tau\sum_k x_k^2 ,
$$
and the mean–variance optimal trajectory is $x_k = X\,\dfrac{\sinh(\kappa (T-t_k))}{\sinh(\kappa T)}$ with urgency $\kappa^2 \approx \gamma\sigma_i^2/\theta_1$. (Cite Almgren & Chriss (2000), *Journal of Risk* 3(2), TITLE-ONLY.) In credit the useful outputs are (a) the *expected* cost, which for a linear schedule reduces to $\varepsilon X + \tfrac12\theta_0X^2 + \theta_1 X^2/T$, and (b) the horizon choice $T$: Li (2022) finds PT bonds sit in inventory 5.3 days longer than voice bonds, because the hedged lump is cheap to hold, so $T\approx 5\text{–}10$ business days for residual lines is empirically grounded.

*Error-prone spots.* (1) **Double counting variance.** $\operatorname{Var}[C]$ above *is* the holding variance of line $i$; if you also put $\sigma_i^2$ in $\Sigma_T$ of (19.1) you have charged it twice. Rule: all variance lives in $\Sigma^{\text{res}}_T$ (DER C), Almgren–Chriss contributes only $\mathbb{E}[C]$ and the choice of $T$. (2) **Duration twice.** LCS for spread-quoted bonds already contains $D^{\text{spr}}$; do not multiply by duration again when converting to price. (3) **Size.** LCS is quoted at $x^{\text{std}}$; applying it unscaled to a \$25mm line understates cost by $5^{\eta}$. (4) **Volume features.** If $\varepsilon,\theta$ are regressed on TRACE volume, deduplicate affiliate prints (up to 8% of HG volume in June 2026) and remove PT prints themselves from the "liquidity" measure, or the basket's own history inflates the liquidity of its lines.

### DER C — The hedge, and why the risk term is on the residual

Let $\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}^N$ be bond price changes and $\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^H$ hedge-instrument returns over $T$ (ETF shares, CDX IG/HY, iBoxx TRS, IBHY/IBIG futures, UST futures), with joint covariance
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{xx} & \Sigma_{xf}\\ \Sigma_{fx} & \Sigma_{ff}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The dealer chooses $\mathbf{h}$ to minimize $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{h}^\top\mathbf{f})$:
$$
\mathbf{h}^\star = \Sigma_{ff}^{-1}\Sigma_{fx}\mathbf{x},\qquad
\Sigma^{\text{res}} = \Sigma_{xx}-\Sigma_{xf}\Sigma_{ff}^{-1}\Sigma_{fx},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}^{\text{res}} = \mathbf{x}^\top\Sigma^{\text{res}}\mathbf{x}.
\tag{19.3}
$$
Substituting into (19.1) gives the master equation with $\Sigma^{\text{res}}_T$ and an added hedge cost $c_h(\mathbf{h}^\star)$ (half-spreads on the hedge instruments, tiny for LQD/CDX, non-trivial for futures blocks). **DTS shortcut.** With a single index hedge and the one-factor model $r_i = \beta_i f + \epsilon_i$, $\beta_i = \text{DTS}_i/\text{DTS}_{\text{idx}}$, we get $h^\star = \sum_i x_i\beta_i$ (a DTS-matched hedge) and $\Sigma^{\text{res}} \approx \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{\epsilon,i}^2)$ plus the factor-model error. This is the one-line reason a dealer quotes a diversified basket *tighter than the sum of its line half-spreads* (Bohn_Stein p.48's "reduce the idiosyncratic impact of inexplicable behaviors of particular securities").

*Error-prone spots.* (1) $\Sigma_{ff}$ estimated from ETF *price* returns contains the premium/discount; in stress the ETF–NAV basis becomes the dominant residual (March 2020: tracking error >200bp vs 0.7bp average, Todorov/BIS in the upstream dossier). Model it as a separate factor whose variance is regime-dependent. (2) The ETF is a creation/redemption instrument only for APs; a non-AP dealer hedging with ETF shares cannot deliver the residue in kind, so its $T$ is longer. (3) Li (2022): only 20–30% of PT bonds are in realized ETF baskets, so the hedge is systematic, not in-kind; do not assume the C/R window absorbs the residual. (4) In HY the correlation of a basket with HYG/JNK is lower and more state-dependent than IG with LQD (Meli–Todorova's 0.85 is an IG number).

### DER D — Residual covariance for 150 stale bonds: factor model plus shrinkage

*Why not the sample covariance.* $N=150$ lines, $T_{\text{obs}}\approx 60$ days, with most lines printing on a minority of days: the sample matrix $S$ has rank $\le 60$, is singular, and its smallest eigenvalues are noise. Any optimizer that touches $S^{-1}$ (the hedge solve, the eviction loop) will find the noise and bet on it.

*Structure.* $\Sigma_{xx} = B\,\Omega\,B^\top + D$ with $B\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times K}$ loadings on $K\approx 10\text{–}30$ factors (rating × sector × duration-bucket excess-return factors, scaled by DTS so loadings are near-unit; Ch 8–9), $\Omega$ the $K\times K$ factor covariance estimated on the *whole* universe (thousands of bonds, so well-conditioned), and $D=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{\epsilon,i}^2)$ idiosyncratic variances from the line's own sparse residuals.

*Shrinkage.* Where a line's own history is too thin, shrink: $\Sigma^{\text{LW}} = (1-\delta)\,S + \delta\,F$ with target $F$ either $\mu I$, $\mu=\operatorname{tr}(S)/N$ (scikit-learn's `LedoitWolf`, VERIFIED-URL), or the structured $B\Omega B^\top + \bar\sigma^2 I$; the optimal intensity has the Ledoit–Wolf form
$$
\delta^\star=\max\Big\{0,\min\Big\{1,\;\frac{\hat\pi-\hat\rho}{\hat\zeta\,T_{\text{obs}}}\Big\}\Big\},
$$
where $\hat\pi$ sums the asymptotic variances of the sample covariances, $\hat\rho$ their covariance with the target, and $\hat\zeta$ the squared misspecification $\|S-F\|_F^2$ (Ledoit & Wolf (2004), *JMVA* 88(2), 365–411; their symbol for misspecification is $\gamma$, renamed here because $\gamma$ is risk aversion). Paleologo p.186 notes the equivalence between linear shrinkage and adding a ridge $\tau^2 I$ to the idiosyncratic block, which is the practical move: $D \leftarrow D + \tau^2 I$ with $\tau^2$ set by the staleness of the line.

*Non-synchronous trading.* Stale prices smooth observed returns and bias $B$ toward zero and $D$ downward. Use summed lagged betas (Dimson/Scholes–Williams; Ch 8) or estimate at weekly frequency, and inflate $\sigma_{\epsilon,i}^2$ by a staleness factor $(1/\phi_i)^{\xi}$ with $\phi_i$ the fraction of days the line printed and $\xi\in[0.5,1]$ (CHECK: calibrate).

*Error-prone spots.* (1) After any manual edit (a trader bumps one line's vol), re-project to PSD by eigenvalue clipping and re-check $\operatorname{cond}(\Sigma)\le 10^6$. (2) A bond downgraded last week still carries last month's IG-sector loading in $B$: refresh loadings on rating-change events, not on a schedule. (3) Kaz's Gaussian: the residual of an LMT-candidate line is a jump, not a diffusion; the AS term of DER G is where that belongs, not an inflated $\sigma_{\epsilon}$.

### DER E — Eviction: the marginal cost of a line and the greedy knapsack

Total cost of carrying the basket:
$$
\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i c_i(x_i) + \tfrac12\gamma\,\mathbf{x}^\top\Sigma^{\text{res}}_T\mathbf{x} + \sum_i x_i\,\text{AS}_i,
\qquad
\text{MC}_i \equiv \frac{\partial\mathcal{C}}{\partial x_i} = c_i'(x_i) + \gamma\big(\Sigma^{\text{res}}_T\mathbf{x}\big)_i + \text{AS}_i .
\tag{19.4}
$$
The dealer's counter-proposal ("clients may get better pricing if they accept suggested portfolio tweaks," IFR 2021) is the solution of: choose an eviction set $\mathcal{E}$ to minimize $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}_{-\mathcal{E}})$ subject to $\sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}}|x_i|\widehat P_i \le \alpha\sum_i|x_i|\widehat P_i$ (keep at least $1-\alpha$ of the notional) and $|\mathcal{E}|\le k$. The exact problem is a knapsack; the 90-second solution is greedy: rank lines by $\text{MC}_i/(|x_i|\widehat P_i)$, evict the top one, **recompute** $\Sigma^{\text{res}}\mathbf{x}$ (the second term couples the lines), repeat until the per-dollar basket cost falls below the target. The buy-side mirror is the *sweetener*: adding liquid, high-ETF-overlap lines lowers the average per-dollar cost because $\mathcal{C}/\|\mathbf{x}\|_1$ is convex in composition, which is exactly Meli–Todorova's "crowd-sourcing" (median 37% liquid bonds even in illiquid PTs; HY needs more sweeteners because its LCS tail is fatter).

*Error-prone spots.* Sorting once instead of looping (the coupling term changes after each eviction); evicting by LCS alone (ignores the covariance and AS terms, which is where the LMT line hides); forgetting that eviction changes the hedge $\mathbf{h}^\star$ too.

### DER F — Stress liquidity commonality: one factor in costs, a rank-one spike in covariance

Model per-line costs with a common multiplier: $c_{i,t} = \bar c_i\,m_t + \epsilon_{i,t}$, $m_t>0$, $\mathbb{E}[m_t]=1$ in calm markets, $\log m_{t+1} = \varphi\log m_t + \xi_{t+1}$ (half-lives of 4–14 weeks in the March 2026 episode give $\varphi$ per week in roughly $[0.84, 0.95]$, upstream dossier; CHECK by market). Basket liquidation cost $L = m\sum_i|x_i|\bar c_i + \sum_i |x_i|\epsilon_i$, so
$$
\operatorname{Var}(L) = \Big(\sum_i |x_i|\bar c_i\Big)^2\sigma_m^2 + \sum_i x_i^2\sigma_{\epsilon,i}^2 ,
$$
and the first term **does not diversify** with $N$. Empirical anchors for $\sigma_m$ and the range of $m$: IG index LCS 0.59% → 3.40% in 2007–09 (≈5.8×), LCS–VIX correlation 0.90 (Ben_Dor p.112, p.166); EUR IG bid-ask +64% vs January in March 2026. In return space the same shock widens the ETF–NAV basis and raises the idiosyncratic variances together, so the stress covariance is
$$
\Sigma^{\text{res}}_{\text{stress}} \;=\; \Sigma^{\text{res}}_{\text{calm}} \;+\; \sigma_m^2\,\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^\top,\qquad g_i = \text{sensitivity of line } i\text{'s price to } m,
$$
a rank-one spike that the eviction loop must see. Cotelioglu (2024) gives the mechanism for why ETF-heavy IG baskets have *more* commonality, not less. Regime trigger: a composite of the ETF discount z-score and the bid-ask percentile (upstream dossier) switches $m$ from its calm to its stress calibration and widens $\gamma$.

*Error-prone spots.* Calibrating $\bar c_i$ on 2023–25 prints (a calm window) and treating $\sigma_m$ as zero; treating the hedge basis and $m$ as independent (they are not: the same one-sided flow drives both, which is why "PTs did not have lower transaction costs during March 2020," Meli–Todorova).

### DER G — Adverse selection in baskets: the mystery box

At the line level, Glosten–Milgrom says the dealer's bid is $\mathbb{E}[V_i \mid \text{client sells } i]$. In a basket the client also chose *which* lines to include, so condition on inclusion: with an informed-inclusion probability $\Pr(I_i\mid \mathbf{z}_i) = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{z}_i)$ (logistic in observable toxicity features $\mathbf{z}_i$: Priming Risk Score from Ch 13, days to the next lender call, Rule 2019 filings/ad hoc group formation, recent RFQ frequency in the name, size relative to the client's usual line) and an expected informed move $J_i$ (fraction of par; for an LMT priming event in HY, tens of points, *illustrative*),
$$
\text{AS}(\mathcal{B}) = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}} |x_i|\,\widehat P_i\,\Pr(I_i\mid \mathbf{z}_i)\,J_i ,
\tag{19.5}
$$
which enters (19.1) as a reduction of $\widehat P_i$ for dealer buys. Li (2022) finds PT-vs-voice cost differentials in IG do not respond to private-information proxies, so $\mathbf{w}$ is small in IG; the HY/LMT tail is where it is not. **The diagnostic is the hand history:** by-line markouts $\text{MO}_i(\tau)=\operatorname{sgn}(x_i)\,(\widehat P_{i,t+\tau}-p_i)$ at $\tau\in\{1,5,20\}$ days; the realized AS of a basket is $\sum_i |x_i|\widehat P_i\,\text{MO}_i(20)$ and its concentration in one or two lines is the "two cards face-down" signature.

*Error-prone spots.* Charging AS to every line (kills the quote) instead of to the flagged tail; using a basket-level markout (it averages away the two lines that matter); forgetting that in competition the winner's curse is itself a basket-level adverse-selection term distinct from (19.5).

### DER H — The buy side's shadow price of all-or-none

Program $\mathbf{x}$; RFQ route fills line $i$ with probability $\phi_i$ at cost $c^{\text{RFQ}}_i$, leaving a random unfilled residual $\mathbf{u}$ with $u_i = (1-\mathbb{1}_i)x_i$; PT route fills everything at $\sum_i c^{\text{RFQ}}_i + \Pi$. Choose the PT iff
$$
\Pi \;\le\; \underbrace{\tfrac12\gamma_B\,\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{u}^\top\Sigma\mathbf{u}]}_{\text{unmodelled exposure}} \;+\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big[\textstyle\sum_i (1-\phi_i)\,c^{\text{retry}}_i\big]}_{\text{re-trade cost}} \;+\; \underbrace{\text{leakage cost of } N \text{ RFQs}}_{\text{Ch 3/18}} ,
$$
so $\Pi$ is the shadow price of the fill constraint. Man's 2021→2022 switch is this inequality flipping: $\Pi$ rose (dealer $\gamma$ up under QT, custom baskets gone) while $\phi_i$ rose (electronic RFQ fill rates and no-touch execution up). The chapter should state the switch as a *calculation*, not a mood.

### 6.3 Academic body numbers (read from PDFs this session; the two load-bearing papers)

**Meli & Todorova (2023-12-21 version), body.** Sample: 12,107 unique IG PTs, c.1 million bond-PT observations, **\$696bn** executed, Oct 2018–2022 (estimation period Jan 2018–Dec 2022). Average PT: **c.100 line items, \$70mm notional, ~equally split across bonds, spanning 12 sectors; average cost 0.84% [LCS]; remaining maturity ~10 years; bonds issued 2.5 years ago.** PT line items are *less liquid* than RFQ trades: volume-weighted LCS **0.84% vs 0.69%** for RFQs; vs the Bloomberg US IG index, 6% less PT volume sits in the two most liquid LCS quintiles, most of it migrating to the 4th quintile. **"Crowd-sourcing": even among illiquid PTs, the median share of liquid bonds is 37%**, i.e., "the more liquid bonds cross-subsidize the less liquid bonds." A maturity-tilt strategy is the most common non-liquidity design (35% of portfolios, mostly long-dated). **ETF overlap: on average 60% of the bonds in IG PTs are owned by LQD, vs LQD owning ~30% of index bonds; >90% of PTs have overlap >30%.** Double sort: very liquid/low-ETF-ownership bonds do ~6% of volume via PT; least liquid/high-ETF-ownership bonds do 15%. **Main result: a line item in a PT is 6.0bp cheaper than the same bond in RFQ form against an average RFQ effective half-spread of 14.5bp, i.e., a 41.7% reduction.** By liquidity: 2.8bp cheaper for liquid bonds (19.3%) and 8bp for illiquid (55.2%). **Hedgeability: average 30-day return correlation of a PT with LQD is 0.85; an interquartile increase in Corr-LQD improves PT execution by 12%; an IQR shift in portfolio ETF ownership gives 15% better execution.** Hedge universe used: LQD, VCIT, VCSH, IGIB, IGSB, USIG, SPSB, SPIB, VCLT, SCHI. Stress: **"initial results indicate that PTs did not have lower transaction costs during March 2020, when volatility spiked due to the Covid-19 outbreak, and flows were one-sided (Meli and Todorova (2022b))."** Also notes delayed spotting: counterparties agree a spread at e.g. 13:30 and price at 15:00 so Treasury hedges can be netted; spotting clusters at 15:00/15:30/16:00. Cited Barclays notes (TITLE-ONLY): Meli & Todorova (2022, July 22), "Portfolio Trading: Part III — In Crisis and Calm," Barclays Research. Also: PTs were "not flagged in the TRACE feed until May, 2023."

**Li, Jessica Shi (Chicago Booth), "Frictional Intermediation, Inventory Hedging, and the Rise of Portfolio Trading in the Corporate Bond Market," version March 23, 2022 (AEA 2023 program paper).** VERIFIED-URL (PDF served from the program page): http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2023/program/paper/9TfsndSa . Abstract: "inventory hedging explains the recent meteoric rise of corporate bond portfolio trading, likely aided by the recent proliferation of credit index derivatives… inventory hedging improves liquidity and transaction costs, and the liquidity benefit is more pronounced when dealers face higher inventory frictions or constraints." Facts: PT defined as ≥30 unique bonds at the exact same time between a dealer and a client; PT reached **>5% of total investor–dealer TRACE volume by June 2021**; PTs with ≥30 line items went from **11 per month (\$284mm) over Mar 2012–Dec 2017 to 68 per month (\$3,954mm) in 2018**. **The overlap between PT bonds and realized ETF-basket bonds is only 20–30%**, and PT does not correlate with C/R activity, so the ETF C/R channel is *not* the driver; instead the hedge is index derivatives (Markit iBoxx standardized TRS from 2015; corporate bond ETF options). **Voice-trade costs respond to dealer inventory shocks; PT costs do not. PT bonds turn over ~50% slower than comparable voice-trade bonds** (dealers hold hedged inventory longer). Cost differentials do *not* respond to private-information proxies (realized equity vol), which argues against adverse selection being the main PT story in IG. Summary stats (verified from her Table 2 text; estimation sample = 820 PTs, Jan–Dec 2018, PT = ≥30 line items): average PT has **112 line items (unique CUSIPs) from 80 issuers; mean notional \$57.9mm; about one-third mix dealer-buy and dealer-sell lines; the majority mix IG and HY; average price 99.94, YTM 5.08%, maturity 10.75y; average portfolio-level transaction cost 8.93bp.** PT bonds "stay in dealer inventories for 5.3 days longer," which is the ~50% slower turnover. → This paper and Meli–Todorova disagree on *mechanism* (ETF C/R outlet vs index-derivative hedge) but agree on *effect* (PT cheaper, more so for illiquid bonds and constrained dealers). The chapter should present both: the hedge collapses systematic risk (Li), and the C/R window is one of several residual outlets (Meli–Todorova; SCOOS).
