## 1. Thesis and the "aha" for the ex-PJT-RSSG reader

**Thesis.** Corporate-bond data is the worst machine-learning dataset in liquid finance, and the correct response is not a bigger model but a *smaller, more honest sample*: fewer effective observations (issuer-grouped, uniqueness-weighted), labels that respect how credit actually pays off (path-dependent, DTS-scaled, censored by calls and exchanges), cross-validation that cannot peek (purged, embargoed, grouped), and a performance statistic that already knows how many things you tried (the deflated Sharpe ratio). The 2019–2026 literature is a live demonstration: a *Journal of Financial Economics* bond-factor paper was retracted in 2023 for one-month label misalignment; a *Review of Financial Studies* ML paper needed a corrigendum for look-ahead; and an April 2026 "factor zoo" of 108 bond signals found that only 26 of 432 specifications (6.0%) survive a false-discovery correction, with short-term reversal's premium falling from −0.99% to −0.09% per month once the same noisy price was stopped from appearing in both the signal and the return denominator. Credit ML in 2026 is, first, a data-integrity discipline.

**The aha.** The RSSG reader already does meta-labeling for a living. A restructuring banker never asks "will this bond go up?"; they ask "given that the thesis is *this covenant trapdoor*, how confident am I, and how much do I bet?" That is exactly López de Prado's primary-model/secondary-model split: the primary model supplies the *side* (the credit-agreement read: J.Crew-style drop-down capacity, Serta-style non-pro-rata uptier risk, a 13-week liquidity runway), and the ML secondary model learns only *when the analyst is right*, outputting a probability that becomes bet size. The pure quant cannot build the primary model because the primary model is legal prose turned into state-space. The banker cannot size it because they have never been made to estimate precision and recall. Chapter 7 is where the two halves meet.

Second aha, smaller and sharper: **the issuer is the observation.** Dynkin et al. report that as of 31 May 2018 the US IG index had 629 issuers and the HY index 784 (p.651). Whatever your bond-month count says, your effective cross-section is closer to a thousand names, each with a dozen correlated children. Forty-seven bonds from one issuer are one data point wearing forty-seven hats. Every method in the chapter is a way to stop counting hats.

## 3. Derivation skeletons

All expectations in this chapter are under $\mathbb{P}$ (we are forecasting realized excess returns and labels). The only $\mathbb{Q}$ objects that enter are *features* (OAS, Z-spread, CDS-implied hazard $\lambda_t$, Merton distance-to-default), and the writer should say so once: features may be risk-neutral quantities; labels and losses are physical.

### 3.1 Fractional differentiation (Hosking 1981; AFML ch. 5)

Backshift $B x_t = x_{t-1}$. For real $d>0$,
$$(1-B)^d = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \binom{d}{k}(-B)^k = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} w_k B^k,\qquad w_0 = 1,\quad w_k = -\,w_{k-1}\,\frac{d-k+1}{k}.$$
So $w_1=-d$, $w_2 = d(d-1)/2$, $w_3 = -d(d-1)(d-2)/6$, and for $0<d<1$ the weights alternate in sign only at $k=1$ and then decay hyperbolically in magnitude, $|w_k|\sim k^{-d-1}/\Gamma(-d)$, which is the "long memory" (Hosking's abstract: persistence that fades "much more slowly" than ARMA). Fixed-width window: keep $k \le K(\tau)$ where $|w_k| \ge \tau$ (e.g. $\tau = 10^{-4}$), producing $\tilde x_t = \sum_{k=0}^{K} w_k x_{t-k}$ with a constant lookback so the transformed series is not non-stationary by construction. Choose $d^\star = \min\{d : \text{ADF}(\tilde x^{(d)}) \text{ rejects at } 5\%\}$ and report the correlation $\rho(\tilde x^{(d^\star)}, x)$ as "memory retained."

*Credit-specific:* apply to $x_t = \log s_t$ (log OAS), not $s_t$, because Ben Dor et al. show relative spread volatility is stable (5–10%/month) while absolute spread volatility scales with the level (p.39). Error-prone spots: (i) the first $K$ observations are NaN, and for a bond issued 14 months ago with $K=60$ you have nothing; use issuer-curve spreads for history; (ii) jumps (defaults, LMT announcements) are not made stationary by any $d$ and will dominate ADF; (iii) stale marks already inject spurious autocorrelation, so a "low $d^\star$" on an illiquid bond is a liquidity artifact (Dynkin p.771 on autocorrelation as staleness).

### 3.2 Triple-barrier labels in DTS space

For bond $i$ at formation time $t$ with spread duration $D^{\text{spr}}_i$ and spread $s_{i,t}$, define cumulative excess return over $u$ trading days $X_{i,t}(u) = \sum_{v=1}^{u} r^{\text{xs}}_{i,t+v}$. Ben Dor et al.: $\sigma(r^{\text{xs}}) \approx \text{DTS}_i\cdot\sigma_{\text{rel}}$ with $\text{DTS}_i = D^{\text{spr}}_i s_i$ (p.50; slope estimates 8.8%–9.8% per month per unit DTS, p.57). Barriers:
$$\bar b_{i,t} = +m\,\text{DTS}_{i,t}\,\hat\sigma_{\text{rel},t}\sqrt{h},\qquad \underline b_{i,t} = -m\,\text{DTS}_{i,t}\,\hat\sigma_{\text{rel},t}\sqrt{h},\qquad \text{vertical at } u = h.$$
Label $y_{i,t} = +1$ if $\bar b$ is touched first, $-1$ if $\underline b$, and $\operatorname{sign}(X_{i,t}(h))$ (or 0) at the vertical barrier. Record $t_{1,i} = $ touch time; the label interval is $[t, t_{1,i}]$ and feeds §3.4–3.5. Asymmetry option: $\underline m < \bar m$ because a CCC bond's downside is a jump, so the lower barrier is a trapdoor, not a wall (Ch 5 mixture distributions). *Censoring:* if the bond exits the sample at $t_e < t_{1,i}$ (call, tender, exchange, maturity), close at the exit price and flag `censored=True`; Ben Dor p.338 handles exits by assigning the peer-group return thereafter, which is a defensible imputation for *portfolio* studies but a leak for *labels* (the exit itself is informative: tenders are above market, distressed exchanges below). Error-prone: detecting a touch on a stale mark that then bounces back (the DRR 2026 "bounce-back filter" exists for a reason); using the same month-end price for the feature and the first return (LIB, §3.6).

### 3.3 Meta-labeling and bet sizing

Primary model gives side $\hat y^{(1)}_{i,t}\in\{-1,+1\}$ (e.g. covenant-capacity score, Merton DD, or the analyst). Define the meta-label $y^{(2)}_{i,t} = \mathbf 1\{\hat y^{(1)}_{i,t}\cdot y_{i,t} > 0\}$. The secondary model estimates $p_{i,t} = \Pr(y^{(2)}_{i,t}=1\mid \mathcal F_t)$. Bet size (López de Prado's form): $z = (p - \tfrac12)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}$, $m = 2\Phi(z)-1 \in (-1,1)$, applied as $\text{size} = m\cdot \hat y^{(1)}$. The objective trade-off is precision vs. recall: the primary model is tuned for high recall (never miss a candidate), the secondary for precision (only bet when confident), and the product $F_1$ is what the desk pays for. Error-prone: training the secondary on the primary's *in-sample* hits (leak); letting the secondary see the primary's output as a feature with a non-stationary scale; class imbalance when the primary is right 70% of the time by carry alone, so "always bet" has 70% accuracy — report $F_1$ and the IDO-stripped PnL ("IDO or it didn't happen").

### 3.4 Uniqueness and the issuer-concurrency correction

Concurrency at time $t$: $c_t = \sum_i \mathbf 1\{t\in[t_{0,i},t_{1,i}]\}$. Average uniqueness of label $i$: $\bar u_i = \frac{1}{t_{1,i}-t_{0,i}+1}\sum_{t=t_{0,i}}^{t_{1,i}} c_t^{-1}$. **Issuer extension:** compute $c^{\text{iss}}_t$ over all bonds of the same ultimate parent, then $\bar u^{\text{iss}}_i$ likewise; final weight $w_i \propto \bar u_i \cdot \bar u^{\text{iss}}_i \cdot \left|\sum_{t} r^{\text{xs}}_{i,t}/c_t\right|$ (return-attribution weighting). The effective sample size $N_{\text{eff}} = (\sum w_i)^2/\sum w_i^2$ is the number the writer should print next to every backtest; for an index of ~800 issuers with 2–3 overlapping quarterly labels per bond and ~3 bonds per issuer, expect $N_{\text{eff}}$ to be an order of magnitude below the bond-month count.

### 3.5 Purged, embargoed, issuer-grouped cross-validation

Let test fold $\mathcal T$ span $[T_0, T_1] = [\min_{i\in\mathcal T} t_{0,i}, \max_{i\in\mathcal T} t_{1,i}]$. **Purge:** drop training observation $j$ if $t_{0,j}\le T_1$ and $t_{1,j}\ge T_0$ (interval overlap). **Embargo:** also drop $j$ with $t_{0,j}\in (T_1, T_1+\eta]$, $\eta\approx 0.01\,T$ (serial correlation of features leaks forward even without label overlap). **Group:** assign folds by ultimate-parent id so no issuer appears on both sides; this removes cross-bond leakage of *issuer-specific* information but not *time* leakage through the common factor, so grouping and purging are both required (a two-way purge). Walk-forward (Bohn–Stein p.467: fit through year $y$, predict $y+1$, roll) is the special case where folds are ordered and the embargo is implicit; combinatorial purged CV generalizes it to many backtest paths. Error-prone: grouping by ticker instead of parent (unrestricted subs, HoldCo/OpCo, LMT SPVs); purging on calendar months while labels are event-timed; forgetting that features computed with trailing windows (e.g. 12-month momentum) extend the label interval backward.

### 3.6 Latent Implementation Bias (correlated errors-in-variables) and Look-Ahead Bias

Observed price $\tilde P_t = P_t(1+\delta_t)$, $\delta_t$ i.i.d. noise (bid–ask bounce, stale mark, dealer-side mix). Past return $\tilde r_t \approx r_t + \delta_t - \delta_{t-1}$; next return $\tilde r_{t+1} \approx r_{t+1} + \delta_{t+1} - \delta_t$. Then $\operatorname{Cov}(\tilde r_t, \tilde r_{t+1}) = \operatorname{Cov}(r_t,r_{t+1}) - \sigma_\delta^2$: pure noise manufactures reversal. Sorting on a price-based signal selects on $\delta_t$ and the same $\delta_t$ sits in the denominator of the next return — a correlated errors-in-variables term that does not attenuate but *inflates* (DRR 2026 §3). Fix: "gap procedures" — signal from the month-end price, return measured from the first feasible execution price next month (month-begin), so the two prices are different draws. **LAB:** winsorizing or trimming returns at percentile thresholds computed from the full sample uses future information; asymmetric thresholds (trimming the left tail) delete defaults and March 2020 from the short leg. Fix: ex-ante thresholds using only data available at formation. The writer should derive the one-line covariance identity above and then quote the 2026 numbers.

### 3.7 PSR, DSR, MinTRL, expected maximum Sharpe, PBO

With $T$ returns, sample Sharpe $\widehat{SR}$ (per period), skew $\hat\gamma_3$, kurtosis $\hat\gamma_4$:
$$\widehat{PSR}(SR^\ast) = \Phi\!\left[\frac{(\widehat{SR}-SR^\ast)\sqrt{T-1}}{\sqrt{1-\hat\gamma_3\widehat{SR}+\frac{\hat\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{SR}^2}}\right].$$
Deflated threshold for $N$ trials with Sharpe variance $V[\widehat{SR}_n]$ across trials:
$$SR^\ast = \sqrt{V[\widehat{SR}_n]}\left[(1-\gamma)\,\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\tfrac1N\right) + \gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne}\right)\right],\qquad \gamma\approx 0.5772,$$
and $\widehat{DSR} = \widehat{PSR}(SR^\ast)$. Minimum track record length: $T^\ast = 1 + \left[1-\hat\gamma_3\widehat{SR}+\frac{\hat\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{SR}^2\right]\left(\frac{Z_\alpha}{\widehat{SR}-SR^\ast}\right)^2$. Effective $N$: cluster the trial-return correlation matrix and count clusters (the "false strategy theorem" bound is in $N$, so inflating $N$ with near-duplicates is cheap and wrong in the other direction). PBO via CSCV: split the $T\times N$ return matrix into $S$ blocks; for each of the $\binom{S}{S/2}$ train/test block combinations pick the in-sample best $n^\ast$, compute its out-of-sample relative rank $\bar\omega_c\in(0,1)$, logit $\lambda_c=\log\frac{\bar\omega_c}{1-\bar\omega_c}$, and $\text{PBO}=\Pr(\lambda_c<0)$. Error-prone: monthly bond returns are negatively skewed and leptokurtic (the $\hat\gamma_3\widehat{SR}$ term is not small); stale marks lower measured vol and raise $\widehat{SR}$ before any deflation; $V[\widehat{SR}_n]$ must be computed across *your* trials, not assumed.

### 3.8 Multiple-testing thresholds to quote

Harvey–Liu–Zhu (RFS 2016): $t>3.0$ for a new factor, catalogued 316 factors; Chordia–Goyal–Saretto (RFS 2020): $t\approx 3.8$ (time-series) and $3.4$ (cross-sectional) from >2 million random strategies, ~45% expected false rejections at conventional cutoffs; DRR 2026: Benjamini–Hochberg FDR as the illustrative benchmark. Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen (JF 2023) is the counterweight: Bayesian hierarchical shrinkage says most *equity* factors replicate. The writer should present both and note that the bond evidence currently sits on the pessimistic side because of data error, not just multiplicity.

## 4. Code spec (reference implementation)

**File/role:** `credit_ml_lab.py` (single python block, numpy/scipy/pandas only, seeded). Computes:

1. **`ffd_weights(d, tau)`** → `np.ndarray[K+1]`; **`frac_diff(x: pd.Series, d, tau)`** → series with fixed-width window; **`min_d(x, grid, alpha=0.05)`** using a hand-rolled Dickey–Fuller regression $\Delta x_t = \alpha + \beta x_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t$ with the $t$-stat compared to an *approximate* 5% critical value (−2.86, label it approximate). Returns $d^\star$ and $\rho(\tilde x, x)$.
2. **Synthetic credit panel** (`simulate_panel(n_issuers=200, bonds_per_issuer~Poisson(3)+1, T=1500 days)`): log-spread paths $\log s_{i,t} = \log s_{j(i),t} + \text{bond noise}$, issuer log-spread = common factor + issuer AR(1) + compensated Poisson jumps-to-default with intensity $\lambda^{\mathbb P}$; staleness: each bond's mark updates with probability $\pi_i\in[0.2,1]$ per day, otherwise carries the last mark (this is the stale-mark model); excess return $r^{\text{xs}} = -D^{\text{spr}}\Delta s + s/252$. Shapes: `spreads[T, N_bonds]`, `issuer_of[N_bonds]`, `dts[T, N_bonds]`.
3. **`triple_barrier(xs_returns, dts, sigma_rel, m, h, exit_mask)`** → `DataFrame` with columns `t0, t1, label, censored, touch∈{upper,lower,vertical,exit}`.
4. **`uniqueness(t0, t1, issuer_of)`** → per-label $\bar u_i$, $\bar u^{\text{iss}}_i$, weights, and $N_{\text{eff}}$.
5. **`PurgedGroupKFold(n_splits, embargo_frac, groups)`** → train/test index generator implementing §3.5. Model: L2 logistic regression fit by `scipy.optimize.minimize` on features `X[N_labels, F]` (no sklearn dependency).
6. **`psr, dsr, min_trl, expected_max_sr, pbo_cscv`** per §3.7.

**Built-in sanity checks (asserted at the bottom of the block):**
- *Leakage detector:* with **pure-noise features** and overlapping labels (h = 60 days), naive K-fold with shuffling produces out-of-sample AUC noticeably > 0.5 (expect ~0.55–0.65 because overlapping labels put near-copies of a test label into training); `PurgedGroupKFold` produces AUC within ±0.03 of 0.5. Assert the ordering `auc_naive > auc_purged` and `|auc_purged − 0.5| < 0.04`.
- *DSR check:* simulate $N=1{,}000$ i.i.d. Gaussian strategies with $T=120$ months, record max $\widehat{SR}$; assert the Monte Carlo mean of the max is within 10% of $\sqrt{V}\,[(1-\gamma)Z_{1-1/N}+\gamma Z_{1-1/(Ne)}]$ and that the selected strategy's DSR < 0.10 while its naive PSR(0) > 0.95.
- *PBO check:* CSCV on the same noise matrix yields PBO within 0.5 ± 0.1.
- *FFD check:* $\sum_k w_k \to 0$ as $K\to\infty$ for $d\in(0,1)$ and $w_k$ matches the recursion against `scipy.special.binom` for $d=0.5$ to 1e-12.
- *Staleness check:* measured daily vol of a bond with $\pi_i=0.2$ is lower than its true vol, and lag-1 autocorrelation is positive (document the direction; do not assert exact magnitudes).

Edge cases to warn ROBO about: labels whose $t_1$ falls after the last date (drop or censor, never fill forward); issuers with a single bond (uniqueness correction is identity); folds with no positive labels (skip and report); kurtosis estimation with $T<30$; $p\in\{0,1\}$ in the bet-size $z$ (clip to $[10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]$).

## 7. Case studies

### 7.1 The one-month lead: Bai–Bali–Wen (2019 → 2023)
Facts: *JFE* 131(3): 619–642 (2019); retraction notice *JFE* 150(3), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2023.103721; found by Dickerson–Mueller–Robotti during replication; two factor return series assigned one month later than they should have been (future returns labeled as current), the liquidity factor misaligned the other way in the final two years; extreme losses removed for some bonds; after correction "no investment advantage at all" (Robotti, WBS 9 Sep 2024); Fama-DFA Prize to DMR; the authors issued an apology. **How the desk would have caught it:** a purged walk-forward on the published factor data cannot catch misalignment inside the published series, but an *implementation audit* can: trade the factor from month-begin prices (DRR's gap procedure) and watch the premium vanish. Lesson for Theo: label timestamps are covenants; read them.

### 7.2 The corrigendum: Bianchi–Büchner–Tamoni (RFS 2021)
Facts: original *RFS* 34(2): 1046–1089, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhaa062; corrigendum with Hoogteijling, *RFS* 34: 1090–1103, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhaa098, which "corrects the original analysis for using information not available when the forecasts were made"; out-of-sample $R^2$ falls, significance survives. **Desk use:** the gold standard for *honest* error handling; shows that look-ahead is a *pipeline* property (macro data revisions, forward-filled vintages), not a modeling one. Pair with Bohn–Stein p.466 n.31.

### 7.3 Short-term reversal, or how a bid–ask bounce became a factor
Facts (DRR 2026): STR premium −0.99%/month (t = −4.46) with month-end prices in both signal and return; −0.09% (t = −0.51) from month-begin returns; >90% of the documented effect is bias; 43–67% of price-based factors show a significant month-end/month-begin gap vs 10–18% of non-price-based factors. Derive the covariance identity (§3.6), then show the trade: on an RFQ desk you cannot buy at the stale month-end print; Ines's hand history is the gap procedure in practice. Tie to Ch 18 (Roll model: bounce as a spread estimator).
