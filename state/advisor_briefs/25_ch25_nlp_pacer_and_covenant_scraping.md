### 6.1 Data infrastructure: EDGAR, EDGAR full-text search, CourtListener/RECAP, PACER, the Rules

**EDGAR APIs (VERIFIED, sec.gov "EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces" page, fetched 2026-10-17).**
- Endpoints, no key needed: `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json` (filing history per filer); `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK##########/us-gaap/<Tag>.json`; `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json`; `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/frames/us-gaap/<Tag>/USD/CY2019Q1I.json`.
- Freshness: submissions JSON updates "throughout the day, in real time, as submissions are disseminated," typical delay under a second; XBRL APIs under a minute; bulk `companyfacts.zip` / `submissions.zip` republished nightly at about 3:00 a.m. ET. data.sec.gov does not support CORS (so: server-side fetch, never from a browser page).
- The CIK must be zero-padded to 10 digits in these URLs; a classic ROBO bug.

**Fair-access rules (VERIFIED, sec.gov Webmaster FAQ "Developers" section).** "our current maximum access rate is 10 requests per second," "carefully monitored to preserve equitable access for all users." Declare your user agent in request headers; the SEC's sample format is `Sample Company Name AdminContact@<sample company domain>.com`. The FAQ does not spell out the penalty; the "Access Denied" FAQ routes you to webmaster@sec.gov with your IP. Spec consequence: a token bucket at ≤10 r/s with a *global* lock across worker processes, exponential backoff on 403, and a User-Agent string that contains a real mailbox.

**EDGAR full-text search API (VERIFIED by live call, 2026-10-17).** `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22unrestricted%20subsidiary%22&forms=8-K&dateRange=custom&startdt=2024-01-01&enddt=2026-10-17` returned JSON with `hits.total.value = 1931` (`relation: "eq"`), 100 hits per page, top-level keys `took, timed_out, _shards, hits, aggregations (entity_filter, sic_filter, biz_states_filter, form_filter), query`. First hits: Vince Holding Corp. 8-K EX-10.3 (filed 2026-08-27), Caesars Entertainment 8-K EX-4.1 (2024-10-17), iHeartMedia 8-K EX-4.9 (2024-12-23). So: 1,931 8-K filings (exhibits included) mention the phrase "unrestricted subsidiary" from Jan 2024 through mid-Oct 2026. This is the chapter's first live demonstration: a phrase-level screen that returns exhibits, not just cover pages, and tags them by exhibit type (EX-4 = indenture family, EX-10 = material contract family). The UI equivalent is `https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/`. Coverage start year for FTS: **CHECK** (believed 2001).

**Which exhibits hold the documents (VERIFIED, 17 CFR 229.601 via LII).** Item 601(b)(4): "All instruments defining the rights of holders of the equity or debt securities being registered," and, for 10-Ks and certain registration forms, long-term debt instruments of the registrant and consolidated subsidiaries; *but* (b)(4)(iii)(A) excuses "Any instrument with respect to long-term debt not being registered if the total amount of securities authorized thereunder" does not exceed "10 percent of the total assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries on a consolidated basis," provided the registrant files an agreement to furnish a copy on request. Item 601(b)(10): "Every contract not made in the ordinary course of business that is material to the registrant"; (b)(10)(iv) lets the registrant "redact specific provisions or terms" that it "customarily and actually treats... as private or confidential" and that are "not material," marking omissions with brackets; (a)(5) lets schedules be omitted if they "do not contain information material to an investment or voting decision," with a list of omitted schedules. **Desk consequence:** the corpus is censored in three ways: small facilities (<10% of assets) may never be filed; private (sponsor-owned, 144A-for-life) issuers file nothing; and filed agreements can have bracketed redactions and missing schedules (which is where the pricing grid and sometimes the "Excluded Subsidiaries" list live). Any backtest on EDGAR agreements is a backtest on the public-filer subsample.

**CourtListener REST API (partly VERIFIED).** `https://www.courtlistener.com/help/api/rest/` now 301-redirects to `https://wiki.free.law/c/courtlistener/help/api/rest/v4/overview` (seen 2026-10-17). v4 details (auth, throttles, RECAP endpoints) are recorded in 6.1b below once fetched. **CHECK** until then.

**RECAP Archive (VERIFIED, free.law/recap).** Free Law Project's RECAP has run "since 2009"; the browser extension "automatically adds your PACER purchases to the archive," and anything already in the archive is served free inside PACER; the page describes "tens of millions of PACER documents" with no as-of date. Coverage of bankruptcy courts is not stated on that page (**CHECK** on the v4 docs; the API's `court` field includes bankruptcy courts in practice, e.g., `txsb`, `nysb`, `deb`).

**PACER pricing (VERIFIED, pacer.uscourts.gov "PACER pricing: how fees work").** "$0.10 per page" for documents, search results and reports; "You won't be charged more than $3 per document"; fees waived if charges are $30 or less in a calendar quarter; "$2.40 per audio file"; "Court opinions are always free." No effective date on the page. Arithmetic for the chapter: a 100,000-page docket (Moyer's WorldCom count, p.387) is at most \$10,000 at the per-page rate and much less under the \$3 cap, which is why a desk can afford to be a RECAP donor rather than a free rider, and why the binding constraint is parsing, not purchasing.

**Rule 2019 (VERIFIED, LII text; amended 1987, 1991, 2011, 2024 [2024 stylistic]).**
- (a) "Disclosable economic interest" = any claim, interest, pledge, lien, option, participation, derivative instrument, or other right that gives an economic interest affected by the value, acquisition or disposition of a claim or interest. To "represent" = take a position before the court or solicit plan votes on another's behalf.
- (b) Groups, committees or entities that represent or consist of multiple creditors/equity holders acting together must file, unless composed entirely of affiliates/insiders of one another; indenture trustees, credit-agreement agents, class representatives and governmental units are excused "solely for that reason" unless the court orders otherwise.
- (c) The verified statement must disclose: the formation of the group (and who prompted it); for each member, name, address, and "the nature and amount of each disclosable economic interest" as of formation or employment; members claiming to represent others must also give the **quarter and year** each interest was acquired unless acquired more than a year before the petition; and a copy of any authorizing instrument.
- (d) A verified **supplemental** statement is required when a disclosed fact has materially changed and the filer takes a position or solicits votes. (e) Sanctions: the court may bar the group from being heard, invalidate its votes/objections, or grant other relief.
- 2011 Committee Note (as summarized on LII): the rule was expanded so disclosure covers any economic interest that could affect legal or strategic positions, naming short positions, credit default swaps and total return swaps; it moved from aggregate to member-by-member disclosure and added acquisition quarter/year. **Quant consequence:** the docket now carries a legally compelled, verified, time-stamped panel of (holder, instrument, amount, acquisition-quarter) tuples for every active ad hoc group, re-issued whenever holdings change materially. That is the Rule 2019 time series. Its known gaps: amounts are often "approximately," face not market value; groups file supplements lazily; CLOs and non-filing holders are invisible; the Northwest Airlines (2007) origin story **CHECK** (not on LII page).

**Rule 3001(e), transferred claims (VERIFIED, LII; restyled Dec 1, 2024, numbering of (e)(1)–(5) unchanged).** Transfer before a proof of claim is filed: only the transferee (or indenture trustee) may file the proof. Transfer *after* a proof is filed, other than for security: "the transferee must file evidence of the transfer," **except for claims based on publicly traded notes, bonds, or debentures**; the clerk mails notice and the transferor "has 21 days after the notice is mailed to file an objection"; absent objection the transferee is substituted. Transfers for security have parallel rules with a statement of terms. **Quant consequence:** every trade claim, vendor claim, lease-rejection claim and bank-loan claim that changes hands after the bar date leaves a docket entry ("Notice of Transfer of Claim"), typically with transferor, transferee and face amount; bond claims do not. So the claims-register transfer feed is a direct, if noisy, time series of distressed-claims buying by named funds, and the bond side of it is dark. Per-claim prices are not disclosed, only faces.

**Shelf anchor for the "then vs. now" box.** Moyer p.286 (2005): PACER "does charge fees... but they are reasonable, and there are usually few practical alternatives"; p.387 n.30: EDGAR free, PACER per page. 2026: RECAP mirrors, the v4 API, and claims agents' free portals (Kroll Restructuring Administration, Epiq, Stretto, Verita; roots **CHECK** in 6.1b).
