## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** A pair is not a view with a hedge stapled on. It is a position with a life: a short leg that has to be found and paid for, a ratio that is a model and drifts, a jump on each side that the ratio does not see, a stop that converts a loss limit into a size, and an explain that pays the pod on the net while judging it on the idio. The chapter follows one pair through that life, in the pod, with the ledger on the wall. By the end the reader can open a pair the way Dov does: thesis sentence with a date in it, the short named with its lender and fee, the ratio chosen and the reason, the size the stop allows, the exit written before the entry.

**The aha for the ex-banker.** At PJT, "short" was a word in a pitch; the pitch book said "the market is short the unsecured" and nobody asked who lent the bonds. In the pod the short is a loan: located in three days, collateralised at 102% in cash marked daily, paid for at the cash rate minus a rebate, recallable on demand, with every coupon owed to the lender (R11 §2). The best RSSG short ideas, distress, downgrade, weak documents, are exactly where the fee goes from 30 bp to 173–245 bp and the lender wants the bonds back for the vote. **The fee is a function of the signal.** That one sentence reorganises everything the reader thought about shorting credit.

**Five beats.** (1) The short leg is a menu and every item has a basis: pick which. (2) Three rulers, one alpha, and the alpha is the size of the ruler error. (3) A pair is two jump processes; DTS is jump-neutral in expectation and nowhere else. (4) The stop turns a loss budget into a size, before conviction gets a vote. (5) The ledger is the product: the platform reads it, the explain feeds it, the exit is written on it.

## 3. Key frameworks and formulas

**The ratio under each ruler** (dollars of short per dollar of long): DV01-neutral $h = D_L / D_S$; DTS-neutral $h = D_L s_L / (D_S s_S)$ (§8.4); empirical $h = \hat\beta\, D_L / D_S$ with $\hat\beta$ from a trailing regression of $\Delta s_L$ on $\Delta s_S$ in relative terms; factor-residual: choose $h$ so that $\sum_f (\beta_{Lf} - h\,\beta_{Sf})\sigma_f = 0$ across the model's factors, which is DTS when the model has one factor and relative spread changes. The shrinkage blend $h^\star = \tfrac12(h_{\text{DTS}} + h_{\beta})$ cut the ratio's standard deviation from 0.7 to 0.2 in §8.4; cite, do not re-derive.

**Pair P&L in one line:** $\Delta P = -D_L N_L \Delta s_L + h\, D_S N_S \Delta s_S + \text{carry} - \text{fee} - \text{financing} - \text{cost} + J$, where $J$ is the jump term, $J = -N_L(P_L - R_L)\,\mathbf 1_{\tau_L \le t} + N_S (P_S - R_S)\,\mathbf 1_{\tau_S \le t}$, with $\mathbb E[J] \approx (\lambda_S N_S (P_S - R_S) - \lambda_L N_L (P_L - R_L))\,t$ and $\lambda \approx s/(1-R)$.

**Size under the stop:** $N_{\max} = \text{budget} / \text{downside in points}$; budget is the distance to the next line on allocated capital; downside is $P - R$ for a long, $100 - P$ (or the auction's $1 - $ price) for a CDS short, and the net of the two recovery-weighted legs for a capital-structure pair. Under borrow: $N_{\max}^{\text{borrow}} = $ the lender's inventory times the pod's share limit.

**Capture ratio:** $\kappa = \text{net} / \text{idio}$, with idio computed on start-of-day betas and leak $= $ market $+$ rates $+$ basis lines, friction $=$ fee $+$ financing $+$ cost.

**Residual correlation test (T1):** regress each pair's daily idio P&L on the others'; a mean pairwise correlation above the model's own noise floor says the book has a factor it has not named (R9).

## 5. Where practitioners and LLM coders go wrong

Practitioners: hedging a pair on face or DV01 and calling the market P&L "idio"; using a 24-week beta that is near zero and oversizing the short; leaving a pair alone after the view pays so it becomes a market short (1-B's lesson, by pointer); shorting the bond they most want to be short without a recall plan; hedging a near-default long with a different issuer. Coders: computing the market line with ex post betas (hides the hedge error); letting the ratio go negative or infinite when the trailing beta crosses zero; summing two legs' half-spreads once; treating the CDS short's cost as the running spread without the upfront and the basis; returning a size when no lender exists.
