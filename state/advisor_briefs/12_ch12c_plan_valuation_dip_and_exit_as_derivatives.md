## 1. Thesis and the "aha"

**Thesis.** Between the LMT (Ch 13–14) and the effective date (Ch 16) the book currently has a black box. Inside it, three things happen that a desk can model: (i) **plan value is set** by a negotiation with a judge as tie-breaker, so $V$ is a random variable whose distribution the disclosure statement partly publishes; (ii) **the attachment points move** while the case runs (DIP priming, roll-ups, adequate protection, administrative burn, post-petition interest), so "the fulcrum" is a probability, $\mathbb{P}(A_j\le V<D_j)$, not a class; and (iii) **§1129 is a constraint set**, not a ceremony: best interests is a floor under every class's recovery, feasibility is the 13-week model's older sibling, and cram-down fixes the price of a take-back note. The DIP and the exit note are therefore the book's own thesis (an LMT is the difference of two waterfalls, §13.3) applied to the in-court transaction: *an uptier with a judge's signature.*

**The aha for the ex-RSSG reader.** You already built the inputs. The disclosure statement's three exhibits map one-to-one onto the desk's three objects: the **13-week model** is the runway covariate and the feasibility test; the **liquidation analysis** is the published, auditable floor of the recovery distribution (§5.5's Beta has a hard left edge, and it is free data); the **recovery waterfall** run under a distribution of $V$ with moving attachment points is `plan_value_engine.py`. The desk's version of your old job is your old job with a probability measure attached.

**Five beats.** (1) Every class is a call spread on $V$. (2) The DIP shifts every strike up by its size and the roll-up transfers recovery inside the tranche. (3) The judge picks a point from two experts' distributions, and confirmed values sit below trading values six months later when the fulcrum controls the process. (4) Best interests, feasibility, the cash wall and cram-down are flags and bounds in the same engine. (5) Hertz's two-bid auction is what a market test looks like; Envision's AmSurg sale eighteen months after emergence is what the regularity looks like.

## 3. Key derivations and frameworks

**Classes as call spreads.** With attachment $A_j$, detachment $D_j=A_j+F_j$ and plan value $V$,
$$R_j(V)=\frac{\min\big(\max(V-A_j,0),\,F_j\big)}{F_j}=\frac{(V-A_j)^+-(V-D_j)^+}{F_j},$$
a long call at $A_j$ and a short call at $D_j$ scaled by face. Under $f_V$, $\mathbb{E}[R_j]=\frac1{F_j}\int_{A_j}^{D_j}(v-A_j)f_V(v)\,dv+\mathbb{P}(V\ge D_j)$ and $\mathbb{P}(\text{fulcrum}=j)=\int_{A_j}^{D_j}f_V(v)\,dv$. A DIP of size $N$ shifts every $A_j$ and $D_j$ above it by $N$; adequate-protection and admin claims do the same with a stochastic $N$.

**The plan-value distribution.** $f_V=\pi\,\text{LN}(\mu_1,\sigma_1)+(1-\pi)\,\text{LN}(\mu_2,\sigma_2)$ with the experts' midpoints as means, their ranges as vols, and $\pi$ the judge's propensity (prior from 13-B's courts table; the Hertz auction is the case $\pi$ is irrelevant because the market set it). State the regularity as a shift: $\mathbb{E}[V^{\text{trade}}_{+6m}]>V^{\text{conf}}$ when the fulcrum controls the process (Moyer p.355/369; Envision–Ascension as a dated instance).

**The roll-up transfer (generalizes 12-B §12B.5).** Tranche face $F$, value available $W$ at plan value, new money $N$, roll-up ratio $k$ (so $kN$ of prepetition paper is promoted to super-priority and paid first). Without the roll-up $R=W/F$; with it $R'=(W-kN)/(F-kN)$ for $W\ge kN$, and
$$R-R'=\frac{kN\,(F-W)}{F\,(F-kN)}.$$
Check against 12-B: $k=1$, $N=150$, $F=1{,}400$, $W=910$ gives $150\times490/(1{,}400\times1{,}250)=4.2$ points. The group's blended recovery rises because it holds the promoted paper; the transfer is from the non-participating holders of the same tranche, which is §13.3's uptier arithmetic with §364 in place of §9.02.

**The take-back note transfer.** For face $F_{\text{tb}}$, modified duration $D$ and a plan coupon set $\Delta s$ below the market spread for that risk, the value transferred from the crammed class to the fulcrum is $\approx F_{\text{tb}}\,D\,\Delta s$ (first order; use 4-B's price–yield for the exact number). \$500mm, $D=4$, 300 bp → ≈\$60mm. The §1129(b)(2)(A) rate fight is a fight over $\Delta s$.

**Best interests as a floor.** For every dissenting holder in class $j$, $R_j^{\text{plan}}\ge R_j^{\text{liq}}$; in the engine the recovery distribution is truncated at $R_j^{\text{liq}}$ with the probability mass that would have fallen below it reported separately as "plan fails (a)(7)". This is the left edge the Ch 5 Beta never had.

**Admin burn as theta.** Equity $E=C(V;K)$ with $K=\sum_j F_j+\text{Adm}(T)$, $\text{Adm}(T)=\text{Adm}_0+aT$. Then $\partial E/\partial T$ carries $-a\,\partial C/\partial K<0$ in addition to the usual time value; in Hertz the fee run-rate was a nine-figure number (verify against §16.6 before quoting).

**Credit bid and stalking horse.** A credit bid at face $F_s$ makes the secured class's reservation price $F_s$ regardless of market; a stalking-horse package (break-up fee $b$, expense cap $e$, overbid increment $\delta$) is a call the estate writes on the auction outcome with premium $b+e$ and the increment as a minimum tick.

**The §382 limit.** Annual limit $L=\rho_{\text{LT}}\times E^{\text{conf}}$; $\text{PV(NOL)}=\sum_t \tau\min(L,\text{NOL}_t)\,(1+r)^{-t}$ under (l)(6), versus unlimited use under (l)(5) with its own continuity conditions; in or out of court changes which branch applies.
