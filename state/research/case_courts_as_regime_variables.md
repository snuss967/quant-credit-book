### LOG-A Serta (5th Cir.) -- primary: fetched PDF of opinion (rev. 2/14/2025 header, filed 12/31/2024), text via pdftotext
- 5th Cir. No. 23-20181 c/w 23-20363, 23-20450, 23-20451; panel Haynes, Willett, Oldham (Oldham, J., author); filed 31 Dec 2024, "REVISED 2/14/2025" (Doc 283). Appeals from Bankr. S.D. Tex. (adversary 4:23-AP-9001, BK 4:23-BK-90020).
- 2016 Refinancing: $1.95bn first-lien and $450mm second-lien syndicated loans. First Lien Term Loan Agreement s.2.18 pro rata sharing; s.9.02(b)(A) requires unanimous consent of each affected lender to alter pro rata sharing; s.9.05(g): assignment to Affiliated Lender (includes Top Borrower) on non-pro-rata basis (A) through Dutch Auctions open to all Lenders or (B) "through open market purchases". "Open market purchase" undefined, contrast with meticulous Dutch auction definition ("deafening silence").
- 2020 Uptier: Prevailing (PTL) Lenders provided $200mm new money for $200mm first-out super-priority; traded ~$1.2bn of 1L and 2L loans for ~ $875mm second-out super-priority debt; held bare majority of 1L; labeled it "Open Market Purchase and Cashless Exchange Agreement"; SSB indemnified Prevailing Lenders (payable within 30 days of demand).
- Chronology: SSB Ch.11 filed Jan 2023 (S.D. Tex.); 24 Jan 2023 SSB + some PTL lenders filed adversary seeking declaration; Bankr. court granted partial SJ: "open market purchase" "clear and unambiguous", uptier valid; certified direct appeal under 28 USC 158(d)(2). Plan s.8.5 indemnity; final second amended plan 23 May 2023 (indemnity covers Class 3 and Class 4 holders as of effective date 29 Jun 2023); bankr court found "settlement indemnity" fair under 1123(b)(3); confirmed (2023 WL 3855820).
- Holding: "an open market purchase is a purchase of corporate debt that occurs on the secondary market for syndicated loans" -> 2020 Uptier "not a permissible open market purchase"; REVERSED 23-20181; 23-20450 VACATED in part and REMANDED for reconsideration of Excluded Lenders' breach counterclaims ("strong case" of breach). Plan indemnity violated Code (1123(a)(4), 502(e)(1)(B) / Jevic reasoning; 1123(b)(3)(A) no escape hatch); equitable mootness did not bar. Court "decline[d] to delimit the exact scope of s.1123(a)(4)".
- Lender names appellees: Barings, Boston Management and Research, Credit Suisse Asset Management, Eaton Vance, Invesco Senior Secured Management; appellants Excluded Lenders; LCM Lenders; Citadel Equity Fund (indemnity appeal).

### LOG-C Mitel + Serta details (Paul Weiss memo 10 Jan 2025, fetched PDF; PW was counsel to Excluded Lenders in Serta -- disclose)
- Serta 2020: participating lenders (bare majority of 1L and 2L) gave $200mm new-money first-out and exchanged existing 1L and 2L loans into second-out at a 26% (1L) and 61% (2L) discount to par. Citations: Excluded Lenders v. Serta Simmons Bedding, No. 23-20181, 2024 WL 5250365 (5th Cir. Dec. 31, 2024). Bankruptcy court reasoning: because different creditor groups had negotiated with the company, uptier was product of the "open market"; 5th Cir. rejected "free competition for restructuring proposals" reading. 5th Cir. said "open market purchase" exceptions in other docs "will often not justify an uptier" but "every contract should be taken on its own" (*24).
- Mitel: Ocean Trails CLO VII v. MLN Topco Ltd., No. 2024-00169, 2024 WL 5248898 (N.Y. App. Div. 1st Dept Dec. 31, 2024). Uptier Oct 2022; participants (majority of 1L and 2L) gave ~ $156mm new-money superpriority first-out; exchanged 1L into second-out at 5% discount to par and 2L into third-out at 18% discount. Trial court (NY Sup Ct, Commercial Div) had dismissed implied covenant, tortious interference, fraudulent transfer but declined to dismiss breach claims ("purchases" ambiguous). App Div: "purchases" exception unambiguous (no "open market" qualifier; nothing says price cannot be paid in new loans); subordination only indirectly affected excluded loans -> no sacred-right violation ("directly" affected); tortious interference barred by economic-interest defense (decidable at pleading stage); implied covenant barred by express provisions (Wachtell). Mitel filed prepack Ch.11 Mar 2025; plan confirmed Apr 2025 (Mayer Brown 30 May 2025). Sources: PW memo; Wachtell via CLS Blue Sky 6 Jan 2025; Mayer Brown 30 May 2025.
- Mayer Brown (May 2025): participating lenders in Serta sought reconsideration / certification to NY Court of Appeals; 5th Cir. denied both; Bankruptcy trial then tentatively Feb 2026 (actual: March 2026 per MB Jul 2026).
### LOG-D Purdue (SCOTUS) -- primary: fetched slip opinion PDF supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-124_8nk0.pdf
- Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P., No. 23-124, argued 4 Dec 2023, decided 27 Jun 2024; 5-4; Gorsuch, J. for Court (Thomas, Alito, Barrett, Jackson); Kavanaugh dissent (Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan). Reversed 69 F.4th 45 (2d Cir.). Holding: Bankruptcy Code does not authorize a release and injunction that, as part of a Ch.11 plan, "effectively seeks to discharge claims against a nondebtor without the consent of affected claimants" (s.1123(b)(6) catchall read by ejusdem generis). Narrow: does not call into question consensual third-party releases; no view on what qualifies as consensual; does not address plans fully satisfying claims; does not address unwinding substantially consummated plans.
- Facts: ~247,000 US opioid overdose deaths 1999-2019; Sacklers withdrew ~ $11bn (~75% of Purdue's assets) over a decade ("milking program"); proposed to return ~ $4.3bn to estate in exchange for release; Purdue filed Ch.11 in 2019; district court had vacated; 2d Cir. divided panel reversed.
