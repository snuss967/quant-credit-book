# Case Autopsy: Courts as Regime Variables

*Dossier slug: `courts_as_regime_variables`. Researcher: Sonnet 5.5. Started 2026-10-18. Feeds Ch13-B (Autopsy Room), Ch14 (Default and Recovery Cycle), Ch16 (Post-Reorg Equities and Claims), App C (Trend Tracker).*

*Integrity: no CUSIPs invented; tranches described descriptively; every figure either sourced (URL in Sources) or marked approximate/illustrative.*

## 0. Scope and one-paragraph thesis

Between mid-2024 and mid-2026, courts stopped being background legal risk and became a dated, tokenized state variable. A ruling lands on a known day, turns on identifiable contract words, and moves the recovery of one group of holders, usually the excluded ones, for every document that carries the same words. This dossier covers 19 rulings (Table 2) and the litigation they spawned. Five findings.

1. **Tokens beat economics.** On 31 Dec 2024 the Fifth Circuit (*Serta*) and New York's First Department (*Mitel*) ruled opposite ways on similar uptiers. The contracts differed by two words.
2. **Remedy moved from rescission to damages**, but damages can be large: the *Serta* remand bill is about $403mm with interest (section 5).
3. **Rulings arrive late.** The median lag from transaction to the ruling that set the regime is 22 months (range 13 to 54), about when the issuer is in or near Chapter 11 (Moody's: over 70% of eventual hard defaults come within 24 months of a soft event). Courts reallocate the estate; they rarely prevent the filing.
4. **Markets adapt in form, not frequency.** Uptier components were still 12 of 17 LMTs in Q1 2025, after *Serta*.
5. **The frontier moved** to antitrust attacks on cooperation agreements and to third-party releases after *Purdue*.

Boundary: Revlon, Envision, Incora, Robertshaw, Lumen, Trinseo and Saks fact patterns live in `case_lmt_autopsies_us_2020_2026.md`; Pluralsight, Zayo, Altice France and Atos in `case_lmt_private_and_european_2024_2026.md`. Direction code: +1 favours excluded or non-consenting holders, -1 the majority, debtor or releasees, 0 mixed. CHECK marks a secondary or single-source fact. Disclosure: Paul Weiss, whose memos are cited, represented the Excluded Lenders in *Serta*.

## 6. Legal and structural mechanics (the contract token each ruling turned on)

1. **"Open market purchase" (undefined) against a defined Dutch auction.** *Serta* s.9.05(g) allowed non-pro-rata affiliate purchases by Dutch auction open to all lenders or "open market purchases". The Fifth Circuit held an open market purchase "occurs on the secondary market for syndicated loans", read the "deafening silence" against the meticulous auction definition, and applied the surplusage canon: a broader reading would swallow the auction exception. It added that such exceptions "will often not justify an uptier" but each contract is read on its own.
2. **Bare "purchase" plus "directly".** *Mitel* permitted "purchases" at any time with no "open market" qualifier, and required consent only from lenders "directly" adversely affected; subordination was indirect. The implied covenant could not add what the express terms excluded.
3. **"Have the effect of releasing" plus a manufactured vote.** *Wesco*: majority consent could issue pari passu notes; two-thirds was needed for any amendment with the effect of releasing substantially all collateral. $250mm of new 2026 notes took the majority past two-thirds. Isgur J: a "domino agreement". Crane CJ: every step complied; unlike the *Serta* loan, the 2026 Indenture needs only majority consent for new notes; rescission would be "plainly inequitable".
4. **Register-based Required Lender.** *Robertshaw*: control passed to whoever held over 50% on the agent's register; the debtor's breach yielded damages, not restored control.
5. **Equal treatment (s.1123(a)(4)) and a market test.** *Serta* struck the participants' plan indemnity; *ConvergeOne* applied *Serta* and *LaSalle*: an exclusive, un-market-tested backstop is unequal treatment, and "could have proposed an alternative" was "illusory at best".
6. **Textual amendment against economic effect.** *Lions Gate*: amending the "form of Notes Guarantee" survives; deleting events of default did not "effectively" amend enforcement rights; the principal change sat in a separate exchange agreement. *STG*: the credit agreement text itself was changed, so *Mitel* was distinguished and three contracts were read as one.
7. **The remedy clause.** *Serta* s.2.18(c): a lender receiving a disproportionate payment must "purchase (for Cash at face value) participations" so benefits are shared ratably. Non-cash exchanges count; damages run on face, not market value; ratification, unclean hands and failure to mitigate all failed.
8. **Consent to releases.** *Purdue* leaves consensual releases open; the live split is opt-in against opt-out, hardest for zero-recovery classes (*Container Store*). *Boy Scouts* shows the escape hatch: a release tied to a sale is shielded by s.363(m).
9. **UK Part 26A.** Cross-class cram-down needs a "relevant alternative" (here special administration) and a fair split of "benefits preserved or generated by the restructuring"; out-of-the-money classes cannot be ignored under a rigid rule.
10. **Antitrust.** Sherman Act s.1 (per se or rule of reason) and the Donnelly Act against co-ops. Defences: antitrust injury, market definition, foreign transaction, and *Sharon Steel* and *United Airlines* (creditor coordination to enforce existing debt is not an antitrust concern). The counter is *Catalano* (blocking a debtor's access to new credit).
11. **Excision over unwinding.** Courts prefer striking the offending clause (*Serta* indemnity) or a "discrete payment and sale of certain equities" (*ConvergeOne*) to unwinding consummated plans.

## 9. Post-Purdue: three 2025-26 plans restructured

*Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, No. 23-124 (argued 4 Dec 2023, decided 27 Jun 2024, 5-4, Gorsuch J): the Code does not authorize a plan that "effectively seeks to discharge claims against a nondebtor without the consent of affected claimants". It leaves consensual releases, the meaning of consent, and plans paying claims in full open. The Sacklers had withdrawn about $11bn and offered about $4.3bn.

The three plans re-cut:

1. **Purdue.** Judge Lane confirmed the revised plan on 18 Nov 2025: Sacklers up to $7bn over 15 years (about $7.4bn all-in per Fierce, snippet), about $850mm to individuals, releases only for those who opt in; non-participants keep the right to sue. Over 99% voting support (Purdue, snippet). Successor: Knoa Pharma.
2. **GOL.** Judge Cote (S.D.N.Y., 1 Dec 2025, 675 B.R. 125) reversed confirmation: the chance to opt out does not make nondebtor releases consensual. The debtor appealed (Second Circuit brief due 22 Apr 2026). The re-cut terms are not in the sources found.
3. **Container Store.** Judge Rosenthal (S.D. Tex., 676 B.R. 356) upheld opt-out consent in principle but held zero-recovery classes 5 (subordinated claims) and 8 (equity) could not be bound, and narrowed a worldwide gatekeeping injunction under *Highland II*, 132 F.4th 353 (5th Cir. 2025). The U.S. Trustee appealed on 10 Apr 2026 (Fifth Circuit No. 26-20166).

Counterweights: *Boy Scouts* (3d Cir., 13 May 2025) left a release plan standing via s.363(m), with Judge Krause writing that it would be "unconfirmable" if proposed today; *Diocese of Buffalo* (27 Feb 2026) required opt-in and certified a direct Second Circuit appeal on 3 Apr 2026; *Pat McGrath Cosmetics* (Bankr. S.D. Fla., Apr 2026) and *Spirit Airlines* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) approved opt-out releases (CHECK, snippets). A split is forming (S.D.N.Y. and W.D.N.Y. against S.D. Tex.); the Second and Fifth Circuit rulings are the next regime dates. For credit, the stake is zero-recovery unsecured and equity classes, whose release now needs affirmative consent in some courts.

## 11. What a systematic desk could have observed beforehand, and how it would flag and trade it

**Observable before the ruling.**

1. **Document tokens.** In June 2020 the *Serta* text was public: an undefined "open market purchase" beside a defined Dutch auction. *Mitel* had a bare "purchase" and a "directly" qualifier; *Wesco* allowed majority-issued pari passu notes beside a two-thirds "effect of" test; *Lions Gate* had a sacred-rights clause with no section cross-reference. All are parseable (Ch 25). The Fifth Circuit itself noted there were "doubtless still many contracts" with the exception. CreditSights found 43.4% of loans (44% of PE-backed) still carried the majority-subordination "Serta loophole" through Q1 2025.
2. **Holder structure.** A fragmented CLO-heavy base with no co-op (*Serta*); a minority able to assemble one-third (the *Wesco* blocking race, 2026s from 85 to par); a co-op at up to 99% (*Optimum*).
3. **The docket calendar.** *Serta* was argued 10 Jul 2024 and decided 31 Dec 2024 (5.7 months). The cert petition ran from filing (18 Jun 2025) to denial (10 Nov 2025) on a public conference schedule, three days after the 7 Nov conference. At the June 2024 closings the *Wesco* judge said he believed "with a high degree of certainty" the $250mm was unauthorized, two weeks before ruling. *ConvergeOne*'s Oct 2024 mootness denial named the remedy 11 months before the merits.
4. **Venue and bench.** The Houston chief judge's Nov 2023 exit, the Fifth Circuit panel, the Commercial Division justice, each district judge's prior mootness record.
5. **The remedy clause.** *Serta* s.2.18(c) fixed damages on face value, so the exclusion claim was priceable at signing.

**How it would flag.** Add a court-regime term to the Chapter 13 Priming Risk Score: per document, the wording classes present, each class's log-odds of surviving challenge, `logit theta_w(t) = alpha_w + sum_r J(r,w) 1{t >= t_r}`, and a venue weight. Feed Table 2 into the Chapter 14 Hamilton filter as the jump dates of its time-varying transition matrix. Jumps are priors elicited from readers of the opinion, not fitted on 19 rows.

**How it would trade.**

- **Before the deal.** Underweight exposed-token paper in majority-friendly venues when a sponsor-plus-lender group is forming; the exposure is the 50-to-80-point coalition gap in section 4, not the lien label.
- **Excluded-claim carry.** After *Serta* the excluded claim is a litigation asset: 25 at closing, later about 45 points of award with interest (win state, before collection risk and a 4-6 year wait). Buy only when P(win) times the discounted award exceeds carry. All four *Serta* defences failed, and Law360's lesson is that holding and preserving claims can beat selling at a loss.
- **Sibling repricing on ruling day.** A ruling shocks every document with the same token. Measure inside-minus-outside spreads over t-1 to t+5, as Ch 14 proposes. *Lions Gate* warns that approval can be priced before the hearing.
- **Model the substitute.** After *Serta* the market moved to structures that avoid the contested words (amend-extend-exchange, drop-downs, co-ops); uptier components were still 12 of 17 in Q1 2025.
- **Binary events** (Optimum and Selecta dismissal rulings, the Second and Fifth Circuit opt-out appeals, *Wesco* and *ConvergeOne* in the Fifth Circuit) go into the regime calendar as dated jumps.

Limits: no TRACE or loan-price series was available, so every markout is a design, not a result; only litigated cases are observed; and with a 22-month median lag the position is usually already in or near Chapter 11 when the ruling lands.

## 12. Which model assumptions broke

1. **Covenant packages are exchangeable across names.** Two words (open market, directly) decided outcomes that deal economics could not separate.
2. **Legal risk is idiosyncratic noise.** It is a common factor with a timestamp: one opinion moves every document with the token.
3. **Seniority predicts recovery.** A first lien was worth 74, 25 or 95 depending on the door; recovery is a mixture over coalition membership (Ch 14).
4. **Rescission restores the priced claim.** *Wesco* (district) and *Robertshaw* turned restoration into damages against a bankrupt borrower, an unsecured recovery; *Wesco* also refused to strip the pari passu status of the $250mm.
5. **A legal win is a credit win.** *Mitel* won on the law and filed ten weeks later; legality and survival are close to independent.
6. **A ruling cuts the behaviour.** Uptier components were 12 of 17 LMTs in Q1 2025; the form changed.
7. **Transition probabilities are stationary.** Persistence shifts at ruling dates (Ch 14.7.1, point 4).
8. **Courts are exogenous.** Forum is a decision: Houston's 2023 judicial exit, S.D. Tex. against S.D.N.Y. on releases, and sale structures that invoke s.363(m).
9. **The opinion is the information event.** Lions Gate notes fell 4 points between 17 and 22 Apr 2025 as the exchange agreement surfaced, and were 5 points higher by approval day.

## 13. What the RSSG banker saw that the quant missed

The restructuring banker reads a credit as a negotiation with a court at the end of it.

1. **He read the sentence, not the score.** An undefined "open market purchase" beside a carefully defined Dutch auction was visible to anyone who read s.9.05(g). The quant had a covenant-quality number.
2. **He counted votes, not prices.** *Wesco* turned on a one-third blocking position and $250mm of new notes that carried a majority over two-thirds in a day. Moyer's holdout section (shelf p.85, printed p.68) frames this as a prisoner's dilemma; the banker knew the threshold, who held it, and what could manufacture it.
3. **He asked which court and which remedy.** Rescission, damages or excision; equitable mootness or s.363(m); S.D. Tex., S.D.N.Y. or the First Department.
4. **He built the record the court would need.** Lions Gate had a solvency opinion (Houlihan Lokey), fairness opinions (Kroll, Houlihan Lokey) and a nearly three-year process before the BC judge, and won there on that record.
5. **He knew a coalition is one counterparty.** At up to 99% of the debt, Optimum negotiates with a steering committee, and the antitrust suit is a negotiating move (Cleary expects a deal before the claims are resolved).
6. **He knew the runway.** $156mm of new money bought Mitel about two and a half years; the legal victory did not change that clock.
7. **He knew the fee stack.** Thames's super-senior money carried a 3% discount (GBP45mm), a 3.5% backstop (GBP52.5mm) and about GBP116mm of early-bird fees: roughly GBP214mm up front on GBP1.5bn (14%), plus a GBP156mm make-whole. A model of "9.75% senior secured" never sees it.
8. **He timed the filing around the calendar.** STG filed nine days after an adverse pleading-stage ruling, which stayed the majority's appeal.

What he missed in turn: the courts stopped cooperating with the playbook. *Serta* (indemnity struck, about $403mm of damages), *ConvergeOne* (backstop reversed) and *Purdue* (releases) each hit a structure that looked legal to its designers. A token map and a ruling calendar would have scored those structures as fragile without knowing the designers' intent.
