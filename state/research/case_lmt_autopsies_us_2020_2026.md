## 1. Revlon BrandCo (2020-2023): the template, and the Citi wire

**Instruments.** The 2016 Term Loan was a seven-year, $1.8bn syndicated loan (Citibank agent, maturity 7 Sep 2023) [2dCir]. The 7 May 2020 BrandCo credit agreement (Jefferies agent; lenders were holders of the 2016 loan) provided a $815.0mm new-money term loan, a $950mm roll-up commitment letting 2016 lenders swap at par into 2025 paper, and a $3.0mm junior roll-up; another $65.0mm was drawn on 28 May and $846.0mm of 2016 loans were rolled up. Elizabeth Arden and other brand IP was moved to BrandCo subsidiaries that did not guarantee the 2016 loan. Revlon delivered a solvency certificate. [Rev10K; 2dCir] At 31 Dec 2020 total debt was $3,434.5mm: BrandCo term loans $1,868.4mm, 2016 term loan $883.9mm, 6.25% notes $431.3mm [Rev10K]. In Nov 2020 $236mm of 5.75% 2021 notes were exchanged for $50mm of FILO and $75mm of BrandCo second-lien loans [Rev10K].

**Mechanics.** Collateral that secured all 2016 lenders was re-pledged to a subset who received priority. Excluded 2016 lenders authorized UMB Bank to sue on 12 Aug 2020 (117 pages: siphoned collateral, manipulated votes, insolvency "by as much as $1.71 billion"); the complaint was never served and was dismissed without prejudice on 6 Nov 2020 [2dCir; Rev10K]. In Aug 2020 the 2016 loan traded at "20 to 30" cents [2dCir].

**The Citi wire.** On 11 Aug 2020 Citibank meant to pay interest to all 2016 lenders (to ease a roll-up for five Angelo Gordon holders) and route about $894mm of principal to an internal wash account. In Flexcube, three reviewers ("six-eye" control, Wipro staff in the chain) set only the PRINCIPAL field, not FRONT and FUND, and the default sent the principal out by wire; a screen warning showed no amount. The defendants' funds held $558.6mm of principal and interest; Revlon's 10-K says holders of about $504mm did not return it. [2dCir; Rev10K] Judge Furman (S.D.N.Y. No. 20-cv-06539-JMF) held on 16 Feb 2021 that discharge-for-value (Banque Worms) let them keep it. The Second Circuit (No. 21-487, 8 Sep 2022, Leval, Sack, Park) vacated: a full early repayment three years before maturity, from a borrower the market priced at 20-30 cents, one day before the lenders' own suit, put them on inquiry notice, and they were not owed payment then. Rehearing was denied 12 Oct 2022 and the money was returned; dismissal with prejudice 19 Jan 2023 [search summary of Revlon FY2022 10-K, CHECK].

**Players (public record).** Revlon and its holding company; Citibank (agent) and Jefferies (BrandCo agent); Angelo Gordon (roll-up holders); Ares (named with Citi and Jefferies in the UMB complaint); defendants Brigade, HPS, Symphony, Bardin Hill and others (loan managers); UMB Bank; Judges Furman and Jones. [2dCir; Rev10K]

**Outcome.** Chapter 11 on 15 Jun 2022 (S.D.N.Y. Bankr., Judge David Jones). An RSA of 19 Dec 2022 was amended 21 Feb 2023 to add 2016 lenders holding over 94%. Plan confirmed 3 Apr 2023: equity and a $670mm rights offering 82% to BrandCo B-2 lenders and 18% to 2016 and B-3 lenders; a $56mm cash pool for non-electing 2016 and B-3 lenders; $44mm for accepting unsecured creditors; B-1 lenders took take-back loans; the 2016 litigation was "conclusively and consensually" resolved after the debtors and BrandCo lenders won a preliminary ruling. Debt cut about $2.7bn, about $1.5bn left [DP-Revlon; Reuters via Business Recorder, search summary, CHECK].

## 2. Envision Healthcare (2022-2025): drop-down, then uptier

**Background.** KKR bought Envision in 2018 (reported $9.9-10bn, about $6.5bn of debt, 7.5x) [HCD; PariPassu preview]. PJT and Kirkland were hired late 2021 [PariPassu, CHECK].

**Structure at filing** (AI-assisted summary, verify in the Kroll first-day declaration [ElevenEnv]). AmSurg silo, about $3.2bn funded: $301mm revolver, $1,358mm first-lien loan (Apr 2027), $1,494mm second-lien loan (Apr 2028), plus about $1.8bn of intercompany loans. Envision physician silo: $5.45bn term loan in four "outs", $938.9mm of unsecured notes, ABL up to $550mm. Total debt about $7.7bn.

**Timeline.**
- Apr 2022 drop-down: a new AmSurg first-lien facility of about $1.3bn, about $1.1bn of proceeds paid up to Envision, and AmSurg designated an unrestricted subsidiary; about 83% of ambulatory-surgery EBITDA moved [ElevenEnv; Octus-Jul25].
- Jun-Aug 2022 uptier: AmSurg raised about $1.3bn of second lien, lent it to Envision, which repurchased about $1.903bn of debt at a discount (about $582mm captured). Participants were prioritized; others went to "fourth-out". Octus puts participation at about 91%, with 9% left behind [ElevenEnv; Octus-Jul25, 91/9 CHECK]. S&P took Envision to SD, then CCC in May 2022 [S&P search result, CHECK].
- Prices: first-lien term loan 73 in March 2022; by Nov 2022 AmSurg debt about 98 and Envision first lien about 25 [CEPR via search summary; advocacy source, CHECK].
- 15 May 2023: Chapter 11 in S.D. Tex. (No. 23-90342, Judge Lopez), no DIP, two silos. 31 Jul 2023: Vibrant/Crescent adversary on unanimous-consent grounds (dismissed with prejudice 9 Nov 2023). 15 Sep 2023: independent director Begeman found about $1.7bn of quantified benefit and no fraud. UCC settled: $35mm cash and 1.5% of Envision equity for noteholders [ElevenEnv; Octus-Jul25].
- Players: KKR (sponsor), PJT and Kirkland (company advisors), Vibrant/Crescent (dissenting lenders), independent director Gary Begeman, Judge Christopher Lopez, later Ascension. [ElevenEnv]
- Plan confirmed 11 Oct 2023, effective 3 Nov 2023. AmSurg: revolver and first lien paid in full; second-lien holders took 100% of equity, with a $300mm rights offering at a 30% discount, projected recovery 73%. Envision: second-out lenders took the equity; unsecured notes warrants only. Debt cut over 70%. 17 Jun 2025: Ascension agreed to buy AmSurg for a reported $3.9bn, about $250mm above the top of the plan valuation; Octus estimates over 1.3x for the drop-down lenders [Octus-Jul25; Becker's, search result].

## 3. Boardriders (Aug 2020 deal, Sep 2023 end): an uptier that was paid at par

**Instruments.** April 2018 credit agreement; about $450mm of first-lien debt held by Oaktree (sponsor), participating lenders and non-consenting lenders. In August 2020 the company raised $110mm of new super-priority money and rolled up $321mm of loans at par into equal super-priority debt ($431mm). Majority amendments removed the debt covenant and the requirement that any intercreditor be junior or pari passu; the roll-up used "open market purchase" agreements. Non-consenting lenders held about $85mm and went last. Participants' loans traded at a 40-50% discount [Goodwin-BR; Skadden; SGB]. S&P: CCC from SD in late Sep 2020, still "tantamount to default" [SGB].

**Litigation.** ICG, York and others sued in Oct 2020 (No. 655175/2020). On 17 Oct 2022 Justice Masley denied dismissal of the breach and implied-covenant claims (the "open market" exception was ambiguous; the no-action clause did not bar suit) and dismissed tortious interference against Oaktree [Goodwin-BR]. Boardriders appealed in Nov 2022. In Aug 2023 the parties announced a settlement tied to Authentic Brands' purchase, repaying non-participants in full; dismissal with prejudice in Sep 2023; Authentic closed about 1 Sep 2023 at an undisclosed price (Moody's: $1.25bn; Bloomberg: about $1.3bn) [Skadden; FashionDive and FashionUnited, search results, CHECK]. Players: Oaktree (sponsor and lender), ICG Global Loan Fund 1 DAC and York Capital (plaintiffs), Justice Andrea Masley, Authentic Brands (buyer). TriMark (Sep 2020: $120mm new money, $307.5mm roll-up; settled Jan 2022 at par into the super-priority tranche) is the same pattern; compare Serta, where non-participants received 0.6-2.4% [Skadden].

## 4. Incora / Wesco Aircraft (2022-2025): "have the effect of"

**Instruments** (Wesco Aircraft Holdings; sponsor Platinum Equity). For the 2020 Pattonair deal Wesco raised over $2bn: $650mm 8.5% senior secured notes due 2024, $900mm 9% senior secured notes due 2026, $525mm 13.125% unsecured notes due 2027. Majority consent could add pari passu notes; 66 2/3% was needed for any amendment having "the effect of" releasing substantially all collateral [DCt].

**Timeline.**
- Late 2021: liquidity crisis; UK auditors wanted a $150-200mm injection or the UK entity faced strike-off. PIMCO and Silver Point (the largest 2024 and 2026 holders) proposed rescue financing in December 2021. [DCt]
- Feb 2022: a minority group announced it held more than one-third of the 2026 notes, blocking the majority's planned two-thirds. It offered about $147.5mm, then $173mm; the board chose the majority's $250mm. [DCt]
- 28 Mar 2022, one day: (1) majority-consented Third Supplemental Indentures allowed $250mm of additional 2026 notes (supporters held over two-thirds of the 2024s, over 59% of the 2026s, over half the 2027s); (2) $250mm wired and new notes issued, lifting the group above two-thirds of the 2026s; (3) Fourth Supplemental Indentures stripped the 2024 and 2026 liens; (4) participants exchanged into super-senior 1L notes (about $1.27bn per a secondary source, CHECK) and 2027 holders into 1.25 lien notes. Non-participants sat behind over $1.7bn of new debt [DCt; Substack, CHECK]. Milbank gave an opinion that it complied. The 2026 notes ran from 85 to above par in the scramble for blocking positions [Substack, secondary].
- Oct 2022: state-court suit. 1 Jun 2023: Chapter 11 (S.D. Tex. No. 4:23-bk-90611), same-day adversary 4:23-ap-3091. Trial 35 days, Jan-Jul 2024. [DCt]
- 10 Jul 2024 oral ruling (Judge Isgur): no breach of the 2024 indenture; breach of the 2026 indenture, because the third amendment, a "domino agreement", needed two-thirds of the 2026s; liens on all 2026 notes survive; relief denied for 2024 holders; 2027 indenture also breached. The pre-ruling plan had given participants 96.5% of equity [Oct-10Jul24; Oct-26Jun24].
- 27 Dec 2024: consensual plan confirmed, premised on the rulings, appellate rights preserved; the participants appealed confirmation on 10 Jan 2025 (No. 25-cv-161, held in abeyance). 17 Jan 2025: bankruptcy court report and recommendation on the non-core contract claims: 2026 liens restored, the $250mm only an unsecured claim [DCt].
- 31 Jan 2025 emergence: secured noteholders 98.4% of equity plus $420mm take-back notes, unsecured about 1.6% and $7.5mm, $300mm DIP exchanged into exit notes plus $100mm new money [DP-Incora; Milbank].
- Players: Platinum Equity (sponsor); PIMCO and Silver Point (majority group, rescue financiers); BlackRock and JPMorgan (minority group, per the court); Carlyle (largest unsecured holder, per a secondary source); Quinn Emanuel (debtor), Kobre & Kim (2024/2026 holders), Milbank (company counsel on the opinion); Judges Isgur and Crane. [DCt; Oct-26Jun24; Substack]
- 8 Dec 2025: Chief Judge Crane sustained the participants, dismissed the contract claims, refused "implied sacred rights", noted single-day closing "is not unusual", and said the remedy would in any case be damages [DCt]. Octus called the court's reliance on Serta (a loan with no unanimous-consent term) "curious"; the minority noteholders gave notice of appeal on about 6 Jan 2026 (Law360 7 Jan 2026, search result, CHECK); no Fifth Circuit briefing schedule or ruling found as of 18 Oct 2026 (CHECK docket) [Oct-Chain; MayerBrown-Wesco].

## 5. Robertshaw (2023-2024): the control flip

**Instruments** [Rob-Conf]. One Rock bought Robertshaw in 2018 with $510mm first-lien loans, $110mm second-lien loans and about $260mm equity, plus an ABL. On 9 May 2023 a New York-law Super-Priority Credit Agreement gave $95mm first-out new money and exchanged first and second lien into second-out and third-out loans, with an ad hoc group (Bain, Canyon, Eaton Vance) and Invesco. "Required Lender" meant more than 50% of first-out plus second-out loans, read from the agent's register. Prepetition funded debt about $833mm [Octus search summary, CHECK].

**Players.** One Rock (sponsor); Bain Capital Credit, Canyon and Eaton Vance (ad hoc group); Invesco; Brigade (an outside financing Invesco rejected); Judge Lopez. **Timeline** [Rob-Conf unless noted].
- Jul 2023: Invesco quietly bought over 50% of first-out plus second-out loans (through a majority of the first-out loans but not of the second-out), becoming Required Lender. Autumn 2023: missed interest; Amendments 1-4 (Oct-Nov) extended the grace period, gave $17mm of bridge money, set a Chapter 11 deadline and an Invesco-chosen independent director; Invesco directed the agent not to post them. The ad hoc group learned about 15 Nov.
- 11 Dec 2023: the ad hoc group and One Rock lent $228.3mm to RS Funding, a vehicle the parent voted and Robertshaw economically owned; the cash repaid the $30mm ABL and prepaid $117.6mm of first-out loans (plus $30.7mm make-whole). Invesco fell below 50%; the ad hoc group became Required Lender and signed Amendment 5 for $228mm of new debt. Invesco got over $90mm of prepayment.
- Late Dec 2023: Invesco sued in New York. 15 Feb 2024: Chapter 11 (S.D. Tex. No. 24-90052, Judge Lopez).
- 20 Jun 2024 (662 B.R. 300): lenders were Required Lenders, Amendment 5 valid, no lender breach, no tortious interference; Robertshaw itself breached by not sending 100% of net proceeds to RS Funding. Remedy: a money claim [PW-Rob]. Invesco claimed about $40mm; the debtors said about 3% recovery.
- Jun 2024 sale by credit bid of about $286mm ($217mm of super-priority claims plus interest and about $65mm of DIP) to the ad hoc group and One Rock, no rival bid. 16 Aug 2024 liquidating plan confirmed over Invesco: $11mm unsecured pool, $10mm deficiency pool; a district court interim stay of the sale (12 Aug) is noted.

## 6. Lumen (Oct 2023 to Mar 2024): the cooperation agreement as the deal

## 10. What a systematic desk could have observed beforehand, and how it would flag and trade

1. **Vote arithmetic from documents.** Incora's group held 59% of $900mm of 2026s; reaching two-thirds by issuing pari passu notes needs x with (531+x)/(900+x) at least 2/3, so x of about $207mm, below the $250mm raised (my arithmetic from [DCt]). A "dilutable-threshold" feature: majority-consent debt-add plus supermajority lien release without an "effect of" blocker. A stake just above one-third is erased by about $18mm of extra notes (900 x (3 x 0.34 - 1); illustration). Flag; underweight the non-coalition tranche.
2. **Blocking-position price runs.** Incora's 2026 notes rose 85 to par; abnormal volume on a distressed secured tranche is a signal of a vote scramble. Flag; do not chase unless you can reach a stake.
3. **State variables on the register.** Robertshaw: Invesco's majority rested on first-out loans alone and could be undone by a borrower prepayment. Track Required Lender share per tranche; flag fragility (majority in a prepayable tranche).
4. **Capacity for asset moves.** Envision (unrestricted-sub designation), Trinseo (JV into unrestricted subsidiary via manufactured basket), Revlon (IP to BrandCo): features from the basket triangle (investment, restricted payment, unrestricted-sub designation) in Ch 25-style parsing.
5. **Liquidity clock and documents.** Auditor deadlines (Incora), grace periods (Robertshaw), rising ABL availability covenants and account-control rights (Saks), coupon dates (Saks: $121mm end-June 2025). Flag within a coupon cycle.
6. **Ratings tells.** SD then CCC (Boardriders, Envision, Saks SD).
7. **Within-issuer wedge.** Lumen's ten-point TSA wedge; after a co-op announcement the long-coalition, short-non-coalition pair captures the wedge, but wall-crossed holders are restricted and price discovery is poor [CS-Lumen]. Without membership you only see the short leg.
8. **Time to refile.** Median about 13.5 months for six filers; schedule the re-default hazard, not a stationary rate.

Trade design: underweight or short the non-controlling tranche at flag; buy excluded paper only when a sale or litigation catalyst is priced (Boardriders par, Lumen about 99); treat rulings as dated jumps (Isgur Jul 2024 up, Crane Dec 2025 down, Robertshaw damages-only).

## 11. Which model assumptions broke

1. **Seniority sets recovery.** Seniority was amended: Incora's non-participating 2026 holders went from first lien to unsecured behind over $1.7bn of new debt in a day.
2. **Default is a state.** Exchanges are defaults to S&P (SD) yet the issuer survives; Lumen and Boardriders never filed.
3. **Recovery independent of identity.** Recovery was coalition-contingent (Serta 0.6-2.4% versus 100%; Trinseo 2-9% versus 60-78%).
4. **Stationary legal rules.** Courts moved: Isgur 2024, Crane 2025, Robertshaw's money-only remedy; Fifth Circuit appeal and Trinseo ruling pending.
5. **Price is a function of seniority and spread.** Within one issuer prices fork by membership (Lumen, Envision 98 versus 25).
6. **A tranche is a homogeneous class.** Votes can be diluted (Incora) or flipped by prepayment (Robertshaw).
7. **Hazard is smooth and slow.** Six filers refiled 9 to 33 months after the first deal (median about 13.5), four inside 24 months; a stationary annual rate misses the clustering after the final step.
8. **Operations are not a risk.** The Citi wire: a control failed on a default checkbox, $894mm wired; ownership of about $504mm turned on inquiry-notice law.
9. **Collateral equals what was pledged.** IP and JV stakes moved to unrestricted subsidiaries (Revlon, Trinseo, Envision).

## 12. What the RSSG banker saw that the quant missed

The banker reads the cap table as a voting table. At Incora the question was not leverage, it was who held 59%, who held one-third, and which clause counted new notes in the vote; the Milbank opinion and the board record show the structure was designed around it. The banker watches the clock: a UK auditor's strike-off, a 30-day grace period, a coupon, a borrowing-base covenant. The banker knows the counterparty's incentives (a sponsor's option value; a lender whose amendments pushed the borrower toward a Chapter 11 sale, as Invesco's Amendment 4 did at Robertshaw), who needs whom (the majority's "power position"), and that PIMCO and Silver Point were also the rescue financiers. The banker reads the register, amendment postings and notice mechanics (Robertshaw's unposted amendments). And the banker knows an LME is a negotiated sale of rights: Lumen's co-op with a backstop fee and Saks's pre-funded priority were priced offers to join. The quant saw spreads, ratings and a lagging default flag; it missed that the instrument's rights were a state variable under negotiation.
