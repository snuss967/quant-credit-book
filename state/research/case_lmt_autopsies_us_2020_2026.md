## 1. Revlon BrandCo (2020-2022): the template and the Citi $900mm wire

**Instruments and dollar amounts (FY2020 10-K, Revlon Inc., filed 2021; sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/887921/000088792121000003/rev-20201231.htm, fetched this session).**
- 2016 Term Loan (Term Credit Agreement of 7 Sep 2016, Citibank N.A. as administrative agent; maturity Sep 2023): $883.9mm still outstanding at 31 Dec 2020 after the roll-up.
- 7 May 2020 "2020 BrandCo Credit Agreement" (Jefferies Finance LLC as administrative and collateral agent; lenders were holders of, or affiliated with holders of, the 2016 Term Loan): (i) $815.0mm new-money senior secured term loan (the "2020 BrandCo Facility"), (ii) $950mm roll-up commitment into which 2016 lenders could swap, at par, (iii) $3.0mm junior roll-up. An extra $65.0mm borrowed on 28 May 2020 repaid the 2020 incremental facility, taking the BrandCo Facility to $910.6mm including $29.1mm PIK closing fees. $846.0mm of 2016 Term Loan was rolled up (remaining capacity $107.0mm). Proceeds repaid about $200mm of the 2019 term loan. Maturity 30 Jun 2025 with springing maturity ahead of the Aug 2024 6.25% notes. Debt issuance costs about $119.3mm. [S: FY2020 10-K, Note 7]
- Collateral and guarantors: the facilities were guaranteed by new "BrandCo" subsidiaries to which Elizabeth Arden, American Crew and other brand IP was transferred; those BrandCo subsidiaries did not guarantee the 2016 Term Loan, although all 2016 guarantors guaranteed the BrandCo debt. [S: FY2020 10-K (summarized from search result), CHECK exact brand list against the 10-K text]
- Nov 2020 exchange offer: $236mm of the 5.75% Senior Notes due 2021 tendered; Revlon issued $50mm ABL FILO term loans and $75mm "New BrandCo Second-Lien Term Loans"; none of the 5.75% notes remained outstanding. [S: FY2020 10-K, Item 1A]
- Total indebtedness at 31 Dec 2020: $3,434.5mm, of which $1,868.4mm 2020 BrandCo Term Loan Facility, $883.9mm 2016 Term Loan, $431.3mm 6.25% Senior Notes, $188.9mm revolver/FILO, $59.2mm foreign ABL, $2.8mm other. [S: FY2020 10-K, Item 1A]
- UMB Bank (as successor agent / holder representative for the 2016 lenders) filed a never-served complaint in S.D.N.Y. against Revlon, Citibank, Jefferies and Ares in 2020 alleging breach of the 2016 credit agreement and fraudulent transfer; dismissed without prejudice on 6 Nov 2020. [S: FY2020 10-K, Legal Proceedings] (A later 2016-lender suit in the S.D.N.Y. bankruptcy court is the litigation settled in the plan.)

**The Citi wire (primary: 2d Cir. opinion, Citibank, N.A. v. Brigade Capital Management, LP, No. 21-487, decided 8 Sep 2022, Leval, Sack, Park JJ.; text fetched from courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/21-487_complete_opn.pdf).**
- Background: the 2016 loan was a seven-year, $1.8bn syndicated term loan maturing 7 Sep 2023 (acceleration risk if the 2021 notes were outstanding on 16 Nov 2020), Citibank the administrative agent. In May 2020 Revlon used the BrandCo structure: $815mm new financing, with $953mm of roll-up rights letting a subset of 2016 lenders swap into 2025 loans secured by the collateral (Elizabeth Arden IP and other trademarks) that had secured the 2016 loan; "over $800 million" was new financing; Revlon delivered a solvency certificate. Holders excluded from the deal, later authorized UMB Bank to sue (complaint filed 12 Aug 2020, 2:06 p.m., 117 pages, alleging the deal "siphoned" the collateral and that Revlon was insolvent by as much as $1.71bn). [S: 2d Cir. opinion, Background A-B]
- 11 Aug 2020: Revlon wanted to pay accrued interest to all lenders to facilitate a roll-up for five Angelo Gordon-managed holders. Citibank's loan operations (Wipro staff in India working in Citibank's "six-eye" maker/checker/approver process on the Flexcube system) meant to send interest and route about $894mm of principal to an internal wash account, but ticked only the PRINCIPAL field, not FRONT and FUND, so Flexcube wired the principal out too. Defendants' clients held principal and accrued interest of $558,558,375.74; the opinion describes the mistaken payment in the aggregate as "approximately $500 million" at issue. [S: 2d Cir. opinion, Background D] The 10-K says holders of about $504mm did not return the money. [S: Revlon FY2020 10-K]
- Price datum: "loan participations were trading at 20% to 30% of the face amount" (Aug 2020), and the Second Circuit used that as a "red flag" (third red flag: if Revlon wished to retire the loan early it could have bought participations at 20-30 cents rather than paying par). [S: 2d Cir. opinion] This is the one court-recorded price point for the 2016 term loan.
- Holdings: Furman, J. (S.D.N.Y., No. 20-cv-06539-JMF, 520 F. Supp. 3d 395), 16 Feb 2021, held after a bench trial that New York's discharge-for-value rule (Banque Worms) let the lenders keep the money. The Second Circuit vacated and remanded on 8 Sep 2022: the lenders were on inquiry notice (an unexpected full-principal repayment three years before maturity, from a borrower the market priced at 20-30 cents, one day before the lenders' own lawsuit against Revlon) and were not entitled to repayment at the time. Park, J., concurred separately. [S: 2d Cir. opinion]
- The incident is a pure operational-risk and legal-mechanics datum for Ch 27: a human-in-the-loop "six-eye" control failed on a default checkbox; the exposure was about $894mm out of a $1.8bn tranche and the price of the asset fell from par to 20-30 cents because of the LMT, not the wire.

**Chapter 11 and outcome.** Revlon filed Chapter 11 on 15 Jun 2022 (S.D.N.Y. Bankr., Judge David Jones); an RSA was signed 19 Dec 2022 and amended and restated 21 Feb 2023 to bring in 2016 lenders holding over 94% of that facility's claims; the plan was confirmed 3 Apr 2023. Reorganized equity and rights-offering participation: 82% to BrandCo B-2 lenders, 18% to 2016 lenders and BrandCo B-3 lenders; $670mm equity rights offering; 2016/B-3 non-electing lenders shared $56mm cash; unsecured creditors who voted yes shared a $44mm cash pool or warrants; BrandCo B-1 lenders received take-back loans; the 2016-lender litigation over the 2020 BrandCo deal was "conclusively and consensually" resolved after the debtors and BrandCo lenders won a preliminary ruling. [S: Davis Polk, "Revlon chapter 11 plan confirmed"; Reuters via Business Recorder: debt cut about $2.7bn, about $1.5bn left outstanding]

## 2. Envision Healthcare (2022): the KKR drop-down plus uptier

**Background.** KKR took Envision private in 2018 in a deal described as $9.9-10bn using about $6.5bn of debt and $3.5bn of equity (about 7.5x leverage) [S: Pari Passu, "Envision Disaster Deep Dive", 6 Oct 2023 (paywalled preview); Healthcare Dive 7 Nov 2023]. Pandemic volume loss, a UnitedHealthcare network exit in 2021 (denials from 18% to 48% on one account) and the No Surprises Act (2022) pressured cash flow. PJT and Kirkland were hired in late 2021, which Pari Passu dates as the start of the LMEs. [S: Pari Passu preview, CHECK]

**Instruments at filing (secondary, AI-assisted summary of the docket; verify against Kroll first-day declaration).** AmSurg silo, about $3.2bn funded: $301mm revolver (Jul 2026), $1,358mm first-lien term loan (Apr 2027), $1,494mm second-lien term loan (Apr 2028), about $1.8bn of intercompany secured loans to Envision. Envision physician-services silo: ABL up to $550mm; $5.45bn term loan in four tranches (first-out, second-out, third-out, fourth-out); about $938.9mm of unsecured senior notes. Total prepetition debt cited as $7.7bn. [S: elevenflo.com/blog/envision-healthcare-dual-silo-chapter-11 (secondary, self-described as AI-assisted); market-scope / Reuters-style summaries for $7.7bn, CHECK]

**Timeline.**
- Apr 2022 drop-down: AmSurg entered a new first-lien credit agreement with about $1.3bn of commitments, and distributed about $1.1bn of proceeds up to Envision; AmSurg Holdco and subsidiaries were designated Unrestricted Subsidiaries. About 83% of the ambulatory surgery business (by EBITDA; the remaining 17% stayed as restricted entities) went to AmSurg. [S: elevenflo; Octus, "Court Opinion Review: July 2025"]
- May 2022: S&P moved Envision from SD to CCC after the exchange, i.e. the rating agencies counted the exchange as a distressed exchange default. [S: S&P RatingsDirect research update 16 May 2022, URL in search results, not fetched; CHECK]
- Jun-Aug 2022 uptier: AmSurg raised about $1.3bn second lien, lent it to Envision, which used it to repurchase about $1.903bn of existing term debt at a discount (about $582mm discount captured, about $90mm annual interest saving per elevenflo); participants received priority and maturity extension; non-participants were put in a "fourth-out" tranche. Octus puts participation in the non-pro-rata uptier at about 91% of lenders, with the other 9% left behind. [S: elevenflo; Octus July 2025 (the 91/9 split is a secondary figure, CHECK)]
- Prices: Envision first-lien term loan 73 cents in March 2022; by November 2022 AmSurg debt traded at about 98 cents against about 25 cents for Envision first lien. [S: CEPR / CounterPunch, 8 May 2023, citing market data; advocacy source, CHECK]
- 15 May 2023: Chapter 11 in S.D. Tex. (No. 23-90342, Judge Christopher Lopez), two separate silos, no DIP; consensual cash collateral orders. Debt about $7.7bn. [S: elevenflo; Healthcare Dive]
- 31 Jul 2023: Vibrant/Crescent adversary proceeding alleging the 2022 transactions violated unanimous-consent provisions; voluntarily dismissed with prejudice 9 Nov 2023. UCC standing motion 17 Aug 2023; UCC settled in the plan: Envision unsecured noteholders $35mm cash plus 1.5% of reorganized Envision equity, other general unsecured creditors an extra $5mm. [S: elevenflo; Octus July 2025]
- 15 Sep 2023: independent director Gary Begeman's report concluded the transactions provided about $1.7bn of quantified benefits and found no actual fraud. [S: elevenflo]
- 11 Oct 2023 confirmed; 3 Nov 2023 effective (about 172 days). AmSurg: revolver and first-lien paid in full in cash; second-lien holders ($1,494mm) took 100% of reorganized AmSurg equity (diluted by a $300mm rights offering at a 30% discount); Octus: projected second-lien recovery 73% in the August 2023 disclosure statement. Envision: second-out term loan took 100% of reorganized Envision equity; unsecured notes only warrants if the class accepted. Debt cut more than 70%. [S: elevenflo; Octus; Healthcare Dive]
- 17 Jun 2025: Ascension announced it would buy AmSurg for a reported $3.9bn, about $250mm above the top of the plan's reorganized enterprise value range (PJT), so AmSurg lenders earned over 1.3x their investment per Octus; FTC cleared with divestitures (six clinics to an Optum subsidiary); closed later in 2025. [S: Octus July 2025; Becker's; Fierce Healthcare]

## 3. Boardriders (Aug 2020 deal, Oct 2022 ruling, Sep 2023 settlement): the uptier that was paid out at par

(Correction to queue framing: the Boardriders uptier closed in August 2020, not 2023; 2023 is the year the case ended.)

**Instruments and dollar amounts.** April 2018 credit agreement financed the Billabong acquisition; first-lien term debt of about $450mm was held by Oaktree (sponsor, through affiliated funds), "participating lenders" and "non-consenting lenders." In August 2020 the company raised $110mm of new super-priority money and rolled up $321mm of existing first-lien term loans at par into equal super-priority debt ($431mm total new super-priority paper), implemented by majority-vote amendments (removing the debt covenant and the requirement that any intercreditor be junior or pari passu) plus "open market purchase" agreements with participants. Non-consenting lenders held about $85mm of first-lien term loans, which became last in line. Participating lenders' and Oaktree's loans were trading at a 40-50% discount to par at the time. [S: Goodwin Procter alert 4 Nov 2022 on ICG Global Loan Fund 1 DAC v. Boardriders, Inc., No. 655175/2020 (N.Y. Sup. Ct.); Skadden, "Lessons learned from recent uptier transaction litigation", Turnarounds & Workouts (Oct 2023); SGB Online 15 Oct 2020 for $431mm and $110mm]

**Timeline.**
- Aug 2020: uptier closes. [S: Skadden Oct 2023]
- Late Sep 2020: S&P raised Boardriders to CCC from SD, saying it still viewed the transaction as tantamount to default because lenders were subordinated without adequate compensation. [S: SGB Online 15 Oct 2020 quoting S&P]
- Oct 2020 (a Friday before 15 Oct): Intermediate Capital Group, York Capital and other excluded lenders sued in New York Supreme Court seeking to unwind the deal; claims of breach of the pro rata sharing sacred right, implied covenant, NY voidable transactions act, and tortious interference against Oaktree. [S: SGB Online; Skadden]
- Dec 2020: motion to dismiss filed. 17 Oct 2022: Justice Andrea Masley denied dismissal of breach of contract and implied covenant claims against the company and participating lenders (the "open market" exception was ambiguous, so a fact question; the amended no-action clause did not bar the suit); dismissed tortious interference against Oaktree under the economic-interest defense. [S: Goodwin 4 Nov 2022; Skadden]
- Nov 2022: Boardriders appealed. Aug 2023: parties announced a pending settlement tied to Authentic Brands' purchase of Boardriders, repaying non-participating lenders in full; Sep 2023: stipulation of dismissal with prejudice. [S: Skadden Oct 2023]
- 1-4 Sep 2023: Authentic Brands Group closed its acquisition of Boardriders (Quiksilver, Billabong, Roxy, RVCA, DC Shoes, Element, VonZipper, Honolua); price undisclosed, a Moody's report earlier in 2023 put it at $1.25bn; Bloomberg had reported about $1.3bn in March 2023. [S: Fashion Dive; FashionUnited 4 Sep 2023; FashionUnited Mar 2023 (secondary)]

**Why it matters for the book.** Boardriders is the control case for "uptier then survive": the transaction bought three years of runway, the business was sold at an enterprise value that covered the whole first-lien stack, and the excluded lenders were repaid at par, so the dissidents' outcome was a function of a sale, not of the law. TriMark (Sep 2020 uptier: $120mm new money, $307.5mm roll-up; settled Jan 2022 by exchanging the excluded lenders' debt into the super-priority tranche at par) is the same pattern. Compare Serta, where the same fact pattern ended in Chapter 11 and nonparticipating lenders got 0.6-2.4% (Skadden). [S: Skadden Oct 2023]

## 4. Incora / Wesco Aircraft (2022-2024): the Isgur rulings

**Instruments (Wesco Aircraft Holdings, Inc., the Incora group; sponsor Platinum Equity).** To fund the early-2020 Pattonair acquisition Wesco raised "over $2 billion" under three indentures: $650mm 8.5% senior secured notes due 2024 ("2024 Notes"), $900mm 9% senior secured notes due 2026 ("2026 Notes"), and $525mm 13.125% unsecured notes due 2027 ("2027 Notes"). [S: D.Ct. opinion, Wesco Aircraft Holdings v. SSD Investments, No. 4:25-cv-202 (S.D. Tex. 8 Dec 2025), 2025 WL 3514358, Findings of Fact A; PDF copy at pbwt2.gjassets.com]

**Contract mechanics.** Each secured indenture allowed additional pari passu notes with a simple-majority amendment (s.9.02) and counted the additional notes toward voting thresholds; lien release needed 66 2/3% of the series because no amendment could "have the effect of releasing all or substantially all of the Collateral" without it. [S: same opinion, FoF A] The whole case turned on the phrase "have the effect of."

**Timeline.**
- Early 2020: Pattonair acquisition; COVID hits aerospace. Platinum injected a further $50mm that did not cure the liquidity gap. [S: D.Ct. opinion FoF B]
- Q4 2021: Wesco feared missing the November 2021 coupon; auditors said a $150-200mm cash injection was needed for a clean UK statutory audit, or the UK entity faced "strike off". Majority Group (PIMCO and Silver Point funds, "the two largest sets of holders of the 2024 and 2026 Notes") made a confidential proposal in December 2021. [S: D.Ct. opinion FoF B]
- Feb 2022: a Minority Group announced it held more than one-third of the 2026 Notes (a blocking position) after the Majority Group had believed it held two-thirds. The Minority Group proposed roughly $147.5mm, later $173mm (or a $250mm letter-of-credit alternative the company judged infeasible); the board chose the Majority Group offer. [S: D.Ct. opinion FoF B-C]
- 28 Mar 2022 (single day, 8:15am closing call; wires at 12:54pm; last exchange step about 3:08pm): (1) Third Supplemental Indentures (majority consent) permitted $250mm of additional 2026 Notes; the consenting group held more than two-thirds of the 2024 Notes, more than 59% of the 2026 Notes and more than half of the 2027 Notes; (2) $250mm new money wired and additional 2026 Notes issued, lifting the Majority Group from 59% to more than two-thirds of the 2026 Notes; (3) Fourth Supplemental Indentures released the liens on the 2024 and 2026 Notes with two-thirds consent counted using the new notes; (4) participating 2024/2026/2027 holders exchanged into new super-senior first-lien notes ("1L Notes", late-2026 maturity) and 2027 holders into 1.25 lien notes (2027). Milbank gave an opinion of counsel that the structure complied with the indentures. [S: D.Ct. opinion FoF C; Octus 10 Jul 2024]
- After closing the company spent about $50mm settling with vendors and $90mm on debt obligations and about $70mm on inventory; Silver Point bought nearly $40mm of unsecured 2027 bonds. [S: D.Ct. opinion FoF C.2] The relief was short-lived.
- Oct 2022: 2024/2026 noteholders sued in New York state court to void the deal. [S: D.Ct. opinion]
- 1 Jun 2023: Wesco and affiliates filed Chapter 11 (S.D. Tex., No. 4:23-bk-90611) and the same day filed adversary No. 4:23-ap-3091 seeking a declaration of no breach. [S: D.Ct. opinion fn 2; Milbank release 13 Feb 2025 gives filing date 1 Jun 2023]
- 30 Jan to early Jul 2024: 35 trial days over six months on the contract claims (equitable lien and equitable subordination counterclaims were dismissed at summary judgment as "disguised avoidance actions" belonging to the estate). [S: D.Ct. opinion]
- 25 Jun 2024: closing arguments; Judge Isgur said he believed "with a high degree of certainty" that the $250mm was unauthorized by the 2026 indenture and called leaving excluded holders with an unsecured claim "the worst possible thing I could do." [S: Octus 26 Jun 2024]
- 10 Jul 2024: interlocutory oral ruling: no breach of the 2024 indenture (two-thirds consent was held), but breach of the 2026 indenture; the third amendment, a "domino agreement", needed 66 2/3% of 2026 Notes the group did not have; all rights, liens and interests of the 2026 holders survived; the 2027 indenture was also breached (partial-redemption fairness). The debtors' plan had given virtually all enterprise value, 96.5% of pre-dilution equity, to Silver Point and PIMCO. [S: Octus 10 Jul 2024; D.Ct. opinion]
- 27 Dec 2024: consensual plan confirmed (Bankr. Dkt. 2528), premised on the bankruptcy court's rulings with all parties preserving appellate rights; First Lien Noteholders appealed confirmation on 10 Jan 2025 (No. 25-cv-161, held in abeyance). [S: D.Ct. opinion; Milbank 13 Feb 2025]
- 17 Jan 2025: bankruptcy court report and recommendation (the contract claims were non-core, hence R&R): 2022 Transactions complied with the 2024 indenture but breached the 2026 indenture; remedy: declare 2026 liens in full force on 29 Mar 2022, leaving the group's $250mm as an unsecured claim. [S: D.Ct. opinion]
- 31 Jan 2025: emergence. First-lien secured noteholders received about 98.4% of new common equity plus $420mm of convertible takeback notes; unsecured creditors about 1.6% of equity and $7.5mm; $300mm superpriority DIP notes exchanged into exit notes plus $100mm new exit money. [S: Davis Polk, "Incora emerges from chapter 11"; Milbank release]
- 8 Dec 2025: Chief Judge Crane (S.D. Tex.) sustained the First Lien Noteholders' objection, overruled the 2026 holders', declared no breach, dismissed the contract and implied-covenant claims; held that "the fact that the 2022 Transactions closed ... over the course of a single day is not unusual," refused "to find any implied sacred rights," and said that even had there been a breach the remedy would be money damages, not stripping the rescue lenders' lien. [S: D.Ct. opinion]

## 5. Robertshaw (May 2023 uptier, Dec 2023 control flip, Feb 2024 Chapter 11): creditor-on-creditor, and the remedy that was only money

(Framing note: the "Delaware/Texas" label in the queue is not supported by the record. The fight was New York state court, then S.D. Tex. bankruptcy court, Judge Christopher Lopez.)

**Instruments (primary: Memorandum Decision on Plan Confirmation, In re Robertshaw US Holding Corp., No. 24-90052, ECF 959, S.D. Tex. 16 Aug 2024, fetched as PDF from hunton.com).** One Rock Capital Partners bought Robertshaw in 2018 with $510mm first-lien term loans, $110mm second-lien term loans and about $260mm of equity, plus an ABL. The May 2023 transaction created a New York-law Super-Priority Credit Agreement (9 May 2023): $95mm of first-out new money, with participants exchanging first- and second-lien loans into second-out and third-out term loans. "Required Lender" meant more than 50.0% of first-out new money plus second-out loans; the register held by the administrative agent was dispositive. Prepetition funded debt at filing was about $832.8mm across seven super-priority tranches plus a Mexican promissory note [S: ION/Debtwire case profile, Octus summary via search; CHECK].

**Timeline.**
- 9 May 2023: uptier with the Ad Hoc Group (Bain Capital Credit, Canyon Capital, Eaton Vance) and Invesco; the SPCA added blockers that, per Invesco's Matthew Brooks, "limited the ability to do another uptier" and "eliminated the ability to do any sort of dropdown transactions." [S: Confirmation decision, part I]
- Jul 2023: Invesco quietly bought more than 50% of first-out plus second-out loans, becoming Required Lender (it owned the majority of first-out but not second-out, so the status was "fragile"); the Ad Hoc Group did not know. [S: Confirmation decision]
- Sep-Nov 2023: missed interest; Amendment 1 (5 Oct 2023) extended the grace period; Amendment 2 (13 Oct) gave a $17mm first-out bridge and a $40mm delayed-draw commitment tied to a repurchase of Invesco's third-out loans at par through "open market purchases"; Amendment 4 (Nov 2023) required a Chapter 11 by 2 Jan 2024 and installed an independent director picked by Invesco. Invesco instructed the agent not to post the amendments; the Ad Hoc Group learned of them around 15 Nov when a third party mentioned it to a Bain employee; they were posted 16 Nov. [S: Confirmation decision, part II]
- 11 Dec 2023 (the December Transactions): Range Investor formed RS Funding Holdings (voting owned by the parent, economically owned by Robertshaw); the Ad Hoc Group and One Rock lent $228.3mm to RS Funding; the proceeds went to Robertshaw, which repaid the $30mm ABL, prepaid $117.6mm of first-out loans (plus $30.7mm make-whole), which pushed Invesco below 50% and handed Required Lender status to the Ad Hoc Group; the Ad Hoc Group then signed Amendment 5 authorizing $228mm of incremental debt and Robertshaw issued $218mm of new first-out and second-out loans, repaying the RS Funding loan. Invesco received over $90mm of prepayment and tried to reject it. [S: Confirmation decision, part III]
- Late Dec 2023: Invesco sued in New York Supreme Court (breach, implied covenant, tortious interference with One Rock, fraudulent transfer). Nov 2023: separate "Guardian Action" by excluded lenders from the May uptier; settled globally Mar 2024. [S: Confirmation decision, parts IV and VI]
- 15 Feb 2024: Chapter 11, S.D. Tex.; adversary 24-03024 filed the same day by the debtors, One Rock and the Ad Hoc Group seeking declarations. [S: Confirmation decision, part V; Paul Weiss memo 24 Jun 2024]
- 20 Jun 2024: Memorandum Decision and Order (662 B.R. 300): Ad Hoc Group and One Rock were Required Lenders; Amendment 5 valid; no breach by lenders, no implied-covenant breach, no tortious interference; but Robertshaw itself breached the SPCA by not remitting 100% of net proceeds to RS Funding. Invesco's remedy: a prepetition money-damages claim only, no rescission, no restored Required Lender status. [S: Paul Weiss memo; Confirmation decision]
- Jun 2024: sale approved to a stalking-horse entity formed by the Ad Hoc Group and One Rock; no competing bid; consideration a Section 363(k) credit bid of about $286mm (principal $217mm of prepetition super-priority claims plus interest, and about $65mm of DIP) plus assumed liabilities. [S: Confirmation decision]
- 16 Aug 2024: liquidating plan confirmed over Invesco's objection; general unsecured creditors about $11mm pool; funded-debt deficiency claims about $10mm pool; Class 3 super-priority holders take an ownership interest in the purchaser; equity cancelled. A U.S. District Court order on 12 Aug 2024 had stayed the sale on an interim basis pending Invesco's appeal. Invesco claimed about $40mm of damages and indemnity and the debtors said such deficiency claims would recover about 3%. [S: Confirmation decision; Paul Weiss memo; Octus 'credit-bid sale approved']

**Take-aways.** (i) The first LMT (May 2023) was ordinary; the lethal one was the control flip, in which an entity "economically owned but not voted" by the borrower let the borrower prepay one tranche to move a 50% threshold. (ii) Judge Lopez found a breach by the borrower but refused to unwind: the remedy was a money claim that recovers about 3 cents. (iii) The structure had been designed to avoid exactly this, by "blockers" that did not address threshold-flipping. For a systematic desk, Required-Lender thresholds and the register are state variables.
