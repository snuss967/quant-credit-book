## 1. Headline trends

1. **Private credit became an official-statistics asset class in Oct 2026, when the Fed's Z.1 began reporting $1.49T of private-credit loans (2025:Q4) growing about 20% a year.**
2. **Public and private leveraged finance are now one borrower pool with two pricing regimes, and the flow direction between them follows the syndicated market's excess loan premium.**
3. **Retail semi-liquid money ($241B net) became the marginal private-credit dollar, which shifted the asset class's failure mode from defaults to redemptions.**
4. **The 2025–26 stress started with collateral frauds outside direct lending (First Brands, Tricolor, MFS) and reached the core through AI-driven software risk, about one-fifth of direct-lending loans.**
5. **Listed prices now openly contradict private marks: the OBDC/OBDC II merger collapsed, non-accruals are 1.3% at cost against 0.7% at fair value, and public LL software spreads moved before private ones.**
6. **Banks did not lose to private credit; they moved up its capital structure, becoming the senior revolver tranche of a CLO-like BDC stack.**
7. **Private credit is building public-market plumbing (Apollo daily pricing, 5,000+ ICE IDs, a $30B+ secondary desk, the PRIV ETF's firm-bid wrapper) that turns marks into prices.**
8. **Insurance RBC arbitrage through private ratings and MM-CLO/BDC stacks is now under live NAIC scrutiny, with SVO discretion over filing-exempt designations operational since Aug 2026.**
9. **Default is migrating into selective forms (PIK, A&E, covenant holidays), so headline private-credit default rates understate distress.**

## 3. Narrative: how the trend evolved, its drivers, and where it is heading

### 3.1 The post-GFC build (2010–2019): access, not price
Private credit began as rationed credit. About a third of 2010–2015 middle-market loans came from nonbanks, to weaker borrowers paying about 190bp more, and bank regulation explains the jump in nonbank borrowing when EBITDA turns negative (Chernenko–Erel–Prilmeier). The BIS panel adds low policy rates and inefficient banking as drivers. A quieter driver was the BDC balance sheet. After the 2018 move to 2:1 leverage, BDC debt/equity rose from about 0.4 to above 1, supplying about 150bp of the fall in BDC funding costs relative to banks. Growth was partly manufactured by cheaper, more levered vehicle funding.

### 3.2 The boom (2020–early 2023): jumbo unitranche and the "steal"
With $1B+ tickets (KBRA: 5 jumbo deals in 2019, 42 in 2023), private credit competed head-on with the BSL market. When the BSL market shut in 2022, the share of LL borrowers migrating to PC peaked at about 50% in early 2023, a "tight LL" window the Fed dates by the excess loan premium (May 2022–Sep 2024). This is hard, regime-conditional evidence that the markets are substitutes and that a public stress indicator predicts the direction of flow.

### 3.3 Convergence (2024–mid-2025): two-way takeouts, banks up the stack, retail and insurance down the funnel
Three forces made the two markets converge.
1. **Two-way refinancing.** When BSL spreads compressed in 2024, borrowers ran dual-track processes, and takeouts in both directions became "considerably balanced" in 2024–9M2025 (BlackRock/LCD). By 2025, about 41% of large middle-market borrowers had used both markets. The borrower pool is shared; only the pricing regimes differ. PC still prices at about S+500 versus S+400 for LL, for 5.0x versus 3.2x leverage (2025 issuance).
2. **Banks re-entered as senior financiers rather than competitors.** Bank lines to PC vehicles grew 145% in five years to about $95B committed (2024:Q4). By end-2025, total bank commitments to NBFIs were $2.6T, with PE, BDC and PC the largest category. The Boston Fed describes the result as a CLO whose AAA tranche is a bank revolver. Bank "partnerships" are therefore origination-and-distribution agreements, not a retreat. Named deal terms were not verified this session; source them before citing.
3. **Retail and insurance became the marginal buyers.** Perpetual BDCs and interval funds grew from about $15B (2018) to $388B (2025) in net assets. EO 14330 (Aug 2025) opened the 401(k) door. PRIV (Feb 2025) wrapped Apollo-sourced private assets in a daily-liquid ETF backed by a contractual firm-bid facility. On the insurance side, packaging MM loans into CLO or BDC stacks cuts RBC charges about 10× (Fed, Mar 2025). Insurer-affiliated managers run about 40% of BDC assets, and private letter ratings were the passport. By mid-2025 the asset class had both a liquidity mismatch and a ratings mismatch.

### 3.4 The stress (Sep 2025–Q2 2026): fraud, AI and redemptions
The break did not come from defaults in core sponsor-backed direct lending. It came at the edges:
- **Collateral fraud in receivables and ABF.** First Brands (about $2.3B allegedly missing via double-pledged receivables), Tricolor (loans pledged to multiple ABS deals and lenders) and the UK's Market Financial Solutions (an alleged £930M double-pledge shortfall) are one failure mode: lenders who never verified that collateral was unique. The Fed's Nov 2025 FSR called them isolated but flagged "opaque off-balance-sheet funding arrangements".
- **The listed-price signal.** The Blue Owl OBDC/OBDC II merger (5 Nov 2025) would have swapped private-BDC NAV into a listed BDC trading below NAV, with 98% portfolio overlap. It was the cleanest natural experiment of the cycle: the same loans, priced at NAV in one wrapper and at a discount in the other. Investors balked and the deal was pulled within two weeks (CHECK). Blue Owl later restricted OBDC II withdrawals and sold about $1.4B of loans (CHECK).
- **AI disruption of software.** Software is about 21% of PC issuance since 2020 and about one-fifth of direct-lending loans, and more than 50% of NAV for some levered vehicles (IMF). Public markets repriced first. LL secondary spreads widened "reflecting a high concentration of leveraged loan borrowers in the software industry", and PC new-loan spreads only "ticked up" afterwards (Fed FSR, May 2026). The IMF (Apr 2026) also documents an equity rotation out of S&P 500 software.
- **Redemptions.** Requests rose in 2025:Q4 and accelerated in 2026:Q1, with several funds receiving requests "much larger than 5 percent of NAV". Most managers capped at 5%, and accepted redemptions exceeded inflows for the first time since perpetual BDCs were created. Barings capped a fund and Moody's cut the BDC outlook to negative (CHECK). The IMF names the mechanism precisely: slow-moving valuations create a first-mover advantage, and discretionary gating "may erode investor confidence across the broader industry".

On the official numbers, credit performance stayed acceptable. KBRA's TTM DL default rate was 1.5% (Oct 2025), CDLI realized losses 61bp, and non-accruals 1.3% of cost. The distress shows instead in **selective** forms: PIK at 7.3% of income, 58% "bad PIK" among PIK payers, amend-and-extends, and rising payment defaults on S&P credit estimates. The RSSG reader will recognize these as the LMT-era redefinition of default, moved into the bilateral market.

### 3.5 Institutionalization under pressure (H2 2026): data, prices and regulation
The response to the stress has been infrastructure, not retreat. Institutional fundraising hit a record ($129B in 9M2026, CHECK), so the money changed channel rather than leaving. Transparency arrived on three fronts in four months:
- **Official statistics.** The Fed integrated private credit into Z.1, with new sectors and instrument series (FL443067205 and others) and quarterly holder detail (16 Oct 2026). It also published notes on the PC/LL substitution, BDC bank funding and semi-liquid funds.
- **Private prices.** Apollo's daily pricing reached all credit vehicles on 1 Oct 2026, with asset-level pricing from 30 Oct 2026. ICE has issued more than 5,000 private-asset IDs, and Apollo reports more than $30B of secondary trading.
- **Regulatory pricing of ratings.** The NAIC's discretion to challenge filing-exempt designations is now live, and the FE treatment of CLOs is under review.

### 3.6 2026–2028 outlook and scenarios
- **Base case (about 55%): growth resumes with a "public-ish" private market.** AUM keeps compounding toward the forecasts (Preqin/Macquarie: $2.8T by 2028; BlackRock: $4.5T by 2030), led by institutional drawdown funds, ABF and IG private credit. Semi-liquid vehicles shrink as a share. Daily marks spread from Apollo to peers because competing for wealth-channel money requires them. Secondary trading of private loans grows into a dealer business, and the PC–BSL boundary becomes a continuous, spread-arbitraged frontier.
- **Bear case (about 30%): an AI-software default wave plus a redemption spiral.** Software borrowers with 2027–28 maturities (the IMF flags heavy software LL maturities by 2028) fail to refinance. Selective defaults rise 2–3×, matching the IMF stress scenario. Semi-liquid funds exhaust their 5–7-quarter buffers, sell liquid sleeves (BSL loans, which spills into public loan prices) and draw bank lines, while BDC bank-funding premia widen as they did in 2022 (+1.1pp). Public BDC P/NAV discounts widen, and secondary trades crystallize marks.
- **Regulatory-shock case (about 15%).** The SVO challenges a large volume of FE private ratings, or CLO FE treatment changes. Insurers would face RBC jumps and rotate from private placements and MM-CLO tranches into public IG and BB, a forced-flow event for public desks.

**Leading indicators to monitor** (definitions in §5):
- **Redemptions:** perpetual-BDC requests as a share of NAV.
- **Marks:** public-BDC P/NAV and the gap between non-accruals at cost and at fair value.
- **Hidden default:** bad-PIK share and the gap between selective and payment defaults.
- **Regime and flows:** the LL excess loan premium against its median, plus steals and takeouts.
- **Funding:** bank utilization and pricing of BDC lines.
- **Sector:** software LL secondary prices.
- **Ratings:** NAIC SVO FE challenges and the CLO FE amendment outcome.
- **Fraud tells:** UCC lien overlap and receivables purchased off-platform.

## 4. Implications for a systematic credit desk

**What to build**
1. **The Mark-Gap Monitor.** For every public BDC, compute P/NAV and translate it into an implied asset markdown: m ≈ (1 − P/NAV) · E/A, adjusted for the fee drag of management and incentive fees, which justifies a structural discount. At D/E ≈ 1, a 20% equity discount implies about a 10% asset markdown, which the desk can compare with the BDC's own marks and with LL index prices for matched sectors. The OBDC/OBDC II episode is the calibration case.
2. **A look-through security master.** Parse BDC schedules of investments (10-Q/10-K), N-PORT for interval and tender-offer funds, and LSEG BDC Collateral. Map borrower names to the LL universe; 41% of large borrowers are dual-track, so the overlap is material. Tag sector, lien, PIK flags, spread, cost and fair value, and attach ICE IDs as they appear. This is Ch 25's NLP and parsing pipeline applied to a new corpus.
3. **A refinancing-flow nowcaster.** Model the probability of PC→BSL and BSL→PC migration conditional on the excess loan premium regime, the DL–BSL spread differential, borrower size and sector. The output feeds loan-supply forecasts for Ch 21's repricing and CLO-demand models: a wave of PC→BSL takeouts is new BSL supply.
4. **A forced-seller map extension (Ch 1).** Add perpetual BDCs, interval funds, MM CLOs and insurer-affiliated BDC/JVLF structures to the flow graph. The critical edge is that semi-liquid funds hold BSL loans and other liquid sleeves for redemptions, so a private-credit run transmits into public loan prices. Non-traded BDCs hold only 0.2% in short-term liquid assets; their real liquidity is bank lines plus the BSL sleeve.
5. **A liquidity-runway model per vehicle** (IMF Annex 1.5 style): (cash + undrawn lines + liquid sleeve) / (redemption rate × NAV), with scenarios for draws on borrower revolvers and unsecured BDC debt maturities. RSSG readers already know this as a 13-week cash flow applied to a fund.

**What to trade**
- **Public BDC relative value:** long scaled, diversified BDCs and short sub-scaled ones (<100 credits), conditioned on software share and bad-PIK share. Cliffwater's ROE gap of 9.5% versus 6.1% is the fundamental anchor. Hedge the beta with LL TRS, BKLN or HY CDX.
- **BDC capital-structure trades (Ch 23):** BDC unsecured bonds versus BDC equity, with bank revolvers senior to both. Widening bank-funding premia are a leading signal for the bonds.
- **Event-driven (Ch 15):** merger proposals, tender-offer outcomes, gate announcements, the conversion of private BDCs to listed (the NAV-to-price crystallization) and secondary portfolio sales.
- **Public-leads-private lead–lag:** trade the expected next-quarter BDC NAV revision implied by current LL and HY sector moves (software especially). The cleanest test is whether quarter-q public LL sector returns predict quarter-q+1 BDC fair-value changes.
- **PRIV and future hybrid ETFs:** premium/discount arbitrage. The AP mechanics (Ch 20) are complicated by Apollo's firm-bid backstop and the contractual stress-redemption limits.
- **Insurance-driven flows:** if SVO challenges or CLO FE changes push RBC up, expect insurer demand to rotate toward public BBB/BB and away from MM-CLO mezzanine. Position ahead of NAIC calendar dates.

**What to worry about**
- **Smoothed marks feeding risk models.** Any MAC optimizer that uses CDLI or a LL proxy for private credit understates vol and correlation. The shelf's Nuveen framework proxies MM direct lending with the S&P UBS LL B index. Desmooth and stress before giving private credit a 35% cap.
- **The liquidity sleeve spillover:** redemption-driven sales of BSL loans can widen public loan spreads without any change in public fundamentals.
- **Fraud and collateral-uniqueness tail risk** in ABF, receivables and warehouse lending. Three such cases in 12 months amount to a pattern.
- **Bank line correlation:** the Boston Fed calls correlated PC defaults hitting bank lines an "underappreciated" tail.
- **The definitional trap:** "private credit size" ranges from $1.3T to more than $3T depending on whether it means loans or AUM, US or global, and with or without dry powder. Always state which.

**For the RSSG-to-quant reader specifically**
- Private credit restructurings are mostly out of court and club-style. "Selective default" (PIK amendments, A&E, covenant holidays) is the bilateral analogue of the LMT. The RSSG instinct for reading amendments maps directly onto building a bad-PIK and amendment classifier from BDC footnotes and 8-Ks.
- Dual-track financing processes, rescue financing for stressed public issuers, and lender-on-lender dynamics inside clubs are RSSG home ground. The quant upgrade is to turn those processes into flow probabilities and pricing distributions.
- The 5% redemption cap behaves like a covenant: discretionary waivers and gates are the fund-level equivalent of amendment fees and forbearance, and they create the same first-mover game.

## 5. Quant hooks

**Variables and signals this trend creates**
- **P/NAV and implied asset markdown** per BDC, daily. Their cross-sectional dispersion is a stress index.
- **Non-accrual FV/cost ratio**, an implied recovery on non-accruals; the CDLI's 4Q2025 0.7/1.3 suggests about 54%, **CHECK**. Track the cost-vs-FV non-accrual gap as an early-warning gauge.
- **PIK share of income, bad-PIK share, and amendment frequency:** hidden-default proxies for Ch 14's regime-switching default model.
- **Selective versus payment default spread**, from S&P credit-estimate statistics.
- **Redemption pressure and runway:** requests/NAV, acceptance ratio and carry-forward queue (8-K, SC TO-I/C); liquidity runway in quarters.
- **Migration probabilities** PC↔BSL conditional on the ELP regime, with the DL–BSL spread differential.
- **Bank funding premium and line utilization** for BDCs (FSR aggregates; 10-K facility terms).
- **Software / AI-exposure loading** per vehicle from the schedules of investments, used as a sector factor in Ch 9's factor framework.
- **Same-loan cross-holder mark dispersion:** the standard deviation of fair value/par across BDCs and interval funds holding the same facility. It measures mark subjectivity, and a high-dispersion facility is a candidate for mark convergence.
- **Daily-price vs NAV smoothing coefficient:** fit an AR(1) or Getmansky–Lo–Makarov smoothing to vehicles that now publish daily prices (Apollo) against their quarterly NAVs.
- **PRIV premium/discount and AOS share** (private-sourced share of holdings).
- **NAIC FE challenge events** and the private-rating notch gap.

**Datasets:** Fed Z.1 PC series (FL443067205, FL453067203, FL463067205, FL673067205, FL893167205, FL263167205, LM443164500, LM893164505); FSR chart data; EDGAR (BDC schedules of investments, 8-K and SC TO tender results, N-PORT); LSEG BDC Collateral; Cliffwater CDLI; KBRA DLD; PitchBook LCD; Lincoln International; S&P credit estimates; Preqin; ICE IDs; Apollo daily prices; PRIV holdings; NAIC SVO and IDAWG materials; PACER and Kroll dockets.

**Signals and assumptions this trend destroys or weakens**
- "Private credit is low-vol and low-correlation." That result comes from smoothing, and daily pricing and listed wrappers expose it.
- "No secondary market, so no price." A $30B+ desk, ICE IDs and the PRIV firm bid now produce observable prices.
- "Headline default rate = credit loss." In the selective-default era this is a biased estimator.
- A LL index as a clean proxy for direct lending (Nuveen). Leverage (5.0x vs 3.2x), size, rating mix (0% BB) and sector mix (more software) all differ.
- "Banks are out of leveraged lending." They are the senior tranche and the liquidity provider.
