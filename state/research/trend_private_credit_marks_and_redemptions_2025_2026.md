## 1. Headline trends

1. **The run became a queue.** Across ten tracked evergreen vehicles, 2026 tender requests ran from 3% to 40% of shares per quarter against 5% caps. Q1 2026 was the first quarter in which accepted redemptions exceeded inflows at perpetual BDCs (Fed), and Moody's moved the BDC sector outlook to negative in April.
2. **The gate held, so the fill rate became the price.** BCRED stretched to 7% once, then paid about half of $4.5B in Q2, carried a $2.3B backlog and filled 5% in Q3. OCIC's fill went 22.8% to 26.6% to about 30% as requests fell from 21.9% to 16.8%. Two thirds of North Haven's Q3 requests were resubmissions.
3. **Demand tracked exposure and channel, not the asset class.** Software-focused OTIC saw 38-40% tendered in two straight quarters, HLEND 9-13%, and Golub, Goldman Sachs and Fidelity 3-5% (paid in full).
4. **Marks moved late and unevenly.** Seven holders marked Medallia from a par-weighted 77.8 (Dec-25) to 49.2 (Jun-26), BCRED from 94.0 (Dec-24), with a 5-14 point spread between holders at every date; the median externally managed listed BDC trades at 0.76 of book while BCRED's portfolio mark moved only from 96.1 to 95.4.
5. **Default depends on the ruler.** The public ladder runs from 2.5% (Proskauer) through 4.0-5.8% (Moody's), 6.3% (Fitch) to 19% (PIMCO shadow); non-accruals at cost are 1.7-3.5 times those at fair value; software defaults were 0.6% (Fitch) while software equities fell about 30%. Price led default.
6. **Sponsors have a five-item remedy menu:** upsizing the cap with house money, return of capital in place of tenders, par loan sales, listed-share buybacks, distribution cuts. All of them move value from stayers to leavers.
7. **Marks are being turned into prices by infrastructure:** Z.1 private-credit series ($1.49T), Apollo's daily pricing (with its own disclaimer), ICE asset IDs, PRIV's firm-bid wrapper, quarterly N-PORT.
8. **Public credit is diverging at the bottom.** HY OAS is 3.2% but CCC is 12.8% (up 308bp since June), the Fed hiked 25bp on 17 Sep 2026, and Moody's puts Caa-equivalents at over half of 2027-28 middle-market maturities.

## 3. Narrative: drivers and the 2026-2028 outlook

Source tags in square brackets, such as [FSR], point to the Sources list at the end.

### 3.1 Cockroaches, then a merger that did not happen (Sep-Nov 2025)

The autumn of 2025 began with two failures that were not, strictly, private credit at all. Tricolor, a subprime auto lender, and First Brands, an auto-parts roll-up, collapsed within weeks of each other, and both stories turned on the same unglamorous fact: the same collateral had allegedly been pledged to more than one lender [SEC-TRI][SDNY-FB]. On JPMorgan's earnings call on 14 October, Jamie Dimon, who had just written off $170 million on Tricolor, said that when you see one cockroach there are probably more [FORTUNE]. The sentence did more work than any default statistic: it gave retail investors a word for a fear.

By then the marginal buyer was retail: semi-liquid vehicles raised more than half of 2025 US direct-lending capital [GFSR-O]. Then came the cleanest experiment of the cycle. On 5 November, Blue Owl proposed to merge its non-traded OBDC II into the listed OBDC; the two funds held about 98% the same loans, because the adviser had been allocating the same investments to both since OBDC II's inception [OBDC-M]. Under the exchange ratio, if the listed fund traded below NAV, holders of the non-traded fund would swap NAV for NAV and so inherit the market's discount. Thirteen days later the merger was cancelled "due to current market conditions" [OBDC-T]. The same loans had been offered to the public at two prices, and the second price had been visible all along: OBDC would later disclose buying back its own stock at 86% of book value [OBDC-Q4].

### 3.2 The week software broke, and a loan sale at 99.8 (Feb-Apr 2026)

In early February an AI company released tools that automated legal, sales and data-analysis work, software equities sold off, and BDCs, which are large lenders to software companies, went down with them; Blue Owl fell as much as 13% and Ares, KKR, Blackstone, TPG and Apollo vehicles 8-10% [ACI-SW]. By the BIS's count, software equities fell almost 30% between October and February while BDC shares fell about 10%, with software-heavy BDCs lagging by around five points; private credit funds had lent over $500 billion to software borrowers, 19% of direct loans [BIS].

On 18 February Blue Owl answered with a number. It sold $1.4 billion of direct loans to pension and insurance buyers at 99.8% of par, $600 million of it from OBDC II, with a fairness opinion, and in the same filing OBDC II ended its tender programme and cut its revolver from $225 million to $75 million [OBDC2-LS]. A second price arrived on 6 March, when Cox Capital and Saba bid for OBDC II units at $3.80 a share, a discount of over 30% to NAV, and found takers [COX][OBDC2-6M]. By then the same portfolio had been valued at par by buyers of the loans, at NAV by the adviser, at about 68 cents on the NAV dollar by buyers of the units, and at 86 cents of book (late 2025) and 71 cents (October 2026) by the market in its listed twin. The unit holders ended up with return of capital, 35% of NAV in two instalments, instead of the right to queue [OBDC2-ROC][OBDC2-10Q]. None of the four prices was wrong. Each answered a different question: what is the loan worth to a pension fund that wants yield, what does the manager say, what is a claim on the fund worth to someone who cannot leave, and what is it worth to someone who can sell tomorrow.

### 3.3 The queue (Q1-Q3 2026)

The five-percent gate is a promise to stand in line, and 2026 was the year people began to read the terms. At BCRED, Blackstone's flagship, first-quarter requests ran to 7.9% of shares, a record by the secondary account; the Board upsized the offer to 7% and Blackstone and its employees put about $400 million into a feeder fund to cover the rest [BCRED-Q1][ALTS-BCRED]. At BCRED that was the last time the cap was stretched. In the second quarter BCRED received $4.5 billion of requests, paid about half, and carried a $2.3 billion backlog into the third, when another $4.3 billion arrived [BCRED-Q2][BCRED-Q3]. The Fed's May stability report recorded that accepted redemptions at perpetual BDCs "somewhat exceeded new inflows in the first quarter of 2026, the first time this has occurred since these vehicles were created" [FSR].

What matters for a modeller is that the queue refills itself. Unfilled requests are not automatically carried over, so holders resubmit, and the resubmissions are the demand: nearly two thirds of Morgan Stanley North Haven's third-quarter requests came from holders who had been prorated before, and Blue Owl described "the vast majority" of OCIC's the same way [NHPIF][OCIC-L]. Fill rates therefore settle at about 5% divided by the request share: 13-14% at the software-focused OTIC, where 38-40% of shares were tendered in two straight quarters; 22-30% at OCIC; 29-49% in the middle of the pack; and 100% at Golub, Goldman Sachs and Fidelity, whose requests stayed at 3-5% [OTIC][OCIC-Q1][TRACKER]. The asset class did not run; funds with software exposure, retail distribution and a headline did.

Sponsors reached for five tools: upsizing with house money (BCRED), return of capital in place of tenders (OBDC II), par loan sales, buybacks of the listed share (OBDC, at 86% of book) and distribution cuts (BCRED, 10.0% to 9.1%) [BCRED-Q3]. Each moves value between those who stay and those who leave, which is why the IMF reads payouts above 5% "despite apparent balance sheet capacity" as proof the gate is discretionary [GFSR-A].

### 3.4 One loan, seven prices (the Medallia ledger)

The most instructive object in the dossier is a single loan. Medallia, a software company taken private in 2021, owes first-lien term-loan lenders about $2.8 billion by press accounts, and at least seven public vehicles show it on their books [OCTUS][MED]. At the end of 2024 they valued it between 86.8 and 98.8 cents on the dollar. At the end of September 2025 the range was 77.3 to 91.2, and by December, by which time the schedules show the 4% PIK gone and a cash coupon of SOFR plus 6.00%, it was 73.0 to 79.2. In the first quarter of 2026 the two Blackstone vehicles marked it from 77.8 to 60.2, to a tenth of a point of each other, and flagged it as non-accrual; in the second they went to 49.5, while Apollo's ADS, Onex and Monroe were at 39 to 41 and HPS and FS KKR at 49 to 50 [MED]. The seven holders' combined par of about $1.97 billion, valued at 77.8 cents in December, was valued at 49.2 in June.

Two readings of this ledger are both true. The marks did their job, converging on a deeply impaired loan within three quarters. They also differed by 5 to 14 points between holders at every date, two of the holders are one valuation process (BCRED and BXSL), and the move from 94 to 77 took a year while the move from 77 to 49 took half of one. PIMCO's cross-section says the same thing at scale: for loans held by at least two BDCs, marks at the end of 2025 were about five points apart, with 83% of loans inside a two-point band and the rest skewed up [PIMCO-DP]. And the market's worry did not wait for the defaults: Fitch's software default rate was 0.6% in August, while software equities were down almost 30% and about 17% of OCIC's shares were tendered [FITCH][BIS][OCIC-L]. The price moved first, the queue second and the mark third; the default, where it came, was a PIK expiry in a 10-Q footnote.

### 3.5 Three kinds of default, and what the public side saw

An outsider asking how bad things are in 2026 receives answers from 2.5% to 19%, and none is wrong. Proskauer's index counts 2.51%; KBRA's forward-looking rate is 3.5%; Moody's counts 4.0% of middle-market CLO obligors as defaulted, or 5.8% with PIK amendments and extensions; Fitch's rate is 6.3%, with PIK and deferrals 47% of events; PIMCO's shadow rate for BDC loans is about 19% [PROSK][KBRA][MOODYS-PC][FITCH][PIMCO-SD]. The same inconsistency sits inside the BDCs: non-accruals at cost run about twice non-accruals at fair value, and 3.5 times at OBDC [EARN]. An RSSG banker will recognise the pattern. It is the LMT-era definition of default, a menu rather than an event, moved into the bilateral market where no docket records it.

Meanwhile the public market told a more concentrated story. High-yield spreads recovered to a 2.6% low in August while the CCC tier widened every month from June, from 9.7% to 12.8% by 15 October; the Fed raised rates 25 basis points on 17 September; Moody's counted Caa-equivalents at more than half of 2027-28 middle-market maturities; and listed BDCs traded at a median 0.76 of book (FSK 0.56) while their bonds recovered, which PIMCO reads as equity holders pricing a mark reset that credit holders do not expect [FRED][MOODYS-PC][EARN][SA][PIMCO-BDC].

### 3.6 Plumbing arrives (Sep-Oct 2026)

The official system answered with infrastructure. The Fed put private credit into the Financial Accounts on 16 October, a $1.49 trillion series at end-2025 with holders broken out [FEDS-Z1]. Apollo extended daily pricing to all of its credit vehicles on 1 October, with asset-level pricing promised from 30 October, while stating that the number is "an estimated fair value" which "should not be used as the basis for any investment, subscription, redemption, or transaction decision" [APOLLO]. PIMCO's reaction was that "daily pricing is not price discovery" [PIMCO-DP]. The ETF version, State Street's PRIV, sits on firm bids from Apollo, the arrangement that SEC staff questioned in 2025 because the bids were redacted, discretionary and open for 15 minutes [PRIV-SEC1][PRIV-SEC2][PRIV-FUND]. Whether more frequent observation of a model-based mark is information or noise at a higher frequency is the open question.

### 3.7 Outlook, 2026-2028

Three paths seem plausible, and the dossier's evidence does not choose between them. In the slow-extension path, fill rates hover at 30-50%, backlogs are rolled, and the trailing default rate falls "in part because funds have provided flexibility ... by amending and extending loans" [GFSR-O]. The IMF's runway arithmetic is that perpetual BDCs could meet 5% of NAV in quarterly redemptions for about 9-11 quarters in mild stress and 5-7 in a moderate shock, which, counted from the first quarter of 2026, puts the first hard decisions in late 2027 and 2028, when middle-market maturities peak [GFSR-A][MOODYS-PC]. In a reset path, the marks catch the listed discounts in one or two quarters, the secondary buyers (Saba among them) and the pension and insurance buyers of par loans absorb the transfer, and the queue clears because the exit price is no longer a bad one. In the tail path a large vehicle suspends or sells loans into a thin broadly syndicated market, and the PIMCO REIT analogy, in which private marks fell to meet public prices, plays out in loans [PIMCO-BDC]. What separates them is measurable soon: Q3 results in late October and November, asset-level pricing on 30 October, Q3 non-accruals, and whether September's rate rise converts PIK (5.6% of BCRED's Q2 income) into non-accrual [BCRED-Q3].

## 4. Implications for a systematic credit desk and for the RSSG-to-quant reader

A systematic desk cannot trade the Medallia loan, but it can trade the loan's shadow, and in 2026 the shadow moved first. Listed BDC equity sits at a median 0.76 of book while BCRED's portfolio mark is 95.4; secondary leveraged-loan spreads had widened on software concentration by May; the CCC tier started widening in June; and each non-traded vehicle's request ratio was publishing a quarterly belief the manager did not control [SA][FSR][FRED][OTIC]. Each of these is a tradable or at least observable proxy for the mark that arrives six weeks later in a 10-Q, and each is dated. The pairing that does the work sets each private mark beside the nearest public prices and dates the staleness of whichever is older.

For the RSSG-trained reader the 10-Q turns out to be a docket. Medallia's story has the beats of a restructuring the reader already knows: a PIK feature written in 2021 that lapsed at the end of 2025, a cash coupon that appears on the schedule as SOFR plus 6.00%, a footnote reading "Loan was on non-accrual status", a first-lien group in which Blackstone's two vehicles hold about $1.57 billion of the roughly $1.97 billion of visible par, and press reports of a debt-for-equity swap [MED][OCTUS]. A banker who has sat through a cash-pay cliff or a lender group's first call has priors for how many quarters separate each beat; encoding them as hazard-model states, with transition times estimated across hundreds of borrowers from the same filings, is the natural bridge from seat to screen (Chapters 14 and 17-B).

The second thing the dossier teaches is humility about which number is in use. A reader who has seen "default" defined by an indenture, a credit agreement and a rating agency in the same week will not be surprised that the private-credit ladder runs from 2.5% to 19%. The practical consequence is narrower: a private-credit default or loss series becomes usable once it carries its basis (count or dollars, cost or fair value, with or without PIK and extensions) the way a bond price carries a day-count.

The queue, finally, is the first public, quarterly measure of what the end investor believes, and it is censored. Requests are observed in full, while fills are capped at 5% of shares, so the latent desire to leave is recovered from the request ratio and the proration factor together; the resubmission share (two thirds at North Haven, "the vast majority" at OCIC) makes the series autocorrelated, so a single quarter's ratio overstates new information [NHPIF][OCIC-L]. In queueing terms the system has an arrival process (new requests), a service rate (5% of shares less subscriptions) and a backlog; the BCRED figures of $4.5 billion requested, about half served and $2.3 billion carried are the first full quarter of that arithmetic in public [BCRED-Q3].

The limits are plain: seven holders and one borrower in the ledger, about ten funds and four quarters in the request panel, a filing lag of about six weeks, and no filing that shows the price at which a manager would clear a position under redemption pressure.

Candidate signals. (1) A lead-lag test of the listed P/B change against the next-quarter change in private marks, borrower by borrower, with holder fixed effects. (2) Cross-holder dispersion as a stress indicator: the Medallia range swung between 5.3 points (Jun-25) and 13.9 (Sep-25) without trending. (3) A censored-demand nowcast of next quarter's request ratio from this quarter's ratio, resubmission share and the BDC equity drawdown. Each needs a null: marks that are right and listed prices that overshoot, as when BDC bonds recovered and BDC equity did not [PIMCO-BDC].
