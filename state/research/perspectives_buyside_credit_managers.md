## 1. Apollo (opportunistic credit, hybrid value, Athene)

### 1.1 The seat (what it buys, horizon, position in the structure, sourcing, funding)

- **Scale and mix.** Apollo's credit arm is "Apollo's largest asset management strategy by AUM": $849B AUM, 600+ investment professionals, 4,000+ issuer relationships as of 30 Jun 2026 (apollo.com credit page, https://www.apollo.com/strategies/asset-management/credit, fetched 2026-10-18). On the 9 Feb 2026 Q4 2025 call (transcript: https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-2-9-apollo-global-management-inc-stock/), President Jim Zelter said Apollo originated over $305B in 2025, of which $282B was debt, "approximately 80% IG with an average rating of single A and 20% sub-investment-grade rating with a rating of single B." Rowan put spread at "350 basis points over treasuries with an average rating of BBB."
- **Where Apollo sits.** Apollo's own page draws a ladder by loan-to-value: Credit 0-50% LTV ("often more liquid and shorter holds ... non-control, thematic or trading-oriented, often dislocation-driven"), Hybrid Value 50-80% LTV (average 3-5 year holds, concentrated, "typically non-control, flexible, value-add solutions provider across the cycle"), Private Equity 80-100% LTV (5-7+ year holds, control) (https://www.apollo.com/strategies/asset-management/equity/hybrid-value). Hybrid Value is described as capital "into underserved segments of the market between traditional debt and equity." That ladder is the clearest statement in the whole peer set of how a house slices the same capital structure by attachment point.
- **How it sources.** Origination is the moat: Rowan said Apollo's "historical investment in origination has given us a bit of a competitive moat that others are trying to catch up to" (9 Feb 2026 call). Apollo's write-up of John Zito's Bloomberg Invest interview (4 Mar 2026; text published 15 May 2026) says (paraphrase, Apollo's words not Zito's) that Apollo built an origination ecosystem of specialty finance companies with more than 4,000 employees plus direct large-cap IG origination with Intel, BP, AB InBev and Air France-KLM ( https://www.apollo.com/insights-news/insights/2026/05/private-credit-now-in-an-etf). Platforms named on the call: Atlas, MidCap, Redding Ridge (Apollo's structured-credit/CLO manager; role description is background knowledge, not from the call).
- **How it is funded.** Mostly not by hedge-fund-style capital. Athene (retirement services, annuity liabilities) is the anchor balance sheet; third-party insurance, wealth vehicles (ADS, the non-traded BDC), the PRIV ETF (with State Street) and institutional "fixed income replacement" mandates are the other sources. On the 9 Feb 2026 call an Apollo executive listed "six pockets of demand": institutional alternatives buckets, fixed income replacement, wealth, third-party insurance, traditional asset managers and 401(k). PRIV had $802.8M AUM and about 22.1% Apollo-sourced assets at 15 Oct 2026 (carried from trend_private_credit_convergence.md, State Street page).

### 1.4 How the Apollo seat looks at the same bond as our desk

Take a single-B 2029 term loan or bond that the systematic desk sees as a spread-per-unit-DTS number with a jump-to-default haircut. Apollo's credit arm sees a possible origination: it would rather be the lender that sized and structured the package than buy it in secondary, and its comparison is "excess spread over Treasuries per unit of rating-agency risk," because the liability on the other side (Athene annuities) is a fixed spread it must earn with a capital charge attached. The desk asks "what does the bond do over the next 20 days relative to its factor loadings"; Apollo asks "would I own this to maturity at this spread, and is there a structure that moves it from B to BBB." Apollo does not mark to the screen and does not need to sell; the desk does. The overlap is the dislocation seat on Apollo's own ladder (0-50% LTV, trading-oriented, dislocation-driven), where Apollo is a competitor for the same bonds when spreads gap, and a natural buyer of the desk's inventory when it wants size. A desk that sees Apollo-sourced paper (PRIV, ADS, Redding Ridge CLOs) in an index or an ETF should read it as a carry-seeking holder with long-dated liabilities, which is why it is slow to sell in a drawdown but is a fast, price-insensitive taker of new paper in a rally.

## 2. Ares (direct lending, ARCC, alternative credit)

### 2.1 The seat

- **Who is who (corrected).** Michael Arougheti is Ares Management's CEO. Kipp deVeer was ARCC's CEO from 2014 and, in Feb 2025, was named Co-President of Ares Management (with Blair Jacobson); Kort Schnabel succeeded him as ARCC CEO effective 30 Apr 2025, and Mitchell Goldstein and Michael Smith remain Co-Heads of the Credit Group (search-engine summaries of the 5 Feb 2025 Ares release, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250205547431/en, page itself returned 403; UNVERIFIED against the release). The ARCC Q4 2025 transcript confirms Schnabel as ARCC "Chief Executive Officer" and Jim Miller as President (Motley Fool transcript, 4 Feb 2026, https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/04/ares-capital-arcc-q4-2025-earnings-transcript/).
- **What it buys.** Sponsor-backed first-lien direct loans (ARCC, plus the US and European direct-lending funds and the senior direct lending program), asset-based finance ("alternative credit", Pathfinder), opportunistic credit, liquid credit/CLOs, real estate debt, infrastructure, secondaries and insurance. On the Q2 2026 call Arougheti said Pathfinder III raised "approximately $8.5 billion" against a $6.5B target and that Ares manages "4 of the 5 largest institutional ABF funds" (31 Jul 2026 transcript, https://www.webull.com/news/15342048458400768). Firm AUM was about $671B at 30 Jun 2026 and the firm raised about $36B in Q2, $66B in H1, with $170B of dry powder (same transcript and a summary of the Q2 release).
- **Position and horizon.** ARCC reports loan-to-value "in the high 30% to low 40% range" on first-lien commitments and 37% LTV in software (4 Feb 2026 call). Holding period is to maturity or refinancing, typically 3-5 years, with long-lived perpetual capital (ARCC is a listed BDC; CLOs; wealth vehicles). ARCC's non-accruals were 1.8% at cost and 1.2% at fair value at year-end 2025, against a 2.8% post-GFC ARCC average and a 3.8% BDC average, per Jim Miller (4 Feb 2026). A Reuters-sourced TIKR summary of Arougheti's 10 Jun 2026 Morgan Stanley conference remarks puts corporate-book LTV at about 45% and non-accruals in the low-2% range (secondary: https://www.tikr.com/blog/ares-management-ceo-just-pushed-back-hard-on-the-private-credit-panic-heres-where-the-stock-could-go-in-2026).
- **Sourcing.** Incumbent relationships: Schnabel said that "across our top 10 incumbent transactions during 2025, we more than doubled our share of the overall financing" (4 Feb 2026 call), i.e. scale lets Ares take a bigger slice of its own deals. Sponsors are the source of risk, and Ares is the permanent capital that does not need a syndication window.
- **Funding.** BDC equity and unsecured bonds ($750M of five-year notes at 180 bps over Treasuries, swapped to SOFR+172, per the 4 Feb 2026 call), on-balance-sheet CLOs ($700M priced December 2025 at SOFR+147), wealth channel (Arougheti: wealth is about 10% of Ares's private-credit business, per the TIKR summary) and institutional commingled funds and SMAs. Ares also bought the quant credit manager BlueCove (agreed 29-30 Oct 2025, closed about Feb 2026) to form Ares Systematic Credit, led by Alex Khein, with insurance distribution in mind (carried from trend_systematic_credit_industry.md).

### 2.4 How the Ares seat looks at the same bond as our desk

Ares sees a unitranche or first-lien term loan with a sponsor behind it, a covenant package it negotiated line by line, and an expectation that it will hold and amend rather than trade. The same B-rated loan that the systematic desk trades as a DTS-adjusted spread with a mark-to-market volatility is, for Ares, an income stream with a 40% LTV cushion, a private mark that moves a few points a quarter and a workout option (own the company, as at Pluralsight). Ares's edge is documentation and relationship access (the "walk away from documentation terms" posture); its weakness, which Arougheti himself names, is the funding side (retail redemption queues, "pro-cyclical" wealth flows). The desk's edge is the opposite: a continuously updated price and no queue. Where the two meet is the syndicated B/BB loan or high-yield bond that Ares's liquid-credit and CLO arms also hold: when Ares CLOs and the desk hold the same name, Ares has an information advantage in private-side data and the desk has one in price discovery. That Ares bought BlueCove (a systematic credit shop) is a public admission that the two seats now need each other's tools.
