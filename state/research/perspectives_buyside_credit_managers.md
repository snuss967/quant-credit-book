## 1. Apollo (opportunistic credit, hybrid value, Athene)

### 1.1 The seat (what it buys, horizon, position in the structure, sourcing, funding)

- **Scale and mix.** Apollo's credit arm is "Apollo's largest asset management strategy by AUM": $849B AUM, 600+ investment professionals, 4,000+ issuer relationships as of 30 Jun 2026 (apollo.com credit page, https://www.apollo.com/strategies/asset-management/credit, fetched 2026-10-18). On the 9 Feb 2026 Q4 2025 call (transcript: https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-2-9-apollo-global-management-inc-stock/), President Jim Zelter said Apollo originated over $305B in 2025, of which $282B was debt, "approximately 80% IG with an average rating of single A and 20% sub-investment-grade rating with a rating of single B." Rowan put spread at "350 basis points over treasuries with an average rating of BBB."
- **Where Apollo sits.** Apollo's own page draws a ladder by loan-to-value: Credit 0-50% LTV ("often more liquid and shorter holds ... non-control, thematic or trading-oriented, often dislocation-driven"), Hybrid Value 50-80% LTV (average 3-5 year holds, concentrated, "typically non-control, flexible, value-add solutions provider across the cycle"), Private Equity 80-100% LTV (5-7+ year holds, control) (https://www.apollo.com/strategies/asset-management/equity/hybrid-value). Hybrid Value is described as capital "into underserved segments of the market between traditional debt and equity." That ladder is the clearest statement in the whole peer set of how a house slices the same capital structure by attachment point.
- **How it sources.** Origination is the moat: Rowan said Apollo's "historical investment in origination has given us a bit of a competitive moat that others are trying to catch up to" (9 Feb 2026 call). Apollo's write-up of John Zito's Bloomberg Invest interview (4 Mar 2026; text published 15 May 2026) says (paraphrase, Apollo's words not Zito's) that Apollo built an origination ecosystem of specialty finance companies with more than 4,000 employees plus direct large-cap IG origination with Intel, BP, AB InBev and Air France-KLM ( https://www.apollo.com/insights-news/insights/2026/05/private-credit-now-in-an-etf). Platforms named on the call: Atlas, MidCap, Redding Ridge (Apollo's structured-credit/CLO manager; role description is background knowledge, not from the call).
- **How it is funded.** Mostly not by hedge-fund-style capital. Athene (retirement services, annuity liabilities) is the anchor balance sheet; third-party insurance, wealth vehicles (ADS, the non-traded BDC), the PRIV ETF (with State Street) and institutional "fixed income replacement" mandates are the other sources. On the 9 Feb 2026 call an Apollo executive listed "six pockets of demand": institutional alternatives buckets, fixed income replacement, wealth, third-party insurance, traditional asset managers and 401(k). PRIV had $802.8M AUM and about 22.1% Apollo-sourced assets at 15 Oct 2026 (carried from trend_private_credit_convergence.md, State Street page).

### 1.4 How the Apollo seat looks at the same bond as our desk

Take a single-B 2029 term loan or bond that the systematic desk sees as a spread-per-unit-DTS number with a jump-to-default haircut. Apollo's credit arm sees a possible origination: it would rather be the lender that sized and structured the package than buy it in secondary, and its comparison is "excess spread over Treasuries per unit of rating-agency risk," because the liability on the other side (Athene annuities) is a fixed spread it must earn with a capital charge attached. The desk asks "what does the bond do over the next 20 days relative to its factor loadings"; Apollo asks "would I own this to maturity at this spread, and is there a structure that moves it from B to BBB." Apollo does not mark to the screen and does not need to sell; the desk does. The overlap is the dislocation seat on Apollo's own ladder (0-50% LTV, trading-oriented, dislocation-driven), where Apollo is a competitor for the same bonds when spreads gap, and a natural buyer of the desk's inventory when it wants size. A desk that sees Apollo-sourced paper (PRIV, ADS, Redding Ridge CLOs) in an index or an ETF should read it as a carry-seeking holder with long-dated liabilities, which is why it is slow to sell in a drawdown but is a fast, price-insensitive taker of new paper in a rally.

### 2.4 How the Ares seat looks at the same bond as our desk

Ares sees a unitranche or first-lien term loan with a sponsor behind it, a covenant package it negotiated line by line, and an expectation that it will hold and amend rather than trade. The same B-rated loan that the systematic desk trades as a DTS-adjusted spread with a mark-to-market volatility is, for Ares, an income stream with a 40% LTV cushion, a private mark that moves a few points a quarter and a workout option (own the company, as at Pluralsight). Ares's edge is documentation and relationship access (the "walk away from documentation terms" posture); its weakness, which Arougheti himself names, is the funding side (retail redemption queues, "pro-cyclical" wealth flows). The desk's edge is the opposite: a continuously updated price and no queue. Where the two meet is the syndicated B/BB loan or high-yield bond that Ares's liquid-credit and CLO arms also hold: when Ares CLOs and the desk hold the same name, Ares has an information advantage in private-side data and the desk has one in price discovery. That Ares bought BlueCove (a systematic credit shop) is a public admission that the two seats now need each other's tools.

### 3.2 The seat, events, and the Oaktree reading of our bond

- **Seat.** Oaktree's own timeline: Marks began in high yield in 1978, Bruce Karsh joined for distressed debt in 1988, and the firm describes an "opportunistic, value-oriented and risk-controlled approach" across credit, equity and real estate; Brookfield and Oaktree "establish[ed] partnership" in 2019, "bringing total credit AUM to ~$109B," and 2019-25 growth came through partners such as Castlelake, LCM Partners and Primary Wave in asset-backed finance (https://www.oaktreecapital.com/about, fetched 2026-10-18). It buys across the stack: distressed and opportunistic funds (control or influence, 3-7 year holds), performing private credit (Oaktree Specialty Lending, OCSL), structured credit and CLOs, with Brookfield's insurance business as a growing balance sheet. It is a price-taker at wide spreads and a seller when spreads tighten, which is exactly what the 2022 and 2025 memos say.
- **Views, summarised.** Oaktree's credit view is cyclical and contrarian: "Sea Change" (13 Dec 2022) said the low-return world was over and credit now offered "higher returns and better creditor protections"; "Cockroaches in the Coal Mine" (6 Nov 2025) uses the First Brands and Tricolor failures to say lending quality decays in good times; "What's Going on in Private Credit?" (9 Apr 2026) says rapid deployment created a "goldrush mentality" but that disciplined lenders will do fine and Oaktree kept direct lending and software exposure limited (paraphrase). On bank-style fragility the 17 Apr 2023 SVB memo argues that "banks are, essentially, highly levered fixed income investors" (verified in page text, https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/lessons-from-silicon-valley-bank).
- **Boardriders (Aug 2020): sponsor and participating lender.** About $450M of first-lien debt was held by Oaktree (sponsor), participating lenders and non-consenting lenders; $110M of new super-priority money and a $321M par roll-up created $431M of super-priority debt; ICG, York and others sued in Oct 2020; on 17 Oct 2022 Justice Masley denied dismissal of contract claims but dismissed tortious interference against Oaktree (carried from case_lmt_autopsies_us_2020_2026.md, citing Goodwin). Oaktree here was the uptier's sponsor-lender, not its victim.
- **Optimum cooperation agreement:** named defendant with Apollo, Ares, GoldenTree, Loomis and PGIM (see 2.3).
- **Pluralsight:** Oaktree Specialty Lending was one of the named BDC lenders and marked its equity to zero in Feb 2026 (carried, secondary).

**How Oaktree would look at our bond.** Marks's test is "what is the price relative to value, and what is the cycle doing to the spread," not "what is the factor exposure." A bond that the desk shorts because momentum and DTS are rich is for Oaktree an unsecured claim on a recovery waterfall it can estimate: it buys below a 60-70 cent recovery estimate, holds through a restructuring, and is happy to take equity. The disagreement is horizon and what counts as evidence: the desk refuses to hold a loser through a drawdown without a signal; Oaktree considers the drawdown the entry point, provided it has a margin of safety and non-forced funding. Where they agree is the insistence that "the worst of loans are made in the best of times," which a systematic desk expresses as a late-cycle underwriting-quality factor (covenant-lite share, PIK share, add-back size).

## 4. Elliott (public letters, positions in credit events)

### 4.1 The seat and what is publicly known

- **Seat.** Elliott Investment Management (Paul Singer; credit led by a large distressed/special-situations team) is a multi-strategy hedge-fund/activist firm: it buys defaulted sovereign and corporate debt, term loans, bonds and sometimes the equity; it forms or joins creditor groups; it sues; and it will buy the asset (Amber Energy). Horizon is "until the legal or negotiation catalyst resolves," frequently 2-8 years (Argentina took 15). Funding is permanent partner capital plus fund capital, with lock-ups that tolerate long litigation. Sourcing is secondary-market accumulation plus information from litigation and negotiation.
- **Public letters.** Elliott's investor letters are private. In the sources I could fetch there is no verifiable Elliott credit letter, and Singer's recent private-credit remarks (a Toronto conference talk mentioning investors providing liquidity to insolvent companies) came from an article I could not fetch (Fa-Mag, 403), so I do not quote them. Elliott's "public voice" in credit is its litigation, court filings and the statements of vehicles it controls. This is a gap, not an absence.

### 4.2 Roles in named credit events (public record)

- **Argentina (default 2001; holdouts; settlement 29 Feb 2016).** NPR (29 Feb 2016): a hedge-fund group "led by Paul Singer's Elliott Management" refused the restructurings and sued; Judge Griesa's injunction barred paying other creditors without paying the holdouts; the Supreme Court in 2014 left the rulings in place; Argentina agreed to pay four holdouts $4.653B, "about 75 percent of what they claimed they were owed" (https://www.publicradiotulsa.org/topics/2016-02-29/argentina-reaches-settlement-with-hedge-funds-ending-15-year-dispute). A search summary lists Elliott, Aurelius, Davidson Kempner and Bracebridge as the four.
- **Citgo / PDV Holding (Nov 2025): plan sponsor in all but name, via Amber Energy.** Reuters (via Pipeline & Gas Journal, 1 Dec 2025): on 25 Nov 2025 Judge Leonard Stark (D. Del.) approved a $5.9B bid from an Elliott affiliate in the court-run auction of Citgo's parent; the bid "offers a $2.1 billion payment to the holders of a defaulted Venezuelan bond collateralized with Citgo equity," and Stark wrote that "The Amber Bid offers the best overall combination of price and certainty of closing of any bid submitted" (https://pgjonline.com/news/2025/december/us-judge-approves-59-billion-elliott-bid-for-citgo-parent-pdv-holding). Gold Reserve alleged conflicts, including "$170 million in fees collected by the special master's advisors from affiliates of Elliott and the 2020 bondholders involved in Elliott's bid" (an allegation by an objector, same article). The sale awaited OFAC approval and Third Circuit appeals (search summary; check status).
- **Veritas (2024): holdout-turned-blocker.** Hedgeweek (25 Jun 2024, citing the FT): Elliott and allies, holding more than half of Veritas's debt ($2.5B loans, $1.8B bonds), objected to Carlyle's proposal that they said paid under 60 cents on the dollar in cash; Elliott "has already formed" its own cooperation group (summary; https://www.hedgeweek.com/elliott-disrupts-carlyles-4bn-debt-restructuring-at-software-co-veritas/).
- **Thames Water (2025-26): Class A creditor leader.** With Apollo, Silver Point and others in London & Valley Water (Hedgeweek, 3 Sep 2025; see 1.3).
- **Braskem (2026):** bought parts of the revolver and international bonds, expected to push for terms different from the company's extension-only proposal, acting independently of other holders (Hedgeweek, 19 Jun 2026, citing Bloomberg; https://www.hedgeweek.com/elliott-and-svp-build-braskem-debt-positions-as-restructuring-negotiations-intensify/).

### 4.3 How Elliott looks at our bond

For Elliott a bond is an option on a legal process. It reads the indenture for the clause that creates blocking power (the one-third, two-thirds or majority threshold), prices the court as the final counterparty, and buys the position that holds the veto. A thirty-cent bond that the desk treats as a binary jump-to-default is for Elliott a long-dated claim whose value depends on a ruling, a sale order or a co-op. It is the buyer of last resort for exactly the paper whose short-horizon holders are forced sellers (CLOs at the CCC bucket, index-tracking funds), and the party that turns "excluded lender" into "plan sponsor." The desk should model Elliott-type holders as a state variable: when a known blocker accumulates, hazard of a non-pro-rata deal falls and recovery variance rises.

### 5.1 Blackstone Credit & Insurance (BXCI)

- **Seat.** BXCI had $443.0B AUM and about 815 employees at 31 Dec 2025, with "liquid corporate credit" strategies that explicitly include systematic strategies; Blackstone bought the quant credit firm DCI in 2020 and runs Blackstone Corporate Bond Strategies (BCBS), described in the shelf PDF as managing "over $40bn" (carried from trend_systematic_credit_industry.md; Blackstone FY2025 10-K). Funding mixes insurance separately managed accounts (the 10-Q says insurance-platform SMAs "can generally only be terminated for long-term underperformance," against 15 to 95 days' notice for other SMAs; Blackstone 10-Q, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001393818/000119312526340208/d158269d10q.htm, read via WebFetch), the semi-liquid BCRED ($94.6B total assets, $42.8B NAV at 30 Jun 2026) and the listed BXSL (ACI, 23 Jul 2026, https://alternativecreditinvestor.com/2026/07/23/blackstone-private-credit-strategy-returns-6-8pc-in-12-months/).
- **Stated view.** Jon Gray, President, 23 Jul 2026 (Q2 call): "It's early in the third quarter but the redemptions in BCRED are down materially, which is positive" (same ACI page). BCRED holders asked to redeem 10% in Q2 versus 7.9% in Q1; Blackstone repurchased the customary 5% (ACI). Schwarzman on the same call credited "our decision to lean into the artificial intelligence megatrend" (not a credit view).
- **Role in a named event: Hovnanian (Jan 2018), CDS-linked lender.** Judge Swain (S.D.N.Y., Solus Alternative Asset Management LP v. GSO Capital Partners L.P., No. 18 CV 232-LTS-BCM, 29 Jan 2018) found: "GSO purchased a substantial position in CDS protection contracts, as well as substantial positions in Hovnanian debt and equity securities, in anticipation of the Hovnanian transaction"; Hovnanian covenanted to default on a payment on bonds held by an affiliate although it "has sufficient resources to make the payment," expected to trigger a CDS "Credit Event," and the court denied Solus's preliminary injunction for failure to show irreparable harm (opinion text, https://cdsdeterminationscommittees.org/documents/2018/03/opinion03132018.pdf). GSO was Blackstone's credit arm. This is the canonical manufactured credit event (Ch 24).
- **Same bond, BXCI view.** BXCI is the one house in the peer set that both originates private loans and runs a published systematic public-bond engine; it sees the bond as a candidate for an insurance SMA with a ratings and capital constraint, and for BCBS as a factor exposure. Its edge over our desk is balance-sheet breadth (the same borrower may be in BXSL, BCRED, CLOs and BCBS); its weakness is that the largest vehicles are semi-liquid, so when redemptions queue, public-credit liquidity is the release valve.

KKR IR head Craig Larson (30 Jul 2026 Q2 call, https://www.webull.com/news/15341674978878464): "we look at private IG as all we are doing in asset-based finance plus bespoke solutions, for large established corporates plus long-duration investments across real estate" and the addressable credit market is "$45 trillion." On software: it is "about 6% of our AUM" and one software business was sold at 4.5x cost (same transcript).

KKR is thus a sponsor-and-equity name here, not a creditor-coop member.

### 5.3 HPS (BlackRock)

- **Seat.** Large-cap, sponsor-backed direct lending and mezzanine/special situations. BlackRock agreed in Dec 2024 to buy HPS in a roughly $12B all-stock deal, with founders Scott Kapnick, Scot French and Michael Patterson leading the combined private financing solutions unit (Bloomberg Law, https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/blackrock-to-buy-credit-firm-hps-in-12-billion-all-stock-deal; search summary, completion date not confirmed). The HPS Corporate Lending Fund enforced a 5% limit on withdrawals in March 2026 (Advisor Perspectives, 29 Jul 2026, paywalled; search summary). I could not fetch a verifiable Kapnick or HPS statement this session, so no HPS quote is given.
- **Roles in a named event: Revlon (May-Aug 2020), excluded lender and then wire recipient.** The Second Circuit (Citibank, N.A. v. Brigade Capital Management, LP, No. 21-487, 8 Sep 2022; opinion text parsed, https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/21-487_complete_opn.pdf) lists HPS Investment Partners among the defendant-appellees, "Loan Managers for lenders of the majority of the loan." In May 2020 Revlon borrowed new money (an $815M facility; a $1.7B new facility in aggregate, over $800M of it new financing) from "a subset of the 2016 Lenders," using collateral that had secured the 2016 loan; all defendants except Tall Tree authorized UMB Bank to sue Revlon and Citibank on 12 Aug 2020, alleging the deal would "siphon away substantially all of the collateral from the 2016 Term Lenders." Then Citibank mistakenly wired principal on 11 Aug 2020; the recipients got "approximately $500 million," while loan participations "were trading at 20% to 30% of the face amount." The court vacated Judge Furman's judgment for the defendants and remanded, holding the discharge-for-value defence unavailable because the debt "was not due for another three years." HPS is therefore both an excluded lender (the BrandCo-style priming) and the beneficiary of an error that was reversed.
- **Same bond, HPS view.** A large-ticket first-lien or unitranche with a bank-like takeout; HPS holds to maturity, is paid for scale and speed, and is exposed on the retail redemption side.

### 5.4 Sixth Street

- **Seat.** "A leading global investment firm" founded in 2009 with "$140B+ Assets Under Management" at 30 Jun 2026, 750+ team members and about 300 investment professionals; it describes itself as "one unified team of investment and control-side professionals" and runs direct lending (TSLX is its listed BDC), insurance solutions (a reinsurance tie-up with Resolution Life for Allianz's U.S. fixed index annuities is listed on its site), ABF and public markets (https://sixthstreet.com/, fetched 2026-10-18). It buys across the stack and across asset classes with flexible capital.
- **Stated view.** Sixth Street's page (8 Apr 2026) for Alan Waxman's "Invest Like the Best" interview says he discusses "cyclical market disruptions and the structural forces," the "factory model" and "the mismatch of assets and liabilities," and that it "all comes down to your clarity of purpose" (https://sixthstreet.com/video_gallery/invest-like-the-best-alan-waxman-on-private-credit-and-the-modern-financial-system/). Secondary summaries (Antoine Buteau, 26 Jun 2026) paraphrase: capital raised in advance pressures managers to deploy; periodic-liquidity vehicles should not hold long-duration illiquid assets. Waxman is therefore the loudest critic, among large managers, of the scale-and-retail model.
- **Role in a named event: Airbnb (Apr 2020).** Silver Lake and Sixth Street invested $1B of debt plus warrants convertible at a roughly $18B valuation, against $31B at the prior round (Skift, 6 Apr 2020, https://skift.com/2020/04/06/airbnb-grabs-1-billion-investment-from-silver-lake-and-sixth-street-partners/). This is the textbook liquidity-provider seat: price and structure paid for speed.
- **Same bond, Sixth Street view.** It asks "what is the counterparty's problem, and can we structure a solution priced above the market's clearing level," so the same bond is of interest only if there is a structuring angle (warrants, secured tranche, or covenant protection).
