# TREND DOSSIER: Listed credit futures (CME, Cboe iBoxx IBHY/IBIG, ICE, Eurex) and CDX/iTraxx index microstructure, 2021-2026

*Researcher dossier for the book "Systematic & Quantitative Credit". Written 2026-10-18. Feeds: ch10b (credit index futures and index microstructure), ch08 (isolating IDO and empirical duration), ch20 (credit ETF creation/redemption arb), appC (trend tracker).*

## 1. Headline trends

Seven trends, each backed by rows in the evidence table below.

1. **A real but small market that has grown fast.** US-dollar listed credit futures open interest went from about $190m (Cboe alone, January 2024) to about $3.8bn (Cboe $2.17bn plus CME about $1.6bn, August 2026), and Eurex adds EUR 3.4-4.0bn. Part of that is new venues arriving (CME June 2024, Eurex USD September 2024, ICE December 2024), not the same contracts growing. Even so, the combined Cboe and CME ADV of about $660m is roughly 2% of CDX IG plus HY average daily volume ($36.9bn in Q2 2026), and open interest is about 0.1% of cleared CDS open interest ($3.08tn).
2. **Volume is partly purchased.** CME turnover (ADV over OI) was 0.76 in February 2026 and about 0.16 in August; Cboe 0.22 and 0.19; Eurex 0.04 and 0.03. Market sources tie CME's high turnover to market-maker rebates, and all three exchanges now run incentive programmes (Eurex from 2025-08-01, CBOT's APAC programme from 2026-10-01). Open interest, block counts and quoted spreads are the safer measures of real adoption.
3. **Size goes to blocks and basis tools, not the lit book.** Cboe blocks passed $1bn a month in July and August 2026 (about a third of IBHY and just under 40% of IBIG volume); Eurex reports 1,604 blocks worth over EUR 41bn; CME's February 2026 blocks were under 1.5% of notional. Trade at Index Close (TAIC), Basis Trade at Index Close (BTIC) and EFP-Index let dealers swap a bond or ETF basket for futures at the index close.
4. **Four exchanges, four index philosophies, no standard.** Cboe: the ETF holdings (HYG, LQD). CME and Eurex: Bloomberg broad and liquidity-screened indices. ICE: MSCI with MarketAxess liquidity scores. CME's duration-hedged pair (DHB, DHY) is the one a quant will want, because it sells credit spread without a separate Treasury hedge, and DHB was about 60% of CME ADV in August 2026.
5. **Positioning became visible in 2026.** The CFTC's weekly Traders in Financial Futures file added CME's IQB and HYB in March and May 2026: asset managers are net short, leveraged funds net long, dealers in the middle. Cboe's iBoxx contracts are not in the file.
6. **The CDX and iTraxx market underneath is growing too, and stays two-tier.** H1 2026 index notional was $12.5tn (+18.5%), 99% of CDX cleared, 98% on SEFs, clearing concentrated at ICE (about 90% of USD CDX), with LCH gaining share; swaptions are the fastest-growing slice. Flow is calendared by the March and September rolls (over $3tn cleared in one week of September 2021).
7. **The evidence is thin and mostly sponsored.** Most listed-credit numbers come from exchange-sponsored trade-press pieces. We found no independent study of tracking error or lead-lag between futures, ETFs and CDX, and the one academic paper on the iBoxx futures has a weak design (see the evidence row).

## 3. Narrative: drivers and the 2026-2028 outlook

**Why a listed layer at all.** Credit has long offered two ways to buy a basket: assemble a thousand bonds, or sign an ISDA and trade the CDX. Listed futures are a third door into the same room. They do not offer a better price (CDX IG trades about $29bn a day, in average clips of $84.5m, 99% cleared and on SEFs). They offer a different bundle of frictions: no ISDA, no borrow to short, a one-legged exposure unlike a total return swap, and margin that nets against rates futures. Eurex's own example, live since February 2025, cuts margin on a hedged EUR IG position from 2.25m to 0.74m. For a futures-only CTA, which cannot sign swap documents and cannot trade European cash credit, this is the first way in. Susquehanna's 2024 forecast of three phases (asset managers replacing long/short beta, then CTAs, then XVA desks hedging for FRTB) now looks well into phase one, with phases two and three still unproven.

**How the volume got built.** The growth curve is steep and uneven. USD listed ADV climbed from about $56m in January 2024 to $398m in August 2025 and about $660m in August 2026, while volume per unit of open interest swung between exchanges and months. CME's ADV was 11x the prior year in November 2025, yet its February 2026 turnover of 0.76 sat beside a thin top of book; market sources attributed that to rebates, and CME declined to specify them and filed a FOIA confidential-treatment request. Eurex started a Credit Index Derivatives Partnership Program on 1 August 2025 with eight named quoters; CBOT filed an APAC market-maker programme for October 2026 to May 2027. Cboe's own history carries a warning: IBHY volume fell to 26% of its 2022 level in 2023 for reasons no document explains. A reader should treat ADV as a policy variable of the exchange and open interest, block count and quoted width as the market's actual vote.

**What the contracts are.** Cboe's choice was to intersect a benchmark with the holdings of HYG and LQD, so the future is in effect the ETF portfolio with a settlement date. Eurex and CME chose Bloomberg indices, the broad US Corporate index (8,611 bonds, $7.3tn) and the HY Very Liquid index (1,102 bonds, $1.0tn); ICE let MarketAxess liquidity scores pick the bonds. CME then went a step further with duration-hedged versions that add key-rate-matched short Treasury futures (2, 5, 10, 30 and Ultra) to the parent index so that option-adjusted duration is zero. The holder is long carry and spread; the rate hedge is a rule run by the index provider, not a decision of the trader. That is attractive, and it is also a model: the residual left by convexity and by mismatched spread and rate sensitivities is the trader's problem, and none of the sources measure it.

**How they trade.** Liquidity is calendared. CFE volume piles up in the last week before quarterly expiry, Eurex roll months run about three times non-roll ADV, and CME ADV was $962m in June against under $300m in July and August. Size moves in blocks: an average CDX HY ticket of $21.5m equals about 115 IBHY contracts, roughly a third of a quiet day's volume, and a $100m clip is more than a whole quiet day. During the April 2025 tariff week the Cboe pair printed about 1,400-2,150 contracts a day, a few tenths of a billion dollars, which was a rounding error next to the swap market. In March 2026, the nearest thing to a test, Eurex posted a record EUR 742m ADV while Cboe ADV fell from $435m to $240m; only the exchanges describe the market as resilient.

**Who holds it.** The CFTC file shows a hedger-lever pairing. In IQB, asset managers held 6,656 short against 1,369 long contracts on 5 May 2026, leveraged funds held 3,006 long against 1,026 short, and dealers sat net long about 3,400. In HYB the May pairing of roughly 3,000 asset-manager shorts against leveraged-fund longs unwound over the summer, leaving dealers net long. The file cannot say whether the leveraged longs are directional, basis or relative-value trades, and a 4,400-contract jump between buckets on 14 July looks like a reclassification, not trading.

**The index market underneath.** CDX and iTraxx remain the deep pool. Collin-Dufresne, Junge and Trolle show a two-tier structure in which dealer-to-client trades cost more than interdealer ones because client flow carries lasting price impact; Riggs, Onur, Reiffen and Zhu show customers asking few dealers for quotes. Cleared flow comes in waves around the IMM roll dates, and the index spread jumps at each six-month roll (Bai and Shachar). The old index-versus-intrinsic basis stays near zero in calm markets and reached 60bp in 2008-09; its new neighbour, the futures-versus-CDX basis, is described in the trade press as an arbitrage but is not published anywhere we could find.

**Outlook, 2026-2028 (extrapolation, not forecast).** If USD listed ADV grows 50% a year and CDX IG plus HY stays near $37bn, listed ADV reaches about $1.5bn by August 2028, roughly 4% of CDX; open interest at +60% a year would reach about $9.7bn, about a quarter of one percent of cleared CDS open interest. Five forces will decide whether that happens. Incentive programmes will end or be renegotiated, and the volumes they bought may leave with them. The index close is a single fragile print: Rapp, Shin, Zhou and Zhu find about 19bp of distortion in end-of-day bond prices from delayed reporting and delayed Treasury spotting, and every futures contract settles to it. Four index families may fragment liquidity as easily as they diversify it. CME's S&P UBS leveraged-loan index future (LVE) points to the same model extending into loans. And the CFTC file, thin today, will become a genuine positioning dataset as trader counts stay above the reporting threshold.

## 4. Implications for a systematic credit desk and for the RSSG-to-quant reader

**For a systematic credit desk.** The first thing the listed layer changes is who can show up. Trend and carry sleeves in credit have run through CDX and iTraxx for years (AQR's out-of-sample test used five CDS indices; Man AHL sold protection on the indices), which meant an ISDA, a clearing relationship and a swap-execution account. A futures-only CTA now needs none of that, and the CFTC's new weekly file will show how many of them turn up. The sleeve's cost curve acquires a calendar: liquidity is cheapest and thickest in the last week before quarterly expiry, the CDX roll weeks compete for the same dealer balance sheet, and a sleeve above roughly $50m of HY beta will be a block customer rather than a lit-book taker. For hedging idiosyncratic alpha, the menu is now CDX HY, IBHY, HYB, DHY and HYG, and the choice is a matter of DTS match, basis noise, margin and tax more than of spread. Capacity is the quiet constraint: the whole US listed layer trades about 2% of CDX volume, so a large book can move the thing it is hedging with. And since most numbers in this market are published by the exchanges that earn from it, the desk's first quant project is its own measurement of tracking error, turnover and quoted width.

**For the RSSG-to-quant reader.** The restructuring seat teaches that a price is a claim on a legal waterfall. The listed index teaches the opposite lesson: here the price is a claim on a rule. The future settles to an index close built from evaluated bond prices, the basket is fixed by methodology and rebalanced monthly, and whether a stressed or fallen-angel bond sits in the basket is a matter of index rules rather than of credit judgement. A reader who knows what happens to a bond between a downgrade and a default can read those rules, and the forced flows around them, better than a pure statistician; the New York Fed's finding that an issuer added to CDX.NA.IG widened about 12bp over seven days is the kind of mechanical flow effect that this seat spots early. CDX also settles credit events through the ISDA auction process the reader has watched from the other side, while a cash-bond future simply lets the bond drop out of the index (the exact treatment of defaulted and fallen-angel bonds should be read in the Bloomberg and iBoxx methodology documents, which we did not retrieve). The bridge is therefore simple to state: the reader already understands the instruments that sit inside the basket, and now needs to learn the rulebook of the container.

## 5. Quant hooks (variables, datasets, signals)

- **Turnover and incentive proxy.** ADV divided by OI by exchange and month (CME 0.76 in February 2026 against 0.16 in August); event study around Eurex's 2025-08-01 programme and CBOT's 2026-10-01 programme. Data: CME, Cboe and Eurex monthly statistics (via The DESK until primary files are pulled), Cboe's cfevoloi.csv daily file.
- **Roll-window share.** Fraction of a contract's volume in the five days before expiry (computable now from cfevoloi.csv) and the cleared-CDX roll-week share (ICE reports; Clarus CCPView); use as a liquidity-timing feature for the cost model.
- **Block share and block size.** Cboe monthly blocks over total (about a third of IBHY, just under 40% of IBIG in August 2026), Eurex blocks and distance from mid (47% of EUR IG block notional within 2.5 cents of mid).
- **Positioning from CFTC TFF.** Weekly net by trader category for IQB and HYB (dataset gpe5-46if): asset-manager short share of OI, leveraged-fund net, dealer net; needs a structural-break filter for reclassification jumps such as 2026-07-14.
- **Tracking and basis series.** Futures versus index (CME's vendor 1.57-2.47% annualised), futures versus HYG/LQD NAV (not published; needs CFE settlements plus ETF NAV), futures versus CDX (needs a spread-to-price conversion with the index DV01); index close distortion (about 19bp, Rapp et al.).
- **Duration-hedge residual.** Regress DHB/DHY returns on Treasury key rates and on the parent index to measure the leftover rate and convexity exposure; compare with a self-built IQB-minus-Treasury hedge.
- **CDX microstructure features.** On-the-run series, days since roll, index-versus-intrinsic basis (duration-weighted constituent spreads), swaption-to-index notional ratio (about 12% in the SDR USD data, March 2026), average trade size, cleared share. Data: ISDA SwapsInfo, DTCC and CFTC SDR, Clarus, ICE and LCH clearing reports.
- **Margin as a cost-curve input.** Eurex Prisma margin per 100m (1.22%-2.68%) against CDS IM, CME's automatic rate offsets; convert to funding cost per DV01.
