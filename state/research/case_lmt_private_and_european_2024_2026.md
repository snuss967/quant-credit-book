## 1. Pluralsight (2024): the private-side J.Crew

**Instruments.** Vista Equity Partners took Pluralsight private in April 2021 (reported value about $3.5bn per Goodwin and L&G; Reuters says $3.9bn, so the basis is CHECK), financed partly by a $1.175bn first-lien recurring-revenue term loan plus a $100mm revolver [Goodwin; L&G; Reuters-Vista]. Pre-LME, Reuters put non-bank debt at over $1.5bn. The loan paid SOFR plus 800 bp (13.56% all-in at 31 Dec 2023) and matured April 2027 [ARCC-Q2]. An ARR loan is sized and covenanted on recurring revenue and liquidity, not EBITDA leverage, so the first signal of trouble is a revenue or renewal miss, not a leverage breach.

**Timeline.**
- Q1 2023: Vista sought covenant relief and put in $75mm of equity; Vista fund documents valued the equity at $2.3bn (Q1 2023), $1.5bn (Q4 2023) and zero (update of 28 May 2024) [Reuters-Vista].
- Apr 2024: a 15 April update to Vista investors warned of "liquidity challenges"; CEO Aaron Skonnard was replaced by Chris Walters; the 2024 renewal rate was projected in the low 70s percent [Reuters-Vista].
- Late May to early summer 2024: Pluralsight moved certain IP into a new wholly owned restricted subsidiary and borrowed about $50mm from Vista (reports differ: loan or preferred) to pay the next interest instalment; KBRA called these "reasonable and allowable stopgap measures" permitted by the documents [KBRA]. Creditors reportedly alleged the transferred assets were mis-valued [Goodwin].
- 3 Jun 2024 (Bloomberg) and 2 Jul 2024 (Reuters): Vista in talks to cede control. 30 Jun: BDCs mark the loan near 47-48 cents [ARCC-Q2; OBDC].
- About 22 Aug 2024: a Blue Owl- and Ares-led group takes ownership with about $275mm of new money ($125mm funded that week) and about $1.2bn of debt converted to equity; lenders about 85%, management about 15%; Vista and co-investors lose about $4bn [BBG-PS]. Goodwin's 12 Aug alert, written before signing, had lenders at 100% and $250mm new money, so the figures moved before signing.

**Price and mark path.** The same loan, same coupon, same maturity was held at different values by different lenders:

| Holder | 31 Dec 2023 | 31 Mar 2024 | 30 Jun 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ares Capital | 89.0 | 84.9 | 48.0 |
| Blue Owl Capital Corp | 96.8 | 83.5 | 47.0 |
| Range across seven BDCs (KBRA) | 89.0 to 99.0 | 83.4 to 97.0 | n/a |

Sources: [ARCC-Q2; OBDC; KBRA]. At 31 Mar 2024 the seven holders KBRA tabulated ran from 83.4 to 97.0, a 13.6-point spread on one loan; the Dec 2023 gap between Blue Owl and Ares was 7.8 points (mine). The 36.9-point single-quarter drop in the Ares mark straddles Vista's 28 May write-off and the IP transfer. KBRA counted BDC first-lien exposure at about $375mm fair value of about $700mm total BDC exposure, below 1% of investments for five of its rated BDCs, and expected no rating effect [KBRA]. Ares booked $(40)mm of unrealized depreciation on the name in Q2 2024 [ARCC-Q2]; Blue Owl Capital Corp's position went from $100.9mm fair value (1.7% of net assets) to $49.7mm (0.8%) [OBDC].

**Dollar outcome and later path.** At 30 Sep 2024 Ares carried $54.7mm of new paper at cost ($21.4mm first lien at S+450 with 1.5% PIK maturing 2029; $19.0mm second tranche at S+750 all PIK; $14.3mm of common units) against $106.5mm of old cost, and booked a $60mm realized loss [ARCC-Q3]. Blue Owl Capital Corp likewise went from about $105mm of par to $54.6mm of new loans and units, carried at cost [OBDC]. Then the marks went down in steps for Ares: 54.7 (Sep 2024), 52.1 (Jun 2025), 46.5 (Sep 2025), 40.1 (Dec 2025), 23.1 (Mar 2026), 21.8 (Jun 2026), which is 20.5% of original cost (mine). By Jun 2026 only the $21.8mm first-lien piece (margin cut to S+300, all cash) sits at par; the second tranche and equity are zero [ARCC-later]. Oaktree and Ares wrote the equity to zero in Feb 2026, and Goldman Sachs BDC placed its first lien on non-accrual at 31 Dec 2025 [BGOV; GSBD]. The post-LME paper was itself re-cut (second-tranche cash margin cut by Sep 2025; first-tranche margin S+450 to S+300 by Mar 2026), so the restructured loan was restructured again [ARCC-later].

**Mechanics and what was new.** Goodwin called it "one of, if not, the first sponsor funded LME" in private credit. There was no court, no vote of excluded holders and no ruling; the lenders were a club of large managers who could and did coordinate. The structural point for Ch 17b is that a drop-down into a restricted subsidiary was permitted by documents that a syndicated-market lawyer would have flagged, and the lenders' answer was not litigation but taking the keys.

**Players on the public record.** Vista (sponsor); lenders Blue Owl, Ares, Goldman Sachs BDC, Franklin BSP, BlackRock, Oaktree, Golub (KBRA, L&G); Guggenheim also named in Bloomberg's account [BBG-PS]. No outside litigation counsel is public.

## 2. Zayo (2025): a fibre credit that amended-and-extended its way across the maturity wall

**Why it is here.** Zayo is the control case: a heavily levered, sponsor-owned credit with a 2027 wall that did the amend-and-extend without a default, a haircut or a court, and whose bonds never left the mid-90s. A model that flags every wall-facing, cooperation-agreement credit as an LME will be wrong here.

**Instruments.** Total debt of about $10bn, including a term loan of nearly $5bn due 2027, other secured debt and unsecured notes due 2028; sponsors EQT and DigitalBridge [BBG-Zayo; Debtwire]. An ad hoc group of lenders and noteholders held about $8.6bn of secured and unsecured debt (Houlihan Lokey deal summary, snippet only, CHECK) [GD].

**Timeline.**
- Jun 2024: Zayo put its non-North American operations into separate entities and received just over $1bn through an intercompany loan and cash (Bloomberg, 18 Jul 2025) [BBG-Zayo].
- 19 Dec 2024: Debtwire reports Zayo asked lenders for a haircut in A&E talks and failed to reach agreement; first-lien term loan quoted "mid-90s". 23 Dec 2024: first-lien lenders sign a cooperation agreement (Bloomberg) [Debtwire; BBG-Zayo].
- Jan 2025: inaugural $1.42bn fiber ABS; Mar 2025: Crown Castle agrees to sell its fiber business to Zayo; 17 Apr 2025: A&E talks revived with a lender steering committee, Gibson Dunn and Houlihan Lokey advising the group, and the Crown Castle financing folded into the talks [BBG-Zayo; GD].
- 18 Jul 2025: 2027 term loan about 96 cents; nearly-agreed deal reported. 14 Aug 2025: transaction support agreement with holders of more than 95% of term loans, secured and unsecured notes [Zayo-PR].
- 26-29 Sep 2025: closing at above 99.8% participation; 2027 secured debt extended three years to March 2030; 2028 unsecured extended 2.5 years to September 2030; $2.0bn secured paydown at closing funded by earlier ABS proceeds; future ABS permitted with proceeds applied to existing debt [Zayo-PR]. Coupons and fees were not disclosed in the release; Houlihan Lokey's summary (snippet, CHECK) cites participation fees, immediate rate increases that escalate, and step-ups if paydown thresholds are missed, plus $200mm of unsecured paper uptiered to first-lien first-out [GD].
- 1 May 2026: Crown Castle closed the $8.5bn gross sale of its fiber and small-cell businesses (Fiber Solutions to Zayo; Small Cell to an EQT fund) with $8.4bn of cash proceeds [CCI-8K]. A further $2.37bn ABS in April 2026 took total Zayo ABS above $6bn (snippet, CHECK).

**Mechanics.** The $2.0bn paydown was about 27 points of the participating secured debt per the Houlihan Lokey summary, which would imply a secured pool of about $7.4bn (mine, CHECK). The economic trade was cash now and a longer tail in exchange for letting Zayo move cash-flowing fibre into ABS vehicles that sit ahead of the loan lenders in the claim on those revenues. That reading is my inference from the documents' permission for future ABS, not a quoted statement.

**Outcome.** No default, no court, no rating of the event as a distressed exchange is cited in the sources. The lenders' lever was cohesion (a cooperation agreement signed within days of the failed talks), and the company's lever was an asset-backed take-out that shrank the loan before the wall.

**Players.** Sponsors EQT and DigitalBridge; ad hoc group with Gibson Dunn and Houlihan Lokey; CFO Jeff Noto; Crown Castle as the asset seller.

## 3. Altice France (2024-2025): Sauvegarde Accelerée, the non-participants and the co-op

**Debt addressed.** About EUR24.1bn of debt (about EUR23.3bn funded): a EUR1.2bn revolver; about EUR7.1bn of term loans in seven tranches (including EUR3.669bn at SOFR plus 550 due Aug 2028); about EUR10.9bn of senior secured notes in eleven series due Feb 2027 to Oct 2029 with coupons from 3.375% to 11.500%; and about EUR4bn at the Altice France Holding (AFH) level [Elevenflo; IFR-Award]. At end-2024 net debt was above EUR21bn, 6.2x EBITDA, against 2024 adjusted EBITDA of EUR3.435bn (-9.9%) and interest above EUR2.6bn [Paperjam]. Disposals of about EUR2.5bn were done along the way: UltraEdge 70% (May 2024), Altice Media for EUR1.55bn (2 Jul 2024) and 49% of La Poste Telecom for EUR500mm (Nov 2024) [Paperjam].

**Timeline.**
- Mar 2024 (exact day not reported): at an investor call Drahi outlined plans to cut debt; the debt fell about 20 cents on the dollar, and a creditor cooperation agreement formed that evening [IFR-Award]. The co-op grew to about 200 institutions holding about EUR19bn of opco-secured debt; named members include Elliott, Anchorage, Sona and Sculptor, with Pimco and BlackRock major holders and Arini leading in the holdco debt [IFR-Award].
- Nov 2024: negotiations begin; late Feb 2025: framework agreement, with an early-signing premium of 2.5% for signing by 19 Mar 2025 (Octus says 12 Mar) [Octus; Elevenflo].
- 15 May 2025: AFH misses interest on its 8% notes due 2027; 27 May: request for accelerated safeguard filed [DC-Hold; Paperjam].
- 9 Jun 2025: the EMEA CDS Determinations Committee votes 11-0 that a Bankruptcy Credit Event occurred on 27 May [AF-DC]. 10 Jun: Paris Commercial Court opens the proceedings; 17 Jun: Chapter 15 petition, S.D.N.Y. No. 25-11349 (Judge Wiles) [Octus; Elevenflo, CHECK against docket].
- 25 Jun 2025: DC finds Failure to Pay on the AFH notes; 2 Jul: no auction for that name [DC-Hold].
- 4 Aug 2025: Paris court approves the plans; 27 Aug: Altice France CDS auction clears at 87.75; 1 Oct 2025: implementation complete; 12 Feb 2026: Chapter 15 cases closed [AF-FY25; IFR-CDS; Elevenflo].

**Terms and non-participants.** Opco term-loan and secured-note holders received about 7.6% in cash (about EUR1.5bn), plus 2.5% for early signers; about 77% of principal in new senior secured debt with coupons 137.5 to 142.5 bp higher and maturities about 2.75 years longer; and about 31% of new equity. The revolver was refinanced at par into a five-year revolver at EURIBOR plus 3.30%. AFH senior bondholders received 2.5% in cash, 20% in new 2033 debt, 14% of the equity and conditional warrants. The Drahi chain fell from 100% to 55%; creditors hold 45% [Paperjam; Delano; Elevenflo]. Headline debt reduction is EUR8.6bn; Elevenflo counts about EUR7.5bn eliminated and Ropes counts over EUR8bn (search snippet) [Paperjam; Elevenflo]. The French process binds a class at two-thirds by value, so non-signers got identical plan terms minus the early-bird 2.5%; the recorded votes were RCF 100% in favour and 100% of the term-loan and notes class on 95.16% participation, with no affected creditor objecting (secondary, CHECK) [Elevenflo].

**Price paths.** The one reported price shock is the 20-cent fall on the March 2024 call [IFR-Award]. The CDS settled at 87.75, or 12.25 per 100 of protection; bank traders quoted 86.25 earlier that day; only EUR38mm of bonds were physically requested and all filled at 87.75, against DTCC net notional of $232mm [IFR-CDS]. A 95% lock-up threatened to starve the auction of deliverables, so the committee, after more than two months and 22 meetings, created a "composite package" of assets from the exchange [IFR-CDS]. No auction was run for AFH.

**After the plan.** At 31 Dec 2025 gross debt was EUR16.6bn, net debt EUR15.7bn, net leverage 5.3x pro forma, weighted average cost of debt 7.7%, liquidity EUR0.7bn and the revolver fully drawn; revenue was EUR9.2bn and pro forma EBITDA EUR2.9bn [AF-FY25]. The only unsecured note line in the table, USD957mm of 10.000% senior notes due 2033, is presumably the AFH take-back paper (CHECK) [AF-FY25]. On 17 Apr 2026 Bouygues Telecom, Free-iliad and Orange entered exclusive negotiations for SFR at an enterprise value of EUR20.35bn excluding an earn-out, and a memorandum of understanding was signed on 7 Jun 2026 [AF-FY25; AF-index]. As rough arithmetic (mine), EUR20.35bn less EUR15.7bn of net debt is about EUR4.6bn, though the perimeters differ and this is not a valuation. On those numbers the equity the plan left with Drahi looks to have been in the money at exit, which a leverage-only view at 6.2x would not have suggested.

**Players.** Lazard (company), Rothschild (creditor co-op), Houlihan Lokey (Arini), White & Case (Altice group, per a firm announcement title), Ropes & Gray (US counsel to the French foreign representative) and Mayer Brown (Altice group) in Chapter 15, Milbank (ad hoc group), judicial administrators Abitbol & Rousselet and 2M Associes [IFR-Award; Elevenflo; Octus].

## 4. Thames Water (2025): Class A/B creditors, the sanctioned plan and the Court of Appeal

**Structure and instruments.** Thames Water's regulated operating company (TWUL) sits inside a whole-business securitisation (WBS) with senior Class A debt and junior Class B debt, and the court plan was run by the holding company above it, Thames Water Utilities Holdings Ltd. At 28 Nov 2024 the judgment records external non-hedging debt of about GBP19bn: Class A about GBP16bn, Class B about GBP1bn, and a subordinated GBP4.25bn loan to Thames Water Ltd (GBP1.98bn principal, GBP1.25bn accrued interest), plus about GBP1.7bn of hedge mark-to-market exposure at March 2024 and undrawn liquidity and reserve facilities of about GBP550mm [Judg-338; PW]. Class A and Class B figures differ across sources by date and definition (Paul Weiss: Class A GBP14.7bn, Class B GBP1.4bn drawn at 31 Mar 2024).

**Timeline.**
- Early 2024: shareholders judge the business uninvestable; Apr 2024 certain indirect holdcos default without cross-defaulting the WBS; Jul 2024 a trigger event limits new debt, Moody's cuts the group below investment grade, and Ofwat's draft determination offers a 23% average bill rise against 44% requested [PW].
- Sep-Oct 2024: senior creditors approve a cash-reserve release subject to a "significant debt restructuring" and begin signing a transaction support agreement; 17 Dec 2024: court convenes seven creditor meetings; 19 Dec: Ofwat's final determination (35% against 53% sought) [PW].
- 18 Feb 2025: Leech J sanctions the interim plan [Judg-338]. 17 Mar 2025: Court of Appeal dismisses the appeals; written reasons [2025] EWCA Civ 475 [TW-Mar25; CoA-Link].
- 3 Jun 2025: KKR, preferred bidder since end-March for over GBP3bn of equity, drops out; Thames pivots to a senior creditors' plan, later London & Valley Water (LVW). Aug 2025: government hires FTI Consulting for special-administration contingency planning; the first GBP1.5bn super senior tranche is fully drawn, funding to mid-December [BBG-Aug25].
- Mar 2026: LVW proposes GBP3.35bn of equity and up to GBP6.55bn of new debt, a 30% Class A write-down and a Class B wipe-out. 16 Jun 2026: the Environment Secretary writes that she is "not yet convinced" of consumer protection; 7 Jul: she tells a committee the special-administration criteria are not met. 12 Jul: Moody's cuts Class A from Caa3 to Ca [Moody-Jul26].
- 16 Oct 2026: a transaction support agreement with holders of over 60% of the roughly GBP1bn of Class B debt gives Class B about 5.9% recovery in post-restructuring equity plus participation rights in GBP150mm of new equity. Thames says it lacks certainty that the restructuring can complete before senior debt matures and the stable-platform period ends in March 2027, so an interim transaction is planned [Inv-Oct26].

**Mechanics.** The plan gave GBP1.5bn of new super senior money with a further GBP1.5bn accordion (priced at 9.75% plus fees per one law-firm commentary, CHECK; the judge called the price "very, very high"), a two-year maturity extension, and a lock-up requirement for the follow-on plan (RP2) from 66 2/3% of super senior and Class A creditors to unlock funding beyond June 2025 [Judg-338; CoA-Link; LegalCheek]. Class B voted 84.5% against, the subordinated creditor against, and was crammed down. The valuation fight was GBP16.7bn enterprise value (company) against GBP23.3bn (Class B group); the judge accepted Kroll's mid-case, found the relevant alternative was special administration, and held Class B out of the money in it (paras 231, 244, 255) [PW; Judg-338].

**Later rulings.** The Court of Appeal held there is no "hard-edged rule" that out-of-the-money creditors can always be excluded from the benefits a plan generates, but on the facts continued interest payments and participation rights were enough; it narrowed the releases to exclude claims by a future special administrator against directors and advisers; it said the choice of special administration belongs to Ofwat and the Secretary of State, not the court; and it found the valuation dispute moot [CoA-Link].

**Price paths.** Late July 2024: Class B bonds about 32p from 57p; the April 2027 euro Class A bond about 76c from 86c; a sterling May 2027 Class B at 51.6p, all Bloomberg-compiled and CHECK (search snippets) [BBG-snippets]. Aug 2025: the April 2029 euro bond about 69c, up from about 66c, as a GBP190mm Class A index-linked loan sold at auction [BBG-Aug25]. Ratings: Class A Caa3 to Ca with an expected loss of 35-65% [Moody-Jul26]. The October 2026 Class B recovery of about 5.9% compares with 32p in July 2024 (price versus plan recovery, not like for like).

**Players.** Linklaters (company), Akin Gump (Class A group), Allen & Overy Shearman (banks), Quinn Emanuel (Class B group), Freshfields (Thames Water Ltd); Charlie Maynard MP as public-interest objector; CEO Chris Weston, chair Adrian Montague; KKR, the LVW consortium, Castle Water as bidders [Judg-338; TW-Mar25]. Members of the Class A group were not named in the sources fetched.

## 5. Atos (2024): sauvegarde accélérée as a forum exhibit

**Instruments.** About EUR4.85bn of debt before the deal [BBG-Atos]: a EUR1.5bn Term Loan A (July 2022, due Jan 2025), a EUR900mm revolver (due Nov 2025), EUR500mm of 2024 exchangeable notes, EUR750mm of 1.75% notes due May 2025, a EUR50mm 2026 NEU MTN, EUR350mm of 2.50% notes due 2028 and EUR800mm of 1.00% sustainability-linked notes due Nov 2029, rated CCC- before opening [Atos-Jul24].

**Timeline.**
- Early Feb 2024: talks begin under a court-appointed mediator. Mid-Mar 2024: the 2025 bond falls 5 cents to 16, the lowest since its 2018 issue (search snippet, CHECK) [BBG-Atos].
- 6 May 2024: rival bids (Kretinsky's EPEI with Attestor, EUR600mm of equity; Onepoint, the roughly 11% holder, EUR350mm for at least 35%; Bain for the Eviden unit; a bondholder plan). Shares EUR2.09, down 83% in a year and 97% since the start of 2021; Paul Saleh was the fifth CEO in under three years [BBG-Atos].
- 30 Jun-1 Jul 2024: lock-up with banks and bondholders: EUR2.9bn of debt into equity, new money about EUR1.68bn of debt and EUR233mm of equity, a 50 bp lock-up fee for signing by 12 Jul; 2025 notes just under 12 cents; existing equity "close to zero" [BBG-Atos]. 24 Jul: Nanterre court opens sauvegarde accélérée; Jean-Pierre Mustier replaces Saleh [Atos-Jul24].
- 24 Oct 2024: plan approved (more than two-thirds of the votes cast, per the court summary); 18 Dec: effective date [Atos-Dec24].

**Terms and outcome.** EUR2.9bn of principal equitised and EUR0.8bn of interim financing repaid, for a gross debt reduction of EUR2.1bn against the lock-up's headline "at least EUR3.1bn" (the difference is definitional); EUR1.95bn reinstated (EUR1.59bn "1.5L", EUR0.36bn "2L"); EUR1.6bn of new preferred financings due Dec 2029 (EUR0.8bn bonds, EUR0.8bn bank lines); about EUR145mm of new-money equity from a EUR233mm rights issue in which about EUR90mm of claims were equitised; S&P and Fitch B-. Ownership afterwards: participating creditors 74.4%, non-participating creditors 15.2%, free float 9.0%, board 1.4%; a 1% holder who did not follow the capital increases was diluted to 0.35%, and the Jul 2024 release put non-participants below 0.1% [Atos-Dec24; Atos-Jul24]. The 15.2% for "non-participating creditors" indicates that creditors who did not join the new financings were still bound and still received equity; the per-euro rate is not given in the sources fetched.

**Forum point.** Sauvegarde accélérée let a pre-agreed plan bind dissenting creditor classes without an insolvency filing (a MarketScreener headline also reports shareholder approval, CHECK), and it only touched financial debt (RCF, TLA, bonds) and the capital of Atos SE; suppliers, employees and governance were untouched [Atos-Jul24]. The political overlay was a French-government bid of EUR700mm (including debt) for strategic parts of the big data and security unit, which creditors reserved the right to re-examine [BBG-Atos].

**Players.** Judicial administrators Helene Bourbouloux (FHB) and Thibaut Martinat (AJRS); creditors' representatives Christophe Basse and Gurvan Ollu; bidders EPEI/Attestor, Onepoint (David Layani), Bain; the bondholder group and bank group are not named in the sources fetched [Atos-Jul24].

## 6. Fossil (2026): Part 26A as a forum exhibit

**Instruments.** USD150mm of 7.00% senior unsecured notes due 30 Nov 2026 issued by Fossil Group Inc. (Delaware; Nasdaq ticker FOSLL), widely held by retail investors (about 25% of the class by value) plus two large institutions [Fossil-J; Fresh]. The plan company was a newly formed English subsidiary, Fossil (UK) Global Services Ltd, which became a debtor under Part 26A after the notes were switched from New York to English governing law with English courts having sole jurisdiction [Fossil-J, paras 10-11, 49-50].

**Timeline.**
- 13 Aug 2025: a new USD150mm asset-based revolver (Ares credit funds, due Aug 2030, SOFR plus 500) and a transaction support agreement with holders of about 59% of the notes [Fossil-TSA].
- 9 Sep 2025: exchange offer with a 90% minimum tender and a Part 26A plan as fallback; it reached 82.67%, short of 90%, so the plan was used [Fossil-J, para 12; Investing].
- 6 Nov 2025: plan meeting: 364 creditors present, 82.88% of the notes by value; 363 for, one against, 99.99% by value of those voting. 10 Nov: Richards J sanctions the plan, [2025] EWHC 3058 (Ch). 12 Nov: Chapter 15 recognition in the S.D. Texas; 13 Nov: closing [Fossil-J; Fossil-8K].

**Terms.** All USD150mm of old notes cancelled; new 9.500% First-Out First Lien Secured Senior Notes due 2029 for holders who funded their pro rata share of up to USD32.5mm of new money (they also received common stock equal to 5.0% of the amount funded), and 7.500% Second-Out Second Lien Secured Senior Notes due 2029 dollar-for-dollar for those who did not; about USD182.5mm of new notes in all (3VB, CHECK); a USD1.625mm backstop premium in first-out notes [Fossil-8K; 3VB]. No trade creditors were compromised.

**Rulings and reasoning.** One class, so no cross-class cram-down and only a "limited rationality" review under the Adler questions; the court found the plan one a rational creditor could approve and better than the relevant alternative (a US Chapter 11 with a section 363 sale) for every plan creditor whether or not it funded; new money open to all and market-tested; it said new money used to improve the seniority of existing debt would attract more scrutiny, and it flagged artificiality and forum-shopping concerns (paras 34-50) [Fossil-J]. A former S.D.N.Y. judge, James Peck, gave expert evidence that a US court would likely recognise the plan. Freshfields reads the case as showing a single-class plan can replace a scheme because it has no numerosity test, and that a US issuer with New York law debt can use the English tool given a credible anchor and likely US recognition [Fresh].

**Why a forum exhibit.** Chapter 11 would have needed an impaired accepting class from a dispersed retail base and faced post-Purdue limits on releases; an out-of-court exchange needed 90% and stalled at 82.67%. The plan used a 75%-by-value threshold to convert a failed exchange into a binding one; the pair a desk should model is take-up of the exchange (82.67% against a 90% minimum) versus the plan vote (99.99% of those voting, with 82.88% present): non-responders hurt an exchange far more than a plan. No price series for FOSLL was accessible, so there is no price path.

**Players.** Retail advocate Jon Yorke; counsel Weil for the company (firm announcement title); William Day of 3VB for the retail advocate; CEO Franco Fogliato; information agent Epiq [Fossil-J; 3VB].

## 7. Wolfspeed (2025): the prepackaged plan, the convert and the equity
