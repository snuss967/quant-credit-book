## 1. Pluralsight (2024): the private-side J.Crew

**Instruments.** Vista Equity Partners took Pluralsight private in April 2021 (about $3.5bn per Goodwin and L&G, $3.9bn per Reuters, so CHECK the basis), financed in part by a $1.175bn first-lien recurring-revenue term loan and a $100mm revolver; Reuters put non-bank debt above $1.5bn [Goodwin; L&G; Reuters-Vista]. The loan paid SOFR+800 (13.56% all-in at 31 Dec 2023) and matured April 2027 [ARCC-Q2]. An ARR loan is sized on recurring revenue and liquidity, not EBITDA leverage, so trouble shows first as a renewal or revenue miss.

**Timeline.**
- Q1 2023: Vista sought covenant relief and added $75mm of equity. Vista fund documents valued the equity at $2.3bn (Q1 2023), $1.5bn (Q4 2023) and zero (update of 28 May 2024). A 15 Apr 2024 update warned of "liquidity challenges" [Reuters-Vista].
- Late May to early summer 2024: IP moved into a new restricted subsidiary, with about $50mm from Vista (loan or preferred, reports differ) funding an interest payment. KBRA called these "reasonable and allowable stopgap measures"; creditors reportedly disputed the valuation of the assets moved [KBRA; Goodwin].
- 3 Jun 2024 (Bloomberg) and 2 Jul (Reuters): Vista in talks to cede control. 30 Jun: BDC marks near 47-48 [ARCC-Q2; OBDC].
- About 22 Aug 2024: a Blue Owl- and Ares-led group takes ownership with about $275mm of new money and about $1.2bn of debt converted to equity; lenders about 85%, management about 15%; Vista and co-investors lose about $4bn [BBG-PS]. Goodwin's pre-signing alert had lenders at 100% and $250mm, so the terms moved late.

**Mark path.** One loan, one coupon, one maturity, many values:

| Holder | 31 Dec 2023 | 31 Mar 2024 | 30 Jun 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ares Capital | 89.0 | 84.9 | 48.0 |
| Blue Owl Capital Corp | 96.8 | 83.5 | 47.0 |
| Seven BDCs (KBRA) | 89.0 to 99.0 | 83.4 to 97.0 | n/a |

Sources: [ARCC-Q2; OBDC; KBRA]. The March spread was 13.6 points; the Dec 2023 Blue Owl-Ares gap was 7.8 (mine). Ares's 36.9-point drop in one quarter straddles Vista's 28 May write-off. KBRA put BDC first-lien exposure near $375mm of about $700mm and expected no rating effect. Ares booked $(40)mm of unrealized loss in Q2; Blue Owl's position went from 1.7% to 0.8% of net assets [ARCC-Q2; OBDC; KBRA].

**Dollar outcome.** At 30 Sep 2024 Ares held $54.7mm of new paper at cost ($21.4mm first lien at S+450 with 1.5% PIK due 2029; $19.0mm second tranche at S+750, PIK; $14.3mm of common units) against $106.5mm of old cost, with a $60mm realized loss [ARCC-Q3]. Blue Owl went from about $105mm par to $54.6mm at cost [OBDC]. Ares's marks then stepped down: 52.1 (Jun 2025), 46.5, 40.1, 23.1 (Mar 2026) and 21.8 (Jun 2026), which is 20.5% of original cost (mine); only the first-lien piece, cut to S+300 all cash, sits at par, and the second tranche and equity are zero [ARCC-later]. Oaktree and Ares wrote the equity to zero in Feb 2026, and Goldman Sachs BDC put its first lien on non-accrual at 31 Dec 2025 [BGOV; GSBD]. The restructured loan was itself re-cut.

**Mechanics.** Goodwin called it "one of, if not, the first sponsor funded LME" in private credit. No court, no excluded-holder vote, no ruling: a club of large managers coordinated and took the keys. The documents allowed the drop-down; a syndicated-loan lawyer would have flagged that.

**Players.** Vista; lenders Blue Owl, Ares, Goldman Sachs BDC, Franklin BSP, BlackRock, Oaktree, Golub (KBRA, L&G); Guggenheim also named by Bloomberg [BBG-PS].

## 2. Zayo (2025): a fibre credit that amended-and-extended its way across the maturity wall

**Why it is here.** Zayo is the control case: a levered, sponsor-owned credit facing a 2027 wall that did an amend-and-extend with no default, haircut or court, while its loan stayed in the mid-90s. A model that flags every wall-facing, cooperating credit as an LME will be wrong here.

**Instruments.** About $10bn of debt, including a term loan of nearly $5bn due 2027, other secured debt and unsecured notes due 2028; sponsors EQT and DigitalBridge [BBG-Zayo; Debtwire]. An ad hoc group held about $8.6bn (Houlihan Lokey summary, snippet, CHECK) [GD].

**Timeline.**
- Jun 2024: non-North American operations put into separate entities, with just over $1bn received through an intercompany loan and cash [BBG-Zayo].
- 19 Dec 2024: Zayo asked lenders for a haircut and talks failed; first-lien loan "mid-90s". 23 Dec: first-lien lenders sign a cooperation agreement [Debtwire; BBG-Zayo].
- Jan 2025: inaugural $1.42bn fiber ABS. Mar: Crown Castle agrees to sell its fiber business to Zayo. 17 Apr: talks revived, with a steering committee advised by Gibson Dunn and Houlihan Lokey [BBG-Zayo; GD].
- 18 Jul 2025: 2027 term loan about 96. 14 Aug: transaction support agreement with holders of over 95% [BBG-Zayo; Zayo-PR].
- 26-29 Sep 2025: closing at above 99.8% participation; 2027 secured debt extended three years to March 2030; 2028 unsecured extended 2.5 years to September 2030; $2.0bn secured paydown from earlier ABS proceeds; future ABS allowed, proceeds to repay debt [Zayo-PR]. Coupons and fees were undisclosed; Houlihan Lokey's summary (snippet, CHECK) cites participation fees, escalating rate increases, step-ups if paydowns are missed, and $200mm of unsecured paper uptiered to first-out [GD].
- 1 May 2026: Crown Castle closed the $8.5bn gross sale of fiber (to Zayo) and small cells (to an EQT fund); $8.4bn cash [CCI-8K]. A $2.37bn ABS in April 2026 took Zayo's ABS above $6bn (snippet, CHECK).

**Mechanics.** The paydown was about 27 points of participating secured debt per Houlihan Lokey, implying a pool near $7.4bn (mine, CHECK). My reading: lenders took cash and a longer tail while letting Zayo put cash-flowing fibre into ABS vehicles that rank ahead of them on those revenues. That is inference from the permission for future ABS, not a quoted statement.

**Outcome.** No default or court. The lenders' lever was cohesion (a pact within days of the failed talks); the company's was an asset-backed take-out that shrank the loan before the wall. Players: EQT, DigitalBridge, Gibson Dunn, Houlihan Lokey, CFO Jeff Noto, Crown Castle.

## 3. Altice France (2024-2025): Sauvegarde Accelerée, the non-participants and the co-op

**Debt addressed.** About EUR24.1bn (EUR23.3bn funded): a EUR1.2bn revolver; about EUR7.1bn of term loans in seven tranches (including EUR3.669bn at SOFR+550 due Aug 2028); about EUR10.9bn of senior secured notes in eleven series due Feb 2027 to Oct 2029 (coupons 3.375% to 11.500%); and about EUR4bn at Altice France Holding (AFH) [Elevenflo; IFR-Award]. End-2024 net debt exceeded EUR21bn (6.2x) on 2024 adjusted EBITDA of EUR3.435bn (-9.9%) and interest above EUR2.6bn; about EUR2.5bn of disposals (UltraEdge, Altice Media, La Poste Telecom) came first [Paperjam].

**Timeline.**
- Mar 2024 (day not reported): at an investor call Drahi outlined debt cuts and the debt fell about 20 cents; a creditor cooperation agreement formed that evening [IFR-Award]. It grew to about 200 institutions with about EUR19bn of opco-secured debt; named members include Elliott, Anchorage, Sona and Sculptor, with Pimco and BlackRock large holders and Arini in the holdco debt [IFR-Award].
- Nov 2024: talks begin. Late Feb 2025: framework agreement; 2.5% early-signing premium for signing by 19 Mar (Octus: 12 Mar) [Octus; Elevenflo].
- 15 May 2025: AFH misses interest on its 8% 2027 notes. 27 May: accelerated safeguard requested [DC-Hold; Paperjam].
- 9 Jun: the EMEA CDS Determinations Committee votes 11-0 that a Bankruptcy Credit Event occurred on 27 May [AF-DC]. 10 Jun: Paris court opens proceedings. 17 Jun: Chapter 15 petition, S.D.N.Y. No. 25-11349 (Judge Wiles) [Octus; Elevenflo, CHECK against docket].
- 25 Jun: DC finds Failure to Pay on the AFH notes; 2 Jul: no AFH auction [DC-Hold].
- 4 Aug: plans approved. 27 Aug: Altice France CDS auction at 87.75. 1 Oct 2025: implemented. 12 Feb 2026: Chapter 15 closed [AF-FY25; IFR-CDS; Elevenflo].

**Terms and non-participants.** Opco term-loan and note holders: about 7.6% cash (about EUR1.5bn) plus 2.5% for early signers; about 77% of principal in new secured debt (coupons +137.5 to +142.5 bp, maturities about 2.75 years longer); about 31% of new equity; revolver refinanced at par into a five-year facility at E+3.30%. AFH bondholders: 2.5% cash, 20% in new 2033 debt, 14% of equity, conditional warrants. Drahi's chain fell from 100% to 55% [Paperjam; Delano; Elevenflo]. Headline reduction EUR8.6bn; Elevenflo counts about EUR7.5bn and Ropes over EUR8bn (snippet). French classes bind at two-thirds by value, so non-signers got identical terms minus the early-bird 2.5%; reported votes were revolver 100% and the term-loan/notes class 100% on 95.16% participation, no affected creditor objecting (secondary, CHECK) [Elevenflo].

**Prices.** The 87.75 CDS auction price means 12.25 per 100 paid; bank quotes averaged 86.25 earlier that day; only EUR38mm of bonds were requested for physical settlement, all filled at 87.75, against $232mm net notional [IFR-CDS]. A 95% lock-up threatened to starve the auction of deliverables, so after more than two months and 22 meetings the committee added a "composite package" of exchange assets [IFR-CDS].

**After the plan.** At 31 Dec 2025: gross debt EUR16.6bn, net debt EUR15.7bn, net leverage 5.3x pro forma, average cost 7.7%, liquidity EUR0.7bn, revolver fully drawn; revenue EUR9.2bn, pro forma EBITDA EUR2.9bn [AF-FY25]. On 17 Apr 2026 Bouygues Telecom, Free-iliad and Orange entered exclusive talks for SFR at EUR20.35bn enterprise value excluding an earn-out; an MoU followed on 7 Jun [AF-FY25; AF-index]. EUR20.35bn less EUR15.7bn of net debt is about EUR4.6bn (mine; perimeters differ, not a valuation), so the retained equity looks in the money, which a 6.2x leverage view would not have suggested.

**Players.** Lazard (company), Rothschild (co-op), Houlihan Lokey (Arini), Ropes & Gray (US counsel to the French foreign representative), Mayer Brown (Altice), Milbank (ad hoc group); administrators Abitbol & Rousselet and 2M Associes [IFR-Award; Elevenflo; Octus].

## 4. Thames Water (2025): Class A/B creditors, the sanctioned plan and the Court of Appeal

**Structure.** Regulated operating company TWUL sits inside a whole-business securitisation (WBS) with senior Class A and junior Class B debt; the plan company was the holding company above it. At 28 Nov 2024 the judgment records external non-hedging debt of about GBP19bn: Class A about GBP16bn, Class B about GBP1bn, and a GBP4.25bn subordinated loan to Thames Water Ltd (GBP1.98bn principal, GBP1.25bn accrued interest), plus liquidity and reserve facilities of about GBP550mm (mine, sum of three) [Judg-338]. Hedge exposure was about GBP1.7bn at March 2024; Paul Weiss counts Class A GBP14.7bn and Class B GBP1.4bn drawn at 31 Mar 2024, so figures vary by date [PW].

**Timeline.**
- 2024: shareholders judge the business uninvestable; Apr, indirect holdcos default without cross-defaulting the WBS; Jul, a trigger event limits new debt, Moody's cuts to sub-investment grade, and Ofwat's draft offers 23% bill rises against 44% asked; Sep-Oct, senior creditors approve a reserve release subject to "significant debt restructuring" and sign a support agreement [PW].
- 17 Dec 2024: seven creditor meetings convened. 19 Dec: Ofwat final determination, 35% against 53% sought [PW].
- 18 Feb 2025: Leech J sanctions the interim plan [Judg-338]. 17 Mar: Court of Appeal dismisses the appeals; reasons [2025] EWCA Civ 475 [TW-Mar25; CoA-Link].
- 3 Jun 2025: KKR, preferred bidder since March for over GBP3bn of equity, withdraws; Thames turns to a senior creditors' plan, later London & Valley Water (LVW). Aug: government hires FTI for special-administration contingency planning; the first GBP1.5bn super senior is fully drawn, funding to mid-December [BBG-Aug25].
- Mar 2026: LVW proposes GBP3.35bn equity, up to GBP6.55bn new debt, a 30% Class A write-down and Class B wiped out. 16 Jun: the Environment Secretary writes she is "not yet convinced" on consumer protection. 12 Jul: Moody's cuts Class A from Caa3 to Ca [Moody-Jul26].
- 16 Oct 2026: a support agreement signed by over 60% of about GBP1bn of Class B gives Class B about 5.9% recovery in post-restructuring equity plus participation in GBP150mm of new equity. Thames says it lacks certainty RP2 can finish before senior debt matures and the stable-platform period ends in March 2027, so an interim transaction is planned [Inv-Oct26].

**Mechanics and rulings.** The plan gave GBP1.5bn of super senior money plus a GBP1.5bn accordion (9.75% plus fees per one law-firm commentary, CHECK; the judge called the price "very, very high"), a two-year extension, and a lock-up condition for RP2 (66 2/3% of super senior and Class A) to unlock funding beyond June 2025 [Judg-338; CoA-Link; LegalCheek]. Class B voted 84.5% against and was crammed down. Enterprise value was GBP16.7bn (company) against GBP23.3bn (Class B group); the judge accepted Kroll's mid-case, found the relevant alternative was special administration, and held Class B out of the money in it (paras 231, 244, 255) [PW; Judg-338]. The Court of Appeal held there is no "hard-edged rule" excluding out-of-the-money creditors from a plan's benefits, found interest payments and participation rights sufficient here, narrowed the releases to exclude later-administrator claims against directors and advisers, left special administration to Ofwat and the Secretary of State, and called the valuation dispute moot [CoA-Link].

**Price paths.** Late July 2024 (Bloomberg-compiled, snippets, CHECK): Class B about 32p from 57p; April 2027 euro Class A about 76c from 86c; a sterling May 2027 Class B at 51.6p [BBG-snippets]. Aug 2025: April 2029 euro bond about 69c from 66c as a GBP190mm Class A index-linked loan sold at auction [BBG-Aug25]. Class A rating Ca, expected loss 35-65% [Moody-Jul26]. The 5.9% Class B recovery compares with 32p in July 2024 (plan recovery versus price, not like for like).

**Players.** Linklaters (company), Akin Gump (Class A group), A&O Shearman (banks), Quinn Emanuel (Class B), Freshfields (Thames Water Ltd); Charlie Maynard MP (objector); CEO Chris Weston; bidders KKR, LVW, Castle Water [Judg-338; TW-Mar25]. Class A group members were not named in the sources fetched.

## 5. Atos (2024): sauvegarde accélérée as a forum exhibit

**Instruments.** About EUR4.85bn of debt [BBG-Atos]: a EUR1.5bn Term Loan A (due Jan 2025), a EUR900mm revolver (Nov 2025), EUR500mm of 2024 exchangeables, EUR750mm of 1.75% notes due May 2025, a EUR50mm 2026 NEU MTN, EUR350mm of 2.50% notes due 2028 and EUR800mm of 1.00% sustainability-linked notes due Nov 2029; rated CCC- before opening [Atos-Jul24].

**Timeline.**
- Early Feb 2024: talks begin under a court-appointed mediator. Mid-Mar: the 2025 bond falls 5 cents to 16, a record low (snippet, CHECK) [BBG-Atos].
- 6 May 2024: rival bids (Kretinsky's EPEI with Attestor, EUR600mm of equity; Onepoint, the roughly 11% holder, EUR350mm for at least 35%; Bain for Eviden; a bondholder plan). Shares EUR2.09, down 83% in a year and 97% since 2021; Paul Saleh was the fifth CEO in under three years [BBG-Atos].
- 30 Jun-1 Jul: lock-up with banks and bondholders: EUR2.9bn into equity, about EUR1.68bn of new debt and EUR233mm of new equity, a 50 bp fee for signing by 12 Jul; 2025 notes just under 12; existing equity "close to zero" [BBG-Atos]. 24 Jul: Nanterre court opens sauvegarde accélérée; Jean-Pierre Mustier replaces Saleh [Atos-Jul24].
- 24 Oct: plan approved (over two-thirds of votes cast, per the court summary). 18 Dec: effective [Atos-Dec24].

**Terms and outcome.** EUR2.9bn of principal equitised and EUR0.8bn of interim financing repaid: gross debt reduction EUR2.1bn against a lock-up headline of "at least EUR3.1bn" (definitional). EUR1.95bn reinstated (EUR1.59bn "1.5L", EUR0.36bn "2L"); EUR1.6bn of new financings due Dec 2029; about EUR145mm of new-money equity from a EUR233mm rights issue (about EUR90mm of claims equitised); S&P and Fitch B-. Ownership: participating creditors 74.4%, non-participating creditors 15.2%, free float 9.0%, board 1.4%; a 1% holder who did not follow the capital increases fell to 0.35%, and the July release put non-participating shareholders below 0.1% [Atos-Dec24; Atos-Jul24]. The 15.2% shows creditors who skipped the new financings were still bound and still got equity; the per-euro rate is not in the sources fetched.

**Forum point.** Sauvegarde accélérée let a pre-agreed plan bind dissenting creditor classes without an insolvency filing (a MarketScreener headline also reports shareholder approval, CHECK), touching only financial debt and Atos SE's capital, not suppliers, employees or governance [Atos-Jul24]. The overlay was a EUR700mm French-government bid (including debt) for strategic parts of the big data and security unit, which creditors reserved the right to re-examine [BBG-Atos].

**Players.** Administrators Helene Bourbouloux (FHB) and Thibaut Martinat (AJRS); creditors' representatives Christophe Basse and Gurvan Ollu; bidders EPEI/Attestor, Onepoint (David Layani) and Bain. The bondholder and bank groups are not named in the sources fetched [Atos-Jul24].

## 6. Fossil (2026): Part 26A as a forum exhibit

**Instruments.** USD150mm of 7.00% senior unsecured notes due 30 Nov 2026 issued by Fossil Group Inc. (Nasdaq: FOSLL), widely held by retail investors (about 25% of the class by value) plus two large institutions [Fossil-J; Fresh]. The plan company was a new English subsidiary, Fossil (UK) Global Services Ltd, brought under Part 26A after the notes were switched from New York to English law with English courts having sole jurisdiction [Fossil-J, paras 10-11, 49-50].

**Timeline.**
- 13 Aug 2025: a new USD150mm asset-based revolver (Ares credit funds, due Aug 2030, SOFR+500) and a support agreement with holders of about 59% of the notes [Fossil-TSA].
- 9 Sep: exchange offer with a 90% minimum and a Part 26A plan as fallback; it reached 82.67% [Fossil-J, para 12; Investing].
- 6 Nov: plan meeting, 364 creditors present (82.88% by value); 363 for, one against, 99.99% of those voting. 10 Nov: Richards J sanctions, [2025] EWHC 3058 (Ch). 12 Nov: Chapter 15 recognition (S.D. Tex.). 13 Nov: closing [Fossil-J; Fossil-8K].

**Terms.** The old notes were cancelled for new 9.500% First-Out First Lien Secured Senior Notes due 2029 (for holders who funded their pro rata share of up to USD32.5mm of new money, who also got common stock worth 5.0% of the funded amount) or 7.500% Second-Out Second Lien Secured Senior Notes due 2029 (dollar-for-dollar, for non-funders); about USD182.5mm of new notes in all (3VB, CHECK) and a USD1.625mm backstop premium. Trade creditors were not compromised [Fossil-8K; 3VB; Fossil-J].

**Rulings.** One class, so no cross-class cram-down and only "limited rationality" review under the Adler questions: the plan was one a rational creditor could approve and beat the relevant alternative (US Chapter 11 with a section 363 sale) for every plan creditor whether or not it funded. New money was open to all and market-tested; the court said new money used to improve the seniority of existing debt would draw more scrutiny, and it flagged artificiality and forum-shopping concerns (paras 34-50). Former S.D.N.Y. judge James Peck gave expert evidence that a US court would likely recognise the plan [Fossil-J]. Freshfields reads the case as showing that a single-class plan can replace a scheme because it has no numerosity test, and that a US issuer with New York-law debt can use the English tool given a credible anchor and likely US recognition [Fresh].

**Why a forum exhibit.** Chapter 11 would have needed an accepting impaired class from a dispersed retail base and faced post-Purdue limits on releases; an out-of-court exchange needed 90% and stalled at 82.67%. A 75%-by-value plan converted a failed exchange into a binding one. The pair to model is exchange take-up (82.67% against a 90% minimum) versus plan vote (99.99% of those voting, with 82.88% present): non-responders hurt an exchange far more than a plan. No FOSLL price series was accessible.

**Players.** Retail advocate Jon Yorke; Weil for the company (firm announcement title); William Day (3VB) for the retail advocate; CEO Franco Fogliato; information agent Epiq [Fossil-J; 3VB].

## 7. Wolfspeed (2025): the prepackaged plan, the convert and the equity

**Instruments.** Three convertible series (1.75% due 2026, 0.25% due 2028, 1.875% due 2029; about USD3.1bn per a 10-Q excerpt, CHECK; Ropes & Gray acted for holders of USD2.4bn); Senior Secured Notes due 2030 (USD250mm issued in Q2 FY2025, with about USD500mm of undrawn commitment lost at filing); and an unsecured Renesas customer deposit of up to USD2.0bn whose interest accrued to principal from Oct 2024, saving about USD120mm of cash interest [WOLF-10K; WOLF-10Q; R&G]. At 29 Jun 2025 debt was USD6.54bn against cash and short-term investments of USD955mm; FY2025 net loss was USD1.609bn after USD864mm [WOLF-10K].

**Timeline.** About 28 Mar 2025: the USD575mm 2026 converts near 60 and the stock down about 50% to USD2.70 (secondary, CHECK) [TipRanks]. 22 Jun: restructuring support agreement with over 97% of the secured notes, over 67% of each convertible series, and Renesas [WOLF-10Q]. The stock fell about 33% to about USD0.60 on a Monday report of the plan (secondary, date CHECK) [Benzinga]. 30 Jun: prepackaged Chapter 11, S.D. Tex. No. 25-90163. 8 Sep: confirmation. 29 Sep: emergence, 91 days after the petition (mine). 29 Jan 2026: all regulatory approvals received [WOLF-10Q].

**Treatment.**
- Secured notes: USD1.3bn of new senior secured notes due 23 Jun 2030 (9.875% cash plus 4.00% PIK to 22 Jun 2026, then 13.875% or 15.875% cash) and a USD277.5mm redemption at 109.875% [WOLF-10Q].
- Convertibles: about 24.5mm shares (about 95% of the 25.84mm at emergence), USD296.4mm of PIK-toggle second-lien notes due 2031, and rights to buy USD301.1mm of 2.5% convertible second-lien notes at 91.3242, backstopped for USD30.3mm [WOLF-10Q].
- Renesas (about USD2.1bn of deposits): USD203.6mm of second-lien converts, 4.94mm warrants at USD23.95, 16.85mm shares conditional on approvals, 9.9% voting cap [WOLF-10Q].
- Old equity: 0.008352 new share per old share, 1,306,896 shares in total (about 5.1% of emergence shares, mine) plus 871,287 contingent shares; the RSA gave 3% or 5% depending on Renesas's approvals, and they arrived [WOLF-10K; WOLF-10Q].
- Result: debt down about 70%; at 28 Dec 2025, long-term debt USD1.43bn, converts USD534mm, cash and investments USD1.29bn [WOLF-10Q].

**Equity behaviour.** Old shares closed at USD1.21 on Fri 26 Sep 2025 and new shares at USD20.89 on Mon 29 Sep (secondary snippets, CHECK); the "+1,700%" headline is a consolidation artefact. At the exchange ratio each old share was worth about USD0.17 (mine), an 86% loss against the Friday close. The old equity was not wiped out, but the plan, not the pre-emergence market price, set its value.

**Signals visible beforehand.** A 60-cent convert due the following year (secondary); a stock down 50% in a day; a USD2.0bn deposit from a counterparty that became a named restructuring party; interest on it paid in kind; USD500mm of secured commitments lost at filing. No convertible, secured-note or CDS series was accessible, so the credit path is two dated points.

**Players.** Wolfspeed and Wolfspeed Texas LLC; Renesas Electronics America (expected loss about JPY250bn, secondary); convertible holders (Ropes & Gray); trustee U.S. Bank Trust Company on the new notes [WOLF-10Q; R&G].

## 8. Cross-case synthesis: what a systematic desk could have observed beforehand

This is a reconstruction, not a back-test: no price series was available, so each row pairs dated observables with a rule and an expression, and quantifies only what the sources support.

**Arithmetic worth keeping.** (1) Altice: the opco plan returned about 84.6 per 100 in cash and new debt (7.6 + 77.0, mine), with the remaining 15.4 exchanged for 31% of equity, and the CDS cleared at 87.75, about 3 points above the cash-and-debt slice (mine), so if the new debt traded near par, the auction implied roughly 3 points for the equity slice (mine, indicative). (2) Wolfspeed: the "+1,700%" headline and the 86% fall in value per old share are the same event. (3) Thames: a Class B holder at 32p in July 2024 faces about 5.9 in October 2026, about -82% (mine, price versus plan recovery).

**Process clock.** Time from cooperation pact, lock-up or support agreement to close: Pluralsight 2.6 months, Fossil 3.0, Wolfspeed 3.3, Atos 5.6, Zayo 9.1, Altice 18.6; median 4.5 months (mine). Thames is about 24 months from its October 2024 support agreement and still open. A feature for the model is the date of the first signed pact, because the expected close and the legal forum both follow from it.

**Where the losses landed.** Secured and first-out creditors got par-like paper, premiums and fees (Zayo, Altice opco, Wolfspeed secured, Fossil first-out). Losses concentrated at holdcos, junior classes, converts and equity (AFH, Thames Class B, Atos shareholders), and in private loans where lenders themselves took the keys (Pluralsight, a 56% realized loss on Ares's cost, mine).

## 9. Which model assumptions broke

1. **Recovery as a function of seniority and collateral.** In Altice, class votes and an early-signing premium, not lien priority, set the outcome; in Thames, the relevant alternative (administration) put Class B at zero and the plan recovery of 5.9% followed from negotiation; in Pluralsight, collateral moved before default.
2. **A default has one date.** Altice France S.A. had a Bankruptcy Credit Event on 27 May 2025, the holdco a Failure to Pay on 14 Jun, and only one name got an auction. A hazard model needs the instrument and the reference entity, not a company flag.
3. **Marks are observations.** Seven BDCs carried one loan 13.6 points apart; the quarter's mark followed the sponsor's write-off by weeks.
4. **CDS recovery is a function of deliverables.** At Altice the auction price embedded take-back paper at near par, and the lock-up nearly starved the auction of deliverables (EUR38mm requested against $232mm notional).
5. **Absolute priority and a single jurisdiction.** Fossil's plan avoided Chapter 11 priority rules; Altice's equity kept 55% and a later exit enterprise value above net debt; Wolfspeed's old equity received 5% by agreement; Thames's Class B was crammed down over its 84.5% vote against because Class A approved by more than 75%. The same claim has different recoveries in different forums.
6. **A restructuring resets the hazard.** Altice's net leverage was 5.3x after the plan, Pluralsight's loan was re-cut, and Thames is on a second plan. The re-default clock restarts at the restructuring, and the first cut rarely fixes EBITDA.
7. **Wall plus cooperation implies distress.** Zayo shows the same signals ending in par-like terms.

## 10. What the RSSG banker saw that the quant missed

The banker looked at who held what: the fulcrum, where two-thirds (France), 75% by value (England) or a Chapter 11 class sits, and which forum turns a failed exchange into a binding plan. Fossil's 82.67% against 90%, then 99.99% of those voting at the plan meeting, is arithmetic a banker has before the first call.

The banker also saw the liquidity calendar. Thames's runway ran to 24 Mar 2025 and its stable-platform period ends in March 2027; Wolfspeed had USD955mm against USD6.5bn; Altice sold EUR2.5bn of assets first. A quant sees prices after these dates are known.

The banker saw the side payments: Atos's 50 bp lock-up fee, Altice's 2.5% early-bird, Wolfspeed's USD30.3mm backstop premium, Fossil's USD1.625mm backstop in first-out notes. Each rewards the early signer and cannot be read from a bond price.

The banker saw sponsor incentives: Vista handing over rather than injecting, Drahi keeping 55%, KKR walking from Thames, Renesas as customer, creditor and later shareholder. And the banker read the valuation fight (GBP16.7bn against GBP23.3bn) as the negotiation, because the court adopts one number and decides who is in the money.

What the quant had instead: a price (87.75, 32p, 60) and no way to ask who would sign.
