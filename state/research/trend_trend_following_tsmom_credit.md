| 2018 (*RFS* 31(5), 1784–1824) | **Cross-sectional versus time-series construction.** Goyal & Jegadeesh: cross-sectional (CS) strategies are zero-net-investment long/short portfolios, while time-series (TS) strategies take a **time-varying net long** position in risky assets. For individual stocks the performance gap is "largely due to this time-varying net long investment". Across international asset classes, **scaled CS strategies significantly outperform similarly scaled TS strategies**. A TS credit-index overlay is therefore a market-timing bet on credit beta, while a CS long/short credit book is a bet on the dispersion of winners over losers, and the two should be sized and budgeted separately. | https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i5p1784-1824..html (abstract; full text paywalled) |
| 2 Jul 2026 (arXiv v1) | **Decay of short-term trend, a 2026 primary result.** Kurth, Eisler, Rej & Bouchaud, "Is Trend Still Your Friend?": on about **100 liquid futures, 1995–2025**, plus a CTA-industry proxy, "since approximately 2009, short-term trends have ceased to deliver reliable returns", while trend overall has been profitable for two centuries.
