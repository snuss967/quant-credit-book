## 2. Evidence Table

| Date | Fact / number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 1992–May 2017 | Barclays QPS "Equity Momentum in Credit" (EMC): bonds of US IG/HY index constituents are sorted monthly by the issuer's trailing equity return. Excess returns rise monotonically across deciles, and the Q5–Q1 quintile strategy earns **6.34%/yr with IR 1.56**, positive in both 1993–2007 and 2007–2017. Multi-horizon (1/3/6-month) signals beat single horizons. | Shelf: Ben Dor, Desclée, Dynkin, Hyman, Polbennikov, *Systematic Investing in Credit* (Wiley, 2021), Ch. 15, pp. 603–607 (PDF indices) |
| Nov 2007–Oct 2017 | EMC scorecard (deciles 1–10) on US IG: the EMC=1 portfolio averaged **−7.2%/yr** excess return and EMC=10 averaged **+8.10%/yr**, against 1.43%/yr for the Bloomberg Barclays US Corporate index. A sector-neutral version still works in every sector, so the signal is issuer selection and not just sector rotation. | Shelf: same, Ch. 16, pp. 640–641 |
| Jun 2017 | A Barclays QPS poll of US institutional bond managers attributes EMC mainly to the segregation of equity and bond investor bases and to stocks reacting faster to news, rather than to risk. The academic alternative (Lin, Wang & Wu 2013) is liquidity risk. | Shelf: same, Ch. 15, p. 604 |
| 2022 | Man Group: credit momentum built from bond prices alone is "insufficiently robust", especially in IG, because buy-and-hold holders make bond prices inefficient. IG shows mean reversion (Khang–King 2004; Gebhardt–Hvidkjaer–Swaminathan 2005), and momentum matters more in HY (Jostova et al. 2013; Pospisil–Zhang 2010). Man's view is that trends are issuer-level and consistent across the capital structure, so equity information should feed the signal. | Shelf: Man Group, *Quantitative Credit: Alpha Opportunities for Early Movers* (2022), p. 25 |
| Q1 2020 (data to Dec 2017) | AQR "Trends Everywhere" (Babu, Levine, Ooi, Pedersen, Stamelos, *JOIM* Q1 2020) is the first out-of-sample TSMOM test on **five CDS indices**: CDX NA IG (from Oct 2003), CDX NA HY (Mar 2004), iTraxx Europe IG and Crossover/HY (Jun 2004), and EM credit (Mar 2004). Annualized volatilities are only 3.9–5.5%. Across all alternative assets, 12-month TSMOM has a gross Sharpe of 1.34, against 1.17 for traditional futures, and **1.60** for the diversified combination. | AQR, https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Trends-Everywhere (PDF: AQR-Trends-Everywhere_JOIM.pdf, pp. 54–55, Table 1) |
| Q1 2020 | In the same paper, credit-index TSMOM is **the weakest alternative class once passive beta is removed**. Regressed on an equal-vol passive long portfolio, 12-month credit TSMOM has annualized alpha 4.4% (**t = 0.49**, not significant), beta **0.52** (t = 8.84), and R² **33%**, the highest R² of any group. FI swaps (19.6%, t = 4.13) and exotic commodities (13.6%, t = 4.44) compare. Trend in CDS indices is largely a time-varying long-credit-beta position, because spreads tighten most of the time. | AQR "Trends Everywhere," Table 3 Panel B, p. 61 |
| Dec 2011 (JFE 2012) | Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen define **time-series momentum**: the past 12-month excess return predicts the next month's return in all **58** liquid equity-index, currency, commodity, and sovereign bond futures. Persistence lasts 1–12 months and then partially reverses. A diversified portfolio earns abnormal returns with little factor exposure and "performs best during extreme markets". The original study contains **no credit instruments**. | Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen, *JFE* 104 (2012) 228–250, abstract; PDF https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf |
| Fall 2017 | Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing" (*JPM* 44(1)): TSMOM is profitable in every decade from 1880 to 2016 across **67 markets** (average per-market Sharpe about 0.4). It is positive in **8 of the 10** worst 60/40 drawdowns. Those bear markets lasted about **15 months** peak to trough, which gives trend time to flip short. The strategy struggles in abrupt crashes such as 1987, and cross-asset correlations rose from late 2008 to mid-2014 (risk-on/risk-off). | AQR, https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing (PDF AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf) |
| Apr 2026 (R&R) | Dickerson, Robotti & Rossetti, "The Corporate Bond Factor Replication Crisis" (SSRN 6088966) audits 108 signals on TRACE, Aug 2002–Dec 2024 (269 months, 52,656 bonds). For **6-month momentum** (Jostova et al. 2013, mom6_1), the 0.30%/month premium is "entirely attributable to asymmetric ex-post winsorization": the base premium is **0.00%** and the CAPM-bond alpha falls from 0.46% (t = 2.39) to 0.22% (t = 1.08). For **12-month momentum**, the base premium is **−0.13%/month**, and a 40bp filtering bias turns it positive (0.26%). "No feasible filter generates a detectable momentum alpha." | https://openbondassetpricing.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/corp-bond-replication-crisis-dickerson.pdf (pp. 1, 22–24); SSRN https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6088966 |
| Apr 2026 | Same paper: only **26 of 432** factor specifications (6.0%) survive a Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction, and "standard momentum is absent". Two momentum-family factors survive, and only in equal-weighted *within-firm* sorts: a sign-corrected systematic 12-1 momentum and an **idiosyncratic 12-1 momentum**. The look-ahead bias that flatters momentum spikes in 2008–09, 2011–12, and Mar 2020, and is convex in the VIX. Right-tail winsorizing caps the rebound of past losers held in the short leg. | Same, pp. 22–25 and Internet Appendix Table IA.IX |
| 2025-12-11 | Open Source Bond Asset Pricing releases the Stage 1 TRACE daily bond panel (public beta). The site hosts bias-corrected factors, the corrected Bai–Bali–Wen factors from Dickerson–Mueller–Robotti (*JFE* 150, 2023, 103707), and "Factor Investing with Delays" (Dickerson–Nozawa–Robotti 2024, 341 factors). | https://openbondassetpricing.com/ |
| Jan 2023 (rev.) → EFA Aug 2025 | Dick-Nielsen, Feldhütter, Pedersen & Stolborg (CBS/AQR), "Corporate Bond Factors: Replication Failures and a New Framework" (SSRN 4586652; 2024 UWFC best paper): **only 23%** of factors that were significant in the literature keep significant alphas once a hand-cleaned TRACE dataset is used (outliers checked one by one, default returns kept) with value-weighted tertile, rating-double-sorted construction. Their summary: "most known factors fail, but so does the CAPM for corporate bonds". Several equity-derived signals do predict bond returns. | https://quantpedia.com/corporate-bond-factors-replication-failures-and-a-new-framework/ (14 May 2024); https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/corporate-bond-factors-replication-failures-and-a-new-framework/ (momentum-specific numbers not retrieved; CHECK) |
| Jul 2013 | Jostova, Nikolova, Philipov & Stahel, "Momentum in Corporate Bond Returns" (*RFS* 26(7)): 81,491 US corporate bonds, 1973–2011, using TRACE and dealer quotes. Over 1991–2011 the momentum spread averages **59bp/month** across all bonds, **192bp/month** in non-IG, and **282bp/month** in non-IG bonds of private firms. The effect is concentrated in HY, grows as HY grows, and is not just equity momentum. This is the paper the 2026 replication audit targets. | https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/26/7/1649/1612627 (abstract) |
| Jun 2017 | Haesen, Houweling & van Zundert (Robeco), "Momentum spillover from stocks to corporate bonds" (*JBF* 79): 2,439 IG and HY issuers, Jan 1994–Dec 2013. Sorting bonds on **total** equity momentum loads on time-varying default risk: losers have higher credit beta after equity sell-offs, so the strategy is crushed in bear-to-bull turns. Sorting on **residual (firm-specific) equity momentum** halves credit-risk exposure, **doubles the Sharpe ratio**, and sharply reduces drawdowns. | CFA Institute Journal Review digest, 1 Oct 2017, https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/en/research/cfa-digest/2017/10/dig-v47-n10-2 |
