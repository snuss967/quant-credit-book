**B1. Dickerson, Robotti, Rossetti (2026), "The Corporate Bond Factor Replication Crisis", arXiv:2604.07880 [q-fin.PR], 9 April 2026. [FT]** Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07880. Key result: 108 signals in nine clusters; month-end price noise creates Latent Implementation Bias (up to 91% of the short-term reversal premium; 15-90 bp/month on price-based factors) and ex-post winsorization creates Look-Ahead Bias (6-month momentum premium 0.30% falls to 0.00%; 12-month momentum +0.26% is really -0.13%); only 26 of 432 specifications (6.0%) survive Benjamini-Hochberg FDR, mostly credit-spread value; non-standard errors (0.35%/month) exceed the average premium (0.33%). Method/data: TRACE Enhanced plus 144A, Aug 2002 to Dec 2024, 52,656 bonds, ~6,790 bonds per month; value-weighted decile long-short and CAPM-B alphas, Newey-West t. Desk use: the audit harness for any bond-factor backtest (signal gap, month-begin returns, ex-ante winsorization); the cost-free survivor list that Ch 9's cost curve must then be applied to. Detailed tables in Section 3.

**B2. Dickerson, Mueller, Robotti (2023), "Priced risk in corporate bonds", Journal of Financial Economics 150(2), article 103707 (Nov 2023); DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2023.103707; also arXiv:2604.05699 (7 Apr 2026). [ABS]** Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05699. Key result: previously proposed bond risk factors, "with traded liquidity as the only marginal exception", add nothing to the corporate bond market factor; the bond CAPM is not dominated by traded- or nontraded-factor models in pairwise or multiple model comparisons. Warwick Business School reports it won the FAMA-DFA Prize for best 2023 JFE paper on capital markets and asset pricing (https://wbs.ac.uk/news/flaw-data-corporate-bond-investors). Desk use: justifies a one-factor (market or duration-adjusted market) null for every credit alpha claim; replication of BBW is the origin story of the field's data-hygiene turn.

Bai, Bali, Wen (2019), "Common risk factors in the cross-section of corporate bond returns" (RETRACTED), Journal of Financial Economics 131(3), 619-642; Retraction notice, JFE 150(3), 103721 (Dec 2023). ## 3. Open-data pipeline: definitions a writer needs to rebuild the panel

### 3.1 What the Dickerson-Robotti-Rossetti (DRR) 2026 paper says about the pipeline (VERIFIED, arXiv 2604.07880v1, Sections 1-3)

Paper identity: Alexander Dickerson (UNSW), Cesare Robotti (Warwick Business School), Giulio Rossetti (Warwick Business School), "The Corporate Bond Factor Replication Crisis", arXiv:2604.07880 [q-fin.PR], submitted 9 April 2026 (63-page PDF). An earlier version circulated as "Common pitfalls in the evaluation of corporate bond strategies". NOTE: the task brief cites "SSRN 6088966"; I could not verify that SSRN number (SSRN returned HTTP 403). The arXiv record is verified; cite arXiv until the SSRN id is confirmed.

Sample and universe (DRR Sec. 2.3): August 2002 to December 2024 (269 months); 52,656 unique bonds; average 6,790 bond-month observations per month. Rule 144A bonds enter from June 2014 (WRDS 144A transaction data begin then; 144A is "over 20% of new corporate bond issuance as of 2025"). Bonds are tracked through and after default (avoids censoring the return distribution, citing Baumann-Kakhbod-Livdan-Nazemi-Schuerhoff 2025 and Baumann-Nazemi 2025).

Bond screens (Sec. 2.1): TRACE Enhanced plus 144A, merged with Mergent FISD; keep USD-denominated, fixed-rate, non-convertible, non-asset-backed bonds with $1,000 par and original maturity of at least one year. Standard Dick-Nielsen (2014) filters remove cancellations, corrections, reversals and agency-side duplicates. Two additional transaction-level correctors: (i) a decimal-shift corrector (price recorded with wrong decimal placement, example 10.5 entered as 105.0, multiplicative correction when acceptance conditions hold); (ii) a bounce-back filter (transient price spike that deviates from a backward-looking anchor and reverts within a short window). Both correct where possible and flag otherwise. Daily aggregation by volume-weighted average price; QuantLib used for duration, convexity, YTM, credit spreads; ratings, equity identifiers and Fama-French industries merged. Four further daily filters target distressed-bond anomalies: isolated ultra-low prices, transient spikes, stale plateaus, intraday inconsistencies. The code generates per-CUSIP price-series reports for every affected bond; all thresholds are configurable (Appendix A of the paper gives parameter values; see 3.4 for what I could extract).

Returns (Sec. 2.2, Eqs. 1-3): monthly price = last available daily price within the last five NYSE business days of the month. Three return types: (a) month-end return r_End = (P_end,t+1 + AI_end,t+1 + C_t+1)/(P_end,t + AI_end,t) - 1; (b) month-begin return r_Bgn = (P_end,t+1 + AI_end,t+1 + C_t+1)/(P_bgn,t+1 + AI_bgn,t+1) - 1, where P_bgn is the first available price within the first five business days of month t+1; (c) duration-adjusted excess returns. Median gap between the month-end price and the first available price next month is one business day. Valid return needs a price in both windows. Excess returns subtract the Fama-French one-month T-bill rate; MKTB = value-weighted excess return on all sample bonds; the single-factor "bond CAPM" (CAPMB) on MKTB is used for alphas throughout. Approximation (Eq. 3, PDF text extraction; check against the paper before typesetting): r_End is approximately (P_bgn,t+1 / P_end,t - 1) + r_Bgn, i.e. the month-end return equals the implementable month-begin return plus the month-end-to-month-begin gap return, which is the latent implementation bias proxy.

Default handling (Sec. 2.2, App. A.5): in the default month compute (1) the standard return, (2) a default-event return excluding the final coupon, (3) a trading-in-default return on a flat basis without accrued interest.

Gap procedures to break the signal-return price link (Sec. 2.2/3): (1) signal gap: observe the sorting variable at least one business day before the month-end price used in the return denominator (signal from P_{t-Delta}); (2) return gap: keep the month-end signal but compute returns from prices observed in the first (last) five business days of the following month (month-begin return). Both rely on serially uncorrelated price noise; if noise is autocorrelated (illiquid, stale bonds) residual bias remains (DRR footnote 12).

Latent Implementation Bias (LIB) definition (Sec. 3): observed price P_hat = (1+delta)P with mean-zero noise delta; observed signal = true signal + eta. When the same delta enters the sorting signal and the return denominator (return error approx delta_{t+1} - delta_t, bias-relevant part approx -delta_t), long legs hold bonds with systematically positive return errors and short legs negative, spuriously widening the premium (correlated errors-in-variables, CEIV; named by Duarte-Jones-Wang 2024). DRR footnote 9 worked example: two bonds with true price 100 and true return 0, noise +0.50% and -0.50%: measured long-short return 1.00% while true is zero. LIB = CEIV bias + non-executability of the observed price (TRACE month-end price is a synthetic bid-ask-averaged volume-weighted price of past transactions, not a standing offer). CEIV is first-order in price noise, unlike the classic Blume-Stambaugh (1983) bid-ask bias (second-order, sigma_delta^2). Footnote 2: in a representative year 70% of bonds trade on 10 business days or fewer and under 0.50% trade every day (Dickerson, Robotti and Nozawa 2025).

Look-Ahead Bias (LAB) definition (Sec. 4): asymmetric ex-post return filtering, i.e. winsorizing or trimming returns at thresholds computed from full-sample data after portfolio formation. Fix: ex-ante filtering where thresholds use only information available at formation.

Open resources: companion site openbondassetpricing.com (error-corrected daily and monthly TRACE data, bias-corrected factors, replication code); data also on WRDS Contributed Data (daily and monthly); software PyBondLab on PyPI (pypi.org/project/PyBondLab/); full replication code on GitHub.
### 3.2 DRR Section 3 results: how much of price-based factor premia is latent implementation bias (VERIFIED, arXiv 2604.07880v1)

Three approaches compared: Approach 1 Unadjusted (month-end price for both signal and return); Approach 2 Adjusted Signal (signal from a price 1 to 10 business days before month-end; median gap 1 business day; capping the gap lower is immaterial); Approach 3 Adjusted Return (month-end signal, month-begin return). Bias measures: Bias(1-2) = (w_t - w_t^a) x r_End; Bias(1-3) = w_t x (r_End - r_Bgn). Seven price-based factors in Table 1 (decile sorts, value-weighted long top decile / short bottom decile; also "within-firm" factors following Dick-Nielsen-Feldhuetter-Pedersen-Stolborg 2023, firms with at least two bonds, aggregated by market value): ytm, cs (credit spread), bbtm (bond book-to-market), dcs6 (6-month change in log spreads, from Kelly-Palhares-Pruitt 2023), val_ipr, val_hz (value signals), str (short-term reversal).

Headline numbers (single-sort, Panel A): str unadjusted premium -0.99%/month (t = -4.46); signal-adjusted -0.09% (t = -0.51); bias 90 bp = 91% of the measured premium; unadjusted CAPMB alpha -0.77% becomes +0.12% after adjustment (bias 89 bp, t = -8.20); return-adjusted premium -0.17% (bias 82 bp). Value factors: val_ipr 0.96%/month falls to 0.51% after signal adjustment (bias 45 bp, t = 6.93, 47% of the premium); val_hz 0.81% falls to 0.45% (bias 36 bp). Spread-based (ytm, cs, bbtm): biases 15-18 bp/month, all t > 5. dcs6: -1.02% unadjusted, -0.50% adjusted (bias -52 bp). Within-firm (Panel B): after adjustment only val_ipr (alpha 0.18%, t = 4.25 with adjusted signal; 0.20%, t = 4.30 with adjusted return) and dcs6 (alpha -0.22%, t = -4.98) keep meaningful alphas; ytm, cs, bbtm, val_hz, str all |t| < 2.
Cumulative-return illustration (Fig. 3, growth of $1 to end-2024, log scale): str single-sort $12 unadjusted vs $1.1 (adjusted signal) and $1.4 (adjusted return); str within-firm $5.6 unadjusted vs $1.3 under both; cs single-sort $14 unadjusted vs $9.3 (adj. signal) and $6.5 (adj. return); cs within-firm $14 unadjusted vs $1.8 and $2.0.
LIB share of the unadjusted premium (Fig. 4): reversal over 80%; value and spread-change factors 45-55%; yield and credit-spread factors 20-50%; within-firm factors have larger LIB shares than single-sort.
Decomposition check (Table 2): correlation between (r_End - r_Bgn) and directly estimated LIB is 0.99 for all seven single-sort factors (residuals at most 0.02 percentage points); within-firm sorts 0.93-0.98 (residuals up to 0.09 pp).
Placebo test (Table B.1, App. B.5): over all 108 factors, among 78 non-price-based factors (where theory predicts no CEIV bias) the average absolute month-end minus month-begin difference is 0.03-0.10%/month and only 10-18% show a statistically significant gap; among 30 price-based factors 43-67% show a significant gap with average absolute differences 0.13-0.30%/month. Illiquidity factors (built from within-month averages of daily observations) are little affected; five illiquidity factors have CAPMB alphas 0.10-0.20%/month with |t| < 1.65 and within-firm alphas indistinguishable from zero.
Summary sentence in the paper: measurement error in transaction prices inflates price-based factor premia by 15 to 90 bp per month; non-price-based factors and within-month illiquidity averages are largely unaffected.

### 3.3 DRR Section 4: look-ahead bias definition (VERIFIED)

Required look-ahead (a bond must trade near both month-ends to have a return) is unavoidable and affects all researchers equally; avoidable look-ahead arises when winsorization/trimming thresholds are computed from the full sample (tau_ex-post = percentile of returns over s = 1..T, using months t+1..T that are not yet realized). Base (feasible) factor: all transaction-level price filters (Dick-Nielsen, decimal shift, bounce-back) plus the Section 3 signal gap, no return-level winsorization. Winsorized (infeasible) factor additionally caps returns at full-sample percentile thresholds. Extreme returns in the data: 15 bond-month observations with month-end returns below -95% and 385 above +95% (Internet Appendix). Comparable equity-options result: Duarte-Jones-Khorram-Mo-Wang (2025) show ex-post filters inflate Sharpe ratios from 0.5 to above 5 (the DRR intro says "over 1000%").

### 3.4 DRR Section 4.1: which factors depend on ex-post winsorization (VERIFIED, Tables 3-4 and Fig. 6 text)

LAB formula (Eq. 6): LAB_{t+1} = (1/N_L) sum_{Long} Delta_i - (1/N_S) sum_{Short} Delta_i, where Delta_i = winsorized return minus raw return; the return-evaluation component dominates the weight component (value-weighted portfolios with many bonds barely change composition). Rule of thumb from the paper: the winsorization tail that "works" is set by the sorting characteristic. Volatility, VaR, ES and macro-uncertainty-beta sorts put large-tail-exposure bonds in the long leg, so left-tail winsorizing protects the long leg and manufactures alpha; momentum sorts put past losers in the short leg, so right-tail winsorizing caps their rebounds and manufactures alpha. LAB is largest in distress (2008-09, March 2020).

Table 4 numbers (value-weighted deciles, long P10 / short P1, CAPMB alpha, winsorized-infeasible vs base-feasible):
- ivol_bbw (Bai-Bali-Wen 2021): alpha 0.53%/month (t = 2.58) with winsorization falls to 0.18% (t = 0.72) without; bias 35 bp = 66% of the measured alpha.
- ivol_vp (Chung-Wang-Wu 2019): 0.40% (t = 2.48) to 0.08% (t = 0.44); bias 31 bp.
- b_dunc3 (Bali-Subrahmanyam-Wen 2023, beta to 3-month change in macro uncertainty): alpha -0.32% (t = -1.87) to -0.11% (t = -0.57); bias 21 bp.
- ltr48_12 (Bali-Subrahmanyam-Wen 2021a, long-term reversal): -0.33% (t = -2.57) to -0.23% (t = -1.56).
- Footnote 15: b_dunc, b_unc, b_dvix_vp, b_psb_m have |t| < 1.96 for both winsorized premia and alphas even with winsorization.
- mom6_1 (Jostova-Nikolova-Philipov-Stahel 2013; staggered six-month holding period): winsorized premium 0.30%/month, base premium 0.00%; CAPMB alpha 0.46% (t = 2.39) falls to 0.22% (t = 1.08).
- mom12_1: winsorized premium +0.26% vs base premium -0.13% (a 40 bp bias converts a negative premium to a positive one).
- Even with winsorization only mom3_1 has a marginally significant premium (t = 1.78); mom6_1 and mom12_1 insignificant; winsorized CAPMB alphas for mom3_1 (t = 2.26) and mom6_1 (t = 2.39) pass 1.96 but are infeasible-spec artifacts, insignificant without ex-post filtering.
Fig. 6 case study (mom6_1, VW): under ex-ante trimming, in both tails and across all thresholds (10%-90% left; >10%-... right), the CAPMB alpha is statistically indistinguishable from zero: "No feasible filter generates a detectable momentum alpha". Under ex-post right-tail trimming at a 10% per-month threshold the alpha is about 0.60% and significant, declining monotonically as the threshold loosens.

### 3.5 DRR Section 4.1 timing and Section 5: non-standard errors (VERIFIED)

LAB time series (Fig. 7): positive spikes in the Great Recession (2008-09), euro-area debt crisis (2011-12) and COVID (March 2020); scatter against VIX is convex (R-squared quoted in the figure: 0.611 and 0.681 for ivol_bbw and 0.187 and 0.377 for others; panel mapping not recoverable from text extraction, so do not quote). Cumulative-LAB figure (Fig. 8, growth of $1 with log axis): the infeasible winsorized factor appears to hedge in crises (ltr48_12, ivol_bbw, b_dunc3, mom3_1); the feasible factor does not. Fig. 6 right-tail ex-post trimming: mom6_1 alpha about 0.60% at a 10% threshold, falling monotonically; left-tail ex-post trimming gives the mirror image, crossing zero only near -40%. "A researcher who chooses right-tail trimming finds that momentum appears to work, whereas a researcher who chooses left-tail trimming finds the opposite."

Non-standard error (NSE, Eq. 7) = Q0.75 minus Q0.25 of estimated premia across estimation paths (following Menkveld et al. 2024 and Walter-Weber-Weiss 2024); NSE/SE above 1 means researcher degrees of freedom exceed sampling noise.
Data uncertainty (Sec. 5.1): decile sorts (long P10, short P1); 108 ex-ante filter configurations per signal = 48 return-trimming (16 thresholds 0.20, 0.25, ..., 0.95; left tail, right tail, or both = 16 x 3) + 30 price-range (lower bound P_L in {2, 4, ..., 20}% of par; upper bound P_U in {150, 165, ..., 285}%; lower only, upper only, or matched pairs [2,285], [4,270], ..., [20,150] = 10 x 3) + 30 bid-ask bounce (absolute price change from previous trade above phi in {0.01, ..., 0.10}; negative, positive or both = 10 x 3). Times 2 weightings (EW, VW) x 3 universes (full, IG, NIG) = 648 paths per factor, 69,984 across 108 signals. Results: average NSE of premia 0.35%/month vs average premium 0.33%; NSE of CAPMB alphas 0.27% vs average alpha 0.15%; NSE/SE 1.15. NSE exceeds the mean premium in every cluster except Value, Credit & Default Risk, and Macro & Other Risk; Spreads-Yields-Size NSE 0.48% vs mean premium 0.45%; Volatility & Risk NSE 0.49% vs 0.44%; NSE/SE above 1 in four of nine clusters (Spreads-Yields-Size 1.43, Value 1.43, Volatility & Risk 1.36). Improvement rates (filter gives significant, larger alpha than baseline) 0-3% for most clusters (Credit & Default 0%); Value is the exception (21% of left-tail trimming paths, 15% of symmetric trimming). Applying all filter categories jointly would give about 4.2 million configurations per signal. "Applying ad hoc data filters to transaction-level-cleaned data is more likely to destroy factor premia and alphas than to reveal them."
Methodological uncertainty (Sec. 5.2): five dimensions: granularity (tercile, quintile, decile), breakpoint universe (all, IG only, large bonds only), weighting (EW, VW), rating subsample (all, IG, NIG), maturity bucket (all, short, intermediate, long): 2 x 3 x 3 x 3 x 4 = 216; minus 24 inadmissible (IG breakpoints cannot sort NIG bonds) and 24 redundant = 168 distinct constructions per signal; 168 x 108 = 18,144 series, of which 16 (0.088%) have empty legs (decile sorts in HY long-maturity), leaving 18,128. Average NSE of premia 0.31%/month vs average premium 0.22%; alphas NSE 0.20% vs 0.11%; NSE/SE averages 1.45 and exceeds 1 for all nine clusters (range 1.02 Market Risk to 2.00 Spreads-Yields-Size). Of 36 top factors (top four in each of nine clusters), 12 flip the sign of the long-short premium and 29 flip the sign of CAPMB alpha across constructions. 94% of methodological variations do not improve the CAPMB alpha relative to baseline; Value improves 16% overall, 36% in long-maturity constructions; long maturity is the main driver (9.5% vs 1.9% for intermediate).

### 3.6 DRR Section 6 conclusion numbers (VERIFIED)

LIB inflates price-based factor premia (yield, credit spread, value, short-term reversal) by up to 91%. LAB inflates momentum, long-term reversal, idiosyncratic volatility, downside risk and macro-exposure factors by 57-100%. Across 432 factor-specification combinations (108 signals x 2 weightings x 2 sorting methods) only 26 (6.0%) CAPMB alphas survive Benjamini-Hochberg FDR; 119 of 432 nominally significant mean premia (t above 1.96) but only 22 survive FDR. Survivors concentrate among credit-spread-based value factors. Among yield, spread, value and reversal factors only two retain significant CAPMB alphas after the measurement-error adjustment: a credit-spread-based value factor (retains roughly half its premium) and a spread-change factor (dcs6). The paper's own suggested extension: test whether survivors remain profitable after OTC execution costs (i.e. this is the gap the book's Ch 9 cost curve fills). The paper does NOT claim the bond CAPM is correct ("Our point is not that the bond CAPM is the correct model").

### 3.8 DRR Appendix A: filter parameters (VERIFIED; all thresholds in % of par, $1,000 face value; code at github.com/Alexander-M-Dickerson/trace-data-pipeline)

Decimal shift corrector (App. A.1): rolling unique-median anchor A_i over a centered window of 2w+1 transactions (w = 5, so 11; falls back to forward/backward windows near sample edges, then to the bond's full-series median). Raw relative error = |P - A|/A; a transaction is tested only if raw error exceeds tau_bad = 0.05. Candidate multiplicative factors F = {0.1, 0.01, 10, 100}. A correction is accepted only if all four hold: (1) raw error above 5%; (2) alignment: corrected relative error at most tau_pct = 2%, OR absolute error at most tau_abs = 8.0 price points, OR both anchor and corrected price within +/-15 points of par; (3) improvement: corrected error at most gamma = 0.20 of the raw error; (4) plausibility: corrected price within [5, 300] percent of par. If several factors pass, pick the one minimizing corrected error.
Bounce-back filter (App. A.2): trailing baseline B_i = median of unique prices of prior w = 5 trades. Detection threshold tau = 35 price points (35% of par, $350). Candidate if (1) one-step jump |dP| at least tau - delta (delta = 1); (2) displacement from baseline |P - B| at least tau - delta; or (3) par spike: P = 100 and |P - B| at least alpha x tau (alpha = 0.25). Reversion scan forward L = 5 rows: Path A opposite-signed move of at least tau - delta; Path B return to within alpha x tau of the original baseline. Flag logic: reassign flag to the preceding row if more displaced (at least 5 points); extend flags forward within five rows while displaced; flag persistent par blocks (at least 3 consecutive trades at par) with a two-row cooldown.

Daily distressed-bond filters (App. A.3; lookback/lookforward L = 5 days; prices in % of par):
- Filter 1, ultra-low outliers: candidate if P_t below tau_low = 0.10 (0.10% of par, $1) or P_t equals a suspicious round number in {0.001, 0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00}; flagged if the median of surrounding-window prices exceeding P_t is at least rho_anomaly = 3.0 times P_t.
- Filter 2, upward spikes: candidate if P_t above tau_high = 5.0 (5% of par, $50) or a round number above 0.50; flagged only if P_t/M_pre at least rho_spike = 3.0 (M_pre = median of prior L days) AND within L days some price falls to at most rho_recovery = 2.0 times M_pre.
- Filter 3, plateaus: candidate if P_t below tau_plateau = 0.15 or a round number, in runs of at least l_min = 2 consecutive identical-price days; flagged if P_pre/P_t >= 3.0 or P_post/P_t >= 3.0 or P_t is a round number.
- Filter 4, intraday inconsistency: flagged if at least one intraday price (high or low) is below tau_intraday = 20 (20% of par) AND (max - min)/mean of intraday prices exceeds gamma_range = 0.75.

Return definitions (App. A.4-A.5): month-end return (A.11), month-begin return (A.12), decomposition (A.13) as in 3.1. Excess return = bond return minus the Fama-French risk-free rate. DURATION-ADJUSTED excess return (A.14) = bond return minus a duration-matched U.S. Treasury return, obtained by linearly interpolating key-rate U.S. Treasury bond returns from WRDS using each bond's modified duration, "following Andreani, Palhares, and Richardson (2024)". (The paper's reference list also carries van Binsbergen-Nozawa-Schwert 2025 "Duration-based valuation of corporate bonds", RFS 38, 158-191, but the DRR appendix attributes the Treasury-matching implementation to Andreani-Palhares-Richardson; attribute accordingly.) Default flag: S&P rating code 22 (D) or Moody's code 21 (D). Default-event return (bond non-default at t, default at t+1): coupon set to zero, r_def = P_clean,t+1/(P_t + AI_t) - 1. Trading-in-default (default at both t and t+1): flat clean-price return P_clean,t+1/P_clean,t - 1, capped at the standard return (A.11).

Formal CEIV results (App. B): Prop. B.1 (sign): if the signal error g(delta) and the return error h(delta) are both decreasing in the price disturbance, then E[eps|L] >= E[eps] >= E[eps|S], so the long-short bias is nonnegative even if the true signal has zero predictive power (Remark B.2). Corollary B.1: non-price signals (ratings, betas) generate no CEIV bias, though noisy signals still attenuate. Theorem B.1 (closed form under linear contamination and joint normality): Bias = 2 kappa rho sigma_delta, with kappa = -E[Z | Z < Phi^{-1}(alpha)] (tail constant; finer sorts amplify it), rho = noise share sigma_eta/sigma_s_hat, sigma_delta = price-noise volatility. Bias is first-order in price noise, so it is large exactly where bonds are illiquid.

### 3.11 DRR Internet Appendix Table IA.VIII: signal definitions a writer needs (VERIFIED)

Value: bbtm = par price (100) over market price (Bartram-Grinblatt-Nozawa 2025). val_hz = (cs - fitted cs)/fitted cs, the percentage deviation of observed credit spread from a fitted "fair" spread estimated each month by cross-sectional regression with controls for rating, industry, maturity, spread change and callability (Houweling-van Zundert 2017). val_ipr = log(cs) minus X'beta-hat from a monthly cross-sectional regression with controls for rating, industry, log duration, volatility and callable (Israel-Palhares-Richardson 2018). val_hz_dts and val_ipr_dts = the same signals demeaned within duration-times-spread (DtS) quintiles to remove systematic spread-duration risk.
