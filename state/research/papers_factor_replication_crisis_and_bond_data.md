## 2. Annotated bibliography

Entry format: authors (year), exact title, venue; link only if fetched this session; key result; method and data; desk use. **B1. Dickerson, Robotti, Rossetti (2026), "The Corporate Bond Factor Replication Crisis", arXiv:2604.07880 [q-fin.PR], 9 April 2026. [FT]** Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07880. Key result: 108 signals in nine clusters; month-end price noise creates Latent Implementation Bias (up to 91% of the short-term reversal premium; 15-90 bp/month on price-based factors) and ex-post winsorization creates Look-Ahead Bias (6-month momentum premium 0.30% falls to 0.00%; 12-month momentum +0.26% is really -0.13%); only 26 of 432 specifications (6.0%) survive Benjamini-Hochberg FDR, mostly credit-spread value; non-standard errors (0.35%/month) exceed the average premium (0.33%). Method/data: TRACE Enhanced plus 144A, Aug 2002 to Dec 2024, 52,656 bonds, ~6,790 bonds per month; value-weighted decile long-short and CAPM-B alphas, Newey-West t. Desk use: the audit harness for any bond-factor backtest (signal gap, month-begin returns, ex-ante winsorization); the cost-free survivor list that Ch 9's cost curve must then be applied to. Detailed tables in Section 3.

**B2. Dickerson, Mueller, Robotti (2023), "Priced risk in corporate bonds", Journal of Financial Economics 150(2), article 103707 (Nov 2023); DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2023.103707; also arXiv:2604.05699 (7 Apr 2026). [ABS]** Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05699. Key result: previously proposed bond risk factors, "with traded liquidity as the only marginal exception", add nothing to the corporate bond market factor; the bond CAPM is not dominated by traded- or nontraded-factor models in pairwise or multiple model comparisons. Warwick Business School reports it won the FAMA-DFA Prize for best 2023 JFE paper on capital markets and asset pricing (https://wbs.ac.uk/news/flaw-data-corporate-bond-investors). Method: portfolio- and bond-level tests and model comparison. Desk use: justifies a one-factor (market or duration-adjusted market) null for every credit alpha claim; replication of BBW is the origin story of the field's data-hygiene turn.

**B3. Bai, Bali, Wen (2019), "Common risk factors in the cross-section of corporate bond returns" (RETRACTED), Journal of Financial Economics 131(3), 619-642; Retraction notice, JFE 150(3), 103721 (Dec 2023). [REC + SEC]** Retraction record verified via Crossref (DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2023.103721) and IDEAS (https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v150y2023i3s0304405x23001617.html), notice text itself not read (paywalled). Per Robotti's Warwick post (https://wbs.ac.uk/news/flaw-data-corporate-bond-investors, 10 Sep 2024): the original data had "lead" errors in two of the remaining factors and a "lag" error in the third, authors also filtered outlying losses after the fact, and after correction the four-factor model adds nothing beyond the CAPM; it was the first retraction of its kind at JFE and had been cited more than 400 times (Google Scholar, per that post). DRR cite Bai-Bali-Wen's var_95 and es_90 downside-risk factors among the ex-post-winsorization-sensitive set (alpha 0.56% and 0.74% winsorized vs 0.24% and 0.31% base). Desk use: a vendor or client "four-factor bond model" built on the retracted series is contaminated; the open site ships the four BBW factors rebuilt "correctly".

**B4. Dick-Nielsen, Feldhütter, Pedersen, Stolborg (2023), "Corporate Bond Factors: Replication Failures and a New Framework", SSRN 4586652 (2023); EFA 2025 Annual Meeting (Paris, 20-23 Aug 2025); 51 pp. [SEC]** Link: https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/corporate-bond-factors-replication-failures-and-a-new-framework/ (abstract paraphrase only; PDF not retrievable). Per QuantPedia (https://quantpedia.com/corporate-bond-factors-replication-failures-and-a-new-framework/): replicating each paper with its own data choices gives same-sign premia but only one significant; using other papers' choices none is significant and some flip sign; only 23% of literature-significant factors have significant alphas in their framework; value-weighted tertile portfolios with double sorts on three rating groups; alphas on a corporate bond market factor (CMKT) and a TERM factor; firm-level aggregation of bonds as test assets; several equity signals work for bonds; CAPM fails. Desk use: the within-firm (same-issuer, different-bond) construction that DRR adopt as Panel B; also the source of the cleaning protocol (individual outlier review, returns kept through default).

**B5. Dickerson, Nozawa, Robotti (2025), "Factor Investing with Delays", Hitotsubashi University IER Discussion Paper A771, 23 July 2025 (IDEAS lists no journal version). [FT intro]** Link: https://www.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/Common/publication/DP/DPS-A771.pdf. Key result: of 341 bond factors, 58 have significant gross mean returns and 42 significant CAPM alpha (average alpha of the 58: 0.28%/month); after delay plus bid-ask costs the average alpha falls to 0.09% (large trades) and 0.05% (small trades), leaving 2 and 1 significant factors; abstract: 51 outperform before costs, "nearly zero" after. Delay costs per month: spread factors 0.11% (about a quarter of gross alpha), equity-reversal 0.22% (over half), equity momentum 0.11%, vol/risk 0.06%, investment 0.01%, value/profits 0.02%. Nine ML models (Gu et al. style) give a 0.82%/month pre-cost alpha (t above 4) that falls to about 0.33-0.35% after delay costs of 0.17-0.36% (large) and 0.09-0.19% (small). Slicing orders, trading only the fastest-to-execute substitutes, and a 3-month holding period do not fix it. Method: exponential trade-arrival simulation, trade intensity from TRACE and MarketAxess (Kargar et al. 2025), ICE/BAML index constituents; in 2022 about 70% of 29,543 bonds traded on 10 or fewer days and only 109 (0.4%) traded daily. Desk use: the delay-cost term for the Ch 9 cost curve; fast signals (reversal, equity momentum) are the ones delays kill.

**B6. Andreani, Palhares, Richardson (2024), "Computing corporate bond returns: a word (or two) of caution", Review of Accounting Studies 29(4), 3887-3906 (online 2023); DOI 10.1007/s11142-023-09777-6. [ABS]** Key result: subtracting a T-bill return from bond returns contaminates inference because the rate component is negatively correlated with the spread component (worst for IG and time series); a WRDS-based fix removes the rate component with key-rate Treasury returns interpolated by duration; TRACE coverage differs from indices; index returns are synchronous and suffer less from stale pricing, so source returns from several vendors. Desk use: the duration-matched-Treasury recipe that DRR Eq. A.14 implements; also the argument for an index-return cross-check on every TRACE backtest.

**B7. van Binsbergen, Nozawa, Schwert (2025), "Duration-Based Valuation of Corporate Bonds", Review of Financial Studies 38(1), 158-191 (online 20 Sep 2024); DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhae054. [ABS]** Key result: decompose bond returns into a duration-matched government bond return plus a duration-adjusted excess return; IG bonds then earn a small credit risk premium comparable to the convenience yield, and duration adjustment resolves the CAPM's failure to price corporate bonds. The open pipeline imports their cleaning filters (GitHub README) and the OBAP site ships Nozawa's duration-matched Treasury returns. Desk use: define excess return versus a duration-matched Treasury, not a T-bill, in every credit backtest (Ch 9B step "excess returns").

**B8. Kelly, Palhares, Pruitt (2023), "Modeling Corporate Bond Returns", Journal of Finance 78(4), 1967-2008; DOI 10.1111/jofi.13233. [ABS]** Key result: a conditional five-factor model with time-varying loadings (IPCA) beats prior bond models "by a large margin"; its systematic portfolio has a high out-of-sample Sharpe ratio, implying a larger credit risk premium than previously estimated; closer debt-equity integration. Source of the dcs6 and age signals in the zoo. Desk use: the characteristic-based benchmark against which a desk's own factor alphas are measured (it is the live alternative to the bond CAPM in the replication debate).

**B9. Jostova, Nikolova, Philipov, Stahel (2013), "Momentum in Corporate Bond Returns", Review of Financial Studies 26(7), 1649-1693; DOI 10.1093/rfs/hht022. [ABS]** Key result: 81,491 US bonds, 1973-2011, TRACE plus dealer quotes; momentum is driven by non-investment-grade bonds; 1991-2011 profits average 59 bp/month across all bonds and 192 bp in NIG (282 bp for NIG bonds of private firms); not explained by risk or frictions. DRR Table 3 lists it as using the 99.50th-percentile winsorization (their footnote 16) and show mom3_1/6_1/12_1 premia of 0.17, 0.00 and -0.13%/month without winsorization. Desk use: the canonical claim that Ch 10's cross-sectional bond momentum has to answer; the replication result is that it does not survive an ex-ante filter.

**B10. Bartram, Grinblatt, Nozawa (2025), "Book-to-Market, Mispricing, and the Cross Section of Corporate Bond Returns", Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 60(3), 1185-1233 (online 12 Feb 2024); DOI 10.1017/s0022109024000048. [ABS]** Key result: bond book-to-market (par over market price) sorts: the top 20% beats the bottom 20% by 3-4% a year among senior bonds (even IG) after non-parametric controls for yield, bid-ask, duration, rating, age, size and structural-model equity hedges; larger for all-bond samples; the predictive power decays with implementation delay, which an efficient market would not do; a method that avoids liquidity filters and censorship. DRR's bbtm signal: unadjusted VW premium 0.93%/month (t 2.19) falls to 0.75% (1.80) and 0.66% (1.80) with the gap. Desk use: a value signal whose survival depends on delay and gap treatment; test it with month-begin returns.

**B11. Houweling, van Zundert (2017), "Factor Investing in the Corporate Bond Market", Financial Analysts Journal 73(2), 100-115; DOI 10.2469/faj.v73.n2.1. [ABS via IDEAS]** Key result: size, low-risk, value and momentum portfolios earn significant alphas, are weakly correlated so a combined portfolio has higher information ratio and lower tracking error, robust to transaction costs and to liquid-bond samples. Source of the val_hz signal (percentage deviation of spread from a fitted spread). DRR single-sort val_hz: 0.81%/month falls to 0.45% (t 2.29) with the signal gap, alpha 0.12% (t 1.13). Desk use: the practitioner multi-factor template in Ch 9, now with a published haircut for each leg.

**B12. Israel, Palhares, Richardson (2018), "Common Factors in Corporate Bond Returns", Journal of Investment Management 16, 17-46. [REC: Crossref lists SSRN 2576784 (2015); journal details from the DRR reference list]** Paper behind the val_ipr value signal (log spread residual from a fair-value regression on rating, industry, log duration, volatility and callability), the most robust survivor in DRR (single-sort VW alpha 0.76% falls to 0.31%, t 2.12; within-firm alpha 0.18%/month, t 4.25). Desk use: use the IPR residual-spread construction for Ch 9 value and add the DtS-quintile demeaning (val_ipr_dts), which also survives FDR within-firm.

**B13. Chordia, Goyal, Nozawa, Subrahmanyam, Tong (2017), "Are Capital Market Anomalies Common to Equity and Corporate Bond Markets? An Empirical Investigation", Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 52(4), 1301-1342; DOI 10.1017/s0022109017000515. [ABS]** Key result: many equity characteristics (accruals, SUE, idiosyncratic volatility) do not predict bond returns; profitability and asset growth are negatively related to bond returns; past equity returns predict bond returns (equity leads bonds); cross-sectional predictors generally do not give materially high Sharpe ratios after trading costs. Desk use: the earliest published statement that the cost-adjusted Sharpe, not the gross premium, is the bar; motivates equity-to-credit lead-lag in Ch 10.

**B14. Ivashchenko, Kosowski (2024), "Transaction Costs and Capacity of Systematic Corporate Bond Strategies", Financial Analysts Journal 80(4), 53-80 (online 17 Jul 2024); DOI 10.1080/0015198x.2024.2360390. [ABS]** Key result: using Kyle-Obizhaeva market-microstructure invariance they estimate implicit and explicit costs with positive market impact; capacity is limited: market impact reduces net returns to zero as fund size grows; high-turnover strategies hit capacity fast, low-turnover credit-risk-focused strategies have much higher capacity; costs do not absorb the bond risk premium even in the largest market portfolios. Desk use: capacity curve for Ch 9; pairs with B5 (delay costs).

**B15. Ghaderi, Plante, Roussanov, Seo (2025), "Pricing of Corporate Bonds: Evidence From a Century-Long Cross-Section", SSRN 5218755; successor NBER WP 35578 (Aug 2026), "Reconstructing a Century of U.S. Corporate Bonds: Credit Risk in Historical Perspective". [ABS of NBER version]** Link: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35578. Key result: a 128-year hand-collected plus modern panel of over 100,000 bonds and 7 million observations; recent samples suggest excess returns mostly reflect the term premium, but the long sample shows a sizable, significant credit risk premium; spreads predict returns and macro aggregates, with weaker business-cycle forecasting when pre-war data are included. DRR cite the SSRN version for "extending the cross-section to nearly a century reveals pricing power for several factors". Desk use: the out-of-sample answer to "26 survivors in 22 years of TRACE": TRACE-era power is low, so null results are not proof of no premium (Ch 7 small-sample argument).

**B16. Dickerson, Julliard, Mueller (2026), "The Co-Pricing Factor Zoo", arXiv:2604.04430 [q-fin.PR], 6 Apr 2026. [ABS]** Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04430. Key result: evaluates 18 quadrillion candidate models for pricing bonds and stocks jointly; equity-based and nontradable factors, controlling for Treasury term-structure risk, explain bond risk premia; a Bayesian-model-averaging SDF of dozens of imperfect proxies beats every low-dimensional model with out-of-sample Sharpe ratio 1.5-1.8; the OBAP site ships the factor files (djm_data.zip). Desk use: a shrinkage/averaging alternative to picking survivors; pairs with Ch 7B shrinkage.

**B17. Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (2023), "Is There a Replication Crisis in Finance?", Journal of Finance 78(5), 2465-2518; DOI 10.1111/jofi.13249. [ABS]** Key result: a Bayesian model of factor replication; most equity factors replicate, cluster into 13 themes, work out of sample in 93 countries, and the evidence is strengthened by the large number of factors. This is the optimistic counterpoint that DRR set against their bond result (equity replicates under consistent methodology; bonds do not under error-prone data and ex-post filters). Desk use: the counter-argument a reader will raise; the answer is that bonds differ in data quality, not in economics.

**B18. Hou, Xue, Zhang (2020), "Replicating Anomalies", Review of Financial Studies 33(5), 2019-2133 (online 10 Dec 2018); DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhy131. [ABS]** Key result: with NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted returns, 65% of 452 anomalies fail the single |t| of 1.96 hurdle (96% of trading-frictions anomalies); at the multiple-testing hurdle of 2.78 the failure rate is 82%; replicated magnitudes are much smaller. The equity precedent for DRR's bond audit (and for value-weighting plus liquid-universe rules as the first defense). Desk use: apply the same VW and breakpoint discipline to any bond factor claim.

**B19. Harvey, Liu, Zhu (2016), "... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 5-68 (online 9 Oct 2015); DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhv059. ## 3. Open-data pipeline: definitions and exact numbers a writer needs

All items here are VERIFIED against the DRR full text (arXiv 2604.07880v1; the 94-page OBAP-hosted PDF adds the Internet Appendix, "IA") and the pipeline repository, read 2026-10-18. Full tables beyond those printed here: DRR Tables 1-6, B.1, IA.VIII-IA.XI.

**3.1 Panel build.** Universe: TRACE Enhanced plus Rule 144A (144A enters June 2014, the WRDS start; 144A is over 20% of new issuance in 2025), joined to Mergent FISD; keep USD, fixed-rate, non-convertible, non-ABS bonds, $1,000 par, original maturity of at least one year. Sample Aug 2002 to Dec 2024 (269 months), 52,656 bonds, about 6,790 bond-months per month. Bonds are tracked through default. TRACE real-time dissemination is capped at $5 million (IG) and $1 million (non-IG), shown as "5MM+" and "1MM+", reported within 15 minutes; uncapped volume is available in daily reports (FINRA TRACE timeframes page). What the Dick-Nielsen step has to act on (FINRA historic file layout): trade status T (report), X (cancel), C (cancelled correction), R (new correction), Y (reversal = cancellation of a report submitted more than 20 business days earlier, flagged by As-Of indicator R); cancels, corrections and reversals carry the Prior Trade Report Date, Prior Reference Number and First Trade Control Number (7 digits); reversals are matched to the original on CUSIP, quantity, price, execution date and time, buy/sell and contra-party indicator; Buyer and Seller Capacity are A (agency) or P (principal), the basis of agency de-duplication.
Stage 0 order (per CUSIP; README_stage0.md): (0) price-scale normalization; (1) Dick-Nielsen cancellations, corrections, reversals, agency de-duplication (Enhanced); (2) decimal-shift corrector: factors {0.1, 0.01, 10, 100}, rolling unique-median anchor over 11 trades, test only if raw error above 5%, accept if corrected error at most 2% OR at most 8 price points OR both within 15 points of par, corrected error at most 20% of raw, corrected price in [5, 300]; (3) intraday window (off); (4) NYSE calendar; (5) price in (0, 1000]; (6) volume screen (OFF by default; if on, $10,000); (7) bounce-back: baseline = median of 5 prior unique prices, trigger at 35 points ($350) via one-step jump, displacement from baseline, or a par spike (100 with displacement at least 8.75), reversal within 5 rows, par runs of 3 or more flagged; (8) drop yield equal to price; (9) drop trades above 50% of offering amount; (10) drop trades after maturity; (11) drop bonds whose first 3 prints are followed by a 50-point move. Daily stage (distressed bonds, L = 5 days): ultra-low price below 0.10 (or round number) with surrounding median at least 3x; upward spike above 5.0 with price at least 3x the prior median and a return to at most 2x within 5 days; plateaus of 2 or more identical days below 0.15 or at round numbers; intraday range above 75% of the mean when any price is below 20.
Returns: month price = last daily VWAP in the last 5 NYSE business days; month-end return r_End = (P_end,t+1 + AI + C)/(P_end,t + AI) - 1; month-begin return r_Bgn uses the first price in the first 5 business days of t+1 as denominator (median gap one business day); r_End is approximately the gap return P_bgn,t+1/P_end,t - 1 plus r_Bgn. Excess return = minus the Fama-French one-month T-bill rate (the headline results use this); duration-adjusted excess return = minus a duration-matched Treasury return, linearly interpolating WRDS key-rate Treasury returns on modified duration (DRR App. A.14 cites Andreani-Palhares-Richardson 2024; the concept is van Binsbergen-Nozawa-Schwert 2025, and the OBAP site ships duration-matched Treasury returns contributed by Yoshio Nozawa). DRR report that multi-factor alphas are lower on average in unreported results. Market factor MKTB = VW excess return of all bonds; alphas on MKTB ("CAPM-B"); Newey-West lags floor(T^0.25). Default month: coupon zero, r = P_clean,t+1/(P_t + AI_t) - 1; trading in default: flat clean-price return capped at the standard return; default = S&P code 22 or Moody's 21.

**3.2 Latent Implementation Bias (LIB).** P_hat = (1+delta)P; the same delta enters the sorting signal and the return denominator (return error is about delta_{t+1} - delta_t, relevant part -delta_t), so the long leg is loaded with negative-noise bonds: footnote example, noise of +/-0.50% gives a 1.00% measured long-short against a true zero. Theorem B.1: bias = 2 kappa rho sigma_delta (kappa a normal tail constant, larger for finer sorts; rho the noise share). Non-price signals (ratings, betas) have no CEIV bias; illiquidity built from within-month averages is also little affected. Fixes: signal gap (signal from a price 1 to 10 business days earlier) or return gap (month-begin returns). Table 1, VW decile, monthly %, premium/CAPM-B alpha, (unadjusted) then (signal gap) then (return gap), t in parentheses: ytm 1.20/0.69 (2.97/2.50), 1.05/0.54 (2.63/1.96), 0.92/0.44 (2.64/1.80); cs 1.13/0.66, 0.96/0.50 (2.52/1.82), 0.82/0.38 (2.45/1.55); bbtm 0.93/0.54, 0.75/0.35 (1.80/1.12), 0.66/0.29; dcs6 -1.02/-0.79, -0.50/-0.29 (-2.52/-1.79), -0.46/-0.25; val_ipr 0.96/0.76 (4.82/5.24), 0.51/0.31 (2.68/2.12), 0.51/0.33 (3.05/2.61); val_hz 0.81/0.47, 0.45/0.12 (2.29/1.13), 0.43/0.12; str -0.99/-0.77 (-4.46/-3.58), -0.09/+0.12 (-0.51/0.68), -0.17/0.03. LIB share of the unadjusted premium: reversal above 80% (91% signal-gap); value and spread-change 45-55%; yield and spread 20-50%; larger within-firm. Decomposition check: corr(r_End - r_Bgn, LIB) = 0.99 (within-firm 0.93-0.98). Placebo (Table B.1): month-end minus month-begin gap is significant for 43-67% of 30 price-based factors (average absolute 0.13-0.30%/month) but only 10-18% of 78 non-price factors (0.03-0.10%). Cumulative $1 of single-sort reversal: $12 unadjusted vs $1.1-$1.4 adjusted.

**3.3 Look-Ahead Bias (LAB).** Winsorizing at full-sample percentiles uses future data; LAB = (1/N_L) sum Delta_i over longs minus (1/N_S) sum Delta_i over shorts. Factors whose significance needs 0.50th-percentile (left-tail) winsorization: macro-uncertainty betas, long-term reversal, idiosyncratic volatility, VaR/ES, Pastor-Stambaugh and Amihud betas; needing 99.50th (right-tail): momentum. Table 4 (VW deciles; CAPM-B alpha winsorized vs base): ivol_bbw 0.53 (t 2.58) vs 0.18 (0.72); ivol_vp 0.40 vs 0.08; var_95 0.56 vs 0.24 (t 1.42); es_90 0.74 vs 0.31 (1.42); ltr48_12 -0.33 vs -0.23 (-1.56); b_dunc3 -0.32 vs -0.11 (-0.57). Momentum, premium winsorized vs base / alpha winsorized vs base: mom3_1 0.33 (t 1.78) vs 0.17 (0.90) / 0.45 (2.26) vs 0.31 (1.65); mom6_1 0.30 (1.45) vs 0.00 (0.01) / 0.46 (2.39) vs 0.22 (1.08); mom12_1 0.26 (0.92) vs -0.13 (-0.39) / 0.46 (1.60) vs 0.14 (0.51). Under any ex-ante trimming of either tail, mom6_1 alpha is indistinguishable from zero; ex-post right-tail trimming at 10% a month gives about 0.60% and the sign flips with left-tail trimming (zero near -40%). LAB spikes in 2008-09, 2011-12 and March 2020, is convex in VIX, sits in the short leg for momentum and the long leg for risk factors, and is 2-3x larger in NIG than IG.

**3.4 What survives (IA.3).** 432 specifications = 108 signals x EW/VW x single-sort/within-firm. CAPM-B alpha t above 1.96: 65 (15.0%); BH-FDR (5%, per panel) survivors: VW single 0, VW within-firm 9, EW single 1, EW within-firm 16 = 26 (6.0%). Mean-return t above 1.96: 119 (27.5%); FDR survivors 22 (5.1%: VW within-firm 5, EW within-firm 17). 25 of 26 are within-firm; the only single-sort survivor is bond age (EW). Survivors by cluster: Spreads/Yields/Size dcs6*, age, ytm; Value val_ipr, val_ipr_dts, val_hz_dts; Momentum sysmom12_1*, idimom12_1 (both EW within-firm); Vol & Risk rsj*, rsk*, dskew*, iskew*; Market b_mktb_up*; Vol & Liq betas b_coskew*, b_illiq*, b_psb, b_rvol*; Macro b_dunc*, b_duncf*; no Illiquidity or Credit & Default survivor. Annualized CAPM-B alpha (t): within-firm dcs6* VW 2.66% (4.98), EW 2.46% (6.77); val_ipr_wf VW 2.22% (4.25), EW 2.30% (5.03); val_ipr_dts_wf VW 2.47% (4.50), EW 2.23% (5.51); sysmom12_1_wf* EW 2.20% (3.04); idimom12_1_wf EW 1.55% (2.94). VW single-sort val_ipr 3.71% (2.12) and val_ipr_dts 2.74% (2.26) pass 1.96 but not FDR. All gross of costs and of the cost of shorting bonds or running same-issuer pairs. None of the seven Table 1 price-based single-sort factors has a significant adjusted alpha in IG or in NIG separately (best: val_hz in NIG, 0.37%/month, t 1.91).

**3.5 Non-standard errors (Tables 5-6).** NSE = IQR of premia across paths. Data paths: 108 ex-ante filters (48 return-trimming at 0.20-0.95 in 16 steps x left/right/both; 30 price-range bounds, P_L 2-20 and P_U 150-285 % of par; 30 bounce filters 0.01-0.10) x EW/VW x all/IG/NIG = 648 per signal, 69,984 total: NSE 0.35%/month vs mean premium 0.33% (alpha NSE 0.27 vs 0.15); NSE/SE 1.15. Construction paths: 168 per signal (tercile/quintile/decile x breakpoints all/IG/large x EW/VW x all/IG/NIG x maturity all/short/intermediate/long, after dropping inadmissible), 18,128 series: NSE 0.31 vs mean 0.22; NSE/SE 1.45 (1.02 Market Risk to 2.00 Spreads-Yields-Size); 12 of 36 top factors flip premium sign, 29 flip alpha sign; 94% of variations do not improve the baseline alpha. Value is the one cluster with a detectable baseline premium and the only one where filters often help (21% of left-tail and 15% of symmetric trimming paths improve the alpha).

**3.6 Signal definitions (IA.VIII).** val_hz = (cs - fitted cs)/fitted cs with monthly cross-sectional fit on rating, industry, maturity, spread change, callable (Houweling-van Zundert); val_ipr = log(cs) - X'beta-hat on rating, industry, log duration, volatility, callable (Israel-Palhares-Richardson); _dts = demeaned within duration-times-spread quintiles; bbtm = par/price; dcs6 = log(cs_{t-6}) - log(cs_t); momentum mom{3,6,12}_1 = cumulative return over t-2..t-1, t-5..t-1, t-11..t-1; sysmom/idimom = sum of fitted values/residuals from a rolling 36-month (min 12) CAPM-B regression (Blitz-Huij-Martens residual momentum); imom = equal-weighted momentum of other bonds in the same FF17 industry (Wang-Wu-Yang). The surviving momentum is residual/systematic, within-firm, equal-weighted; plain price momentum, industry momentum (VW single-sort imom12_1 3.4-4.5% alpha, t 2.1-2.4) and short-term reversal do not survive FDR.

**3.7 Open infrastructure (2026-10-18).** openbondassetpricing.com: daily and monthly panels (also WRDS Contributed Data), 108-factor zoo, 341-characteristic ML panel (2002-07 to 2022-12), the four corrected Bai-Bali-Wen factors, Nozawa's duration-matched Treasury returns, 400-page data-error reports; no data licence stated; paper under R&R. Pipeline repo: five stages (0 and 1 on WRDS; 2-4 local), v4.2.1, MIT, September 2026, about 5 hours and 5 GB end to end, needs WRDS TRACE, FISD and ratings (public Stage 1 download omits ratings). PyBondLab 0.3.0 (24 Sep 2026; MIT; Python 3.11+): SingleSort, DoubleSort, WithinFirmSort, batch classes, DataUncertaintyAnalysis, AssayAnomaly, turnover, banding; needs columns date, ID, ret, VW, RATING_NUM.

## 4. Synthesis: open debates and replication status

**4.1 Replication status (as of 2026-10-18).**
- BBW four-factor model: independently replicated and refuted. DMR found timing (lead/lag) errors and after-the-fact filtering of extreme losses; JFE retraction notice Dec 2023. Corrected factors are hosted on the OBAP site. I found no Georgetown review outcome, so the book should state the retraction and the errors, and nothing about intent.
- "Most published bond factors": two independent teams with different cleaning reach the same direction. DFPS: 23% of literature-significant factors keep significant alphas, CAPM fails. DRR: 26 of 432 specifications survive FDR; most spanned by the bond market factor. They differ on mechanism (data errors and construction vs LIB/LAB) and on the null (DFPS reject the CAPM; DRR use it only as a benchmark).
- Price-only bond momentum (JNPS): does not reproduce under feasible filters (DRR); only residual/systematic 12-1 momentum survives, EW within-firm.
- Credit-spread value: the most robust family (retains about half its premium; within-firm FDR survivor; VW single-sort t of 2.1 to 2.6, borderline).
- Illiquidity premium: no significant alpha in five measures (DRR), consistent with Richardson-Palhares (2018).
- ML bond alphas: 0.82%/month gross, about 0.33% net of delay costs (DNR); no independent reproduction of the 341-characteristic panel found.
- DRR itself: one team, open code and data, under revise-and-resubmit, pipeline still changing (v4.2.1, Sept 2026): not yet independently reproduced. Pin versions.

**4.2 Open debates.** (1) The null: bond CAPM (DMR, DRR) vs duration-adjusted CAPM (vBNS: duration adjustment fixes the IG failure) vs IPCA five-factor (KPP) vs Bayesian-averaged SDF with equity factors (Dickerson-Julliard-Mueller, out-of-sample Sharpe 1.5-1.8) vs "CAPM fails" (DFPS). Note DRR's headline excess returns subtract the T-bill rate, which APR and vBNS argue is the wrong benchmark for IG and time series. A survivor list is only as good as its null. (2) Bias or cost? LIB mixes a statistical bias with a real implementation wedge (month-end print is not executable); the one-business-day median gap is a floor, and DNR show longer delays erase most of what is left. (3) Power: 22 TRACE years against a 128-year panel with a credit premium (Ghaderi et al.); a 6% FDR hit rate may partly be low power. (4) Outliers: DFPS review outliers one by one and keep default returns; DRR use rule-based filters and ex-ante winsorization; both keep defaults. (5) Equity optimism (JKP, Chen-Zimmermann: factors replicate with consistent methods) vs bonds. (6) Multiple-testing standard: BH-FDR is lenient next to HLZ's t above 3.0 or HXZ's 2.78 (DRR note general-dependence procedures would only strengthen their result). (7) Within-firm survivors are 25 of 26, a different object from the long-only sector-neutral portfolios that desks run: shorting bonds and issuer pairs, untested for costs. (8) Quotes or prints: DRR concede dealer quotes can be stale; APR show index returns suffer less from stale pricing: use both.

**4.3 The five must-reads.** (1) B1 Dickerson-Robotti-Rossetti 2026, with its Internet Appendix IA.3 (the survivor list). (2) B2 Dickerson-Mueller-Robotti 2023 together with the BBW retraction notice B3. (3) B4 Dick-Nielsen-Feldhütter-Pedersen-Stolborg (independent, AQR-affiliated, different data). (4) B5 Dickerson-Nozawa-Robotti 2025 (what delay costs do to every survivor). (5) B7 van Binsbergen-Nozawa-Schwert 2025 (with B6 Andreani et al.: the return definition every other number depends on). Read B17 Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen for balance.
