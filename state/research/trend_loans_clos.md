# TREND dossier: Leveraged loans, CLOs & loan-market structure

*Researcher dossier for "Systematic & Quantitative Credit: From PJT RSSG to the Automated Desk". Compiled 2026-10-17. Builds on (does not duplicate) `trend_lmt_default_recovery_cycle.md`, `trend_electronification_pt_etf_liquidity.md`, `trend_private_credit_convergence.md`.*

## 1. Headline trends

1. **The market is at a record size, but it grows by recycling, not by new money.** Institutional leveraged loans reached about $1.4–1.55trn at 2025:Q4. The Fed's Table 1.1 shows $1,549bn and 12.6% average annual growth since 2000, the fastest of any asset class it lists. Yet 2025 gross issuance of about $400bn produced only about $106bn of net new loans against $294bn of repayments. H1 2026 was $440bn gross against $131bn net. Most "issuance" is the same risk refinanced, repriced or extended.

2. **Optionality is the dominant P&L driver, on both sides of the CLO balance sheet.** On the asset side, borrowers hold a near-par call. The 2024–25 repricing cycle peaked at $52.5bn/month (632 borrowers, 57% of the index), and about half the market repriced in 2025, yet average coupons fell only about 20bp. On the liability side, CLO equity holds the call. Refi plus reset volume ($308bn in 2024, $337bn in 2025) exceeded record new issuance ($203bn, $209bn), and in H1 2026 new issue was only about 35% of the roughly $230bn of activity. Ignore these two embedded calls and you model the wrong instrument.

3. **The CLO is the marginal buyer, so CLO rules shape prices.** CLOs buy nearly three-quarters of new leveraged loans. Their rule-based constraints (the 7.5% CCC bucket, haircuts that mark excess CCCs at the *lowest* CCC price, the 90c discount-obligation rule, junior OC/ID tests) produce predictable, non-fundamental selling. Market-average CCC exposure (4.3–4.6%) and junior OC cushion (3.8–3.9%) in 2025–26 are healthy on average but dispersed by vintage. Pre-pandemic CLOs sat at a 2.76% cushion and 7.08% CCC in April 2025.

4. **Settlement remains the market's defining friction, and the law keeps it that way.** Par trades follow a T+7 convention but have averaged about 20 business days over the decade (**CHECK**). *Kirschner* (2d Cir., Aug 2023) held that syndicated term loans are not securities, so no T+1 mandate, DTC eligibility or securities-law disclosure regime is coming. Even so, secondary turnover hit a record above $1trn LTM in 1Q26.

5. **Loan e-trading has passed its first inflection.** Octaura's share of secondary loan volume went from 1% (Apr 2024) to 7.5–7.7% (Jan–Jun 2026), with 30+ dealers and about 170–180 buy-side firms, a CLO BWIC protocol (Feb 2026) and record $19.6bn in Q1 2026. In parallel, the agent-bank consortium Versana took amendment voting digital (Oct 2026). That matters for LMTs, which are fundamentally consent games.

6. **The retail wrapper moved from loans to AAA CLO debt.** Bank-loan mutual funds shrank 21% to $71bn (2019–2025), and SRLN is about $5.4bn. US CLO ETFs went from about $38bn (Sep 2025) to above $50bn (Jul 2026), and JAAA alone passed $30bn (Aug 2026). JAAA holds about 62% reset paper, so ETF inflows are effectively a bid for CLO resets. That bid lowers the equity's cost of liabilities and keeps the CLO bid for loans alive.

7. **Defaults are low on payment, high on economics, and increasingly sector-shaped.** The TTM payment-default rate is about 1.0–1.2% (2025–H1 2026), but the dual-track rate including LMEs was 4.70% at end-2024, the highest since 2016. Defaults including distressed exchanges "remained relatively high" (Fed, May 2026). The 2026 stress vector is software and AI disruption: the Feb–Apr 2026 "SaaS-pocalypse" froze repricings ($11.8bn over three months after $108bn in January), and many MM CLOs carry more than 30% software.

8. **Regulators are re-plumbing CLO demand.** NAIC's ratings-plus-thickness C-1 factors for CLOs take effect with YE2026 RBC, including an 11.77% surcharge on thin (≤4%) below-IG BSL tranches. N-PORT moves to quarterly reporting in 2027–28.

## 3. Narrative: drivers and the 2026-2028 outlook

### 3.1 The machine: how a leveraged loan is priced in 2026

The RSSG mental model (a TLB at the top of the capital structure, with recovery a fight over collateral and consent) is right in distress but misses where a performing loan's price comes from. A CLO warehouse (about 300 open in the US at YE2025, 351 by March 2026) buys loans financed by a bank. Once the manager can sell AAAs at a spread that leaves the equity a target return, the warehouse is termed out into a CLO. The CLO then reinvests for 3–5 years, and a series of rules (CCC bucket, WARF, diversity score, OC/IC tests) determines what it can buy and when it must sell. Because CLOs take nearly three-quarters of new loan supply, the clearing price of a new B2/B loan is effectively *the price at which the CLO arbitrage still works*. That arbitrage is loan spread minus the weighted cost of CLO liabilities minus fees, levered about 10x into the equity.

Two sources of optionality sit on top of that machine. First, borrowers can reprice at par once the soft-call period lapses. When the share of loans bid above par is high (58% at YE2025, 76% of BB- loans by May 2026), repricing becomes nearly automatic. The 2024–25 wave averaged $52.5bn per month at its peak and touched 57% of index borrowers. The result is negative convexity: upside capped near 100.5, a full credit tail below. Second, CLO equity holds the call on the liabilities. When AAAs tighten, the equity refinances (keeping the reinvestment period) or resets (extending it). In 2025 resets ($232bn) were more than double refis ($105bn). By September 2026, refis exceeded resets for the first time since April, with reinvestment periods on new refi/reset deals shortened to about 3 years and non-call to about 1.3 years.

### 3.2 Drivers, 2024–2026

**(a) Rate plateau plus scarce net supply leads to repricing waves.** With the Fed at 3.50–3.75% after the Dec-2025 cut, floating coupons stayed high, which kept retail and CLO demand strong. When demand outruns net supply (2025 net new was $106bn against $294bn of repayments), loans trade to par and borrowers reprice. The January 2026 burst ($108bn) shows how quickly the call is exercised once the window opens.

**(b) Liability-side tightening leads to a reset/refi boom, which lowers the AAA cost of funds.** CLO ETFs, Japanese and US bank demand, and insurers compressed AAAs to about 122–124bp (US new issue, mid-2026). Because JAAA holds about 62% reset paper, ETF flows feed directly into reset demand. The circle is self-reinforcing: ETF inflow → tighter AAA → more resets → longer CLO life → more loan demand → tighter loans → more repricing → thinner equity arbitrage. The 2025 market-wide CLO equity total return of about −15% (**CHECK**) shows that the circle eventually eats its own equity.

**(c) Stress no longer arrives through payment default but through LMEs and sector shocks.** The payment-default rate is near 1%. The dual-track rate including LMEs is several times higher, and the Fed notes that distressed exchanges remain "relatively high." For a CLO, an LME is a rating event (often SD/D, then CCC) and a price event at the same time. It hits the CCC bucket and, via haircuts, the OC test, which puts it on the forced-selling list. The shock of 2026 was sector-shaped. Software accounts for more than 5% of the loan index and more than 30% of many MM CLOs. AI-disruption fears in Feb–Apr 2026 drove the index average bid to 94.17 and stalled repricing for three months. A one-industry shock is exactly the shape that Kundu (2023) shows spreads through CLO constraints to *unrelated* loans.

**(d) Plumbing: e-trading and digital agency, but the settlement bottleneck stays.** Octaura's 7–8% share, CLO BWIC auctions, and Versana's agent data and voting rails are digitizing the front and middle office. The settlement cycle remains a 7-day convention averaging about 20 days. There is no legal forcing function: loans are not securities after *Kirschner*, and the trade is an assignment that needs agent and often borrower consent. Turnover is nevertheless at a record (more than $1trn LTM by 1Q26, against $821bn in 2024), so the gap between execution speed and settlement speed is widening. That gap creates delayed-compensation economics and settlement-risk carry, both of which a systematic desk must model.

**(e) Regulation of the buyers, not the loans.** The NAIC's YE2026 CLO factors, with the thin-tranche surcharge for below-IG BSL, move insurer demand toward thicker tranches and toward MM CLOs, which are exempt from the surcharge. Separately, MM CLOs (about $40bn of 2025 issuance, $6.8bn in Sep 2026 alone) keep growing as the securitized funding line for private credit. That links this dossier to `trend_private_credit_convergence.md`.

### 3.3 Outlook 2026–2028 (scenario-weighted, for the desk)

- **Base case (about 55%): "Refinancing machine continues."** US CLO new issue lands near $180–200bn in 2026. Consensus was just under $200bn, with forecasts from $190bn (DB) to $220bn (KBRA); the Voya YTD count of about $127bn to early October points to the lower end (**CHECK** definitions). Total activity including refi/reset reaches about $450bn+, given $361bn YTD through September. A deep pool of about $422bn exiting non-call keeps refi/reset volume dominant into 2027. Loan repricing comes back in windows whenever the share bid above par exceeds about 50%. M&A-driven net supply finally rises in 2027, which relieves the repricing pressure. The CLO ETF stack keeps growing: extrapolating about $10bn per half-year gives a speculative $70–80bn by end-2027.
- **Stress case (about 30%): "Sector shock plus maturity wall."** The 2028 maturity tower (mostly 2021 vintage loans) meets the LMT playbook. CCC buckets in pre-2020 and 2021 vintages rise above 7.5%, junior OC tests begin failing in clusters, and equity distributions are diverted. Forced selling concentrates in the same names across CLOs, given high manager overlap: JAAA alone spans 117 managers, with the top 10 at about 40%. This is the regime where the forced-flow alpha in Section 5 pays.
- **Tail (about 15%): "Wrapper run."** A sharp AAA spread gap plus ETF outflows test JAAA-type vehicles' in-kind and cash redemption mechanics against a thin CLO secondary market (about $219bn TRACE in 2025, 79% IG). Bank-loan fund liquidity (median about 4.5% cash-like) is the weak link on the loan side. The April 2025 episode showed outflows of about 4% in a week for BL funds.

Structural calls with high confidence: (1) loan e-trading share above 10% by 2027 (Octaura grew from 1% to 7.7% in 21 months); (2) no move to T+1 for loans without a legal or regulatory forcing function; (3) refi/reset continuing to exceed new issue whenever AAAs are inside about 130bp; (4) defaults reported on two tracks (payment vs. dual-track) indefinitely, with the gap a regime variable.

## 4. Implications for a systematic credit desk and for the RSSG-to-quant reader

**For the systematic desk.**
- *Price formation is rule-driven.* Model the CLO constraint set (CCC bucket, haircut-at-lowest-price, 90c discount rule, junior OC/ID cushions) as a state vector per CLO. Aggregate it to a per-loan "forced-seller pressure" score. Kundu's estimates are the prior for the price impact: −61 to −180bp per 1pp of CLO-routed shock exposure, and 180–404bp per $1m sold.
- *Negative convexity is the default.* Treat every loan as a credit-risky floater short a par call. Expected return = spread − expected loss − E[repricing haircut], where the haircut depends on price relative to par, soft-call expiry, rating (BBs reprice first: 11 of 15 May 2026 deals) and the open/shut state of the repricing window.
- *Settlement is a risk factor.* With par trades averaging about 20 days, inventory, financing and delayed-comp economics differ from bonds. A loan PT or BWIC engine (Ch 19/21) must price settlement-time distributions, not T+1.
- *The ETF stack is a flow signal.* CLO ETF creations/redemptions (JAAA at about 88% of the tracked cohort) lead AAA spreads, and AAA spreads lead reset volume and loan demand.
- *Execution venues are now measurable.* Octaura's buy/sell inquiry ratios (70% selling in Mar 2025, 72% buying in Jun 2025) and cost-to-buy/sell estimates (May 2025: 13c / 9c) are directly usable for pre-trade cost models.

**For the RSSG-to-quant reader.**
- What you called the "CLO bid" or "CLO puke" in restructuring rooms is a documented, quantifiable mechanism. The CCC haircut rewards managers for selling their *higher-priced* CCCs ("contractual arbitrage"). The forced seller often sells the wrong bond, the one you want to buy.
- Your LMT instincts transfer into CLO tests. An uptier or drop-down is an OC-test event for every excluded or participating CLO. The participation decision is constrained by CLO documents and buckets, not only by economics. Holder-structure modeling (Ch 13/14) needs CLO trustee data as an input.
- The shift from "will this company survive?" to "what distribution of forced flow hits this loan?" is the cognitive move. RSSG supplies the per-name judgment; the CLO state vector supplies the base rates.

## 5. Quant hooks (variables, datasets, signals)

**Variables (per loan *i*, CLO *c*, date *t*).**
- `ccc_share_c,t`, `ccc_excess_c,t = max(0, ccc_share − 7.5%)`, `min_ccc_px_c,t`, `jr_oc_cushion_c,t`, `id_cushion_c,t`, `reinvest_end_c`, `noncall_end_c`, `vintage_c` (pre-/post-2020)
- `forced_pressure_i,t = Σ_c w_i,c · f(jr_oc_cushion_c,t, ccc_excess_c,t, rating_i,t)`, the holder-weighted constraint tightness. This is Kundu's Bartik-style exposure turned into a live signal.
- `px_rel_par_i,t`, `softcall_expiry_i`, `pct_index_above_par_t` (repricing-window state), `repricing_hazard_i,t`
- `lme_flag_i,t`, `dual_track_gap_t = DR_dual − DR_payment` (regime variable)
- `aaa_spread_t` (new issue vs. refi/reset), `mm_minus_bsl_aaa_t`, `clo_etf_flow_t` (JAAA/CLOI/JBBB creations), `bl_fund_flow_t` (Morningstar weekly)
- `settle_days_i` (expected settlement distribution by par/distressed and agent), `etrade_share_t` (Octaura share of LSTA volume)

**Datasets.** CLO trustee reports via vendors (CLO-i/Creditflux, Intex, Octus); TRACE for CLO tranches (since 2019; $219bn in 2025); Morningstar LSTA/PitchBook LCD index constituents, prices and default series; LSTA quarterly trading and settlement studies (member-gated); Octaura and Versana feeds; N-PORT (quarterly from 2027–28) for loan-fund holdings; ETF daily holdings and creations (JAAA, SRLN, BKLN); NAIC Schedule D for insurer CLO holdings; Fed FSR and Z.1 series.

**Signals.**
1. *Forced-flow liquidity provision.* Buy loans with high `forced_pressure` after downgrades to CCC or LME events, conditional on fundamentals. Size by expected reversal; Elkamhi–Nozawa document temporary price pressure. Exit as the reversal completes.
2. *Repricing-hazard carry.* Underweight above-par loans with soft-call expiry inside 90 days when `pct_index_above_par` is above 50%. Prefer discount loans with the same rating, which carry convexity instead of short convexity.
3. *Contractual-arbitrage short.* Within a CLO-heavy name set, the higher-priced CCCs are what constrained CLOs sell. Fade their relative price ahead of month-end test dates.
4. *Wrapper-to-collateral lead–lag.* CLO ETF flows → AAA spread → reset count → loan secondary bid, a 2–8 week cascade to test.
5. *Vintage stress map.* Rank CLO vintages by OC cushion and CCC share (S&P: pre-pandemic 2.76% / 7.08%), and predict which manager cohorts sell first in a sector shock (software 2026).
6. *Dual-track regime switch.* Use `dual_track_gap` as a regime input to the default/recovery mixture (Ch 14) and to CLO equity valuations.
