**F1. Barzykin & Ciceri (HSBC), arXiv:2604.20406, "Bond Market Making with a Hit-Ratio Target"** [fetched arxiv.org/abs/2604.20406 and the full PDF, 2026-10-18]. v1 22 Apr 2026, v2 27 Jul 2026; q-fin.RM; MSC 93E20, 91B24, 91B70; 16 pages, 9 figures; no journal ref listed. Authors affiliated with HSBC, 8 Canada Square (ack. R. Anthony, J. Freeland, A. Cottrell; views not HSBC's).
- Setup: bonds m=1..M, mid dS=sigma dB; client tiers tau in T, targeted subset A; size ladder z_k; RFQ arrival intensity lambda^{s,k}_{m,tau}; fill probability f(delta); fill intensity Lambda = lambda f(delta) (eqs 1-3). Size-weighted instantaneous hit ratio r_tau(delta) = (1/W_tau) sum z_k Lambda (eq 7), W_tau = sum z_k lambda (eq 6). Objective (8): sup E[ X_T + Q'S_T - (eta/2) Q'Sigma Q - int ( (phi/2) Q'Sigma Q - sum_{tau in A} (kappa_tau W_tau / 2)(r_tau - r*_tau)^2 ) dt ]. NB the sign convention: penalty on squared deviation of an instantaneous hit ratio, a Markovian surrogate for the reporting-window KPI (the paper says a literal ratio-of-counts target needs cumulative-count state variables and breaks tractability).
- Key result 1 (dualization): -(kappa/2)(r-r*)^2 = inf_xi [ xi (r - r*) + xi^2/(2 kappa) ] (eq 12), so the HJB stays separable across (bond, tier, side, size): Hamiltonian H(p) = sup_delta Lambda(delta)(delta - p) (eq 14); exact optimal control delta* = f^{-1}( -H'(p)/lambda ) with p = Delta u - xi_tilde for targeted tiers, p = Delta u for untargeted (eqs 15-18). Dual closure (19): xi_tilde(t,q) = kappa_tau ( r*_tau + (1/W_tau) sum z_k H'(p) ) (implicit, since p depends on xi).
- Key result 2 (quadratic/BEGV approximation, after Bergault-Evangelista-Gueant-Vieira): u(t,q) ~ -(1/2) q'A(t)q - C(t); matrix Riccati A' = A D A - phi Sigma, A(T) = eta Sigma (eq 29) with D = diag(d_m), d_m = sum_{tau,s,k} z_k H''(0) (eq 30), so ALL tiers (targeted or not) contribute to inventory curvature; closed form A(t) = D^{-1/2} G [ (sinh(tG) + (eta/phi... ) G cosh) ... ] D^{-1/2} (eq 32), stationary A = sqrt(phi) D^{-1/2} (D^{1/2} Sigma D^{1/2})^{1/2} D^{-1/2} (eq 33). Dual closure becomes inventory-independent in the symmetric case: xi_tilde = kappa_tilde y_tau with 1/kappa_tilde = 1/kappa + (1/W) sum z_k H''(0) (eq 27) and y_tau = r* + (1/W) sum z_k H'(0) + (1/2W) sum z_k^2 A_mm H''(0) (eq 28). Asymmetric intensities add a linear term B(t)'q and a q-dependent dual (Appendix A, eqs 41-46).
- Key result 3 (linearized quotes): riskless spread delta_0 = argmax Lambda(delta) delta (eq 34); delta* ~ delta_0 + (1/c)[ e_m'A(+/-q + z_k e_m /2) - xi_tilde ] for targeted, same without xi for untargeted (eq 38), where c = 2 - f f''/(f')^2 evaluated at delta_0 (eq 36); H'(0) = -Lambda(delta_0), H''(0) = Lambda(delta_0)/(delta_0 c) (eq 37). So the paper's own decomposition has THREE terms: riskless spread + inventory-risk correction + hit-ratio correction (NOT five; the five-term decomposition is Niang 2026, F2).
- Numerics: single bond, single tier; size ladder z=(1,5,20) mm; intensities lambda=(500,200,50)/day; sigmoid fill with alpha=(2,1.5,1), beta=(2,1.5,1) per bp; r*=0.1; phi=1; T=1 day; qmax=100mm; eta=0; explicit Euler in JAX; kappa=10 or 100. Findings: BEGV with exact nonlinear quote map and exact xi(q) closure is "impressively accurate"; constant closure xi_0 good only for small inventories because exact xi(q) is approximately quadratic in q (Fig 3); a non-targeted "background tier" shares inventory risk, so the targeted tier's skew falls, the riskless spread at q=0 widens and the hit ratio for given kappa improves (Figs 6-7; background intensity 10x targeted); an offsetting correlated background bond (rho=0.8) cuts the skew and raises the hit ratio (Figs 8-9). Spending on the target "generally implies less P&L". All numerics are synthetic: no real RFQ data.

**F2. Niang, arXiv:2605.30643, "Quality-Adjusted Hit-Ratio Targeting in Corporate Bond Market Making" (subtitle on PDF: "Credit Alpha, Client Flow Quality, and Style-Aware Warehousing")** [abs page + full PDF read 2026-10-18]. SINGLE AUTHOR: Bouna Niang (the book's earlier "confirm authors" flag resolves to one author). v1 28 May 2026 (PDF dated 1 Jun 2026), q-fin.TR primary with q-fin.RM, CC BY 4.0, 17 pages, no journal ref. Footnote: published "in a personal capacity as an independent quantitative trader/researcher"; disclaims views of "any former employer, including HSBC and Citi". Builds directly on Barzykin-Ciceri (F1) and cites Avellaneda-Stoikov and GLFT. NO proprietary data; all numerics synthetic.
- Markout decomposition (eq 10): M_raw = M_factor + M_carry/rolldown + M_issuerRV + M_index/ETF + eps; only the residual eps defines toxicity chi_i^res = E[eps | bucket i, fill] (eq 11). Quality weight w_i = exp(-gamma chi_i^res) (eq 13). Quality-adjusted instantaneous hit ratio r^Q_tau = sum z_i w_i Lambda_i / W^Q_tau, W^Q = sum z_i w_i lambda_i (eq 14). Same dualization as BC: -(kW/2)(r-r*)^2 = inf_xi W[xi(r-r*) + xi^2/(2k)] (eq 16).
- Prop 1: after dualization the bucket contribution is z_i H_i( p_i[u] + chi_i^res - w_i xi_tau ) (eq 18); toxicity and the target enter as a scalar SHIFT of the Hamiltonian argument. Exact dual: xi_tau = kappa_tau [ r^Q,* + (1/W^Q) sum z_i w_i H_i'(p0_i - w_i xi) ] (eq 21) = kappa (r* - r^Q(xi)) (eq 22), p0_i = p_i[u] + chi_i^res. Prop 2: F(xi) = xi - kappa[...] has F' = 1 + (kappa/W^Q) sum z_i w_i^2 H_i'' > 0, so a unique root exists, solved by 1-D root finder (Newton in the simulations).
- Closed form for logistic fill f(delta) = 1/(1+exp(alpha + beta delta)): x(p) = W0(exp(-alpha - beta p - 1)) (Lambert W, principal branch), delta*(p) = p + (1+x)/beta (eqs 25-27), H'(p) = -lambda x/(1+x), H''(p) = lambda beta x/(1+x)^3 (eq 28).
- FIVE-TERM quote decomposition (eq 37): quote offset = riskless spread + inventory skew - credit-alpha skew + residual-toxicity charge - quality-hit-ratio subsidy. Riccati A' = A D A - phi Sigma (eq 33), D_mm = sum z_i H_i''(pbar_i^ref) (eq 34), stationary A = sqrt(phi) D^{-1/2}(D^{1/2} Sigma D^{1/2})^{1/2} D^{-1/2} (eq 35); linear alpha term ell = (AD)^{-1} mu (eq 36). Full quote argument pbar_i = p_i + chi_i^res - w_i xi_tau (eq 32). NOTE for the book: the 5 terms are Niang's, the 3 terms are BC's.
- Synthetic design: d=20 EUR IG bonds with factor covariance Sigma = B Omega B' + Sigma_eps; two flow types (carry/RV low residual toxicity; residual-toxic); Monte Carlo 300 paths per target/policy for the frontier, 100 paths per policy for the 8% benchmark over five trading days; PnL in bp*MM per day.

**F3. Fermanian, Gueant & Pu, arXiv:1511.07773 (v1 24 Nov 2015; v4 22 Mar 2017; q-fin.ST/q-fin.TR), "The behavior of dealers and clients on the European corporate bond market: the case of Multi-Dealer-to-Client platforms"; published Market Microstructure and Liquidity 2(03n04): 1750004 (volume year 2016 per the Barzykin-Ciceri reference list; arXiv v4 dated 2017)** [abs page + full PDF read 2026-10-18]. Funded by the Europlace Institute of Finance research initiative financed by BNP Paribas; authors at ENSAE-CREST and LPMA/IEF; "does not reflect the practices of BNP Paribas".
- Data: RFQs received by BNP Paribas (the "reference dealer") via Bloomberg FIT MD2C over nearly two years (2014-2015), a fraction of the RFQs; after filtering 209,069 buy RFQs and 272,189 sell RFQs (n>=1 only; n=0 excluded as "specific"). Platform limit: up to 6 dealers per RFQ on Bloomberg FIT, so n_i in {0..5} other dealers plus the reference dealer. Dealers see client identity and the number of dealers requested, not rivals' streamed prices, only the CBBT composite; the winner usually learns the cover price.
- Outcome taxonomy per RFQ: I=1 Done; I=2 Traded Away with J=1 Tied Traded Away, J=2 Covered (reference dealer had the unique second-best price), J=3 Other Traded Away (no information, may have been covered); I=3 Not Traded. Table 1 (buy): total 209,069 = 44,745 Done + 2,892 Tied + 44,054 Covered + 69,716 Other Traded Away + 47,662 Not Traded. Table 2 (sell): 272,189 = 52,624 + 3,562 + 55,726 + 112,147 + 48,130. n=5 dominates (109,530 buys, 123,662 sells); n=1 is 5,256 buys; Done share by n for buys: n=1 1845/5256 = 35.1%; n=5 20777/109530 = 19.0% (my arithmetic from the table).
- Model: client reservation value V_i (deal iff best dealer price below V_i for a buy; deal price = min(Y_i, min_k W_k,i)); competitors' quotes W_k,i i.i.d. from F, reduced by CBBT mid and normalised by CBBT half-spread Delta_i: (W - CBBT)/Delta; F0 = skew exponential power (SEP, Azzalini asymmetry; alpha<=2 for fat tails); G0 = Gaussian N(nu, tau^2). Likelihoods in closed form for each outcome: Done with cover C: n f(C)(1-F(C))^{n-1}(1-G(Y)); Done, no cover: (1-F(Y))^n (1-G(Y)); Tied: n(1-F(Y))^{n-1} f(Y)(1-G(Y)); Covered: n(1-F(Y))^{n-1}[ 1-(1-F(Y))(1-G(Y)) - int (1-F)g ]; Other traded away: 1-(1-F(Y))^n(1-G(Y)) - int_{-inf}^Y (1-F(v))^n g(v)dv; Not traded: int_{-inf}^Y (1-F(v))^n g(v)dv. Cover price is what identifies F from one side: it is an order statistic of rivals' quotes observed ONLY when you win (one-sided identification).
- Full-participation results (Tables 4-5): quotes of rivals get MORE conservative as n rises (buy-side SEP scale sigma 1.41 (n=1), 2.58, 3.81, 4.90, 5.72 (n=5); sell 1.16, 2.09, 3.08, 4.12, 4.67) and heavier right tail. The authors explicitly say this is a no-answer artefact, not discouragement: "Another important - and in fact better - explanation is related to dealers who do not answer".
- Partial-participation model (each requested dealer answers with prob p, binomial effective answers; Table 6): overall p about 0.40 buy (0.400) and 0.424 sell: only about 40% of requested dealers actually answer. Client reservation: nu = 1.72, tau = 1.92 (buy), nu* = -1.80, tau* = 1.68 (sell) in units of CBBT half-spread, i.e. on average clients think CBBT mid misprices by about 90% of the bid-ask spread; mean of dealers' quotes is only 30-40% of CBBT bid-ask spread away from mid (0.748 buy, -0.605 sell).
- Table 7 (buy, partial participation, answer probability by n): n=1 p=1.00; n=2 0.655; n=3 0.498; n=4 0.417; n=5 0.351. Table 8 (sell): 0.998, 0.695, 0.522, 0.437, 0.373. Expected answers (n+1)p(n) = 2.00, 1.97, 1.99, 2.09, 2.11 (buy): the paper states "always around 2 (in fact slightly above 2 for large values of n)", "clients often wait for only two answers before dealing". Mean of rivals' answered quotes (buy) falls 1.36 (n=1) -> 1.14 -> 0.933 -> 0.720 -> 0.647 (n=5): the monotonicity REVERSES in the partial model, i.e. answering dealers quote tighter when more dealers are requested ("the larger the number of requested dealers, the stronger the competition between the competing dealers"). Sell: -1.18, -1.01, -0.712, -0.577, -0.518.
- Client-side: reservation std tau falls with n for sells (7.35 at n=1 to 1.44 at n=5): clients requesting few dealers are heterogeneous (large size to limit leakage, or illiquid bond with fewer streamers, or off-platform agreement then 2-dealer RFQ to document best execution).
- CAUTION for the book: Ch 3 summary says quotes "become more conservative as the number of requested dealers rises" - that is the FULL-participation model; the paper's preferred partial-participation model reverses it. The robust finding is the effective-answers ~2 result, which motivates n_eff.

**F5. Hendershott, Li, Livdan, Schurhoff & Venkataraman (2026), "Quote Competition in Corporate Bonds", Journal of Finance 81(4): 2165-?, August 2026, DOI 10.1111/jofi.70048 (open access, CC BY-NC-ND)** [read full published PDF from https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/hender/Quote_Competition.pdf, header "THE JOURNAL OF FINANCE VOL. LXXXI, NO. 4, AUGUST 2026", 2026-10-18]. Authors: Hendershott (Haas), Dan Li (Federal Reserve Board; views not the Board's), Livdan (Haas, CEPR), Schurhoff (Lausanne, SFI, CEPR), Venkataraman (SMU; former FINRA visiting economist). Hendershott discloses expert-witness work.
- Data: BondCliQ (CliQ) dealer "runs" (emailed indicative quotes) Oct 1 2019 to May 1 2020; 35 dealers; 8.5 million bid/ask quotes on roughly 24,000 bonds; CliQ dealers ~25% of TRACE dealer-to-client volume (range 15%-40%), mid-tier dealers (largest CliQ dealer in top 10 TRACE; 19 in top 50); the very largest dealers do not feed CliQ. Matched to TRACE (and to insurers' trades).
- Headline numbers (verified in text): quoting raises the dealer's probability of trading the bond that day by 4.8 percentage points (extensive margin) and the number of trades by 4.4% (intensive margin); quote presence improves client execution quality by 6 to 7 bp (Table VII Panel B coefficient delta = 0.066, "4bps to 7bps" in Section IV text); pass-through of quote quality to execution quality is 84% to 110% (beta = 0.838 pooled, 1.102 conditional on quoting). Quotes matter for large trades (>= $100K); not significant for small trades (<$100K).
- Relationship substitution: insurers' likelihood of trading with a non-relationship CliQ dealer: nonrelationship dealers handle 23% of trades when they quote vs 11% when they do not.
- Imperfect competition: the best-quoting dealer attracts only 15% of trades; Table VIII Panel A trade-through (trade at a price worse than the best dealer quote less than one hour old): best-quoting dealer 17% overall (16% large, 23% small); other CliQ dealers about 31% overall (25% large, 40% small); non-CliQ about 33%. Footnote: Harris (2015) finds 43% trade-through when defined on all standing bids and offers. "Good quotes are firm, bad quotes get improved": dealers with inferior quotes give more price improvement over their quote than dealers with the best quotes (quote leakage -> price matching, which dampens the incentive to quote aggressively).
- Not zero-sum (shift-share / Bartik-type IV following Goldsmith-Pinkham-Sorkin-Swift and Borusyak-Hull-Jaravel): a one-SD rise in predicted aggregate quote supply raises CliQ dealers' share of bond-day volume by 2.9pp (OLS) / 2.1pp (IV), total trading activity by 3.9% (OLS) / 4.7% (IV), and improves bond-level execution quality by 2.3 bp.
- Determinants of quoting: dealers quote to manage own inventory but are largely insensitive to the inventory of connected dealers; lead underwriters quote their deals more; higher-rated, longer-duration, older, larger-issue bonds are quoted more; quoting shows NO relation to dealers' private information on rating changes.
- Equity analogy: no Reg NMS-style trade-through rule or quoting obligation in bonds; best-execution unclear (Bessembinder-Spatt-Venkataraman 2020).
- Relevance to RFQ engine: shows (a) value of being visible to non-relationship clients (the franchise subsidy has an external-validity anchor) and (b) that indicative runs and firm RFQ quotes are different channels, so the dealer's run (indicative quote) activity is a candidate input to whether it is invited onto a client's RFQ panel (an inference, not a result of the paper).

**F6. Ciceri, Cottrell, Freeland, Frye, Hirai, Intallura, Kang, Lee, Mitra, Ohno, Pemmaraju, Proissl, Quanz, Rajan, Shimada, Yograj (HSBC + IBM Quantum), arXiv:2509.17715 (22 Sep 2025, quant-ph), "Enhanced fill probability estimates in institutional algorithmic bond trading using statistical learning algorithms with quantum computers"** [abs search + full PDF read 2026-10-18; press: fi-desk.com "Quantum leap: HSBC, IBM improve bond RFQ fill rate by 34%"; HSBC said the test did not involve live trades]. Authors alphabetical; Ciceri, Cottrell, Freeland (HSBC Credit Algorithmic Trading) overlap with the Barzykin-Ciceri acknowledgments.
- Data: 294 trading days (1 Sep 2023 to 29 Oct 2024) of intraday algorithmic RFQ responses: 1,073,926 RFQs linked to 5,166 bonds and 747 tickers, primarily European corporate bonds; microsecond timestamps; each RFQ is a 216-feature event vector (RFQ plus market state with time lags, bond/ticker/sector levels) with binary label 1 = the submitted response was accepted (the fill), about 37% positive. Experimental window: 69 trading days from 24 Jul 2024, bonds not maturing in-window: 143,912 RFQs, 3,425 bonds, 652 tickers, about 9 million intraday signals; 16,000-event random sample per day.
- Metric: out-of-sample test AUC under a rolling, time-ordered "trade execution backtest" with a blinding period (the model is denied the most recent market information for 0 to 4 trading days). Four classical learners: logistic regression, gradient boosting (XGB), random forest, neural net.
- RESULT (classical baseline): average test AUC about 0.63 +/- 0.04, flat across 0-4 days of blinding, "no sensitivity to temporal trading signals". Noiseless quantum simulator features: about 0.60 +/- 0.03. IBM Heron hardware-transformed features ("shorter" circuit) median test AUC about 0.75 +/- 0.02 without blinding, decaying toward the simulator level; "longer" circuit about 0.97 +/- 0.02 decaying linearly; Table 2 hardware rows e.g. LR 0.75, XGB 0.77, RF 0.75, NN 0.74 vs classical 0.61-0.64; headline "relative gain of up to ~34%" in out-of-sample test scores. Reproduced on ibm_fez and ibm_marrakesh (median AUC within ~1%).
- The authors' own caveat: heuristic method, "without any generalization guarantees", "the magnitude of the time-dependent out-of-sample AUC uplifts seems questionable and cannot be explained", the role of hardware noise "is not understood"; "we do not infer any generalizable theory or causal economic effect".
- Use for the book: (1) the credible, boring benchmark is AUC about 0.6 for RFQ fill prediction on production-scale data with 200+ features, i.e. fill prediction is a weakly separable problem and the dual-variable/hit-ratio machinery should not be fed a fill-curve with false precision; (2) a 0.97 AUC from a noisy-hardware feature map that decays with a "blinding" gap is the classic signature of leakage/construction artefact, and the book should treat it as an exhibit in why backtests for fill models need time-blinding and event-matching audits; (3) the cleanest statement in the paper is the Figure-1 architecture: fill-probability estimator feeds the quote optimiser.

**F7. Marin (Martinez), Ardanza-Trevijano & Sabio (BBVA CIB Madrid; Univ. Navarra), "Causal interventions in bond multi-dealer-to-client platforms", PLOS ONE 21(1): e0341369, published 27 Jan 2026, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0341369 (open access); arXiv:2506.18147 (v1 June 2025; v2 17 Oct 2025, q-fin.TR)** [read arXiv v2 full PDF 2026-10-18; PLOS/PMC page itself blocked by captcha for fetch, so publication metadata is from search results (PLOS ONE record, UNAV portal) and the Barzykin-Ciceri citation [19]; published numbers may differ slightly from v2].
- Framework: RfQ process as a probabilistic graphical model with latent nodes (client reservation spread delta_res, dealer quotes, price-discovery intent PD, information asymmetry IA, axe) and do-calculus; three dealer interventions: (i) optimal pricing, (ii) revenue estimation under fixed policy, (iii) matching axes to likely client demand ("uplift" of a call). Back-door results: the minimal adjustment set for the hit-probability model P(hit | do(delta), RfQ) is volatility sigma, RfQ features RF, bond features BF, client features CF (eq 24 integrates over the excluded variables); the revenue-on-hit model P(R>0 | do(delta), hit) CANNOT be identified from sigma and RF alone: conditioning on RS (RfQ status = hit) is a collider on the path delta -> RS <- delta_res <- IA -> mu -> R, so one must model the information-asymmetry state IA explicitly (eq 25). I.e. the winner's-curse / selection problem is a collider bias, stated in do-calculus.
- Models: (a) generative, extending Fermanian-Gueant-Pu (SEP-distributed rival spreads, Gaussian client reservation on normalised half-spread delta/Delta, binomial answering, linear parametrisation of all parameters in bond/client/RfQ features); (b) logistic regression; (c) LightGBM.

Selected features: normalised half-spread, 30-day yield volatility, bond DV01, average days between buy and sell RfQs per ISIN (15-day), 15-day average dealers per ISIN, days to maturity, number of dealers in competition, RfQ DV01.
- Table 1 (test set): ROC-AUC / balanced Brier skill score (BBSS): generative 0.742 / 0.238; logistic 0.684 / 0.446; LightGBM 0.743 / 0.406; majority class 0.5 / 0. Generative matches LightGBM on AUC (0.742 vs 0.743), has worse class-balanced calibration, but is monotone in the spread by construction (Fig 5: LightGBM violates monotonicity: hit probability does not always rise as the quote tightens). Generative is slower at inference.
- Use: (1) gives the book a public, citable fill-curve benchmark with real RFQ data: AUC about 0.74 on EGBs with 8 features, vs about 0.63 on HSBC's 216-feature European corporate dataset (F6) - the difference is partly instrument (government vs corporate), label definition and hit rate (5.6% vs about 37%); not comparable head to head; (2) monotonicity in the quote is a REQUIREMENT of the HJB (f must be decreasing), so a gradient-boosted fill model must be wrapped in a monotone constraint before it is plugged into Barzykin-Ciceri's inverse-f formula; (3) collider bias on winners: markouts conditional on a win are biased; the paper gives the adjustment set.

**F8. Kargar, Lester, Plante & Weill (2025), "Sequential Search for Corporate Bonds", Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia Working Paper 25-08 (March 2025; paper dated 20 Feb 2025), DOI 10.21799/frbp.wp.2025.08; earlier NBER WP 31904 (2023)** [read PDF https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/working-papers/2025/wp25-08.pdf 2026-10-18; RePEc handle fip:fedpwp:99648]. The acknowledgements thank "Antoinette Schoar (editor)" and two referees, so it is in journal review; I did not find a confirmed journal publication, so cite as WP.
- Data: complete record of all customer inquiries and dealer replies on MarketAxess (MKTX) 3 Jan 2017 to 31 Mar 2021, matched to TRACE to catch failed electronic inquiries completed by voice. MKTX about 21% of total US corporate bond volume in 2022Q3. RFQ goes to the customer's pre-authorised dealer list; dealers see the customer's identity; reply not obligatory; the customer accepts one offer or rejects all. Auction 5-20 minutes. All-to-all "Open Trading" added 2012.
- Key results: inquiries fail to trade about 30% of the time (consistent with HM 2015's about 30% non-trade); benchmark trade (odd-lot IG purchase, above-median issue size, well-connected customer) takes about 2 days to complete after an initial failure ("2-3 days" in the abstract); block trades (>$5mm) take about 1 day longer than micro (<$100K); bonds below median amount outstanding +0.5 day; selling is about twice as fast as buying; customers in the bottom 60% of "connectedness" (number of replies per inquiry) take nearly twice as long as the top decile; initial-inquiry failure rate is about 14% for the most connected customers on the benchmark trade and above 50% for the least connected on harder trades; customers who reject the initial quotes eventually trade at a later inquiry about 40% of the time at a price on average about 10 bp better than the best initial offer (gains from search); the distribution of replies does not deteriorate over the search ("fairly stationary"); dealer inventory drives responses: dealers are more likely to reply to a customer buy (sell) and quote better when holding more (less) of the bond.
- Use: (a) RFQ outcomes are a sequence, not an independent draw: a "Not Traded" RFQ is the first of a repeated game with the same client, so a dealer that wins the third inquiry is partly the beneficiary of the first two; the hit ratio per RFQ understates the dealer's conversion of client intent into trades; (b) justifies 1-, 5- and 20-day markout horizons (the book's Ch 3 uses it); (c) supplies the stationarity result that lets a desk model re-inquiry as a search problem (reservation price) rather than re-pricing against a moving mid.

**F9. Barzykin (HSBC), arXiv:2603.10569 (11 Mar 2026, q-fin.RM), "Win-score promotion gates in aggregator-routed RFQ markets: A two-tier stochastic control model"** [abs + first pages of PDF read 2026-10-18; 12 pages, 8 figures, single author, HSBC, no journal ref]. Model: tier A = aggregator flow whose opportunity intensity is multiplied by a promotion gate G(R) driven by the dealer's slow win score R (stylised "top list + exploration": the N_top highest-score LPs always invited, the rest sampled at random from the pool); tier B = background flow not gated and not updating the score. Win probability p_z(delta) on a size ladder; conditional on winning the edge is delta + mu_z(delta), mu <= 0 an adverse-selection correction (benchmark constant slippage -theta_z). Reduced HJB in Bergault-Gueant operator form with explicit win/lose branches for the score; optimal controls from derivatives of 1-D reduced Hamiltonians (inverse map from optimal win probability to offset); adiabatic separation of fast inventory and slow score (long-memory EMA); quasi-stationary inventory curvature scaling and a 1-D score drift field. For steep (logistic) gates the score dynamics show fold bifurcations, bistability and hysteresis, giving an endogenous "campaign vs harvest" pattern in optimal quoting; background flow stabilises inventory mixing even when the dealer is weakly promoted. Intro cites Oomen (2017) that the winner's curse in an aggregator strengthens as more LPs compete (rationale for limiting the LPs per RFQ). Useful claim for the book: "how many dealers are asked is a first-order primitive in RFQ economics"; and (empirical fact stated, not tested here) inclusion and rank depend on slowly varying performance scores. FX-focused; extension to bonds is an inference. No empirical data.

**F12. Riggs, Onur, Reiffen & Zhu, working-paper version "Mechanism Selection and Trade Formation on Swap Execution Facilities: Evidence from Index CDS" (CFTC, 29 Sep 2017) = Journal of Finance-adjacent published version "Swap trading after Dodd-Frank: Evidence from index CDS", JFE 137(3): 857-886 (2020)** [read the CFTC PDF https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/idc/groups/public/@economicanalysis/documents/file/oce_mechanism_selection.pdf 2026-10-18; published-version abstract via Semantic Scholar]. - Data: message-level data for May 2016 from Bloomberg SEF and Tradeweb SEF, index CDS (CDX, iTraxx).
