# TREND DOSSIER: Re-default and recovery dataset (a hand-built LME-to-outcome dataset, 2016-2026, and the published moments it must match)

Researcher session date: 2026-10-18. Queue entry: state/research_queue_ed2.json -> re_default_and_recovery_dataset. Feeds: ch14, ch13b, ch11, ch05.

## 1. Headline trends

1. **The default label is now a modeling choice, and DEs dominate it.** Distressed exchanges were about 50% of rated US default events for three straight years to 2024 against 32% in 2010-2023 (S&P via FTI), 64% of Moody's 1H25 defaults, about 65% for 2025 (Moody's), and nearly 60% of defaulted US leveraged loans since early 2024 (Fitch via FTI). Moody's HY default rate was 5.8% including and 2.1% excluding the 51 distressed restructurings (June 2025); the leveraged-loan 2024 pair was 4.0% vs 1.5% (Marquette).
2. **Re-default is fast and cohort-dependent.** About 46% of 2023 DEs had re-defaulted by April 2025, mostly in under a year against a 3.5-year historical average (Moody's via Bloomberg Law). Roe and Rotaru's 89 coercive non-pro-rata LMEs: only 22% avoided both bankruptcy and re-default within two years. Moody's long-run 1,173-borrower study is gentler (about 1 in 4 hard default, over 1 in 3 hard or repeat event, over 70% of hard defaults within two years, under 0.5 pp a year after year five). The numbers are different objects, not contradictions (section 3).
3. **Recovery is conditional on participation and on which event is being priced.** Moody's first-lien DE recovery was 71% at end-2024 (55% in 2023); the excluded Serta lenders were marked at 25 cents and a court awarded $261.13mm of damages; Octus's value-transfer table runs from 100% (Wesco, Mitel, Robertshaw) to 12-38% (Valcour, EyeCare, GoTo, Apex). The queue's Fitch 23%-vs-53% emergence-recovery contrast could not be verified this session.
4. **Law is the exogenous regime variable.** Serta (5th Cir., 31 Dec 2024) and Mitel (1st Dept, same day) split on a single word; Wesco's bankruptcy-court win for excluded holders was reversed on 8 Dec 2025; cert was denied in Serta on 10 Nov 2025; ConvergeOne extended equal-treatment scrutiny into Chapter 11. Deal count fell from 38 (2024) to 24 (2025) but LMEs stayed two-thirds of default activity.
5. **Measurement is migrating to private credit.** Moody's CE default rate is 4.0% reported and 5.8% with PIK amendments and maturity extensions (607 issuers, June 2026); PIMCO's BDC shadow rate is about 19%; Fitch's private-credit rate hit a record 6.1% (Aug 2026); Proskauer's index is 2.51%. Same assets, 2.5% to 19%, depending on what counts.
6. **The unresolved cohort is large.** 2024 was the LME peak (38 deals), the 2027-29 wall is $580bn of loans and $625bn of HY, Caa-equivalents are over half of 2027-28 CE maturities, and live situations (Optimum, Hertz, Cogent) are right-censored observations. Any 2026 re-default rate is a lower bound that rises as cohorts age.

## 3. Narrative: drivers and the 2026-2028 outlook

**Why the published numbers disagree.** Each moment answers a different question, so a dataset must carry the fields that separate them. (a) Population: Moody's 1,173 soft events (1979-2026) mix old pro-rata exchanges with the modern LME; Roe and Rotaru's 89 are coercive non-pro-rata deals; S&P's 33/56/10 split is for CLO-held issuers after an LME in 2024. (b) Event: hard default (missed payment or bankruptcy), "hard or repeat event", and "re-defaulted or rated CCC+ or below" are three different outcomes; the last is a state, not an event. (c) Horizon and vintage: Moody's 46% is the 2023 cohort at about 1.5-2 years; Moody's 1-in-4 is lifetime. (d) Censoring: cohorts from 2024-26 are mostly unresolved.

An illustrative conversion (my arithmetic, constant-hazard approximation, so CHECK before reuse): 46% re-default at roughly 1.5-2 years implies about 0.3-0.4 per year; Roe-Rotaru's 22% clean survival at two years implies about 0.76 per year; Moody's lifetime figures (25% hard default, at least 70% of them inside two years) imply about 0.10 per year in the first two years (about 0.13 if "hard or repeat" is used). The modern front-end hazard is thus roughly 3-6 times the long-run one. The FT's "about 80% within three years" (via Wachtell, source unread) would imply about 0.54 per year, higher than Moody's modern cohort, so it likely counts a wider event set. S&P's 63% "avoid Chapter 11" (via the HLS Antill post, no sample given) and the 33% filed (via Wellington) are near-complements but come from different S&P studies, and the 56% bucket shows that avoiding Chapter 11 is not the same as being healthy. None of this can be settled without row-level data, which is the case for the build.

**Drivers.** (1) Contract permissiveness, then repair: 40% of 600+ loans restricted uptiers before 2020, 85% of new loans by mid-2022, yet drop-down and amend-extend-exchange routes remained. (2) Cov-lite scale: over 90% of the Morningstar/LSTA index in 2024, so default is triggered by liquidity and maturity, not covenants. (3) Sponsor and private-credit incentives: distress concentrates in PE-backed, loan-only structures; borrowers prefer runway over a costly Chapter 11, and participating creditors get an inside track (Wachtell). (4) Courts: each ruling re-prices the technique; Robertshaw (money damages only), Serta (damages $261.13mm), Mitel (upheld, then Chapter 11 anyway). (5) Creditor technology: co-ops (Optimum 99%) shift the contest from exclusion to carve-out premiums. (6) Measurement: DE-inclusive LCD series exist only from Dec 2016 (Fed FSR), so the 2016-2026 window is the full history of consistent public data.

**Time-to-failure drift (small n, conditional on filing).** Dates verified this session: J.Crew Dec 2016 to 4 May 2020 (about 41 months); Serta June 2020 to 23 Jan 2023 (31); Robertshaw May 2023 to 15 Feb 2024 (9); Wesco Mar 2022 to Jun 2023 (about 15); Mitel 2022 (date disputed) to Mar 2025 (about 30-36, or longer if Jones Day's 2020 is right). Carried from the case dossier: Revlon 25, Envision 9-13, Saks 13. First-generation deals took 2.5-3.5 years to fail, consistent with Moody's 3.5-year historical average; 2023-vintage deals failed in under a year, consistent with Moody's 46%. Survivors (Boardriders, Lumen, Carvana) are absent from this list by construction.

**Outlook 2026-2028 (inference from the evidence, not a forecast from a source).** Baselines are benign at the index level (Moody's spec-grade default drifting toward 3.2% by end-2026; S&P near 4.0% with 3.75% by June 2027), but the structure is not: PIK use at 17% of Moody's CE issuers, Caa-equivalents over half of 2027-28 maturities, 2027 CSC Holdings maturities of about $6.2bn, and a record Fitch private-credit rate. The 2024 record cohort reaches its 24-36 month window in 2026-27, so measured re-default shares for 2024 deals should rise from today's levels. The Fifth Circuit decisions pending on Wesco and ConvergeOne decide whether the 2026-28 wave uses multi-step uptiers (if Crane stands) or consent-heavy, carve-out co-op structures (if equal-treatment scrutiny spreads). Expect headline default rates to understate stress while DE-inclusive and shadow measures converge upward; the Moody's and PIMCO series are the leading ones to watch.

## 4. Implications for a systematic credit desk and for the RSSG-to-quant reader

**For a systematic credit desk.**
- *Labels.* Train PD and spread models on a multi-state outcome (cure, further LME, payment default, Chapter 11), not a binary default flag; a DE-exclusive label throws away 50-65% of events and a DE-inclusive one mixes cures with failures. Report both (Moody's 5.8% vs 2.1%; Marquette 4.0% vs 1.5%).
- *Recovery.* Model LGD by participation status and by which event is priced (DE recovery, 71% in 2024, versus recovery at the later bankruptcy). Treat the excluded-lender distribution as a bimodal mixture (about par after an asset sale for Boardriders and Lumen per the earlier case dossier, 25 cents for Serta, about 3% deficiency claims for Robertshaw) rather than a Beta mean.
- *Screens.* Contract tokens (open-market-purchase language, sacred rights, J.Crew/Serta/Chewy blockers), cov-lite status, PIK share, interest coverage near 1.0x, Caa share, distance to the maturity wall, and co-op coverage are all observable ex ante; build an LME-propensity score for the defensive HY sleeve and the CLO-equity overlay.
- *Timing.* Re-default is front-loaded (over 70% of hard defaults inside two years), so post-LME names should carry a high but decaying hazard, a window in which a desk can price rather than avoid.
- *Index risk.* Published default rates lag stress: use DE-inclusive and shadow measures as the forecast target, and expect the PitchBook with-LMT series to diverge from the ex-LMT one.
- *Right-censoring.* Never compute a re-default share on the 2024-26 cohorts without survival methods; a naive share understates by construction.

**For the RSSG-to-quant reader.** The banker's edge is the unwritten fields: who sits in the co-op, what the sponsor will do about the next maturity, which counsel and adviser teams are engaged (FTI's Octus count: legal counsel on over 60% of 124 LMT mandates; Hertz working with PJT Partners while lenders hire Milbank and Evercore/Gibson Dunn). The quant converts these into dated, public proxies: 8-K disclosures of co-op coverage (Optimum's 99%), adviser-hire headlines, creditor-group formation (Huang-Lewellen-Wang link coalition announcements to higher bond prices), and BDC mark paths. The honest message is that the banker's tacit state (who will vote with whom) is partly recoverable from filings and partly not; the dataset should record which fields are inferred.
